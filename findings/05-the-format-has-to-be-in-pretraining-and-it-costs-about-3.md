# The tool-call format must be in pretraining, and costs about 3%

**Full result:** The format has to be in pretraining, and it costs about 3%

**Cost:** 69 s of scoring on the 1 card first cited here (`nano20m_pilot`); it also reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (`nano20m`). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' `seconds`; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

DATA.md §4 makes a bet most small-model recipes don't: put the tool-call format in
*pretraining* at 15% of the budget, rather than fine-tuning it in afterward. The argument is
that at 250M there isn't capacity for a light fine-tune to install a format the base model has
never encountered. `nano20m_pilot` is that bet at nano scale — the same 25.6M shape and the
same 12,000 iterations as `nano20m`, differing in one field, `data_dir`. It reads
`data/mix_pilot` (85% TinyStories, 15% generated temporal) instead of TinyStories alone.

28.7 minutes, best val 0.3881 at iter 11,500. Against the frozen ruler:

| row | `nano20m` | `nano20m_pilot` | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| `narrative` nats/byte | 0.3974 | 0.4117 | 1.1MB |
| `temporal` nats/byte | 3.8911 | **0.1000** | 1.0MB |
| `temporal.route` | 0.0% | **99.8%** | 400 |
| `temporal.copy` | 0.0% | **100.0%** | 353 |
| `refusal.clarify` | 0.0% | **100.0%** | 47 |
| `refusal.no_guess` | — of 0 | **100.0%** | 47 |

**Every behaviour row went from zero to approximately one**, and `temporal.copy` is the one
that matters most. It scores the turn *after* the tool answers, and a date that is not the
tool's fails even when it is correct — so 100% says he is carrying the tool's answer rather
than doing date arithmetic in the weights, which is the single behaviour VISION §5.2 is built
around. `refusal.no_guess` reading 47 items rather than `--` of 0 is the gated denominator
working: nothing the old checkpoint emitted was in-format enough to score.

**The narrative row is the price tag, and it is the useful number.** 0.3974 → 0.4117 is what
spending 15% of a fixed budget on temporal costs the other 85% — the first measured point on
DATA.md §1's claim that quality per token is the only lever. The true cost is smaller than the
3.6% it looks like, because `nano20m`'s 0.3974 was mildly flattered: 16 of 16 narrative probes
were in its val split, so it was selected on the text it was then scored on. `mix_pilot`
reserves those bytes, and the pilot's number is clean on both splits. Format exposure for
under 3.6% of general fluency is cheap for what it buys.

**A val figure would have told you the opposite, and this is the clearest example yet of why
`evals/` exists.** The pilot's blended val is 0.3881 against `nano20m`'s 0.4265 — apparently a
9% improvement, entirely an artefact. Per-source val at the same checkpoint reads narrative
0.4487, temporal **0.0946**: the temporal slice is templated and trivially predictable, so
mixing 15% of it into the validation set drags the blend down while general fluency
*regressed*. Two runs' val figures are not on one scale, the blended number moved the wrong
way, and only the fixed ruler caught it.

**What this does not show**, in descending order of how much it should temper the result:

- ~~**The bet is half-tested.**~~ The control arms have since run — the sixth finding below.
  The short version: "fine-tuning it wouldn't" is false for three of the four behaviour rows
  and true for the one that matters.
- **99.8% is format compliance, not generalization.** The eval items are drawn from the same
  334 expressions the training slice was generated from. Anchor dates are structurally held
  out, so this is not memorized answers — but it says nothing about a phrasing the generator
  has never emitted, and real speech is full of those. The surface-variety floor in
  `check_temporal.py` is the instrument for that; the eval set cannot be.
  **Tested at last — the twenty-eighth finding, and it was right.** On carriers no generator
  emits the row falls 398 → 348 of 400 — against the thirtieth finding's paraphrase-arm floor of
  24 rather than the frozen 2 this originally quoted, still 2.1x — every failure a
  `wrong_expression`. What it was wrong about is scope: the same arm leaves `temporal.copy` at
  353/353, so the caveat applies to recognising the request and not to deferring to the tool.
- ~~**`tool.validity` and `tool.selection` are still `--`.**~~ Both are live as of the tenth
  finding, and on a checkpoint that has seen one tool they read 61.9% and 0.0%. The caveat
  underneath survives unchanged: what has been demonstrated is **one tool, generated by one
  program**, and no model has yet been trained on the other four.

### The decontamination flag no longer cries wolf

The temporal row of the probe search read `CONTAMINATED` on a corpus that was provably clean:
6 of 16 probes turned up in `train.bin`, and they were exactly the windows containing no date.
Every probe carrying a held-out `2029-` date was absent. The search was finding shared
*format* — the `<|call|>` envelope and the templated utterances the slice exists to teach —
which no amount of n-gram decontamination could remove without destroying what the row
measures.

That was going to read `contaminated` by design on every future mix, and a status field that
fails on every run is one everybody learns to ignore. So probes now come in two populations: a
**bearing** probe is cut from a window carrying a date in the eval text's own anchor year or
later, which the generator stops a full year short of and cannot reach; a **format** probe is a
uniform window. Only bearing probes set the status. Format hits are reported as context and
can never fail a run. Both `mix_pilot` slices now read `clean`.

Bearing probes are *selected* rather than filtered, so all 16 carry a date instead of the 9
that happened to — the fix roughly doubles the test's power rather than just quieting it. And
a text that yields no bearing probe reads `indeterminate`, never `clean`, because a check that
cannot fail is the thing this harness was written to prevent. `check_evals.py` carries the
false positive as a regression test alongside the true positive it must not cost.

`HARNESS_VERSION` stays at 1: no scored quantity changed meaning, and every row above came
back byte-identical after the fix.
