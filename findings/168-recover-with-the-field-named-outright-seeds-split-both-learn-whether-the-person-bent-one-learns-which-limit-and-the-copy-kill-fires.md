## What it licenses

By the table: **a direction, no third seed.** Read with finding 167, the two
arms bracket the cause: with the limit named by meaning, nothing formed at
either seed -- not even whether the person bent; with the key word in the
request, the bend formed at both seeds and the limit at one. **The meaning
hop is the larger wall, and the choice of limit is fragile at nano even
without it.** For recover that points one way: **the flex reading is the
runtime's** -- a table of flex phrasings names the limit, as combine's clause
became a lookup -- and the move can be the runtime's too, since the one part
that installed at both seeds (bent or not) is the same lookup's other answer.
For the ladder's other menu rungs (branch, loop, replan): a pick after the
observation can depend on an earlier key when the key is the option's own
word; when it must be read by meaning, two rungs in a row (findings 156, 167)
formed a fixed ranking instead. The copy kill is the third reason not to put
this payload in the run as it stands.

**What it does not settle.** Why seed 1337 formed a ranking with the word
right there; whether a second pass or a lower share of `ask` traces would
steady it; and whether the digit shift in the copy band is this payload's or
nano's noise between payloads -- a third seed of either arm would say, and
none was registered.

## Amended 2026-10-14 -- the copy kill is not readable (finding 170)

The standing kill above was read with a binomial half-width of 3-4 items.
Seeds of one arm on identical data spread 0-54 items on `tool.copy_wide`
(median 8, 90th percentile 20), so by EXPERIMENTS §5.2, and §5.7 as amended,
the gap here is **not readable**: every seed of this arm would have to sit
below arm E2's lowest seed (293) by more than 20 items. The hand-read misses
are real outputs and stay as written. What does not stand is "the copy kill
fires at both seeds" as a reason against the payload (finding 170).
