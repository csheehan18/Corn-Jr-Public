## What it licenses

By the pre-registration, its second outcome with the third beside it: **the
lines add up on the far shelf and do not reach the question he is asked,
and shared pieces still scramble -- less, not none.**

* **A far line is a far line.** One step or a hundred, in every weight or
  in the word table, what is written from afar is read from afar. Findings
  229 and 230 together close the simple form of the distance idea: asking
  from further back does not teach the next-piece question.
* **Nothing washes out and nothing compounds.** The first 50 pairs keep
  their far read with 50 more on top; the per-pair gain is the same at 50
  and at 100; the damage adds up a little faster than the steps.
* **Gentle is cheaper than hard, again.** Half the strength: half the far
  pair, a fifth of the damage, half the scrambling (finding 226 saw the
  same). If far lines are ever worth writing, they are worth writing small
  and often -- which is a training run, not a press.
* **The scrambling is the place, not the question.** Far or next-piece, a
  fifth of the pairs nobody touched move a quarter nat. Finding 226's
  licence stands: a private place has to be a row a word.

**What it does not settle.** Whether a far read is worth having for its own
sake: the book answers the ruler from afar, and a net that held far lines
could be *asked* from afar by the runtime rather than through a sentence --
nothing here read the ruler that way. A trunk trained with both questions
from the first step, where the two stores might not be two. Pairs met often.
