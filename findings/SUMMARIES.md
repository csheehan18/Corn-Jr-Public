written.** This is the third column of `README.md`'s table, moved here on

read, and an index nobody scans is how two findings came to share the number

section below reads as a restatement of the heading, that is because the
index's original cell was one — the finding itself is the long form.

**Nothing here replaces a finding.** Each section is a summary written beside
the record, not the record: where a summary and its finding disagree, the
finding wins. The finding is linked from every heading.

## 54 — Thirty-two windows to a hundred and twenty-eight, and both predictions about why were wrong

[Fifty-fourth finding](54-thirty-two-windows-to-a-hundred-and-twenty-eight-and-both-predictions-about-why-were-wrong.md)

`StaticCache` sized every row to `block_size` -- 40 MB to hold a 147-token sequence -- because a conversation cannot know its length and a worker can; `slots` took 64 windows from **390 seconds of paging to 737 ms**, and 32 windows 33% faster on a change made for footprint. Then `--dtype bf16` was predicted to win big at batch 1 (bandwidth-bound) and little at 64 (compute-bound) and did the opposite: **2% slower at N=1, 1.73x at N=64**, because 32 tokens in 336 ms is kernel-launch-bound -- what `GraphedDecoder` fixes and `BatchedReader` deliberately does not capture. Second decode-time optimisation mispredicted after `torch.compile`, the same error both times: assuming which resource was scarce instead of measuring both ends. Ceiling 32 -> at least 128 windows, **4,096 tokens in one batch in 534 ms**, beating fp32 at half the batch. Four windows still cost only 6% more than one, so a single-process device keeps its quorum. bf16 ships off by default and named in `wiring`: every published row is fp32, and greedy decoding turns a swapped logit into a different sentence

## 58 — The corpus was not the prior, and tripling the type share moved it by nothing

## 62 — The grammar knows the next 44% of the bytes, and the tokenizer is what stops it saying so

[Sixty-second finding](62-the-grammar-knows-the-next-forty-four-per-cent-of-the-bytes-and-the-tokenizer-is-what-stops-it-saying-so.md)

**The decode grammar already determines 44.5% of the bytes of a call and can emit almost none of them, and the gap is the tokenizer.** No GPU, no checkpoint: 400 real calls from `tools.txt` walked through the real `CallConstraint`. ## 64 — Prefill costs launches, not tokens, so 42% redundancy was worth 0.22 ms

[Sixty-fourth finding](64-prefill-costs-launches-not-tokens-so-forty-two-per-cent-redundancy-was-worth-nought-point-two-milliseconds.md)

**42.1% of a turn's prefill is tokens the previous round already read, and removing it is worth 0.22 ms.** A turn is a round per tool call plus one for the reply; `stream` opens with `cache.reset()`, so round 2 re-encodes from token zero everything round 1 read *and wrote*. Counted over 300 real single-call turns: **49,001 of 116,338 prefill tokens = 42.1%**, mean 163 a turn, with the 151-token declaration re-encoded every round of every turn. Keeping it is *exact* rather than approximate -- `wpe` makes position i's K and V independent of everything after i, the same property that forbids left-padding in `BatchedReader` -- and it verified at 24/24 token parity with byte-identical agent output. **Then it timed at 1.02x: 72% of round 2's tokens skipped, 0.22 ms of 10.63 saved, 0.3% of the round.** The reason is the finding: prefill against prompt length reads **8.82 / 8.48 / 8.57 / 8.36 / 8.65 / 8.12 / 8.45 ms at 16 / 32 / 64 / 128 / 225 / 384 / 511 tokens** -- flat, so **prefill costs launches and not tokens**, and cutting the token count optimises a variable the clock does not depend on. That is the *second* finding's diagnosis on the path nobody re-checked after decode was graphed, sitting in `bench.py`'s `prefill_ms` column at ~9 ms all along. **What does move it is static shapes:** `torch.compile(dynamic=True)` is *slower than eager* (0.76x, guard overhead) while `dynamic=False` reads **1.68x at 128 tokens, 1.71x at 256, 1.20x at 512** -- ~3.4 ms a round, fifteen times what reuse bought, from the opposite end. Padding to a bucket is safe where it counts: right-padded with **random garbage**, layer 0's KV over the real positions is **exactly 0.000e+00** (the causal mask doing its job) and layers 1+ drift 2-3 bf16 ULPs from SDPA tiling a longer sequence -- so it lands in the `--fast-attn` category, not the `--buckets` one. **Kept, off by default, with the null in its docstring**, because deleting it would invite the same day's work again; and the chunked prefill it required (`CornModel.step` at T>1 against a live cache) is the primitive the sixty-second finding's draft-and-verify needs. **Not settled:** bucketed static prefill is timed, not built; prefill is 16% of a round so 1.68x of it is ~7%; and nobody has explained why 20 eager layers cost 8 ms. **Method note, twice in one week now:** an intervention reads near zero and the measurement that kills it finds the real mechanism -- bytes not tokens in the 62nd, launches not tokens here
