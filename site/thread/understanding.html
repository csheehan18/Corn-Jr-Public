The 1.0 run and small-scale arcs

Understanding at small scale

Can shape, steering or a far lesson buy word understanding at the nano budget?

27 findings, 186 to 237, filed 20 October 2026 to 15 November 2026

The findings, in order

  1. 186
    He knows roughly what descriptions mean but ignores a described menu 20 October 2026 ·

    At 20M, a one-line description beside each field moves the phrase -> field read by -0.04 to +0.03 on four checkpoints, the same menu with its descriptions rotated onto the wrong names reads within 0.03 of it on all four, and greedy decodes with and without the rotation open with the same words on nine of ten — he does not read the menu — while the descriptions alone, with no names, pick the right field 0.43-0.49 against a chance of 0.24; prediction held on the menu, missed high on descriptions alone

  2. 187
    Trained on invented names, he learned to copy one, never which 20 October 2026 ·

    At 20M, a fine-tune on 60,000 described-menu lines (an invented name beside each property's description, the request phrased by meaning) teaches him to answer with one of the menu's names — 197-200 of 200 fresh training-format lines — and not which one: the right name on 0.21-0.30 of them against a chance of 0.25, and every held read at chance on all three starts (ruler 0.23-0.25, held kinds 0.17-0.26, held phrases 0.20-0.28); the pick does not move with the request; the pre-registered row "the link does not form at this dose", with finding 186's plain read 0.02-0.05 lower

  3. 188
    His surprise spots unusual words, not words he misunderstands 20 October 2026 ·

    On the shipped 260M, the summed surprise of each word of a request picks out the one hard word or phrase in it 28 of 40 times (single words 25 of 32, idioms 3 of 8), separates hard words from ordinary content words at AUC 0.873, and at the threshold that flags 75% of the hard words fires on 0 of 16 plain requests and on every name (8 of 8) and typo (8 of 8); it is no higher where he gets the meaning wrong (AUC 0.530), and a plain count of how often each word appears in his web text does better (33 of 40, AUC 0.918)

  4. 196
    Loop fails because the small model lacks the knowledge, not the format 21 October 2026 ·

    On loop's own 436 answer-bearing batches, asked in plain sentences outside any trace, which place best fits the wish is near chance at 20M and grows with size — the place that most raises the wish phrase is the right one 0.45 at loop's 20M start, 0.48 after the balanced fine-tune, 0.55 at 100M and 0.63 at 260M, against a chance of 0.41 — while the trace format neither hides nor adds a judgment the model has (trace pick 0.45 at the 20M start, 0.52 after the balanced fine-tune); so loop's collapse (findings 184, 195) is a fine-tune with no signal learning the base rate, and the missing signal is knowledge of what a description implies, which 20M does not hold for these items

  5. 197
    A model a quarter as wide nearly matches the 20M's understanding 21 October 2026 ·

    On finding 189's blend and the 20M's own 128.7M-token schedule, a 6.95M-parameter model (6 layers x 256, lr 3e-3 from a five-point sweep) reads the understanding ruler's fields 0.56 and 0.53 against the 20M blend's 0.55 and 0.54 and its 192 wishes 0.35 and 0.32 against 0.37 and 0.38, the plain lift 0.49 and 0.47 (chance 0.41), no everyday word in its own words at either seed, and a stock-answer pull (0.28, 0.27) between the blend's and Edu's — while its language loss sits about 0.045 nats a byte above the 20M's on all three text rows; at this budget the understanding reads barely move between 5M and 20M, so they are not reading width there

  6. 198
    Surprise-steered reading lowers rare-word surprise but not understanding at 5M 21 October 2026 ·

    At 5M, replacing up to 7 of each step's 48 rows — only web rows, 14.6% of all reading — with fresh, varied web text carrying the words whose surprise is falling lowers his surprise on rare words in sentences he never read (the held-out rare-word gap 7.65 and 7.80 nats against the base's 7.90 and 8.06, most in the rarest band: a drop of 0.54 at 10-30 sightings, 0.16 at 30-100, 0.07 at 100-300), and the drop is the steered words': held-out targets the scheduler happened to steer fall 1.75 and 0.98 nats at 10-30 against 0.25 and 0.18 for the rest; fluency holds on every text row and in the hand read; no understanding read moves (fields 0.55 / 0.53, wishes 0.32 / 0.33, plain lift 0.47 / 0.48 against 0.56 / 0.53, 0.35 / 0.32, 0.49 / 0.47) — pre-registered outcome (b), the mechanism works and 5M is too small to turn it into understanding

  7. 199
    Steering mostly teaches how likely a word is, partly where it fits 22 October 2026 ·

    On finding 198's runs, a steered rare word's surprise falls in sentences where it does not fit as well as where it does: on the held-out targets the scheduler steered at 10-30 and 30-100 sightings, the drop in eight wrong sentences is 0.58 and 0.71 of the drop in the word's own eight (seed 1: own −1.75 / wrong −1.12 at 10-30, −0.41 / −0.17 at 30-100; seed 2: −0.98 / −0.81, −0.44 / −0.23) — the pre-registered mixed row at both seeds, against a predicted 0.8. The unsteered targets and the common words move neither column. By hand, the two columns move together word by word (celebs −4.3 own / −4.0 wrong, nosebleeds −6.1 / −4.3); how much more he expects a word where it fits grows on 20 of 27 and 16 of 29 steered words

  8. 200
    A model that cannot learn picks the same words the scheduler did 22 October 2026 ·

    Run with the finished base 5M frozen (learning rate 0), the surprise scheduler of finding 198 fills its cap (36,630 rows, 99.9% of steps at the cap) and steers 9,912 words that overlap the surprise arm's seed 2 at Jaccard 0.534 against 0.590 between the arm's two seeds (random sets of the same band sizes: 0.223 and 0.243) — an excess ratio of 0.90, the pre-registered noise row; the learning progress its picks carried is no smaller than the arm's (median 0.675 against 0.637), and the arm's most-steered words are the frozen run's to the word (smiled, recall, scared, toys, laughed). One reading of a word varies 1.2 nats a token from sentence to sentence, which by itself puts the scheduler's progress score above its 0.05 bar for 46% of words at any moment

  9. 201
    Words picked at random gain less than words the scheduler picked 22 October 2026 ·

    With the scheduler's words drawn at random — as many of each frequency band as the surprise arm steered, under the same cap, spacing and variety — the held-out rare-word gap reads 7.814 and 7.923 against the surprise arm's 7.650 and 7.802 and the base's 7.896 and 8.064: the pre-registered between row (m), random choice helps less and not separably at the gate. The steered-target split separates them: rare targets (10-30 sightings) chosen at random fall 0.70 and 0.13 nats in their own held-out sentences against 1.75 and 0.98 for those the surprise scheduler chose, though the random ones got more rows each (4.2 against 2.7-3.2). The progress score is noise (finding 200), so the difference lies in what comes with it: the surprise scheduler steers a word only once he has met it three times and still finds it harder than average (z +1.2 at the first steer, random picks +0.1), which lands its rare-word steering mid-run (median first steer at step 2,600-3,100 against 900-1,100). Fluency, chats and the understanding reads hold at both seeds

  10. 202
    A shape built for small models cost language and taught only spelling 22 October 2026 ·

    At the plain 5M's parameter count and on its text, the wordloop arrangement — a hashed table for the word so far, three blocks run four times, and his own surprise read back as an input — is worse on every language row at both seeds (narrative 0.5111 / 0.5069 nats a byte against 0.4864 / 0.4883, web 0.9690 / 0.9654 against 0.9512 / 0.9522, conversational 0.5799 / 0.5773 against 0.5579 / 0.5599): the pre-registered row (d), the arrangement costs language. No reply read moves: blind by hand it answers 51 and 65 of 148 everyday messages against the base's 70 and 55, and admits not knowing on unanswerable questions more than on answerable ones by +2 and +2 against -4 and +1. Its held-out rare-word gap falls far (7.13 / 7.06 against 7.90 / 8.06), but the whole fall is in spelling the word out once its first piece is chosen (later pieces 8.54 / 8.48 nats against 10.01 / 10.13 at 10-30 sightings); choosing the word is unchanged (first piece 8.01 / 8.04 against 7.93 / 8.03), and how much more he expects a rare word where it fits shrank (1.99 / 2.02 against 2.25)

  11. 203
    His surprise flags unfamiliar names, not questions he cannot answer 22 October 2026 ·

    On the plain 5M at both seeds, the surprise of the most surprising word in a question separates the unknowable questions of probe_reply.py from the answerable ones at AUC 0.67 — 0.92 and 0.91 among the questions that name something, 0.47 and 0.46 among those that do not; the mean word reads 0.70 / 0.69, 0.90 / 0.86, 0.54 / 0.59. An invented name is the most surprising thing he reads (Feruzza's 36-40 nats, Areskog's 35); a question about the sender's own life is ordinary words (How old is my grandmother? 6.5-7.0, below lie-in at 21-24 in an answerable one). So a surprise input could at best teach him "unfamiliar name -> I don't know", the shortcut the knowing set was balanced against

  12. 222
    Pressing harder where the book points taught the 20M nothing more 13 November 2026 ·

    With finding 221's book (rebuilt without the ruler's words) setting how hard each web word of mix_v3_webblend presses on the loss — 0.5 to 4 times, mean one, the web sources only — the 20M reads the understanding ruler at 72 and 69 wishes of 192 and 77 and 81 fields of 141, against the unpressed controls' 71 and 73 and 78 and 76, and against 66 and 71 and 82 and 87 for the same presses dealt to other words. Both registered rows fail; where the press is aligned adds nothing. And the kill fires, in both cells: conversational and narrative text, whose press is exactly one, read 0.5249 / 0.5235 and 0.4612 / 0.4606 nats a byte under the book press and 0.5236 / 0.5220 and 0.4602 / 0.4588 under the shuffled one, against lines of 0.5182 and 0.4579 — about a hundredth of a nat on conversation, thirty times the controls' seed spread. A control retrained the same day on the same seed reads within 0.0005 of the old one on every language row, so the cost is the uneven pressing and not the code. That retrain also prices the ruler's item-level read: against its own twin it gains 30 items and loses 30 of 333 and agrees on the pick for 234, so the 39 gained and 39 lost between a control and its pressed twin are a retrain's worth of noise, and by hand they are his default label per kind moving (finding 192).

  13. 223
    His word vectors hold less than his framed answers do 13 November 2026 ·

    Asked the way finding 221's book is asked — add up, over a description's words, how close each word's vector sits to each candidate label, no sentence and no next-word prediction — the two 20M controls answer 27 and 32 of the second 96 wishes and 61 and 57 of 141 fields from their word table, under their own framed read (35 and 37, 78 and 76) and far under the book (51 and 97). Every one of seven cells reads under the frame: the word table, his state half-way up and at the top for a word read alone, raw or with the common direction taken off, and his own top state for the whole description. The same holds at size: the 100M reads 27 and 69 from its table and the 260M 19 and 60 (34 and 63 at its best cell) against framed reads of 37 and 80 and 50 and 101. So the knowledge the ruler asks for is not sitting in him unread; what he holds comes out through the sentence, and a bag of his word vectors holds less. The book-pressed runs read 23 and 27 and 62 and 50, with the shuffled ones and the controls.

  14. 224
    One hard press makes the word likelier everywhere, and writes no line 13 November 2026 ·

    On the trained 20M controls, one gradient step on a word's loss at one meeting of a pair the book knows from two to four meetings, sized to take two nats off the word there (it takes 1.84 and 1.87), takes 0.49 and 0.43 nats off the same word at the pair's other meeting — and 0.48 and 0.44 off it at an occurrence where the book says nothing about it. The line, what carries because the earlier word is there, is +0.02 nats at both seeds over 193 and 192 pairs. So a hard press is possible — that step costs held-out text 0.00019 nats a token, and even eight nats costs 0.0037 — but what it writes is how likely the word is anywhere, a quarter of the press, and not the pair. Pressed only into the word's own rows of the word table it carries further (0.80 and 0.77 of 1.93) at a tenth of the damage (0.00002), and it carries to the unrelated occurrence just the same (0.77 and 0.75; line +0.08 and +0.03). Pressed only into the think-alone matrices it carries least (0.38 and 0.33). No place and no strength writes a line.

  15. 225
    Pressing the difference a neighbour makes writes some of the pair 13 November 2026 ·

    Finding 224's hard press, with the part of its gradient that would move the word in the pressed sentence with the earlier word swapped out taken out first, takes 0.40 and 0.34 nats off the word at the pair's other meeting and only 0.07 and 0.05 off it there with the neighbour swapped out, over 192 and 191 pairs at two seeds: the pair line, B - Bx, is +0.24 and +0.21 where the plain press reads +0.01, with 48% and 49% of pairs over +0.25 and none under -0.25. The registered row asked 0.25 in every weight, so it is a direction and not a pass, and the hold itself leaks (the word gets 0.18 and 0.16 nats costlier in the held sentence). Pressed only into the two words' own word-table rows the same step reads cleaner: 0.47 and 0.53 off the word at the other meeting, 0.04 and 0.02 where the book is silent, 0.05 and 0.00 with the neighbour swapped, for a third of the damage (0.00063 and 0.00037 nats a token against 0.0020 and 0.0018) — printed beside the row, not registered. Holding the word at eight book-silent occurrences instead writes no pair at all (+0.01). So what a press writes follows what it is held against: nothing, and it writes the word; the word elsewhere, and it writes less of the same; the same sentence without the neighbour, and what is left is the neighbour.

  16. 226
    Kept presses last, but break the text and churn the words 13 November 2026 ·

    150 of finding 225's pairs pressed one after another and kept — the held step in the pair's own word-table rows, two nats each — stay pressed (0.98 and 0.97 of what each press took off its own sentence is still off at the end; 0.90 and 0.91 for the first 50), and the word comes a median 0.33 and 0.42 nats down at the pair's other meeting. But against a swapped meeting whose stand-in word the hold never saw, the pair is 0.11 and 0.20 nats, a direction and half of finding 225's read; the held-out text costs 0.102 and 0.057 nats a token more, breaking, and adding up press by press; and the medians sit on churn — at the other meeting the word is a quarter of a nat easier on 53% and 54% of pressed pairs and that much harder on 28% and 27%, down to 12 nats harder, and of the pairs never pressed 36% and 39% are a quarter of a nat harder. No compounding. At half the strength the damage is seven times less (0.014 and 0.011) for about half the pair (0.07 and 0.11); in every weight the text loses 0.20 nats a token and the never-pressed pairs 0.38 and 0.89; and the plain press kept is harmless (0.005 and 0.004) and writes 150 words, not pairs.

  17. 227
    A short word vector holds field knowledge; frozen, he cannot read it 13 November 2026 ·

    The book with the ruler never read — a page for each of the 20,000 commonest words — answers 91 wishes of 192 and 95 fields of 141 against the 20M's 71 and 73 and 78 and 76, and squeezed to a short list of numbers a word it keeps the fields and loses the wishes: 85 fields and 72 wishes at 256 numbers, 92 and 75 at 512, and still only 83 wishes at 4,096. So the two halves of the understanding ruler are two kinds of knowledge: what a phrase's content word says about a field is compact, and what a whole description implies is a long tail of separate lines that does not fit a short vector. Handed 512-number homes through a 262,144-number input adapter, the frozen 20M reads neither: wishes 68 and 71, fields 74 and 78, failing the registered row at both seeds, though held-out web text gets 0.017 and 0.018 nats a token easier where shuffled homes give 0.000 and 0.003. The same homes laid on his next-piece scores did nothing at the registered settings (it had barely trained), and at ten times the step size and three times the steps — a cause read — lift fields to 83 and 80 and wishes to 73 and 77 and take 0.3% to 0.5% off held-out text of every kind: a direction. A first squeeze had read 47 of the second 96 wishes at 256 numbers; that book had a page for every ruler word, and with 5,000 pages and none of them the whole book reads the wishes at the 20M's own level, 71.

  18. 228
    The fields stall is not the word-pieces, and not hidden in him 13 November 2026 ·

    Three reads of why the 20M answers 76 to 78 of the ruler's 141 fields where a count of the same text answers 95. Split by piece count, his shortfall from the book is the same whether the gold label and the phrase's content words are one piece or several (-4.5 and +0.6 points where 15 was asked): the cut into pieces is not why. A book counted over his own 8,192 pieces, the ruler never read, answers 83 fields at the registered window and so does one tied table of 512 numbers a piece, the shape of his word table, where his own trained table answers 58 to 61 by the same read — a direction on both rows, and 83 is inside the spread of seven 20M runs (76 to 87). A ridge reader from his state to the book's 512-number home, fitted on words the ruler never uses, answers 70 and 72 fields at the middle of the net and 77 and 80 at its best depth: what he holds is about what his framed answer already gives, so it is not sitting in him unread. One thing nobody predicted: the piece book answers more fields the further it looks — 83, 86, 86 and 91 at windows of 5, 10, 20 and 40 pieces — while its wishes fall (33, 30, 30, 28 of the first 96), and the wide-window knowledge does not squeeze (79 tied at 512 numbers).

  19. 229
    Asked from afar, a step writes a pair the next-piece question misses 13 November 2026 ·

    One step on the far cost — what a word's first piece costs him at every position from its neighbour's last piece to two before the word — writes the word's likelihood, like a press on the next piece: the drop where the book is silent is 80% and 84% of the drop at the pair's other meeting, and B - Bx is 0.00. Holding the word's commonness at eight random windows leaves no pair either (B - Bx +0.01 at both seeds, 3% of the drop at B): Q1 fails, as finding 225's hold Q did at the next piece. Held against the pressed sentence with the neighbour swapped out, the far step does write the pair — as read from afar: B - Bx +0.40 at both seeds, with the swapped meeting moving 0.08. Read as the next piece, the way he is asked, the same step reads +0.05 and +0.06, under the next-piece step's +0.24 and +0.20 (the anchor, reproducing finding 225): Q2 fails, by -0.19 and -0.14. Each question writes what the other does not read. The exception is the two words' own word-table rows: there the far step reads +0.98 and +1.03 from afar and +0.21 and +0.18 as the next piece, the next-piece step +0.46 and +0.34 and +0.33 and +0.25 from afar, each at under 0.001 nats a token of damage.

  21. 231
    A far lesson in training takes, costs reading, and lifts nothing clearly 13 November 2026 ·

    A 5M trained from its first step with the far lesson beside its reading — 2,048 pairs a micro-batch off the reading's own scores, which of two pieces stands 2 to 40 ahead, a piece's general likelihood cancelling — learns the lesson at weight 0.5 (far pairs right on held-out text 0.711 and 0.700 against its controls' 0.631 and 0.627) and reads 80 and 83 of the ruler's 141 fields: a direction by the registered row, and inside what twelve 5Ms with no far lesson read (65 to 84, the controls 79 and 75). It pays 0.160 and 0.156 nats a token on held-out text, three and a half times the gap between a 5M and a 20M (0.045, finding 197), on every source. At weight 0.1 the text costs 0.017 and 0.019, the lesson is half learned (0.665 and 0.663) and the fields read 71 and 74. The one read nobody called — the light cell's wishes at 72 and 80 of 192, at and over the top of the no-lesson runs' 61 to 72 — was registered for replication and failed it: two more seeds read 69 and 61.

  26. 236
    Eight times the nudges reads worse; the short test had misled 14 November 2026 ·

    A 5M that reads the control's 128.7M tokens in one pass at 6 rows a step instead of 48 — 41,896 updates for 5,237 — ends reading held-out text 0.193 nats a token worse than the two controls (3.235 against 3.041), where a fifth-of-a-run smoke of the same recipe had read 0.15 better.   27. 237
    Decay and end noise both cleared: the small-step run just learned less 15 November 2026 ·

    Finding 236's small-step 5M was re-run with its weight decay cut from 0.1 to 0.0188, so that 41,896 steps would apply what the control's 5,237 do. The audit row fails by its own rule — the weights did not come back to ordinary size but went past it, to 1.6 to 2.2 times six ordinary runs' — so this is not a "decay matched" run. It is the other side of a bracket: with weights at 0.6 of ordinary size (decay 0.1) the recipe reads held-out text 3.235, and with weights at twice ordinary size (decay 0.0188) it reads 3.224, both against the controls' 3.041. The decay is not why it reads worse. Nor is noise in where it ended: its two saved iterates averaged take 0.022 off, and 4.9M more tokens with the step size taken down tenfold take 0.029 off where the same treatment takes 0.018 off the control. About 0.15 of the 0.18 is neither. The lift is under every ordinary run again (0.41 and 0.27), and read with the writers hidden, 2 of 48 continuations carry the phrase's meaning for this run and 1 of 48 for the control.
