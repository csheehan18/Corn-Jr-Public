PartV

The 1.0 run and small-scale arcs

The reply judge

A judge and a picker beside or inside the model, steering what he writes.

20 findings, 204 to 240, filed 22 October 2026 to 15 November 2026

The findings, in order

  1. 204
    Running the same layers twice moves nothing measurable at 5M 22 October 2026 ·

    The plain 5M's six blocks run twice — twelve deep, nothing given up, 6,950,656 parameters against 6,950,144 — move no coherence read at either seed: the within-category reply-fit margin of the new swap test reads 7.65 and 8.19 against the control's 8.27 and 8.08 (the gate was 8.57 at both), the blind hand read of 148 greedy replies 65 and 71 against 70 and 55, and the language rows are inside the control pair or barely past it (conversational 0.5560 and 0.5522 against 0.5579 and 0.5599): the pre-registered row (n), nothing measurable, against a predicted (c). The rulers, the rare-word gap and word choice are level. Read beside it on the control: the plain 5M picks the fitting reply over a swapped one in 91-96% of pairs while its greedy replies fit about half the time — most of what fails in his replies is what he generates, not what he recognises

  2. 205
    Choosing replies by what the message raises varies them, not their fit 22 October 2026 ·

    On the plain 5M at both seeds, with no training, choosing each reply's first five words by log p(w | message) - 0.5 log p(w | empty message) among the twenty most likely leaves the blind hand read level — 66 and 64 of 148 replies answer the message against greedy's 70 and 58 on the same checkpoints (the pre-registered gain needed 7 more at both seeds) — and makes the replies far more varied: 123 and 124 distinct three-word openings of 148 against 86 and 82, the commonest opening used 3 and 4 times against 7 and 15. The two decoders fail on different messages (13 and 29 replies pass only under the new one, 17 and 23 only under greedy). Loops stay rare (1 and 2 against 0 and 0)

  3. 206
    A matching lesson at a tenth of conversation reading did not take 22 October 2026 ·

    Graded from the first step on whether a reply belongs to its message — two quads a micro-batch from the 717,092 message-reply pairs of his own conversational reading, scored lp(r1|m1) + lp(r2|m2) - lp(r2|m1) - lp(r1|m2) so a reply's general likelihood cancels, weighted like one token of the reading — the 5M matches no better afterwards: same-dialogue quads right 0.873 and 0.867 against the control's 0.872 and 0.858 (the gate was 0.887 at both), the pre-registered row (u), the exercise did not take at this weight. Its margins rose a little at both seeds (11.00 / 10.30 against 10.43 / 9.69) and its training log climbed to 0.885 by step 5,000; language, the rulers and the swap test are level; the blind hand read gives 73 and 63 of 148 against 70 and 55. By hand, a large part of what the control gets "wrong" has no right answer: two turns of one conversation often carry replies that fit either message

  4. 207
    The pairs he fails are mostly decidable: he loses the conversation's thread 22 October 2026 ·

    Matching blind — two messages from one conversation, their two replies shuffled, the answer key closed — a person decides 42 of 50 same-dialogue quads the plain 5M (seed 1) gets wrong, and 38 of those correctly; on 25 random quads (which the model mostly gets right) 23 decided, 23 correct. So three quarters of the model's same-dialogue failures (38 of 50) are pairs a reader can settle, and only 8 have nothing that decides them — the opposite of finding 206's hand read of six quads, which is corrected here. What decides the quads the model fails is mostly the conversation's order (20 of 38: which reply sets up which message — "Why did you choose Max as your new name?" is what "I don't really know, it just felt right" answers), then a picked-up detail (10), then a direct question answered (7); what decides the ones it gets right is mostly a direct question answered (16 of 23)

  5. 208
    The shipped model answers most one-off messages coherently 22 October 2026 ·

    Asked the 148 everyday messages of probe_reply.py one at a time, greedy, in the plain chat frame, the shipped 260M (corn_jr_260m_v4_ft_social8, server.json's checkpoint) answers 123 coherently by a blind hand read against the plain 5M's 70 (read beside it, blind; the 5M's labels came out at 70 for the fourth time) — 11 of 12 on good news, 16 of 18 on bad, 17 of 18 small talk, 13 of 18 advice, 15 of 15 feelings, 12 of 12 plans — and is weakest on the short and vague ("Hmm.", "Maybe.", "Never mind.": 11 of 20, below the 5M's 14). On the swap test it prefers the fitting reply in 99-100% of pairs (margins 20.6 within a category and 26.1 across, against the 5M's 8.3 and 11.5). The 5M's coherence problem, which findings 202-207 could not move by shape, decoding or a matching lesson, is mostly absent at the size and reading that ship

  6. 209
    The small model answers half of even its own style's openers 22 October 2026 ·

    The 148 messages of the reply read are not his conversational style: at matched length (4-9 words) the plain 5M finds them 3.91 nats a token surprising against 2.25 for real conversational openers he never read and 2.39 for ones he did — so he has not memorised his openers, and the hand-written messages sit far outside the SODA-style dialogue he learned conversation from. But the register is the smaller part of his incoherence: on 60 real openers in his own style that he never read, he answers coherently 29 and 37 times (0.48 and 0.62, blind; the real replies 60 of 60) against 0.47 and 0.37 on the everyday messages. 21 of the 148 messages appear verbatim as a turn in his reading (mostly "Hi.", "Thanks!", "How are you doing today?"); he passes them a little more often (0.67 and 0.43 against 0.44 and 0.36), as do short easy messages generally

  7. 210
    Real everyday dialogue fits the owner's messages best; Reddit worst 22 October 2026 ·

    Of four conversational sources read on CPU, EmpatheticDialogues talks most like the 148 everyday messages of the reply read: a word-bigram model fitted on a million words of each source's messages scores those messages at perplexity 142, against 180 for SODA (what the mix reads now), 288 for OpenSubtitles and 359 for PEC (Reddit r/happy and r/offmychest) — and it is twice as varied as SODA on its own held-out messages (171 against 86; SODA is the most formulaic of the four). By hand it reads as real people texting about their day ("Oh man I hate when that happens...did you dry it off or change to a new pair?"). PEC is long, confessional, often off-thread, carries slurs, and was lowercased and tokenised by its authors, so it is unusable as text; subtitle lines that follow each other are rarely a message and its reply. EmpatheticDialogues is small (17,844 training dialogues, 1.26M words) and CC BY-NC 4.0

  8. 211
    Real everyday dialogue moves him toward how the owner talks not yet committed ·

    With a sixth of his conversational reading moved from SODA to EmpatheticDialogues (3.4M of 19.6M tokens; the budget and every other source the control's), the 5M finds the owner's own typed messages less surprising — 4.880 and 4.963 nats a token against the control's 5.218 and 5.105, both under the pre-registered 4.992 — with narrative and web holding (0.4876 / 0.4898 and 0.9520 / 0.9568 against lines of 0.4913 and 0.9552, the web row over at one seed only): the pre-registered row (r), real everyday dialogue moves him toward how the owner talks. His replies stay level at 5M, as predicted: 64 and 66 of 148 coherent by a blind hand read against 70 and 55. Held-out SODA text costs what a sixth less SODA should (lm conversational 0.5671 / 0.5717 against 0.5579 / 0.5599)

  9. 212
    People asking an assistant fits the owner best; the answers do not 22 October 2026 ·

    After the owner kept Apache 2.0 for the weights and left the non-commercial EmpatheticDialogues out (2026-10-22), two permissively licensed real-dialogue sources were read the same way as finding 210. OpenAssistant OASST1 (Apache 2.0, human-written; English: 15,979 prompts and 25,326 replies, 3.6M words) fits the owner's own typed messages best of any source read — bigram perplexity 426 against Taskmaster's 648, EmpatheticDialogues' 718 and SODA's 938 — because its prompts are people asking an assistant things, which is how he writes to Corn-Jr. Its replies are the problem: median 93 words, list-heavy and encyclopedic, some in a model's voice ("As a language model AI..."), one confidently wrong. Google's Taskmaster (CC BY 4.0, verified in the official README) is short task talk ("I need a seafood place.", "Make it a medium.") but narrow and the most formulaic source read (perplexity 55 on its own held-out messages, under SODA's 86)

  10. 213
    A small dose matching how the owner talks moves him furthest 22 October 2026 ·

    Short-reply OpenAssistant exchanges — people asking an assistant things, best-ranked answers of 60 words or fewer, 252,008 tokens read twice in place of the same 504,016 tokens of SODA (Apache 2.0) — make the owner's own typed messages less surprising to the 5M at both seeds: 4.937 and 4.958 nats a token against the control's 5.218 and 5.105, under the pre-registered 4.992. Narrative and web hold (0.4890 / 0.4898, 0.9539 / 0.9563, over the web line at seed 2 only). The pre-registered row is (r), against a predicted (n): a seventh of finding 211's EmpatheticDialogues dose moves his register as far (4.880 / 4.963 there). It does not move the 148 stand-in messages (3.833 / 3.862 against 3.987 / 3.828), which EmpatheticDialogues did: each source moves the register it carries, and the owner writes questions and requests. Replies stay level or below at 5M (68 and 47 of 148 by a blind hand read against 70 and 55; 47 is the lowest of any 5M arm read, inside the control's spread)

  11. 214
    A reply judge beside the 5M moves his replies level 24 October 2026 ·

    A judge block fitted beside the frozen plain 5M on swapped replies — 853k parameters, 20,000 steps on 702,653 pairs of his own conversational reading — recognises held-out conversation as well as he does or better (same-dialogue quads right at the end of the reply 0.913 and 0.912 against his own 0.904 and 0.881; on the first word 0.793 and 0.785 against 0.792 and 0.760), and re-ranking his top 20 words by it moves the blind hand read level: 55 and 51 of 148 replies answer the message against greedy's 53 and 46 on the same checkpoints (the gate was +7 at both seeds), the pre-registered row (b), as predicted. On the 148 hand-written everyday messages the judge is far weaker than he is — swap pairs within a category right 0.73 and 0.75 at the end of the reply against his 0.91, 0.58 and 0.64 on the first word — and its fit on the words that decide a reply ranges only 0.45-0.9, about 0.7 nats of push against LM gaps of one to two: where it is wrong it does no harm, and where it is right it is outvoted. The free control, sixteen samples re-ranked by his own whole-reply score, reads 54 and 56, also level

  12. 215
    Looking a few words ahead with the judge lifts his replies 24 October 2026 ·

    Choosing each reply's opening by a beam five branches wide and five tokens deep, scored by his own mean log-probability plus the reply judge's verdict on the newest word, lifts the plain 5M's fitting replies to 58 and 61 of 148 against greedy's 53 and 46 by a blind hand read — +5 and +15, short of the pre-registered +7 at both seeds at seed 1, so the registered row is (b), level — and every paired count points one way: 38 replies pass only under the look-ahead and 18 only under greedy (sign test p = 0.010), and against the same beam with no judge 32 to 17 (p = 0.044), so the gain is the judge's and not the search's (the beam alone: 53 and 51, 27 to 22). A judge continued on short-reply OpenAssistant and EmpatheticDialogues reads the everyday messages better (within-category swap pairs right 0.78 and 0.81 at the end of the reply against the SODA judge's 0.73 and 0.75, under his own 0.91, the predicted fail) and steers worse: 51 and 51 with the look-ahead, 51 and 50 one word at a time. It learned EmpatheticDialogues' habit of answering with one's own feelings

  13. 216
    The look-ahead holds on new messages; finishing and penalties add nothing 24 October 2026 ·

    On 100 new hand-written messages, the look-ahead of finding 215, settings unchanged, lifts the plain 5M's fitting replies to 39 and 39 against greedy's 31 and 33 by a blind hand read — 21 replies pass only under it and 7 only under greedy (sign test p = 0.013) — so 215's direction replicates on messages it was never read on. Neither of the two changes aimed at its losses adds to it: finishing the five kept branches and re-ranking the finished replies reads 40 and 40 (80 pooled against the look-ahead's 78, 14 to 12 paired), under the pre-registered +5, so no new arm goes to the exam; penalising the bland reply raises variety by half (80 and 88 distinct three-word openings against 58 and 60) and costs fit (32 and 34, 66 pooled, 13 to 25 paired), and both together read 33 and 39. Finishing changes 54-70 of 100 replies and mostly swaps a failure for a failure: the finished score is led by fluency and the judge's end-of-reply vote is too small to overrule it. A linear probe on the 260M's states, fitted on 2,072 hand labels, counts every practice arm within 5.3 of the hand count — it meets its bar, though its error is the size of the gains it would have to see

  14. 217
    A judge built into the 5M learns, is ignored, and costs him 24 October 2026 ·

    A reply judge built into the 5M from the first step — one block inside the model, taught on swapped replies with its gradient reaching every layer, its fit for every word added to the word scores at a weight the model learns — learns its lesson (0.90 and 0.89 of its training quads right at the end, from chance) and is switched off by the model it sits in: the learned weight falls from 1 to about 0.2 by iteration 750 and stays near zero from 1,500 on (0.007 at the end, both seeds). The lesson costs language at both seeds — narrative / web / conversational 0.5156 / 0.9862 / 0.6040 and 0.5141 / 0.9817 / 0.6009 against lines of 0.4913 / 0.9552 / 0.5629, the pre-registered row (d), as predicted — and replies far more: on the practice set 21 and 6 of 100 answer the message against the plain 5M's 31 and 33 (12 replies pass only under the arm, 49 only under the control), and its own beam reads 26 and 12 against the plain look-ahead's 39 and 39. Decoding with the weight forced to 0 changes 0 and 1 replies of 100: the damage is in the trunk, not the vote

  15. 218
    The built-in judge's cost is not the word table 24 October 2026 ·

    Finding 217's built-in judge, rebuilt so its lesson never touches the shared word table — it reads its inputs and scores its tokens through a detached embedding, its gradient reaching only the trunk's blocks — costs the 5M exactly what the first build did: narrative / web / conversational 0.5150 / 0.9869 / 0.6031 against 217's 0.5156 / 0.9862 / 0.6040 (the control 0.4864 / 0.9512 / 0.5579, lines 0.4913 / 0.9552 / 0.5629), validation 2.7191 against 2.7187, and on the practice set 20 of 100 replies answer the message against 217's 21 and the control's 31 (6 pass only under it, 17 only under the control). The pre-registered row is (n), against a predicted (p): the word table was not the channel, and finding 217's named cause is withdrawn. The lesson learns as before (0.897 right at iteration 5,000) and the model switches its vote off as before (alpha 0.007)

  16. 219
    A 5M reading only conversation answers far more messages 24 October 2026 ·

    The same 6.95M-parameter model, the same 130M tokens read once, with every token conversation — 55% SODA dialogue, 40% SODA exchanges in the one-off reply frame, 5% EmpatheticDialogues and short-reply OpenAssistant (research only) — answers 34 and 36 of 100 fresh hand-written one-off messages by a blind hand read, against 26 and 20 for the 5M that reads the usual mix (half web, 15% conversation): +8 and +16, 42 replies passing only under it and 18 only under the control (sign test p = 0.003), the pre-registered row R. With the judge-picked look-ahead on top it reads 41 and 41 (+7 and +5 over its own greedy, 22 to 10, p = 0.05 — L holds at seed 1, misses by 2 at seed 2), +15 and +21 over the control's greedy and +17 and +14 over the control's own look-ahead (47 to 16). It is not yet a coherent 5M by the registered bar (60 of 100 at both seeds, R+ and L+ not met). The gains are in plans (1-2 of 8 to 6-7), feelings, good news and bad news; advice, opinions and the short and vague stay where the control is. The price: narrative and web text cost twice as much (1.03 and 1.41 nats a byte against 0.49 and 0.95); held-out SODA costs less (0.456 against 0.558)

  17. 220
    Judging his own drafts does not help; plain beam search might 1 November 2026 ·

    On the shipped 260M (corn_jr_260m_v4_ft_social8), in the plain chat frame, three decoders over finding 208's 148 one-off messages and exam 2's 100 by one blind hand read: greedy answers 122 and 72 (208 read 123 on the 148 by the same rule), the look-ahead judged by his own recognition — the posterior that a draft was written for this message among it and 16 others, corn/judge.py section 9 — answers 127 and 70, and the same beam with no judge answers 126 and 79. Every pre-registered row misses: L (self over greedy by 7 and 5) at +5 and -2; Lp (paired) at 22 : 19, p 0.76; J (self over the plain beam by 5) reversed, 197 against 205, 15 : 23. The posterior measures whether a reply identifies its message, and among his own drafts the replies that identify a message best include the ones that do not fit it — "I didn't steal your bike. You're just paranoid." to a stolen bike, a tool hatch's "I can't make bookings" to a day off. The plain beam over the opening, predicted 0-4 below greedy, reads 205 against 194, 19 : 8 (p 0.052) — not a registered row, so a candidate, not a result

  18. 238
    He writes worst exactly where he reads worst, message by message 15 November 2026 ·

    Over finding 208's 148 one-off messages, a message's mean swap margin on the shipped 260M — how strongly he prefers a hand-written fitting reply over a swapped one — predicts whether his own greedy reply to that message was hand-labelled coherent at AUC 0.713, and at 0.713 counting only pairs inside one of the ten message categories. Message length predicts the label at 0.54 and taking it out of the margin leaves 0.713. Pre-registered row R: linked (0.65 or more). On the plain 5M the same read is 0.544 (0.586 within category): near chance. The share of pairs he gets right predicts nothing at either size (0.544, 0.516) — the 260M gets nearly every pair right, so only the margin's size carries the signal.

  19. 239
    The count book judges replies a little, and picks no better 15 November 2026 ·

    Finding 221's book — counted lines, PPMI at window 10, nothing re-tuned — scores each of finding 220's 744 hand-labelled 260M replies by how much its message raises the reply's content words over any other message, with every word the message itself used struck out. It separates passing replies from failing ones at AUC 0.652 over the 663 it scores (it abstains on 11%), and picking the highest-scoring of greedy, plain beam and self-judge per message passes 206 of 248, against the beam's 205, greedy's 194 and his own judgment's 197. Both pre-registered rows land live, B1 by 0.002 and B2 by one message; paired against the beam the pick wins 13 messages and loses 12, so it is level with the beam. Without the swap subtraction it reads 0.667 and 207. Echo among its picks rises from 7 to 11. Read by hand, it rewards a reply that names the topic and is blind to its stance.

  20. 240
    Real exchanges around "never mind" taught the 5M nothing measurable 15 November 2026 ·

    Thirty-six hand-written situations — a line of his, a short turn ("Never mind.", "Maybe.", "Hmm.", "Fine.", "What?", "Whatever."), the reply that fits — read by a swap inside each situation, so only the short turn can decide. The shipped 260M reads them at 0.75 of pairs right on the wordings the lesson would hold and 0.64 on reworded ones never in it ("Forget it.", "Perhaps.", "Um.", "Suit yourself."); ten ordinary 5Ms read 0.47-0.69 and 0.39-0.64, near chance. Two 5Ms reading 0.5% of the run as two-speaker subtitle exchanges around the seen wordings (2,636 windows, each read about nine times) against two reading the same amount of plain two-speaker exchanges: seen-half mean margin 1.04 and 1.24 against 0.84 and 0.60 (gap +0.42, rule 0.66), unseen 0.77 and -0.07 against -0.09 and 0.42 (gap +0.19, rule 0.93). Neither row holds; every arm sits inside the ten ordinary runs' band (seen 0.29-1.40, unseen -0.57-0.99). Cost nil (narrative and web within 0.005). Blind hand read of 144 replies to the reworded items: 0 pass, at every run. Before any run, the first cut of windows failed its own hand read, 14 of 50, and was replaced by a registered second cut that passed 31 of 50.
