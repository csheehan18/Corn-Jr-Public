Routing and declining · 25 August 2026

Tripling a fact type in training left his declines unchanged

The result, as the finding states it

The corpus was not the prior, and tripling the type share moved it by nothing

What it cost

7.0 min of scoring on the 2 cards first cited here (nano20m_v3_extract_narrowtype, nano20m_v3_extract_widetype); it also reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (nano20m_v3_extract). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

configs/nano20m_v3_extract_widetype.py ×2 against configs/nano20m_v3_extract_narrowtype.py ×2. One variable: corn/tools.py's FACTS gains 3,000 composed other-typed facts, and the tools slice is regenerated from it. Every other share is mix_v3_extract verbatim to the token. Trained 2026-08-25, 1,136s per seed, four runs.

This is a pre-registered null and the threshold was written down before the numbers existed. docs/PLAN-WEB-SEARCH.md §WS-E, registered before the corpora were built: primary read is the other decline rate, 91.7% → 70% or below; honest null is still at or above 85% ⇒ corpus type-frequency is not the cause and the line retires.

The dose

The fifty-seventh finding's probe found the arm declining on 91.7% of the answerable windows whose gold is an other — no digit, no leading capital — against about half of names and numbers, identically across both seeds and flat in gold position. The extraction slice was not the suspect: it carries other at 20.6% against the eval's 18.0%. FACTS was, at 89.7% name-or-number, being what follows a web_search answer field everywhere else in the corpus.

FACTS before 8,697 name 27.6% number 62.1% other 10.3% after 11,697 name 20.5% number 46.1% other 33.3%

as it lands in narrow name 28.4% number 61.3% other 10.3% the corpus wide name 20.9% number 45.8% other 33.2%

A 3.2× dose, confirmed on the artefact rather than on the table it came from.

The null

Decline rate on the answerable half, by gold answer type, corn.evals._is_miss on the retained reply:

other86.1% (31/36)86.1% (31/36)88.9% (32/36)91.7% (33/36)
number56.8%77.3%68.2%45.5%
name56.7%64.2%63.3%40.0%

Both arm seeds sit above the 85% null line, and the arm is not lower than the control — it is marginally higher. Tripling the share of other answers in the corpus moved the rate the intervention was aimed at by nothing.

What replicates is the phenomenon, not the explanation

Across all six runs on this eval — the two WS-B pilots and these four — other decline never leaves the 86–92% band:

PILOT s1 91.7% PILOT s2 91.7% ctl s1 86.1% ctl s2 86.1% arm s1 88.9% arm s2 91.7%

while name and number swing between 40.0% and 77.3% on the seed alone. So the type asymmetry is about as stable as anything measured in this project, and it is not driven by the answer-type frequency of the corpus it was trained on. The seventeenth finding's warning applies to the other two types rather than to this one: the rows that move on the seed are the ones that look responsive.

FACTS is the table tool.copy copies from and it grew by 3,000 rows, so this row was the change's registered risk. It did not fall: the one 118 is two items on a row whose effective resolution is about twenty, and the lowest tool.copy_wide in the set belongs to a control seed.

And the fifty-seventh finding's unresolved 20-item tool.copy fall did not reproduce. The pilot read 100/120 on one seed and 120/120 on the other, which two seeds could not separate into wobble or trend. Four further runs on the same row read 118-120. That is weak evidence for wobble, not proof, and it is worth exactly what four more seeds are worth on a row with six effective facts in it.

What this retires, and what it leaves standing

Retired: the hypothesis that the other prior comes from the answer-type frequency of the corpus. A 3.2× dose on the largest carrier of that frequency is a strong enough test that a second, larger dose is not the next move. The sibling proposal — oversampling other inside the extraction slice — is retired with it and for the same reason: it was the twenty-third finding's shape even before this, and now there is no mechanism for it to act through.

Standing: extract.abstain's vocabulary gate, the fifty-sixth finding's third premise — the eval row bounds what the slice is allowed to teach. That is a slice-side and scorer-side hypothesis rather than a corpus-side one, and it is where WS-E's failure points.

The 260M stays gated, and for a better reason than before. The execution order said not to spend 2.5 days until the abstention question had an answer. It has one: not the corpus. Seventy-seven minutes of nano time retired a hypothesis that would otherwise have been carried into a run measured in days.

Two things about the method, which cost more than the result did

The pilot could not be reused as the control, and finding that out was the expensive part. The first design read the arm against nano20m_v3_extract ×2 on the grounds that it differs only in the fact table. It does not: data/raw/tools_names.txt was built 2026-08-18, before the escalation tool existed, and carries zero escalation transcripts against the ~605 today's generator emits — 6% of the slice. An arm read against it would have varied the fact table and the tool inventory together and separated neither. The check that caught it is cheap and belongs on any reused corpus: regenerate the file from HEAD and compare it to the copy on disk. It did not reproduce, and the reason was not the seed.

The isolation was verified on the artefact, not argued from the config. The two mixes were compared stream by stream after prepare_mix:

val_chat, conversational, extract, narrative, structured, temporal, toolspace, web 8 byte-identical val_tools 1 differing

Eight of nine validation streams hash the same across the pair. Whatever these four runs read, it is not a share that drifted somewhere else.

A denominator this arm's own pre-registration got wrong

extract.abstain is scored out of 200, not 400; extract.brevity is the /400 row. The fifty-seventh finding and the handoff both had it right — the error was introduced when WS-E was registered, and it propagated to PLAN-WEB-SEARCH.md, HANDOFF.md and both mix configs as 140-158/400 with a guard at 120/400. Corrected in all four places.

No reading changes, which is the only reason this is a footnote rather than a retraction: the guard was an absolute count either way and the arm's 130-172 clears it. Worth recording because a wrong denominator in a pre-registration is the kind of error that is invisible until the result arrives and then looks like a moved goalpost.
