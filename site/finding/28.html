Routing and declining · 17 August 2026

Tool routing was template matching; deferring to the tool was not

The result, as the finding states it

Routing was template matching, deference was not, and the difference is one token

What it cost

9.2 min of scoring on the 2 cards first cited here (nano20m_v1, nano20m_v1_wide); it also reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (nano20m_conv20). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

Every behaviour number in this document was measured on utterances the training generators wrote. EVAL_WORLD holds out the facts, the contacts and the anchor years; it holds out nothing about how the request is phrased, because make_evals.py renders its items through corn/temporal.py's and corn/toolgen.py's own template pools. The fifth finding said so and called it "format compliance, not generalization" — and then nothing could test it, because the harness was built from the same pools it would have to escape.

scripts/paraphrase_evals.py builds two variants of the frozen items. Everything except the user's words is byte-identical: same anchor, same expression, same gold_call, same observation, same gold_after, same ids, same order. The build fails if any line other than the <|user|> line differs, and nothing under evals/ is touched — the arms are separate directories with their own manifests, so all eleven sha256 and every scorecard written against them are unaffected.

armthe utterance
retemplatere-rendered from a different template in the same training pool — in-distribution by construction
paraphraserendered from 136 carriers written for this experiment, which no generator has ever emitted

The retemplate arm is what makes it an experiment rather than an anecdote: without it, a fall under paraphrase could be these rows being item-fragile rather than distribution-sensitive. Two further rows are invariance controls — schema.conform and tool.copy are scored from files neither arm rewrites, so any movement in them is the harness disagreeing with itself. Both came back exact, on both checkpoints, in both arms.

nano20m_v1. The rows that ask him to understand the request move; the rows that ask him to use what the tool returned do not. temporal.route loses 50 items against a floor of 2 — and against the arm-measured 19 it is still 2.6x, while the in-distribution control loses none at all. temporal.copy reads 353/353 in all three arms.

It replicates on a second corpus. nano20m_conv20 moves temporal.route 399 → 349 and tool.selection 155 → 131, holds temporal.copy at 353/353 and tool.copy_wide inside its floor, and reproduces both invariance controls exactly.

That is the good outcome for §4. The load-bearing claim of this project — the sixth finding, VISION §5.2, the whole argument for pretraining the format — is about deference, and deference does not care how the request was worded. The number that does not survive is temporal.route 400/400, which has been quoted since the fifth finding and was never the argument.

Every one of the 52 failures is wrong_expression

Not one empty, not one unparseable, not one wrong_tool. Under paraphrase the model still emits well-formed JSON and still names resolve_date, on 400 items of 400 in every arm. What it gets wrong is which words of the sentence are the expression.

said   I'm trying to work out the date of coming Monday
want   coming Monday
got    the coming Monday

said   I'd like the notes last Tuesday
want   last Tuesday
got    the notes last Tuesday

said   so tomorrow -- that's which date?
want   tomorrow
got    this morning

Two modes, in roughly equal number. It absorbs a neighbouring carrier word into the span, or — where the span has no left context at all — it substitutes a high-frequency training expression. Three different expressions under the so {e} carrier all came back this morning.

score_route's docstring bundles two abilities: "it called resolve_date and put the user's own words in the argument." Only the second is template-dependent, and the row has never been able to say which of them a number came from.

The mechanism is the token immediately to the left of the span

scripts/paraphrase_attribute.py re-runs the same generations item by item and joins each verdict to the carrier that produced it. The obvious hypothesis — that failure tracks how different the sentence is overall — is visible in the raw numbers and is a confound:

96.9% is the frozen set's own number, reached on carriers the model has never seen, twice. And once the left edge is held, similarity flattens: within seen edges, far/mid read 97.7% and 96.1% on v1 and 95.5% and 96.1% on conv20 — no gradient at all. The dose-response by similarity was this effect wearing a disguise.

The worst left edges are the ones that could plausibly be part of a temporal expression — so 25.0%, slot-initial 44.4%, the rundown 63.6%, the notes 66.7%, of 72.7% — while unseen edges that could not be (knowing, had, out, saying) read 100%. The model is not reading the phrase. It is segmenting it off a left-boundary cue, and when that cue is a token it never saw in that role it mis-segments, always toward including it.

This is §11's copyable-span rule pointed at the prompt instead of the answer. The rule says a span the model must copy has to come from a space too large to memorise as a set. What this adds is that the span's boundaries are learned the same way, out of a set that is far smaller — the generators vary what goes in the slot exhaustively and leave what sits immediately to its left nearly fixed. Six distinct left edges across the whole of DATE_TEMPLATES_PAST.

tool.selection falls for a different reason, and it is not explained

The same analysis run on tool.selection does not separate: 68.7% on seen left edges against 63.8% on unseen for v1, 67.9% against 60.9% for conv20 — a gap far smaller than temporal.route's and in a row that fell 22 items. So the left-boundary mechanism is specific to pulling an argument out of the sentence, which is what temporal.route scores and what tool.selection does not.

What that row does instead is fall back to a default. chose_save_note goes 8 → 20 on v1 and 6 → 20 on conv20, with chose_send_text 0 → 6 and 2 → 9. Faced with a request shaped unlike anything it was trained on, it does not decline and it does not pick at random — it picks save_note. Nothing here says why, and the similarity split is the one that shows a gradient on this row (100% / 58.5% / 68.2% near/mid/far on v1, monotonic 88.9% / 65.9% / 50.0% on conv20), which is the reverse of temporal.route and is unexplained.

Annotated after the twenty-ninth finding: the 22-item fall is not attributable, and this section claims more than it can carry. tool.selection moves 41 items on a paraphrase arm across one seed pair of nano20m_v1_wide, so a 22-item drop on a single checkpoint sits well inside the noise of the row on that arm. What survives is weaker and worth stating exactly: the contrast between arms went the same way on two independently trained checkpoints — retemplate −4 and +4, paraphrase −22 and −24 — and a control that moves ±4 while the treatment moves ∼23 twice is not nothing. But the size is unpriced, the save_note mechanism rests on failure-mode counts inside an unreadable row, and the right next step for this question is a better instrument rather than another arm. The left-edge negative above is unaffected: it compares two buckets within one arm and one checkpoint, where the seed does not enter.

What this does not show

  • These are one person's paraphrases, not recordings. The carriers are hand-written to be ordinary speech at the originals' length, and check_disjoint proves none is a training template — but a real speech recogniser's output is still a different distribution.
  • Two carriers collided by prefix and were caught after the fact. give me {q} and let's {r} match training lines in other pools (give me the date for {e} → resolve_date, let's call {c} → place_call), which the whole-string check missed. They cover 5 of tool.selection's 200 items and 5 of its 66 losses; quarantining them leaves 139/195 and changes nothing. Neither touches temporal.route.
  • The exhaustiveness claim is checked, and it is what makes the arm safe. 100.00% of the <|user|> lines in data/raw/temporal.txt and data/raw/tools.txt are matched by a training template, so "no generator emits these carriers" is a property of the population rather than of a sample. It says nothing about the web and conversational slices, which are real text and may well contain them — and if they do, that is the more interesting version of the question rather than a threat to this one.
  • It does not price a fix. Widening the left context of every template is one arm and has not been run. Nothing here says whether the model would then segment properly or merely learn a larger set of boundaries.
  • Still 25M, and this is exactly the kind of result that should move with scale. A larger model has more room to represent the phrase rather than its frame, which makes this a candidate for the 100M check to carry rather than a settled number.
