Routing and declining · 17 August 2026

More phrasing templates fix only the phrasings that were added

The result, as the finding states it

The boundary is a set, and enlarging it mostly enlarges the set

What it cost

0 s of new scoring on record; it reads 3 cards scored for an earlier finding (nano20m_conv20, nano20m_v1, nano20m_v1_wide). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

The twenty-eighth finding ended with a diagnosis and no fix. configs/mix_v1_wide.py spends it: corn/temporal.py gains opt-in wide pools taking the left edges from 6 → 24 on date_past, 6 → 23 on date_nonpast and 16 → 27 on recall, including the two shapes that finding implicated — the slot placed first, where there is no left cue at all, and edges that are prepositions the model could read as part of the span (of {e}).

One variable, and the corpus proves it. Every other source is byte-identical to mix_v1; temporal_wide.txt is the same generator at the same --target-mb 52, the same seed, the same anchor window, the same expressions and the same resolve_date payloads. check_copyable.py reads its copy slots at 1,442 effective values and 122.2 uses each — temporal.txt's figure to the decimal. prepare_mix lands on 130,279,711 train / 654,670 val, mix_v1's counts to the token. The utterance line is the only thing that moved, and the base arm still regenerates byte-identical, so nothing already scored was invalidated to get this.

The paraphrase gap goes from 50 items to 10, with the frozen row unchanged at 399/400 — nothing was traded away in-distribution to buy it. temporal.copy is 353/353 in all six cells, and the failure mode never changes, only its count: wrong_expression is the whole of it throughout, 52 → 11 on paraphrase, 33 → 17 on held-out, 2 → 1 on frozen.

Every non-temporal row is inside its floor — and the floors below are the ones a seed replicate on this corpus measured, not the borrowed ones this section originally quoted: tool.selection +7 against 41 on an arm and 19 frozen, schema.conform −1 against 5, tool.copy −8 against 8, tool.copy_wide +9 against 5 frozen. See the two sections below; the seed turns out to matter more than any of them.

Most of the gain is the edges that were added, which is the uncomfortable half

The wide pools were written after seeing which left edges failed, so a single before/after number cannot read this arm. Splitting the paraphrase items by whether their left edge is one the wide pools introduced:

There is no transfer, and the seed replicate is what says so

evals_heldout was pre-registered — built and hashed before nano20m_v1_wide finished training — precisely because the still-uncovered bucket above is 153 items with no measured floor and cannot carry a conclusion. Its 400 temporal items use 24 left edges appearing in neither pool, chosen to be absorbable in the way the mechanism predicts matters; check_heldout_edges fails the build if any is an edge either pool contains.

The first seed read 366 → 383, +17, and was written up here as a small real transfer with a caveat that a net +17 assembled from a +9 on one edge and a −6 on another was not something one arm at one seed could attribute. Both sides replicated, and the effect stops needing a floor at all

Everything above is four seeds of the treatment against one of the baseline. Four seeds of nano20m_v1 close that: with both sides replicated the question is no longer "does the delta clear a floor" but "do the two ranges overlap", which needs no borrowed constant and is in the same units as the rows. scripts/effect.py prints it.

So the intervention does exactly one thing, and the design can now say so without a floor: it moves temporal.route under paraphrase, entirely through the bucket whose left edges it added, and it moves nothing else anywhere.

The two claims this finding has carried since its first draft both survive, and one of them is now stronger than the version that was withdrawn and restored: the treated bucket separates with eight seeds and a 16-item gap between non-overlapping ranges, and the held-out arm overlaps with eight seeds, which is a negative that no further seeding will rescue.

The cost was overstated by an order of magnitude. lm temporal was reported as +0.0033 against a floor of 0.0009 — one seed against one seed. Four-a-side, mix_v1 spans 0.0939–0.0973 and mix_v1_wide spans 0.0976–0.0985: still separated, and by 0.0003. lm narrative, lm web and lm conversational all overlap, so they cost nothing measurable rather than "inside their floor". Widening the left context is close to free.

What this does not show

  • ~~One arm, one seed.~~ The replicate ran and took the transfer claim with it — see above. What survives is two seeds, which is a range and not a floor, and the nineteenth finding's own lesson is that treating one pair's spread as a threshold repeats the error one level down. The reproducible part of this arm is 88/88 on the treated bucket, twice.
  • It does not reach tool.selection. That row fell 22 items under paraphrase, the left-edge split never explained it, and widening the temporal templates moved it +7 against a floor of 8. The save_note fallback is still unexplained, and the equivalent widening of corn/toolgen.py's pools has not been run.
  • The gap does not fully close. 389 and 383 against a 399 ceiling.
  • The held-out edges are hand-written. Held out by construction and checked mechanically, but still one person's idea of an absorbable left edge rather than transcribed speech.

The rule this leaves

§11's copyable-span rule has been about a span's contents since the fourteenth finding. This adds the other half, now demonstrated rather than inferred: a span's boundaries are learned the same way, and enlarging that set behaves like enlarging any other memorisable set — it buys exactly what it contains, twice over, and transfers nothing. A generator emitting ten thousand distinct expressions behind six distinct left edges has built exactly the defect §8's guard exists to catch, and check_copyable.py — which counts effective values in the slot and passed this corpus at 1,442 — cannot see it.

The conversational decontamination flag was crying wolf, and had been since mix_conv20

Scoring this arm surfaced something unrelated to it: every scorecard with a conversational slice reads conversational CONTAMINATED (1/16 probes found in .../train.bin). It is on nano20m_conv20, nano20m_v1 and nano20m_v1_wide alike, and it is the third appearance of the same bug shape.

The fifth finding fixed a status that failed by design on the temporal slice — a substring search finds the <|call|> envelope in any corpus that contains the slice, so probes were split into bearing (carrying a held-out date, discriminating) and format (reported as context, never fatal). Marker-less texts were exempted with an argument that is correct for prose and wrong for SODA: "narrative is held out by reserved bytes, so any probe of it in the stream is a leak."

The hit is this probe:

"rvice for about 4 years now. I've had a lot of experience dealing with different types of custom"

It occurs 201 times in data/raw/conversational.txt — 200 in the region every mix legitimately reads, and once in the reserved tail. reserve_tail is working exactly as designed: the eval region begins at byte 780,537,489 and the only occurrence inside it is at 781,628,638. The probe is boilerplate, SODA is generated dialogue that repeats itself, and its presence downstream says nothing whatever about the eval text.

So the fix is the fifth finding's rule applied to the population it did not have to consider: a probe that is not unique in its own source file cannot discriminate a leak, exactly as a format probe cannot for a generated slice, and is partitioned off the same way. corn/evals.py reads the source path from the manifest's {name}_source record, counts each probe's occurrences in one pass, and only unique probes set the status. conversational now reads clean (15 of 16 probes unique to the reserved tail) with the duplicated hit printed beside it. Counted rather than filtered before sampling, because the count is what lets a text with no discriminating probe left read indeterminate rather than clean — the rule that a check which cannot fail is the thing this module exists to prevent.

Two properties worth stating, because a decontamination fix that quietly weakens the check is worse than the false alarm it removes. narrative and web keep all 16 probes: prose cut from a real file does not repeat itself, so nothing about them changes. And a planted probe of the reserved tail still reads CONTAMINATED — check_evals.py group 7 carries that as a regression test beside the new false positive, which is how the fifth finding's fix was guarded and for the same reason.

HARNESS_VERSION stays at 1. No scored quantity changed: the edit is confined to contamination() and two helpers, and nothing in nats_per_byte, evaluate_behaviour, evaluate_variety or evaluate_register was touched. The cost is ~25 s added to a full scoring run, almost all of it reading the 2.2 GB narrative source.

What it does not settle. Uniqueness in the source is a proxy for discriminating power, not the thing itself — a probe occurring twice, once in the tail and once by coincidence, is now discarded rather than investigated. At 96 bytes that coincidence is vanishingly unlikely in prose and evidently routine in generated dialogue, which is the asymmetry the fix is built on and not something it measures.
