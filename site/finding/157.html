Writing his move first steers the reply and beats the old regex

The result, as the finding states it

A3, the move line: written before the say, it steers the reply at both seeds (a forced move that is not the item's own is followed 509 and 552 of 580 times against 133 and 182 off), it beats the regex it would replace, and it costs no routing row at both seeds; the install gate splits, 84% and 64% of trained phrasings, carried by the thinnest family; unseen phrasings default to one value, and the reply follows the wrong label as faithfully as the right one

What it cost

60.0 min of training four nano runs (14.9-15.1 min each, card_cost.py; registered at ~16 min) and 13.5 min of scoring four standard cards; probe_move.py about 1 min a checkpoint, its post hoc pass about 26 s, the repeat probe 12-15 s; two mix builds about 10 min on the CPU; the build and the rulers' floors about 45 min (17:01-18:55 on 2026-10-09 end to end, against a registered ~1.5 h of card time — the card time came in at ~1.4 h).

ROADMAP A3, PLAN-TAGS §9, pre-registered in configs/mix_v3_a3_on.py before a byte trained (1c19480). The one variable is the move: line (corn/moveline.py, SPEC-LANGUAGE §4): the same 14,568 declared social transcripts — arm 11's recipe at four times its pairs, 1.8% of mix_v3 — trained once with a move: <family> line under every social user line and once without, two nano seeds a cell (1337, 2337), compile=True. 16,586 move lines, 5.9 tokens each, 4.0% of the slice; thanks 6,317, wellbeing 3,116, greeting 2,631, ack 2,421, open 1,513, consolation 588, farewell none. Cards: evals/results/nano20m_v3_a3_{off,on}{,_seed2}.json (standard), __a3.json and __a3_items.jsonl (the staged probe), __a3_posthoc.json (post hoc, below); harness/results/*__depth.json (the repeat). probe_move.py is a hand-built instrument held by scripts/check_moveline.py and nothing more: every staged number is a bound (rule 28).

The registered reading

The arm is readable: the off cells' declared social openers take no call on 104 and 99 of 112 (the unreadable bar was 68 — A2's off cells, with no social slice, read 4 and 68).

By the table, the reading is "seeds split on a gate — a direction": conditions and replaces pass at both seeds, no routing row falls at both (the kill needs both; tool.validity falls by one item at seed 1337 only), and installs splits. The registered row "the move line rides in 1.0" needed all four at both seeds, and this arm does not license it as written. No third seed was pre-registered and none is run.

The split is the thinnest family

tag.trained by family greeting wellbeing thanks ack consolation on, seed 1337 20/20 20/20 20/20 14/20 13/24 on, seed 2337 16/20 19/20 20/20 11/20 1/24

Without consolation the four well-supplied families read 74 and 66 of 80 (92%, 82%). consolation is 588 of 16,586 lines (3.5%) — the generator's supply rule holds it to its 21 usable replies — and it is 24 of the cell's 104 items (23%). At seed 2337 sixteen of its 24 are written open.

Read on the frames the slice trained on (post hoc, EXPERIMENTS §5.6), it installed at both seeds: 194 of 200 single-turn slice transcripts cut after the user line, at both seeds (off: 101 and 110, its prior); the same carriers under the eval's declaration 188 and 186. So the gate's miss is the eval cell's weighting of a thin family, not a failure to learn the line — which is the hand read and not the registered reading, and the finding says both.

The line steers the reply — harder than the utterance does

Every item under each of the seven move lines in turn, then <|say|> (forced): the reply answers a forced move that is not the item's own on 509 and 552 of 580 draws, against 133 and 182 on the off cells, which inserted the same line untrained (the word alone primes "thanks" there on 140 of 140; the shipped 260M follows 197 of 580). Read by hand, the forced reply is the move's prototype whatever was said: forced wellbeing gives "I'm doing well, thank you. How are you?" to "rough day at work", forced consolation gives "I'm sorry to hear that." to "got it". The label outweighs the utterance — which is what makes it worth writing, and what makes a wrong one expensive. farewell, never trained, is followed 0 of 120 at all four checkpoints; forced, it often reads as a refusal ("I'm sorry, but there's nothing I can do.").

Unseen phrasings default to one value

tag.probe_trained (100 unseen phrasings of the five trained families, PLAN-TAGS §10's set, under the eval's declaration) off 15, on 32; off 20, on 20 post hoc, under the slice's own declarations (200 draws) off 28, on 74; off 40, on 92

What he writes on a phrasing he has not seen is mostly one value, a different one at each seed: greeting at seed 1337 (16 of 20 farewells, 12 of 20 wellbeing questions, 11 of 20 acknowledgements), open at seed 2337 (77 of 120 — PLAN-TAGS §5's escape, the honest label for a move the list does not name, which is what it was defined to be and which one seed found). Rung 0's reading (PLAN-TAGS §10, not a finding: 0.902 trained against 0.475 unseen off the shipped vector) holds for the written label: it is attached to the phrasing, and the runtime cannot trust it on a phrasing no pool holds.

And because the reply follows the label (above), the label's error carries into the reply (post hoc): on the 120 unseen phrasings the reply answers the right family 37 and 25 times under his own label, against 32 and 20 for the off cells under the card's forced <|say|> — level — and 90 and 100 times under the gold label. The line neither helps nor hurts what he says on unfamiliar phrasings; naming the move is the whole bottleneck, and the reply would follow a right name nearly every time.

The cue D-A1.11 named — real, and not where it was predicted

Registered: a header cue (the line written under a declaration and rarely without one). It does not read that way: on bare openers he writes it on 103 and 79 of 112 against 101 and 105 declared, and tags them about as well (90 and 79 of 104 bare, 87 and 67 declared).

Post hoc, on the chat slice's own turns — SODA openings, trained in both cells with no move line (D-A1.11) — he writes it 0 of 200 at both seeds, and still 0 with the <|day|> line removed (187 and 199 go straight to a say). So the line is read off the register: a short social carrier gets a move, a scene opening ("Hey, Mattea. What's up?") does not — the slice it was left off is the shape on which it is absent. D-A1.11's cost is real; it is a register cue, not a header cue.

The repeated question does not fall — it rises, and the right family is why

Predicted unmoved (PLAN-TAGS §2). probe_depth.py, eight draws a case at the shipped 0.3, the on cells with --move written:

repeated / askable own/route own/bare given/route given/bare off 14/38 12/32 6/48 16/48 on 12/47 22/47 24/48 17/48 off, seed 2 3/27 1/22 32/48 21/48 on, seed 2 35/47 20/48 35/48 24/48

The off cells swing 6 to 32 between seeds on one arm, so these are directions: the on cell re-asks as often or more on seven of eight arms. By hand (seed 1337, given/route): to "good thanks, yourself?" the on cell writes move: wellbeing and answers in the slice's wellbeing register — "I'm doing well, thanks for asking. How's your day going?" — which hands the question back; the off cell's commonest reply to the same turn is "No problem. I'm happy to help." (8 of 48), a thanks reply that asks nothing. The line makes him answer the move that was made, and the slice's answers to that move ask back — 396 of 492 mined wellbeing replies do (configs/mix_v3_turn2_state.py, the dependent class's case). The repeat is the corpus's, and the line amplifies whatever a family's replies carry.

Instrument notes

  • The standard card cannot read an on cell's social rows, as the probe's docstring registered: it reads one line, so social.open reads 0 of 78 and social.self 10 and 2 of 47 on the on cells' cards — the first line is move: open — against 52 and 69 (open) under the staged route. check_moveline.py states the hole as a check.
  • Every move line he writes is followed by a say (60 of 60, 60 of 60, 101 of 101 at seed 1337), so on an on cell the route is decided at the first token: a call, or move:. Seed 1337 called on 13 more of the 120 unseen phrasings than its off cell and 11 more of the 78 open moves (net); seed 2337 called on 9 and 8 fewer. No direction.
  • tool.selection reads 46 and 90 on the off cells against 128 on mix_v3 with no social slice — the payload's own cost, both cells alike, and the on cells read above it at both seeds (87, 106).

What it licenses — for the owner, at D7

  • The move line is load-bearing at nano: written on trained phrasings, followed by the reply at both seeds by more than sixty points over a control that saw the same word, better than the regex it would replace, no routing kill. By the registered table it is a direction, not a pass — the install gate splits on the thinnest family.
  • What it cannot do is the generalisation the thesis asks for: on phrasings no pool holds it writes one default value, and the reply follows that value. If it rides, the runtime must not trust it there — PLAN-TAGS §6's comparison against the detector is the guard — and the corpus needs carrier variety per family (diversity-denies-shortcuts) more than it needs the line.
  • Two corpus consequences, named before the freeze: labelled moves on the conversational register too (C1's conversations carry gold moves), or he never writes the line where people talk like the chat slice; and a thin family under the supply rule does not install — consolation needs more distinct replies, not more draws.
  • The repeated question is the slice's replies, amplified: the line makes the family right and the family's replies ask back. The dependent class (social_dep) is where that is fixed, not the line.

What it does not say

Anything at 260M (nano flips sign there, finding 51; D11's pilot is where it is read). That the line installs with a fuller consolation — untested. That the post hoc frame reads are the registered reading — they are not. That the move line helps the reply on unfamiliar phrasings — it is level there.

Amended 2026-10-09: why it fails, read on the four checkpoints (post hoc)

Four reads, no training, written after the registered read and labelled post hoc wherever they are quoted (probe_move.py --swap, and the items files already on disk). Each answers a failure above with a cause.

Why the line steers so hard — the corpus made the move sufficient. The slice draws every reply from its family's pool independently of what was said (corn.socialgen._draw_reply), so in the training data the move tells you everything about the reply and the message adds nothing. The swap read holds the item's gold move and replaces the message (__a3_swap.json):

reply after the message is replaced off on off s2 on s2 word-for-word the same (of 140) another message of the same family 19 32 27 75 a message of another family 14 39 19 71 a request ("set a timer for ...") 4 27 28 51 still answers the move it was given 26 99 36 116 (another family's message)

By hand: under greeting the on cell answers "set a timer for ten minutes" with "Not bad. How are you doing?"; under thanks, "copy that" and the timer request both get "No problem, I'm glad I could help." The off cell's replies move with the message. He has not stopped reading — the wording still shifts on most swaps at seed 1337 — but the move outranks the message. This one cause accounts for three readings above: the steering (+64 and +65 points), the narrow replies (under the gold move 25 greetings get 6 and 2 distinct replies, 28 low moods 7 and 2, against 15 and 12, 14 and 19 off; the slice carries 272 and 21), and the repeat (the wellbeing pool asks back, and the move picks the pool). How hard the line steers is a property of the slice it rides on, not of the line.

Why unseen phrasings fail — he falls to a default, not to a neighbour. A letter-trigram lookup against the slice's 2,913 distinct tagged user lines names the move of 49 of the 100 unseen phrasings; he names 32 and 20. Of his wrong labels only 13 of 68 and 9 of 80 are the nearest phrase's move: the rest are the seed's default (greeting, open). The label is recognition of a trained phrasing, narrower than surface similarity; 446 distinct greeting lines and 136 consolation lines are not enough to teach what the move is.

Whether his confidence gives him away — at one seed. The gap between his best and second-best option (summed NLL) is 3.66 and 3.36 median when right on trained phrasings, 1.38 and 0.48 when wrong. Trusting a label only at a gap of 3 or more keeps 104 of 298 labels at 90% right and flags 100 of 110 wrong ones at seed 1337; at seed 2337 it keeps 129 at 83%, because its wrong labels on unseen phrasings are confident (median gap 2.14 against 1.69 for its right ones) — they are its default, open.

open is doing two jobs. PLAN-TAGS §5 made it both the open move (a joke, a view) and the escape for a move the list does not name. Read as "don't know", the seed split mostly closes: on trained phrasings each seed is right on 85% of the labels it commits to (87 of 102, 67 of 79), seed 2337 abstaining on 25. But the reply follows open's register: seed 2337 answers "barely slept" with "I'll have to go to the park. What's next?". An escape the reply obeys is not an escape; the runtime would have to take the reply over when he writes it.

Amended 2026-10-09 (second): is what he says coherent, and who should name the move (post hoc, no GPU)

His own reply, the move his to choose (the probe's staged route, off the items files): it answers the move that was made on 82 and 75 of 104 familiar phrasings against 67 and 54 for the off cells — so on the turns he knows, the line makes him more coherent — but it answers the kind of turn and never its content: "my mom is sick" and "my cat is sick" get the same "I'm sorry to hear that. What's been going on?". On the 100 unseen phrasings it answers the move on 26 and 18 (off 21 and 10) and declines on 44 and 31 (off 37 and 37); read by hand, "you in good form" gets "I'm sorry to hear that. What's been going on?" under a greeting label and "life treating you well" gets "I'll take the spoken date. What's next?" under open. No farewell is answered as one, on or off.

Who names the move (probe_move.py --trust, __a3_trust.json). The shipped runtime's own test is corn.agent.social_family, an exact match against the hatch pools. Right / wrong / none of four policies:

detector his both guarded (gap 3, open = none) familiar (104) 92/0/12 87/17/0 98/6/0 92/0/12 67/37/0 92/12/0 92/0/12 unseen (100) 0/0/100 32/68/0 32/68/0 13/4/83 20/80/0 20/80/0 3/0/97 open moves (78) 0/0/78 58/20/0 58/20/0 0/3/75 76/2/0 76/2/0 0/0/78

(seed 1337 above, seed 2337 below.) Where the detector fires it is never wrong — on these items by construction: the familiar carriers are the hatch pools. Where it is silent his label is the only source, and it is reliable for one move and not the others: right on 58 and 76 of 78 open moves, wrong on 68 and 80 of 100 unseen phrasings of the five others. The guard (a confident label, open read as don't-know) keeps the wrong count near zero and buys almost nothing (13 and 3 of 100) — and throws away the open moves he gets right. And because the reply follows the move, the turns the detector covers are better served by the runtime writing the move than by trusting his: under the gold move his reply answers it on 111 and 120 of 140 against 95 and 82 under his own. That is SPEC §0's rule — a line the runtime can compute is the runtime's — arriving at the move line on the turns the hatch knows.

Amended 2026-10-09 (third): the conditions count was inflated — corrected

A blind second read of the raw cards (a separate analyst, CPU only, not shown this finding) caught it, and it is confirmed here. forced.cf counts a reply as following the forced move when that move is among the families reply_families reads in it — and many replies carry two ("No problem, I'm glad I could help. How are you doing?" reads as thanks and wellbeing): 324 and 302 of 980 forced replies on the on cells, 145 and 122 off, and in the slice itself 2,653 of 6,317 thanks replies and 1,832 of 3,116 wellbeing replies. Such a reply "follows" two forced moves at once. Counting only a reply whose sole family is the forced one:

off on off s2 on s2 forced.cf, registered (loose) 133 509 182 552 of 580 forced.cf, that family alone 114 271 99 365

+27 and +46 points instead of +65 and +64. The gate still passes at both seeds (its bar is 10), so the registered reading does not change; every size quoted above for the steering is the loose count and overstates it.

Two more points from the same read, both checked against the cards:

  • The conditions gate could hardly fail on this slice. The slice makes the move a sufficient statistic for the reply (the first amendment), so teacher forcing all but guarantees the reply follows the line. The contrast that says what the line buys is his own label against no line: his reply answers the move made on +15 and +21 more of 104 familiar phrasings (82, 75 against 67, 54) and +5 and +8 of 100 unseen ones.
  • Labelling tracks how often a phrase was seen (the second analyst's count of exposures per normalised carrier in the slice): carriers seen 61 or more times are labelled 47 of 47 and 45 of 47; carriers seen 10 or fewer, 9 of 17 and 0 of 17. A bag-of-words classifier trained on the slice's user lines reaches about 37-44 of 100 unseen phrasings (its figure, not re-run here) — the nets read no better than counting words.

Amended 2026-10-09 (fourth): how much the runtime's detector covers, and how much the seven tags cover, on natural openings

Asked because the second amendment's plan — the runtime writes the move wherever its detector fires — is only worth what the detector covers, and the second analyst named under 30% as the coverage that would change the plan. No GPU.

Natural dialogue. 150 first user turns of the chat slice (SODA scene openings, data/raw/chat_day.txt's first 12 MB, random.Random(20261009)), each labelled by hand with the move the reply must answer — evals/results/a3_soda_moves_hand.json, one labeller (Claude), the owner's labels may differ:

share (news, feelings, excitement, a volunteered view) 34 request (a task, a question for information, "can we talk") 24 consolation 15 wellbeing 13 greeting 12 thanks 10 open 9 ack 2 farewell 1 other (concern for the listener, an offer, congratulations) 15 complaint 8 apology 7

corn.agent.social_family names a move on 0 of the 62 that make one of the seven, and fires on 0 of 150. It is an exact match against short stock forms: "hey, how's it going?" is a greeting, "Hey Timothy, how's it going?" and "hey how's it going" are nothing. On the owner's own typed turns (Saved Sessions/turns.jsonl, multi-turn sessions typed at human pace, counts only — no text read) it names a move on 8 of the 26 turns he answered without a call, all short stock greetings and thanks. So the runtime writing the move covers stock phrasings and almost nothing a person says at length; the move on natural turns is his to name or nobody's, and that is the labelling the arm read at 20-32 of 100 unseen.

And the seven tags cover half of the social turns. Of the 126 openings that are social at all, 62 are one of the seven and 64 are not: sharing news or a feeling is 34 — more than any tag in the set — complaints 8, apologies 7, and 15 more (concern for the listener, an offer, congratulations). With open as the residual it would take half of every social turn, and because the reply follows the move, half of all social replies would be open's. These are scene openings between people, not what people say to an assistant — the distribution there is unmeasured — but on the only natural sample in the repo the set is too narrow by half, and the largest missing move (share) is larger than any present one.

Amended 2026-10-09 (fifth): the summed tag score leans short, by a little; and the slice held more than the nets took

The instrument. tag_of sums the NLL of the whole move: <value> line, and the values differ in length on bpe8k ( open, thanks one token; gre et ing, well be ing, cons ol ation three). Re-scored on the value tokens alone (probe_move.py --tagscore, __a3_tagscore.json):

summed per-token mean first token trained (104) 87 67 91 75 92 69 unseen (100 + 20 fw) 32 20 37 26 36 19 natural (62) 33 28 28 31 48 30

(seed 1337, seed 2337.) The short-value lean costs the unseen cell 4-6 items, not the gap: under every scoring he names fewer unseen phrasings than a bag-of-words classifier fit on his own slice's tagged lines does (scripts/probe_move_gate.py: 48 of 100). The slice held the word evidence and the nets did not take it — at nano, on one pass, the label is memorised per phrasing. First-token scoring — the choice a grammar-bound greedy decode makes — reads natural openings 48 of 62 at seed 1337, and is reported beside the summed score from A3b on. The untrained floor's first token prefers f (farewell, 19 of 20 goodbyes at both off cells), a generic token: no scoring is free of a floor.
