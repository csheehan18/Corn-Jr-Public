Social replies install by fine-tune; the fix moves failures elsewhere

The result, as the finding states it

The social class installs on top of ft4g, the family repair moves the failure rather than removing it, and the vague tail closes by a line

What it cost

0 s of new scoring on record; it reads 2 cards scored for an earlier finding (corn_jr_260m_v4, corn_jr_260m_v4_ft4g). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

The declared social turn, docs/PLAN-SOCIAL-SLICE.md, three fine-tunes of corn_jr_260m_v4 on the 3080 over 2026-09-01 and 2026-09-02, one seed each. The second seed the plan pre-registered was passed over by the owner on 2026-09-02 and this finding is written on one seed per arm; every number below is one checkpoint's, and is quoted as that.

The class the corpus had zero of

No transcript in mix_v4_5b put a declaration above a <|user|> answered by <|say|> with no call — a hard zero over six marked slices at 60MB each — and the runtime assembles exactly that context on every social turn. Measured on the shipping checkpoint over evals/social.jsonl (112 items, three prompts each), the declaration cost 12.5 points of abstention on identical carriers and collapsed a five-tool spread onto decline:

social.bare_nocall 53/112 47.3% web_search 28 resolve_date 12 decline 12 social.declared_nocall 39/112 34.8% decline 47 web_search 26

The slice that supplies the class is 1,821 matched pairs on one declaration each — a social move answered by speech beside a request the declared set does not cover answered by decline — with the replies mined from SODA and narrowed to the vocabulary of the runtime's own 97 hand-written pool lines.

Arm 1 and arm 2 — the frame installs, and the residue is a carrier shape

The declaration's effect reversed sign (-12.5 to +3.6). Arm 1 lost three novel decline turns of one shape — verb + pronoun object, no noun ("ping me in twenty minutes") — which is structurally what a social move looks like and which the balancing half had no example of; arm 2 supplied the shape and closed two of the three, while "return the boots that didn't fit", never the shape, still missed. The regression set was clean on both: every frozen copy row at ceiling, refusal.say 270/270, tool.selection back at 198/200.

The row that was thin, and the payload that taught the failure

social.family — does the reply answer the move made — was added after a live conversation produced two replies every other row passed. On arm 2 it read 48.2%, and 40 of its 58 failures were "no family marker". Re-decoded and read one by one: ack was a collapse (2/22), about nine of the forty were answers the marker list could not see, and thirty-one answered nothing. The payload told the same story — under the same markers, 402 of the 1,821 replies arm 2 trained on carry no family and 180 answer a different one — because the generator's tagger accepted a word where a family is a move, and its exclusion clause could never fire.

The ruler was widened first, with the reply-side forms the reads produced, and re-run greedy on all three checkpoints so the correction is a number:

social.family first cut widened ft4g 33.0% 47.3% arm 1 43.8% 63.4% arm 2 48.2% 63.4% ack 9/22, greeting 15/25

Then the generator: reply-side tags, a working exclusion, four scene rules, six register words. The pool went 1,704 → 1,048 distinct at 0 of 97 pool lines refused, and 98.8% of it sits inside the eval's markers — which is the caveat, stated before the arm ran: from arm 3 on, social.family is a capability floor in exactly the sense social.voice already is.

Arm 3 — on top of ft4g, and what the two variables bought

Owner's decision before the relaunch: the extraction work on disk is a set of reader and verifier heads trained on corn_jr_260m_v4_ft4g's cached states, and ft4g is what server.json serves, so arm 3 initialises from ft4g rather than the frozen base. Same recipe as arms 1 and 2, the revised generator's file, best at iteration 1,000 (val 0.5874). Two variables against arm 2, one against ft4g; the plan says which row is read against which.

row ft4g arm 2 arm 3 social.bare_nocall 47.3% 84.8% 84.8% social.declared_nocall 34.8% 88.4% 85.7% social.voice 50.9% 76.8% 80.4% social.family 47.3% 63.4% 58.0% ack 9/22 -> 5/22 social.farewell 55.0% 65.0% 60.0% social.heldout_carrier 66.7% 83.3% 83.3% grid A, pooled decline 206/208 207/208 206/208 novel 32 / 31 / 30 of 32 refusal.say 270/270 270/270 270/270 extract.span / abstain 97/111 99/107 91/116

The target fell by six items, and the species say why. "No family marker" went 23 → 19 and "answers greeting" 3 → 14: "cool" → "I'm doing alright, thanks for asking. How are you?", "alright" → "Hey there. How are you doing today?". The tightened greeting tag anchored the register on a hello and a question back — 278 of arm 3's 443 greeting payload replies open with a hello against 134 of arm 2's 466 — and a concentrated register generalises to the wrong carriers. ack is the collapse before and after; its pool is 91 lines at five uses each, and the two lines drawn ten times are scene lines the tag let through. So the payload's labelling was not what limited the row, and ack is supply. social.voice +3.6 is the tighter pool doing the one thing it was built for.

The gates hold from a second-stage init. Every frozen copy row at its ceiling, refusal.say unmoved, tool.selection -1, note_fidelity -3, and the extract pair -6 / +5 — inside finding 60's floor, and the one number the extraction session should read before this trunk is served under heads trained on ft4g's states. Grid A is ft4g's 206/208: trained decline improved by two, novel lost two, one of them arm 1's "snooze that for ten" returning.

The vague tail closed by a line, mostly. On a 22-utterance battery from a cleared history, arm 2 declines 5 and arm 3 declines 15; on the 16 unseen surfaces, 5 → 11, and every longer tail ("when you get a chance", "at some stage", "after a while") reaches decline. What still speaks is the one-word tail — "later", "in a bit", "sometime" — on both held-out probes and four others. The residue is a word wide rather than a shape.

Verdict: arm 2 stays the keepable social checkpoint. Arm 3 carries the class on top of ft4g at nearly arm 2's level with every gate inside its floor, but is below arm 2 on the target and on grid A and above it only on voice and the vague tail. Kept from it on their own merits: the vague-tail carriers, the generator's exclusion fix and scene rules. Not kept: the greeting tag's hello anchoring. If the ft4g-based trunk is wanted, the next arm is arm 2's file from the ft4g init — one variable, 25 minutes.

Two things that are not the model

The hatch was the wrong way round. check_hatches.py recorded twelve social phrasings that reached the router, held back on 2026-08-29 because a wider hatch was then a wider frozen set. Since arm 2 he writes those replies himself either way (heldout_carrier 10/12), so a hatch miss had come to mean only that social_rejection never saw the sentence: speaking on more turns grew the unguarded surface. The twelve are in the pools now (18 → 30 of 30), and stay out of the corpus by name so the row stays a generalisation row.

The supply number was two numbers. The plan's "1.07 uses per distinct" was draws over pool size; draws over replies actually used read 2.06 on arm 2 and 2.38 on arm 3, with ack near five on both. Finding 34's band was measured on a whole slice, and the thin families sat outside it on an arm that landed.

Limits

One seed per arm, by the owner's decision. social.voice and, from arm 3, social.family are floors rather than unbiased estimates, because the filter that narrowed the payload resembles the ruler; the routing pair is the clean read and the reads-by-eye are the unbiased half. n=12 on the held-out carriers and n=24 on grid A's novel column, so single items move those rows by four to eight points. The vague-tail battery is 22 hand-written utterances against two checkpoints.
