Moving the date header recovers about ninety items it was costing

The result, as the finding states it

Decorrelating <|day|> recovers about ninety of the items it was costing

What it cost

0 s of new scoring on record; it reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (nano20m_v1_chat). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

configs/mix_v1_chatday.py ×2 against nano20m_v1_chat ×2. One file swapped in a 5% slice: chat.txt → chat_day.txt, same 194,337 transcripts, same weight to the token, a <|day|> header above 96,755 of them.

The controls hold, so the arm is readable. register.conv is the one that decides this — the twenty-fifth finding priced it at exactly this dialogue share against a 1.0-point floor, the tightest in the project.

The header cost 137 items on average and now costs 46

daycross is a paired difference — the same 200 tool requests scored bare, then again with a header above them — and neither cell means anything alone.

The ranges do not overlap and the gap is about 90 items against a within-arm spread of 34. By the thirtieth finding's rule that is a size worth quoting, not merely a direction.

The mechanism moved the way the config said it had to. The header's damage was specifically a collapse onto resolve_date, and that is what receded:

A pass rate that moved while the naming did not would have been a different result from the one this arm asked for. It moved together, which is what makes the reading the intended one.

Capacity was making it worse, which is why this mattered

The bug is not a missing capability and no amount of temporal data addresses it. daycross read −126 at 29.6M and −150 at 91.6M — a bigger model read the spurious cue harder. That is the signature of a correlation in the corpus rather than a limit of the model, and it is why the fix had to be corpus-side and why "it will wash out at scale" was never available as an answer.

What this does not settle

  • The residue is still −29 to −63. Half the effect at best. 5% of the budget carrying a header on half its transcripts is up against a temporal slice where the header sits above every transcript, and nothing here says where the remaining cost lives. The obvious next dose is the share; the obvious next axis is headers on the tools slice, which the config names as out of scope for one-variable-at-a-time reasons.
  • chat.abstain_oos rose from [7, 29] to [26, 39] and that is not claimed as a second result. The config asked only that it not fall. The ranges overlap at 26–29, it is one row, and nothing in the arm's design predicts it.
  • chat.engage fell by 1 item on both seeds. Reported because it is a control and controls get reported, not because 1 of 100 means anything.
  • Two seeds, and daycross had no measured floor before this arm. What licenses the size claim is that the two ranges do not overlap and the gap is roughly three times the wider within-arm spread — not that either spread is known.
  • Nothing here is measured above 29.6M. The bug is known to be worse at 91.6M, so the fix surviving scale is exactly the thing this cannot assume. Re-reading daycross on a 91.6M chatday checkpoint is the cheapest thing that would settle it.
