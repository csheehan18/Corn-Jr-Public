Measurement and noise · 17 August 2026

That 18-item swing was one fact; the test held only six answers

The result, as the finding states it

A hundred and twenty items, six answers, and the rule was already written

The seventeenth finding said tool.copy moves 18 items on the seed alone and called it noise. Regenerating the row on both checkpoints and diffing item by item says otherwise. The row fell 18 items and 17 of them are one answer:

1337 <|say|>Alexander Fleming. 1338 <|say|>It's Flemingbelander Flemingbel.

17 of the 20 the discoverer of penicillin items flipped from pass to fail, and nothing else did. Flemingbel appears in none of the 8,697 training FACTS — the model is not reciting a memorised answer, it is garbling a copy mid-name, which is a different failure from the one trained_answer was built to catch and scores as wrong_answer correctly.

evals/tool_copy.jsonl is 120 prompts over six answers. HELD_OUT_FACTS is six hand-written facts and the builder wraps each in ~20 of the 25 SEARCH_TEMPLATES:

answeritems
1,642 metres at its deepest22
the Mekong22
Alexander Fleming20
Kinshasa20
up to 3.5 metres18
3,422 degrees Celsius18

So the row's effective sample size is 6 and its resolution is one fact — about 20 items, or 17 points. Every movement the project has read off it is a whole number of facts: 120→111, 120→114, 120→102 and 120→99 are all ±1, and the thirteenth finding's 27→120 is about five, which is why that one was never in doubt. The small ones never meant anything.

The number was in the manifest the whole time

make_evals.py has written tool_copy_answers into evals/manifest.json since the day that row was built, with a comment saying it plainly — the prompts are 120 distinct sentences; the answers are six. It has read 6 for five findings. Nothing consumed it, no check asserted it, and every finding since quoted the row out of 120.

It is §11's own rule, pointed at the eval instead of the corpus

§11 states it: any span the model is meant to copy must come from a space too large to learn as a set. It cost three findings in the corpus — 28 search facts, 400 field names, and the sixth finding's ANCHOR_END read backwards — and check_copyable.py now enforces it over data/raw/*.txt so it cannot recur there. Nothing enforced it on evals/, and the row built to test that very rule was the smallest set in the project. The corpus-side guard and the eval-side hole were written eight days apart.

The widened row, and why it had to be invented

evals/tool_copy_wide.jsonl is the same items from a search index wide enough to resolve something: 300 prompts over 232 answers, 1.29 each against 20.00. It went in with make_evals.py --add, so all eight prior sha256 and built are untouched and every existing scorecard stays comparable — tool_copy.jsonl keeps its quantum and is still scored, first, in the same place on every scorecard.

Hand-writing 200 more real facts was the obvious route and is the wrong one, for the reason corn/tools.py already gives about the training index: every real fact written down is one a larger model could know, and a model answering Kinshasa from its weights scores identically to one that read the observation. So HELD_OUT_FACTS_WIDE is invented — 560 facts over the same six shapes, drawn from the master name pool past what _invent_facts consumes, which makes the two worlds disjoint by partition rather than by luck. FACTS is byte-identical before and after (sha256 verified), which matters more than it looks: score_tool_copy reads FACTS to decide what counts as a trained_answer, so redefining it would have silently changed what fourteen existing scorecards mean.

The same substring bug, a third time, in the numbers

Three of the six shapes draw from disjoint name pools and collide zero times. The two integer shapes were designed the same way — depths 2,010–4,990 against training's 120–1,990, melting points 3,810–5,900 against 230–3,800, ranges chosen so the numbers cannot coincide — and it does not work:

held out '2,566 metres at its deepest' training '566 metres at its deepest' <- matches, word-bounded

The thousands comma is a word boundary, so every comma-formatted four-digit answer contains a three-digit one. 40 of 600 candidates are dropped for it, all depths and melting points. This is the twelfth finding's bug a third time — a short numeric answer found inside a longer one — and it means the collision filter is the mechanism on two of six shapes rather than the guard it was written as. Wingspans escape only by accident of format: three decimals against training's two puts a digit where the boundary would have to be. The docstring claiming range-disjointness was wrong and now says this instead.

The guard, and what it caught on its first run

check_evals.py gains two groups and is now 16. Group 15 is the general form: every .jsonl in the manifest must declare a maximum items-per-target, and a row that does not declare one fails. That is check_copyable.py's argument about undeclared slices — all three instances got in because nobody thought to look — so the fix is a table nobody can add a row without editing.

schema.jsonl2002001.00≤ 1.0

The frozen row is declared as broken rather than quietly exempted: it cannot be rebuilt without moving its sha256, so it keeps its quantum, the number is pinned so it cannot get worse, and tool.copy_wide is the row to read. On its first run the group failed — variety.jsonl was undeclared — which is the guard doing its job on a row that legitimately has no gold target; it now declares None explicitly, on the same argument check_copyable.py makes for an empty tuple. Group 16 is the wide row's holdout, and the queries and answers were also checked against data/raw/tools.txt directly: 0 of 560 queries, 0 of 560 answers and 0 of 300 user lines appear in the corpus that was trained on.

What this does not say

Nothing here is a model result — no checkpoint changed and no corpus moved. It is a measurement about a ruler, and the ruler was wrong in the direction that inflates confidence: five findings quoted a six-way row out of 120. The seventeenth finding's general claim is not withdrawn, because tool.validity moved 13 items over 105 distinct targets and variety.distinct3 moved 1.85 points, and neither has a clustering story. Those two are what the seed replicates are actually measuring.
