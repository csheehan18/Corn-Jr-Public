Conversation and the second turn · 18 August 2026

Reply length inflates the measured style gain, but the gain survives

The result, as the finding states it

The length confound is real, it is small, and turn 1 was the control sitting in the file the whole time

What it cost

8.0 min of scoring on the 2 cards first cited here (nano20m_v1_chat_seed2, nano20m_v1_seed2); it also reads 2 cards scored for an earlier finding (nano20m_v1, nano20m_v1_chat). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

The handoff that opened turn2_reply.* named the thing that stopped it being a claim: replies after a talk preamble are longer than after a tool one — median 7/8, 7/10, 11/12, 11/12 across the four checkpoints — and pooled distinct-3 is not independent of length. "A conversational preamble carries register into the next turn" and "a conversational preamble makes him say more" both fit every number in that table, and it said so rather than picking one.

This is the length-controlled read. The confound is real, it inflates the gap by roughly 0.005 to 0.015, and the gap survives removing it on four checkpoints of four. No GPU was needed for the analysis and none of the published numbers moved.

Nothing was re-generated, and that is checkable

_reply_aggregate was already pure — "no model, no rng, no clock" — and _reply_records seeds generation to seed + i per item index. So retaining every reply instead of max_samples' eight cannot change a scored quantity, and re-scoring the four checkpoints reproduces every aggregate in every cell to the digit: zero mismatches across first, second, first_by_preamble and second_by_preamble on all four. The records block added to evaluate_turn2_reply is the whole change, and it makes every future length question a re-analysis rather than another 4× pass.

HARNESS_VERSION stays 1. No scored quantity changed meaning; a field was added beside them.

The second confound, which the handoff table did not mention

The 200 utterances are disjoint between the cells — 100 carry a tool preamble, 100 carry a talk one, and check_evals.py asserts no utterance appears in both. So the raw talk − tool column is partly a difference between two sets of openings rather than between two preambles.

The control for it was already in the harness and unused: first_by_preamble scores those same two item sets at turn 1, where there is no preamble at all. Differencing it out is the difference-in-differences, and it matters — at turn 1 the item sets do not favour talk consistently, and on nano20m_v1 they run 0.9332 against 0.9056 the other way.

Length is a separate problem and DiD does not touch it, because the length gap is created by the preamble rather than inherited from the items: the medians are equal or near-equal at turn 1 and split only at turn 2.

The matched t1 column is the sharpest thing here. Length-matched, with no preamble in front of either cell, the talk items carry no advantage at all — two of four are negative. The gap appears exactly when the preamble does.

Not one of the four clears the seed spread that produced it. scripts/effect.py's argument applies here unchanged — two seeds are a spread and not a standard deviation — so what this finding adds is that length does not explain the gap, and no interval for its size exists. The thirtieth finding's rule is the reason this table is printed rather than a single number.

This is a within-checkpoint contrast and is not the trap. The handoff's standing caution is about the tool cell separating arm from baseline at a margin of 0.0106 against a within-arm spread of 0.0474. Nothing above quotes that comparison. talk against tool inside one checkpoint is a different contrast with a different denominator.

The mechanism that would have explained it away, ruled out

The obvious way length produces this without any register story: short replies are where canned text lives, twenty replies of "Okay." share every trigram they have, and the shorter cell scores low for being repetitive rather than for being flat.

It is not happening. Duplicate-reply rate in the tool cell is 0.010, 0.020, 0.010, 0.030 — the most repeated reply in any cell appears two or three times in a hundred ("i'm doing", "i don't have a problem", "of course what's up", "not much what's up with you"). The talk cell is 0.000–0.020, i.e. the same. Whatever the tool cell is doing worse, it is not saying the same sentence repeatedly, and the diversity difference is finer-grained than verbatim repetition.

The one read that dissents, kept because it does

Truncating every reply to its first K words gives each one exactly K − 2 trigrams:

The gap is large in the opening words and decays toward zero, crossing it on two of four checkpoints by K ≈ 12. Two readings fit: the effect lives in how he opens the second turn and washes out once enough of the reply is read, or the long tail is thin and this is the sample shrinking. Both are consistent with the matched read, which is why the matched read is the headline and this one is printed underneath it rather than averaged in.

What it costs to go further

Nothing, for the re-analysis: scripts/length_control.py reads the retained records and the four result files are on disk. Sizing the effect needs more seeds, and the thirty-first finding is the standing warning about what that buys — this one does not claim the extra seeds are worth it.

.venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/length_control.py \ --base nano20m_v1 nano20m_v1_seed2 \ --treat nano20m_v1_chat nano20m_v1_chat_seed2
