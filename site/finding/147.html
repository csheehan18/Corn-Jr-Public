The tag spine · 2 October 2026

When the runtime holds the structure, invented words vanish; grouping stays his

The result, as the finding states it

The nested bind owns the form and the copy pointer, not the count: invented spans, repeats, splices and swapped number words go to zero, legal lines go from 516 to 2,917 of 3,456, and a marked chain passes the three-level depth wall for the first time, while where a group closes, whether a nested group opens at all and which request words he drops stay his — he skips words where he used to invent them, and the struct column's rise on nested rows is half a broken line becoming readable

What it cost

about 100 min of GPU, no training — 20 min for arm F seed 1's six cards (2026-10-01, 22:13-22:33) and 80 min for the other 26 (2026-10-02, 08:55-10:15), a bound ladder or nestgen card 4-6 min except on G2 seed 2, where the stranding hole below took them to 13.5 and 10.3 min; the read, its tables and the grammar repair on CPU, under 5 min.

PLAN-TAG-LANGUAGE §6 rung 0 and SPEC-LANGUAGE §9, read 2026-10-02. The decode-time bind for the nested parse line (ParseConstraint(line="nested"), corn/grammar.py, with the four rules rung 0's first decodes added at 59cc8f8) against free greedy decoding on the four checkpoints on disk: arm F (nano20m_v3_fw_armf, seeds 1337 and 2337) and arm G2 (nano20m_v3_fw_ladder_add, both seeds). No weight trained. The bound decoder is probe_nestgen.bound_line: greedy wherever a token is legal, and it takes a token back when stranded. Instruments: probe_ladder.py (576 lines a checkpoint), probe_nestgen.py (288), and the two hand probes, probe_hand_arity15.py and probe_hand_depth.py --marked, run by scripts/run_rung0.py; read by scripts/read_rung0.py, which decodes nothing. Cards: evals/results/<ckpt>__r0_* and evals/results/rung0.json — arm F seed 1's committed at 444dd67, the rest land with this finding. read_rung0.py's second table, added for this read, and the hand counts are a hand-built reader over those cards, checked against the probe's own struct to the line but not pinned by a guard, so every number below is a bound (rule 28).

The pre-registered expectation (PLAN-TAG-LANGUAGE §6, before any bound line was decoded): repeats, splices, invented spans and unbalanced lines vanish; the depth ceiling and the flattening do not. Three of those four held. The depth ceiling did not.

What the bind owns — form

Pooled over both probes, all four checkpoints (3,456 lines an arm):

species free bound legal 516 2,917 invented span 1,255 0 out of order (repeat/splice) 81 0 unbalanced 1,604 334 case (a capital lowercased) 0 205

Per row, free legal is 1-29 of 96 and bound 49-94 of 96 (read_rung0.py's first table). Invented and out-of-order are zero on every row of every checkpoint, as the grammar guarantees. A line he already had right comes back byte-identical: 504 of the 516 free-legal lines; the other twelve are lines the stream is stricter about than nested_accepts (a word between two top-level parts, a leading space). On the ladder the number word follows the same rule — "a run of three" written for "a run of four" goes from 118 lines to 0, and the request's number words survive whole on 902 lines against 608.

The struct question, settled by reading

The ladder's struct rises on every nested row (arm F nest4 0 -> 21, nest3 6 -> 22) and is flat or down on outer rows. Three things are mixed in that column, and reading the lines separates them.

It cannot read a line that runs out. probe_ladder.py scores structure through parse_nested, which raises on an open bracket, so a free line that never closed scores struct false whatever it wrote — 225 of the 1,152 free nested-row lines cannot be read at all, of 570 unbalanced one way or the other. Closing each free line's brackets at its end and scoring again is what the bind would get for making lines readable and nothing else.

It passes a k-member group of the wrong lookups. So read_rung0.py's second table adds group: a group at the row's position whose members are the gold group's lookups. Pooled over the four checkpoints, 1,152 lines a cell:

position column free free, closed bound nested struct 72 161 240 nested group 32 83 135 outer struct 392 415 359 outer group 285 298 271

On nested rows half the struct rise is a broken line becoming readable (72 -> 161 of the 72 -> 240), and only 130 of the 240 bound struct hits are the right group — the rest are a group of k members in the wrong place, often inside the label stacks below. On the right-group column the bind's own contribution is about 50 lines of 1,152 (83 -> 135): real, and small.

What the real part is, read on 16 pairs where the bound line has the gold group and the free line does not (four a checkpoint): one mechanism. Inside a top-level part the bind admits no jump — the part's words must be one contiguous stretch of the request — so the inner carrier the free line skipped or rewrote has to be copied: "four in one go" (free: dropped, or "two go"), "the four together" ("the three"), "a triple", "all four", "i only need in one go", "both". With the carrier written he opens the group at it, and the members follow. A refused repeat does the rest: arm F flad-027-nest4 wrote "the same for the chief export of Orlunstad" twice for five members, and bound wrote it once for four. The group is right; the line around it usually is not (whole-line exact match is 0-3 of 288 nested lines in either arm), because of what the bind leaves to him.

What is left to the weights — structure and vocabulary apart

left to him column free -> bound (ladder, 2,304 lines) where a group closes structure arity hand probe: no width past ten whether a nested group opens structure nested items with none: 819 -> 561 of 1,152 empty wrappers structure one-member groups: 188 -> 2,891 which request words to drop coverage legal lines dropping 5+ words: 35 -> 499 which label vocabulary an invented do: 359 -> 413

He skips where he used to invent. Legality never required coverage: a top-level part may start anywhere after the previous one ends. When the bind refuses the word he wanted, the best legal move is often a later word — arm F seed 2 flad-039-outer4, "i ought to know four things: the chief export of Fenennddia, the currency of Halirvik, ..." comes back social[i ought to know] ask[halirvik, ask[the currency of Ithithnia] ..., a carrier and two members gone. The gold never leaves five request words uncovered; 499 of the 2,026 bound legal ladder lines do, against 35 of 413 free. Nearly all of the case species is this skip landing on a name: 200 of its 205 lines open a part on a capitalised request word written lowercase, which the one-way fold (D-A1.14) admits, and 159 drop five words or more. On outer rows this is why the bind costs a little: where the free line invented the outer carrier ("have a spare minute", "it's one", "daft") and still counted four, the bound line skipped past it.

A refused word leaves empty labels behind. A label is legal at any word edge up to three in a row (MAX_OPEN_RUN, the corpus's own maximum), so when the next word is refused the open is the best legal token: find[find[note[four in one go, ..., social[chat[social[and]]]. Lines with three labels open in a row go from 2-9 to 118-172 of 288 on nested ladder rows; one-member groups from 92 to 2,616 there. They are legal and wrong.

He still flattens (finding 143): with every free line closed so it can be read, 819 of 1,152 nested ladder items have no nested group of two or more; bound, 561. A third fewer, and still most.

The count is his. The arity hand probe (four phrasings and a control at arities 2-15, 160 cells an arm across the checkpoints): every member copied once in 53 cells free and 114 bound, the right width with every member once (clean) in 36 and 75, unbalanced 77 of 200 rows and 0. But no cell at twelve is clean in either arm, and where the bind made the copy perfect the close is still his: arm F seed 2 at fifteen writes all fifteen members once, in all four phrasings, and closes the group before the last ("... and the river through Corithburg" as a sibling) — width 14; G2 seed 1 at six closes after four.

The depth wall falls on a marked chain

This is the half of the expectation that failed. probe_hand_depth.py --marked puts a carrier at every level ("the two, A and the pair, B and ..."); its column is carrier levels reproduced in order, ask[<carrier>, ask[<member>]. On the 72 items with four or more carriers (18 a checkpoint, three a depth at 6-15), free decoding never runs past three — the wall this probe found on 2026-09-25 — and bound:

items with 4+ carriers free bound run of 4+ from the top 0/72 20/72 4+ found in order 0/72 45/72 the whole chain 0/72 11/72 longest run 3 13 of 13 (G2 seed 1, depth 15)

Read by hand on G2 seed 1's first depth-15 item: free rewrites the first carrier ("upper,"), fuses the next two, then jumps to the twelfth and loops on it — "the two things, the river through Elmeholm" three times; bound opens all thirteen in order, each over its member. That is finding 145's diagnosis from the other side: the wall was the copy pointer jumping and looping at a carrier, not the depth, and the bind forbids both inside a part. What it does not buy is the tree: the exact shape is right on no item free and on at most 2 of 3 a depth bound (at depth 4-5 only), because the bound lines carry the empty wrappers (deepest bracket 30 where the gold has 15) and the outer core still splits off (the level-1 boundary, finding 138). Three items a depth, one greedy draw: a hand probe, not a measurement.

The token limit (220, the probes' default; every gold fits, the longest at 219 tokens). And the bind cannot write a fifth of w_d5's golds: 19 of its 96 open four labels in a row, which MAX_OPEN_RUN refuses (the same 19 with or without the repair; none at a cap of nine). The held-out depth-5 row is capped by the bind's own rule.

What it licenses

The split the rung was for, measured. The runtime can own the form — every invented span, repeat, splice and swapped number word — and, by owning form, the copy pointer inside a part, which is what carried the marked chain past the depth wall and repaired about one nested group in twenty. The weights keep where a group closes, whether a nested group opens, which label, and coverage; under the bind the last one changes species, from an invented span to a skip, and the skip is legal. A bind armed in H3 needs three things this read names: the backtracking decoder (SPEC-LANGUAGE §9 says so already), the _room_ahead refusal, and something for coverage — a top-level part that starts at the request's next word or after a connective (the gold never skips five) — which is not built and would need its own refuses-nothing-legal proof against the generators' connectives. The empty wrappers are a decoder question, not a grammar one: the stacks are legal and trained, and greedy reaches them only because the word he wanted was refused.

What it does not say. That the bind makes him better at nesting: the right-group gain is about 50 of 1,152 and mostly a carrier he was made to copy. That depth is solved: one hand probe on a chain marked at every level, three items a depth, one greedy draw each. Or anything about the copy-pointer hypothesis beyond this: holding the pointer inside a part fixed the copy at arity, the chain at depth and some nested groups together, and did not fix the close. Nano, the synthetic world, one greedy decode a line.
