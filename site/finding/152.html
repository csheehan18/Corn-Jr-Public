What each layer of the tag language costs in tokens

The result, as the finding states it

What each layer of the language costs: on v0.5's own corpus the four render-pass layers add 21 tokens to a 96-token turn and 4.6% to the mix, the parse line is 5.5 of his 30.7 decode steps, and the conversational slice the spec counts as turn-shaped has no turn in it

What it cost

CPU only. scripts/price_layers.py runs in ~22 s; building it, the

survey's --nested mode, two hand-read rounds and this write-up took about 30 min (16:05-16:35 on 2026-10-07). No GPU.

ROADMAP A4, PLAN-TAG-LANGUAGE: the cost of each layer: tokens a turn, share of the mix, decode steps, against v0.5's measured turn — one table, a row per layer. Read for D7 (2026-11-26), which takes which layers ride. A price, not a capability read: nothing here says a layer works, only what it charges.

How it was read

Every layer priced on what renders it, never on a hand example where a rendering exists. The four layers with a render pass — parts: (corn/parseline.py), state: (corn/stateline.py), the day marker and name: — were applied to twelve evenly spaced 200 KB windows of every slice of mix_v4_5b, v0.5's own corpus, and priced as the token difference through bpe8k, document by document. The rung-world layers (traces, templates) are priced per occurrence on their world's file, the views on G1's own card, and the three with no rendering (move: values, who:) on SPEC-LANGUAGE's own example, each saying so. Decode steps are the tokens of the lines SPEC §0 makes his; a runtime line costs prefill and no step.

v0.5's measured turn, the same sample with nothing added and weighted by the mix: 96.3 context tokens, 30.7 of them his (call and say lines). Only 20.73% of the mix is turn-shaped — chat, temporal, tools, toolspace, daylog, extract, turn2, paired, compound.

What the table says

1. The render-pass layers are cheap per turn and not cheap in the mix. All four together add 21.2 tokens to a 96-token turn (22%) and 4.58% of 5B — as much as the whole toolspace slice. The cost lands on 20.7% of the mix, so each layer's share is about a quarter of its per-turn price.

2. The day marker is the dearest header, and it is the tokenizer. <|day|>2026-01-19 Monday is 13 tokens on bpe8k, ten of them the date's digits one at a time. D-A1.2 puts it on every transcript, so on the slices that lack it (tools, toolspace, extract, paired) it is the largest single addition — 13 tokens against parts:'s 5 — and 1.18% of the mix. A shorter form is a format question for the owner, not taken here.

3. His decode cost is the parse line and nothing else that rides everywhere. parts: is 5.5 of his 30.7 steps a turn (18%); 2 of them are the key's grammar-forced tokens (forced_token_survey.py --parse --nested, 0 of 400 lines refused on turn2_v1_parse.txt, 38% of the line's tokens forced, all in the prefix — the label is a real choice of nine; on compound_v2_parse.txt 0 of 400 refused and 3.7 a line forced, 22.6%, the copied spans finishing themselves, a slice at 0.14% of the mix). A runtime that writes the key, as G1 writes the open carrier, leaves him 3.5. state: is 4.4 more if he writes it and none if the runtime does — SPEC §4 left that to H3 and A5's second seed reads whether his writing it adds anything.

4. Traces and templates cost per occurrence, and the template header is the biggest line in the language. A filter turn's goal: and pick: are 26.7 of his steps, 87% of an average turn's whole decode; the runtime's check: adds 19.8 to the context. Three or four template header lines are 71.2 context tokens — three quarters of a v0.5 turn — before he writes his 9.8 for use:. At the 512 that ships, the header lines of two templated turns are 142 tokens, 28% of the block; at 2048 (D2's direction), 7%.

5. Views are a serving cost, three times a one-shot line. On G1's arm S seed 1 card (102 requests, mean utterance 104 tokens) a windowed request takes 3.91 steps, each showing 42.3 tokens, and his lines total at most 433 tokens against the same requests' one-shot line of 146 — 3.0x, an upper bound because each step's line holds the carrier the runtime writes in. Each step's prefill also re-reads the tools: header, which is not counted. Views add no syntax and no corpus line (SPEC D-A1.7), so they have no share.

6. The conversational slice has no turn in it. SPEC-LANGUAGE §0 counts the conversational slice (14.09%, the second largest in the mix) as turn-shaped and has it carry parts: and state:. On disk conversational.txt is unmarked dialogue — 0 <|user|> and 0 <|say|> lines in its first 5 MB — so no render pass that writes under a <|user|> line touches it, and the 20.73% above is the true coverage, not 34.8%. Marked as turns, every line a turn, parts: chat and state: alone would add 11.0 tokens to a 22.8-token line, 6.80% of the mix before the markers themselves — more than every other layer in the table together. Whether that slice becomes turn-shaped is a corpus decision for D7 and C6, not a pricing one; until it is taken, SPEC §0's coverage line overstates what the render pass reaches.

What was fixed on the way

scripts/forced_token_survey.py --parse walked the retired line-per-level grammar and refused 400 of 400 1.0 lines on turn2_v1_parse.txt, so the forced share it printed there (56.2%) was the share of a refused prefix. It now takes --nested: the 1.0 one-line tree and nine labels through ParseConstraint(line="nested"), the bind SPEC §9 specifies. The flag is additive; the per-level mode is unchanged for the decomposition corpora it was written for.

Bounds

  • Sampled, not counted: twelve windows a slice, 63,014-67,281 lines a layer. Per-document encoding forgoes merges at document boundaries (~1%, mix_v4_5b's own note).
  • move: is priced on SPEC §4's seven values; its coverage is a D-A1.11 question (dialogue with gold only) and has no rendering to count.
  • who: is the spec's example line, uncapped; PLAN-PEOPLE §6 caps it.
  • The forced column is measured for the parse line only. Every other key line's bind is unsurveyed and reads --.
  • Views are read off one checkpoint's card on G1's own items, which are long by design (arity up to thirty), not a v0.5 turn.
