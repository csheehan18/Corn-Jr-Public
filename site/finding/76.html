The corpus mix · 28 August 2026

A single-request control removes the compound requests' copying cost

The result, as the finding states it

The single-request control returns the copy rows on both vehicles, and what is left over is the control's own failure species

What it cost

0 s of new scoring on record; it reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (nano20m_v3). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

Four nano arms trained 2026-08-28 from configs/mix_v3_compound_v2.py and configs/mix_compound_v2_ft.py — finding 74's own design re-run on data/raw/compound_v2.txt, two seeds a vehicle, same payload weight and same schedule as the v1 pair to the token. Controls are the four already-scored nano20m_v3 seeds and the seven v1 compound cards; nothing retrained and nothing was re-scored. All four arms are eager, like their controls — the signed compile=True is scoped to the H200 family.

Pre-registered in docs/PLAN-MIX-V4-BUILD.md Phase 2 and in configs/mix_v3_compound_v2.py's docstring, both written before a byte of the file existed. docs/PLAN-5B-SUPPLY.md §7a's compound entry ordered the rebuild; the share it prices is the owner's and is not decided here.

What was wrong, and what the file changed

Finding 74: compound.pipe installed completely on either vehicle and took tool.copy to 0–1/120 and tool.copy_wide to 0/300 on the fine-tune arm, with the copied span exact every time and the why bucket malformed on all 420 items. Copy discipline had not broken; the decision to stop had. All 43,626 v1 transcripts put a second call after the first observation and none ended a turn after one, so the model learned the rule the corpus stated.

compound_v2.txt is the same three classes with a matched single-request control written atomically beside each one: one carrier drawn once and rendered twice, around a compound core and around the first of its requests alone. 50/50 by construction, identical carrier distributions on the two halves, identical opening-tool distributions — all asserted off the bytes by scripts/check_turn2.py.

This is the pre-registered "fixed" outcome, on both of its clauses and on both vehicles. tool.copy returns inside the ~10-item wobble of the control's 118–120 on all four arms. tool.copy_wide reads 294–298 on three arms — two of them above the control's 281–296 — and 278 on the fourth, three items below a band whose own four-seed spread is fifteen. And compound.pipe holds at or above 40/48 on every arm, so the class the file exists to teach is not traded for the rows it was breaking.

The fine-tune arm is the one to look at, because it is where the bill was largest: 0 and 1 of 120 become 115 and 120; 0 and 0 of 300 become 298 and 294.

The failure species is the finding, and it is not the totals

Totals can return for the wrong reason. These do not:

row arm malformed the rest tool.copy v1 FT ×2 120, 116 wrong_answer 3 v2 FT ×2 5, 0 — tool.copy v1 MIX ×3 70, 16, 58 wrong_answer 14, 0, 13 v2 MIX ×2 0, 1 wrong_answer 9, 0 tool.copy_wide v1 FT ×2 300, 300 — v2 FT ×2 0, 0 wrong_answer 2 / trained_answer 3 tool.copy_wide v1 MIX ×3 110, 4, 37 — v2 MIX ×2 0, 1 trained_answer 3, 19

malformed is the never-stop signature and it is gone: 420 of 420 on the v1 fine-tune arm, 5 of 840 on the v2 one. What is left on v2 is wrong_answer and trained_answer — which are the control's own failure species, at the control's own rates (the four control seeds read wrong_answer 0–16 and trained_answer 1–4 on copy_wide). So the arm is not passing by writing a different kind of wrong thing; it is failing the way an untreated checkpoint fails, on the few items it fails at all.

That is the mechanism confirmed rather than the outcome observed. Finding 74 inferred the cause from a corpus property and a retained generation; this predicts what removing the property does, and it does that.

What it costs, named rather than buried

The control class occasionally stops one call early, which is the behaviour it teaches arriving where it was not wanted. said_instead on compound.items goes 1–5 on the v1 arms to 9–21 on the v2 arms, and appears on compound.pipe for the first time (0 on every v1 arm; 4, 0, 2, 0 on v2). compound.pipe still clears the registered 40/48 on all four arms, so this is a direction rather than a failure — but it is the honest other side of the trade and it is the first thing to re-read if the H200 run is weak on composition.

compound.items remains characterisation and is not part of the read: it did not install in v1 (2–9/48) and it does not install in v2 (MIX 4 and 1, FT 11 and 7). The 11 is the highest any arm has read and it is one item outside v1's range on a 48-item row; nothing there is a separation. Finding 66's first-taker shape still dominates the bucket — wrong_text 16–22 across every v2 arm — which is the same diagnosis v1 got and a different problem from this one.

refusal.say on the MIX vehicle is a pre-existing property of this family, not something v2 added. 249 and 259 sit against v1 MIX's 251, 266, 257, and the why is reworded in every case (21 and 11 against v1's 19, 4, 13). The compound MIX arm rewords refusals; it did so at v1 and it does so at v2, and the two ranges overlap. The fine-tune vehicle shows none of it (268 and 266, reworded 2 and 4, inside the control band).

temporal.copy 339/353 on the v2 fine-tune's first seed is the one genuinely new watch, and it is reported as unresolved. That row reads 353/353 on all four controls, all three v1 MIX seeds and one of the two v1 fine-tunes; the other v1 fine-tune read 349 with wrong_tool 4. Here seed 1 reads 339 with wrong_tool 14 and seed 2 reads 352 with wrong_tool 1. So the defect is not new — configs/mix_v3_compound.py predicted it in as many words, because this family trains resolve_date in a new envelope and made temporal.copy a load-bearing watch rather than a conservative one — but 14 items is the largest it has been, and the two seeds disagree by 13 on a row whose control spread is zero. The direction is a real one and the size is not readable at two seeds, which is the same sentence finding 74 had to write about tool.copy and is no more satisfying here.

What this licenses, and who decides it

Against the pre-registered table this is fixed: the file is the freeze candidate, and the share decision goes to the owner with these numbers. This finding does not pick one, configs/mix_v4_5b.py builds cleanly with the line at zero, and §7a's compound entry stays ORDERED until somebody signs it.

What the numbers say for that decision, stated plainly and without a recommendation:

  • The mechanism finding 74 identified was the whole of the cost, and removing it removes the cost on both vehicles.
  • Class I is bought at 44–48 of 48 against a control of 0.
  • Class L is not bought, on either file, and is not evidence either way.
  • The price is a visible tendency to stop a call early, and one unexplained 14-item loss on temporal.copy at one seed of one arm.

Limits

Two seeds per arm, so directions rather than sizes (rule 6). And these are nano readings: §3 item 4's caveat applies to the whole freeze, and the sizes here transfer to the H200 run by argument rather than by measurement.

Both fine-tune vehicles were scored at ckpt.pt and last.pt, and the two seeds differ in whether that was worth doing. Seed 1 selected best-val at 551, its final iteration, so its two files are the same weights and their identical cards say nothing. Seed 2 selected at 490 of 551, so its two cards are genuinely different weights — and every registered cell reproduces: tool.copy 120 both ways, compound.pipe 48 both ways, temporal.copy 352 both ways. What moves is copy_wide 294 → 296, items_first 6 → 7, refusal.say 266 → 265 and tool.selection 128 → 130, all inside a single run's wobble and none of them on a row this finding reads. So selection is not flattering the arm, which is the sixth finding's rule answered by measurement rather than by design, and the tables above quote ckpt.pt throughout.
