A bigger phrasing test; its no-transfer reading was later withdrawn

The result, as the finding states it

The bucket works at n=398, and the transfer is still zero

What it cost

4.4 min of scoring on the 1 card first cited here (nano100m_v1). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

The thirty-first finding built evals_wideonly and could not read it: twelve carriers on one left edge, an untreated range of 8–12 touching its own ceiling, and a transfer question that no number of seeds could answer. The arm rebuilds that bucket at 398 of 400 items across all fourteen edges, and it has now been scored on all ten checkpoints. No training was involved; every one of them was already on disk.

The negative below is withdrawn — see "Added later" at the end of this finding. The positive control stands and strengthens. The transfer claim was read off pass/fail totals on an arm that had no attribution files at 29.6M, and the totals cancel the effect twelve edges actually have.

The reading was pre-registered as three-way, and it comes out that way:

The control has to be read first, and it separates by 44. That was the precondition the thirty-first finding stated before the result rather than after: a flat transfer number on an insensitive arm means nothing, and this is the failure mode the n=12 bucket had already had once. scripts/effect.py puts the treated group above the baseline by 44 and above the mid group by 43. The arm can see an effect nearly three times its own 17-point seed spread, so whatever it says about mid, it is not saying it for want of sensitivity.

And mid does not move. 336–344 against the baseline's 326–343 is an overlap, on items whose left edges mid was trained on six more of — none of them these. The mid arm learned twelve edges completely, as the thirty-first finding showed on its own bucket, and carried nothing to the other fourteen. Two doses, two directions, same answer: the twenty-ninth finding's "buys exactly what it contains and transfers nothing" now holds where it was designed to be visible instead of where it happened to be.

The failure mode does not change, only its count

Every failure on this arm is wrong_expression — right tool, well-formed JSON, wrong words — as it was in the twenty-eighth finding. It is 67 / 71 / 57 / 74 on the baseline seeds, 63 / 55 on mid, and 5 / 12 / 8 / 13 on the treated arm; the only non-wrong_expression failures anywhere are two wrong_tool and four unparseable across ten checkpoints. So the treatment does not change what going wrong looks like. It removes instances of one thing, on exactly the edges it covers, which is what enlarging a memorised set predicts and what a repaired segmenter does not.

The floor does not order with the difficulty of the arm, which is worth recording because the obvious guess is that it should. Wide-only items score lower than paraphrase items (326–343 against 324–348 is nearly the same, on carriers chosen to be harder) while spreading less, 17 against 24, and held-out spreads 45 on items no harder than these. A floor is a property of the arm, and the thirtieth finding's point survives the one new arm that could have contradicted it.

The rows the arm does not rewrite behave: tool.copy and schema.conform are identical to the frozen set on every seed, which is floors.py's own self-consistency check passing, and temporal.copy reads 353/353 on nine of ten checkpoints — the tenth being mix_v1_mid's first seed at 352, the same checkpoint that read 352 under paraphrase.

What this does not settle

  • Mid is two seeds against four, and it is the side carrying the negative. The overlap is wide enough that more seeds are unlikely to change it — mid's whole range sits inside the baseline's — but two points are a range and not a floor, and this is the third finding in a row to say so about the same two checkpoints.
  • It says nothing about why. That the boundary is memorised as a set is now measured three ways; no instrument here distinguishes a model that cannot segment from one that has never had a reason to.
  • Capacity is untested. Every checkpoint above is 29.6M. Whether a larger model transfers across left edges is configs/nano100m_v1.py's pre-registered question, and the still uncovered bucket at 119–139 is the number to read it on.

Added later — the negative above is withdrawn, and the row is why

scripts/paraphrase_attribute.py had never been run on this arm at 29.6M. Ten checkpoints were scored on it and none of them had an attribution file, so "mid does not move" was read off pass/fail totals and nothing else. Running the fifteen missing passes and splitting wrong_expression by what the backend does with the emitted span changes the reading:

checkpointstotalunresolvablewrong date
mix_v1 ×4326–34310–2136–52
mix_v1_mid ×2336–34425–2629–30
mix_v1_wide ×4387–3951–23–8

resolve_date returns None for an expression it does not recognise, so an emitted span splits three ways: it resolves to the gold date, it resolves to a different date, or it does not resolve and the tool comes back with a clarify. The first two are a right and a wrong answer; the third is Corn-Jr asking. The row adds all three together, and VISION §9's most important behaviour is the difference between the last two.

Mid separates from the baseline on wrong dates — 29–30 against 36–52 — where the totals overlap. Its unresolvable count rises by about as much as its wrong-date count falls, so twelve left edges convert roughly ten confident errors into roughly ten refusals and the total scores that as a wash. It is not a wash. The positive control is unaffected and gets stronger: the treated arm separates by 44 on totals and by 28 on wrong dates, on a bucket where it reads 3–8 against the baseline's 36–52.

So the finding's positive half stands and its negative half should not be quoted: twelve edges transfer something, and the instrument that said otherwise cannot see the thing they transfer. What it does not become is a claim in the other direction — mid is still two seeds, a spread of 1 across two points is not a floor, and more mid seeds are the only thing that settles it. evals_heldout was re-read the same way and overlaps on every column with spreads of 34–45, which is that arm being unreadable rather than flat.
