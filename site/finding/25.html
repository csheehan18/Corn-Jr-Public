Dialogue data buys conversational vocabulary, not conversational style

The result, as the finding states it

The slice buys conversation and does not buy register

register.conv is the sixth addable row and the first that asks the question §4 buys the conversational slice for. Nothing in evals/ measured register: the nats/byte rows say a corpus was learned, and variety.distinct3 says a model does not repeat itself, which a fluent storybook also does not.

No threshold anywhere, which is the point after the twenty-third finding. Five coordinates are measured identically on a continuation and on windows of the frozen eval texts, and a reply is scored by which of three measured registers it is nearest to. The scale is the range the registers themselves occupy on each feature, so nothing here is a constant anybody chose.

Prompted with dialogue, the slice moves the row 23% → 76% — 53 points against a floor of 2.0. That is the largest behavioural effect of any share change the project has measured, on a row with the tightest floor it has. The conversational slice does what it was bought to do, and variety.distinct3 could not see it: the same comparison moves that row 0.73 points against a floor of 1.85.

And prompted with a story, nothing moves at all

Every checkpoint scores 0–2% on story openings, including the one with 20% dialogue. §5.0's complaint is that he answers a story opening in storybook register whether narrative is 47% of the corpus or 10%; with a fifth of the budget replaced by conversation, he still does. The slice bought conversation and did not buy a register the prompt did not already choose.

Whether that is a failure is a real question and this row does not settle it. A base language model continuing a story opening as a story is doing its job — prompt-following is not a defect, and 0% on that half may be correct behaviour rather than a regression. What it establishes either way is that the conversational slice does not change register when the prompt has already chosen one, which is worth knowing before anyone prices the share on register grounds. It is also why the two halves are reported separately: pooled, this row would read 38% and hide that one half tripled while the other did not move.

~~He sounds conversational and is not shaped like conversation~~ — withdrawn

This section reported a median turn of 35 words against a conversational corpus that breaks at 13, and concluded the slice bought the vocabulary of conversation and not its shape. The 35 was a pooled statistic and the conclusion was wrong — see the twenty-sixth finding, which was built to explain it and disproved it instead.

median_turn was pooled across both prompt sources. The story half produces unbroken prose that reaches the 40-word cap, so a median over 100 of those and 100 dialogue replies lands at the seam between two distributions and describes neither. Every checkpoint already produces conversational-length turns on dialogue prompts. conv20 breaks at 14 words against a corpus that breaks at 13. There was never a shape problem, and the 23% → 76% the slice buys is carried by the person and question coordinates because turn length was near-correct on both sides of the comparison all along.

The 80-word re-score in the commit before this one read the same pooled number and inherited the same error; its one durable result is that the conversational anchor is 13.5 at an 80-word window, so the reference side was never censored.

What the mistake cost is worth naming: this row reports its score by source precisely because pooling two prompt types hides the answer, and then its own diagnostic pooled them.

What it does not say

Neither half is unprompted, so §11's standing request — what register does he fall into when nothing has chosen one — is still unanswered, and this row inherits that gap rather than closing it. The anchors are three registers because those are the three held-out texts that exist; temporal has one too and was left out because a transcript slice is a format rather than a register. And the row is nearest-anchor, not quality: a model emitting fluent nonsense in short second-person turns would score well here and badly on every nats/byte row, which is the same caution variety.distinct3 carries one row over.
