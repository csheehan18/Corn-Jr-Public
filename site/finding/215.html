The reply judge · 24 October 2026

Looking a few words ahead with the judge lifts his replies

The result, as the finding states it

Choosing each reply's opening by a beam five branches wide and five tokens deep, scored by his own mean log-probability plus the reply judge's verdict on the newest word, lifts the plain 5M's fitting replies to 58 and 61 of 148 against greedy's 53 and 46 by a blind hand read — +5 and +15, short of the pre-registered +7 at both seeds at seed 1, so the registered row is (b), level — and every paired count points one way: 38 replies pass only under the look-ahead and 18 only under greedy (sign test p = 0.010), and against the same beam with no judge 32 to 17 (p = 0.044), so the gain is the judge's and not the search's (the beam alone: 53 and 51, 27 to 22). A judge continued on short-reply OpenAssistant and EmpatheticDialogues reads the everyday messages better (within-category swap pairs right 0.78 and 0.81 at the end of the reply against the SODA judge's 0.73 and 0.75, under his own 0.91, the predicted fail) and steers worse: 51 and 51 with the look-ahead, 51 and 50 one word at a time. It learned EmpatheticDialogues' habit of answering with one's own feelings

What it cost

train_judge.py --pairs/--init, the look-ahead in corn/judge.py and its guard (check_judge.py 6), the probe's decoders, a settings check and the pre-registration 15:06-15:28 on 2026-10-24 (0a1c2c1); the chain 15:29-15:33, 4.3 min (two register fits 62 s each, contended at 45%, a bound); the blind hand read of 1,480 replies ~15 min. ETA ~1.5 h, actual ~50 min.

The question (owner, 2026-10-24: "Do the probe and also let's do the look ahead idea as well"). Finding 214 named two causes for a level judge: it was fitted on SODA turns and reads the owner's everyday messages weakly, and one word is too early — the word that decides a reply ("That's great") comes second or third, and the judge reads better the more it has seen. One arm each (docs/PLAN-REPLY-JUDGE.md Phase 1b).

Read by hand

What the look-ahead fixes (seed 2's 22, seed 1's 16): the stock "What? Why?" and wrong-role openings give way to replies built from the message — "Why do you want to repaint the bedroom?", "What kind of garden are you looking for?", "Where did you find twenty dollars?", "What made you decide to start?" to a pottery class, "Why not?" to "Maybe." — and the mood turns where greedy had it backwards: "Oh my gosh, that's so sad. I'm sorry to hear that. What happened?" to a numb face (greedy: "Oh, that's great! I'm so glad you're here"), "I'm sorry to hear that. What happened?" to a missed bus (greedy: "That's great, honey!"), "Yeah, it was pretty bad" to the traffic. Five words is far enough to see "great" coming after "That's" and to drop it.

What it breaks (18): a bad-news message it reads as good ("Oh, wow! That's awesome! What's wrong with it?" to a cracked screen, "Oh, wow! That's great! How did you get it?" to a cancelled flight — the judge's weak mood read, finding 214), and the beam's own pull toward the generic: "I don't know. I just feel like I'm not doing anything right" rises to the commonest opening, and variety falls (86 to 80, 82 to 73 distinct openings). The beam with no judge shows that pull alone and nothing else (53 and 51).

Why the register judge steers worse. Where the SODA judge's look-ahead passes and the register judge's fails (15 at seed 2), the register judge chose "I don't know. I just feel like I'm not good enough" (three times), "I'm sorry, I didn't mean to hurt you", "Oh no, my grandma is in the hospital" — the listener speaking about themself. EmpatheticDialogues' replies often share the replier's own feeling, so a judge fitted on them learns that a first-person disclosure fits; it recognises the hand-written swaps better and prefers the wrong kind of reply when it steers. Its better J2 is pair recognition; steering asks it to rank the model's own candidates, which J2 does not read.

The cause of the seed-1 miss

The 18 losses are the two above: the judge's mood read on bad news (finding 214's cause 1, unfixed by the register data) and the beam's pull to the generic opening. Neither is a fault of the build (check_judge.py 6 holds the beam against an exhaustive by-hand best and the mean against the sum).

What it licenses

  • The owner's look-ahead is the first decoding change that moves the 5M's fitting replies in one direction at both seeds — by a margin a paired sign test puts at p = 0.010, under a pre-registered per-seed gate it missed at one seed by 2. Search alone does nothing; the judge one word at a time does nothing; together they help. It is a direction at two seeds, not a capability.
  • The register judge is dropped: better at pairs, worse at steering, and not under a licence the weights can carry.
  • The probe the miss names: the same look-ahead, settings unchanged, on the 20M pair (nano20m_v3_webblend, _seed2), the next size with two seeds, against the same gate — and then the shipped 260M (Phase 2), where greedy's misses are stock lines and his own recognition is 99%. A judge trained to read mood on bad news (cross pairs whose messages differ in mood and match in topic) is the fix for the losses, after the replication.

What it does not settle. Other beam widths, depths or lam; the beam with sampling for variety; any size above 5M; the app's full prompt.
