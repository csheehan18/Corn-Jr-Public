Tool calls and copying · 17 August 2026

The tool-call format must be in pretraining, and costs about 3%

The result, as the finding states it

The format has to be in pretraining, and it costs about 3%

What it cost

69 s of scoring on the 1 card first cited here (nano20m_pilot); it also reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (nano20m). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

DATA.md §4 makes a bet most small-model recipes don't: put the tool-call format in pretraining at 15% of the budget, rather than fine-tuning it in afterward. The argument is that at 250M there isn't capacity for a light fine-tune to install a format the base model has never encountered. nano20m_pilot is that bet at nano scale — the same 25.6M shape and the same 12,000 iterations as nano20m, differing in one field, data_dir. It reads data/mix_pilot (85% TinyStories, 15% generated temporal) instead of TinyStories alone.

28.7 minutes, best val 0.3881 at iter 11,500. Against the frozen ruler:

Every behaviour row went from zero to approximately one, and temporal.copy is the one that matters most. It scores the turn after the tool answers, and a date that is not the tool's fails even when it is correct — so 100% says he is carrying the tool's answer rather than doing date arithmetic in the weights, which is the single behaviour VISION §5.2 is built around. refusal.no_guess reading 47 items rather than -- of 0 is the gated denominator working: nothing the old checkpoint emitted was in-format enough to score.

The narrative row is the price tag, and it is the useful number. 0.3974 → 0.4117 is what spending 15% of a fixed budget on temporal costs the other 85% — the first measured point on DATA.md §1's claim that quality per token is the only lever. The true cost is smaller than the 3.6% it looks like, because nano20m's 0.3974 was mildly flattered: 16 of 16 narrative probes were in its val split, so it was selected on the text it was then scored on. mix_pilot reserves those bytes, and the pilot's number is clean on both splits. Format exposure for under 3.6% of general fluency is cheap for what it buys.

A val figure would have told you the opposite, and this is the clearest example yet of why evals/ exists. The pilot's blended val is 0.3881 against nano20m's 0.4265 — apparently a 9% improvement, entirely an artefact. Per-source val at the same checkpoint reads narrative 0.4487, temporal 0.0946: the temporal slice is templated and trivially predictable, so mixing 15% of it into the validation set drags the blend down while general fluency regressed. Two runs' val figures are not on one scale, the blended number moved the wrong way, and only the fixed ruler caught it.

What this does not show, in descending order of how much it should temper the result:

  • ~~The bet is half-tested.~~ The control arms have since run — the sixth finding below. The short version: "fine-tuning it wouldn't" is false for three of the four behaviour rows and true for the one that matters.
  • 99.8% is format compliance, not generalization. The eval items are drawn from the same 334 expressions the training slice was generated from. Anchor dates are structurally held out, so this is not memorized answers — but it says nothing about a phrasing the generator has never emitted, and real speech is full of those. The surface-variety floor in check_temporal.py is the instrument for that; the eval set cannot be. Tested at last — the twenty-eighth finding, and it was right. On carriers no generator emits the row falls 398 → 348 of 400 — against the thirtieth finding's paraphrase-arm floor of 24 rather than the frozen 2 this originally quoted, still 2.1x — every failure a wrong_expression. What it was wrong about is scope: the same arm leaves temporal.copy at 353/353, so the caveat applies to recognising the request and not to deferring to the tool.
  • ~~tool.validity and tool.selection are still --.~~ Both are live as of the tenth finding, and on a checkpoint that has seen one tool they read 61.9% and 0.0%. The caveat underneath survives unchanged: what has been demonstrated is one tool, generated by one program, and no model has yet been trained on the other four.

The decontamination flag no longer cries wolf

The temporal row of the probe search read CONTAMINATED on a corpus that was provably clean: 6 of 16 probes turned up in train.bin, and they were exactly the windows containing no date. Every probe carrying a held-out 2029- date was absent. The search was finding shared format — the <|call|> envelope and the templated utterances the slice exists to teach — which no amount of n-gram decontamination could remove without destroying what the row measures.

That was going to read contaminated by design on every future mix, and a status field that fails on every run is one everybody learns to ignore. So probes now come in two populations: a bearing probe is cut from a window carrying a date in the eval text's own anchor year or later, which the generator stops a full year short of and cannot reach; a format probe is a uniform window. Only bearing probes set the status. Format hits are reported as context and can never fail a run. Both mix_pilot slices now read clean.

Bearing probes are selected rather than filtered, so all 16 carry a date instead of the 9 that happened to — the fix roughly doubles the test's power rather than just quieting it. And a text that yields no bearing probe reads indeterminate, never clean, because a check that cannot fail is the thing this harness was written to prevent. check_evals.py carries the false positive as a regression test alongside the true positive it must not cost.

HARNESS_VERSION stays at 1: no scored quantity changed meaning, and every row above came back byte-identical after the fix.
