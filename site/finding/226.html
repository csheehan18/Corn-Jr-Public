Understanding at small scale · 13 November 2026

Kept presses last, but break the text and churn the words

The result, as the finding states it

150 of finding 225's pairs pressed one after another and kept — the held step in the pair's own word-table rows, two nats each — stay pressed (0.98 and 0.97 of what each press took off its own sentence is still off at the end; 0.90 and 0.91 for the first 50), and the word comes a median 0.33 and 0.42 nats down at the pair's other meeting. But against a swapped meeting whose stand-in word the hold never saw, the pair is 0.11 and 0.20 nats, a direction and half of finding 225's read; the held-out text costs 0.102 and 0.057 nats a token more, breaking, and adding up press by press; and the medians sit on churn — at the other meeting the word is a quarter of a nat easier on 53% and 54% of pressed pairs and that much harder on 28% and 27%, down to 12 nats harder, and of the pairs never pressed 36% and 39% are a quarter of a nat harder. No compounding. At half the strength the damage is seven times less (0.014 and 0.011) for about half the pair (0.07 and 0.11); in every weight the text loses 0.20 nats a token and the never-pressed pairs 0.38 and 0.89; and the plain press kept is harmless (0.005 and 0.004) and writes 150 words, not pairs.

What it cost

probe and pre-registration committed at 12:34 on 2026-11-13 (8bd4d57; a 30-pair smoke read, 125.1 s, seen before the commit and after the prediction was written); the read 213.2 s of card for four passes of 150 presses at two checkpoints; the hand read's printout 55.8 s. ETA ~8 min of card; 3.6.

The question (owner, 2026-11-13: "let's keep going", and while it ran, "is there any chance we get a compounding result in our favor?"). Finding 225 wrote some of a pair with one held press, measured and put back. A way of teaching is many presses, kept. The pre-registration is scripts/probe_kept_press.py's docstring: three ways it could end cheaply — later presses overwrite earlier ones, the damage piles up, or the pair gap was opened from the wrong side.

Why (causes named from the items; one read each still owed)

  • The private place is not private. The word table has 8,192 rows of pieces for 393,826 words. relied, weighed and dame are spelled from pieces other words use, so a press on one word's rows moves every word that shares a piece — which is how a pair nobody pressed loses four nats. One press cannot show this; 150 do.
  • The held step is a long step in a thin direction. Once the swapped sentence is taken out, 3% to 4% of the gradient is left in those rows, so taking two nats off means a step about thirty times longer than the plain press's. The plain press, kept, costs the text twenty times less.
  • Damage grows faster than strength. Half the nats cost a seventh of the damage (0.014 and 0.011 against 0.102 and 0.057) for about half the pair. Whatever writes pairs this way is many small steps, not few hard ones.

What it licenses

By the pre-registration this sits between two rows: P is a direction and not a hold, and D breaks. Hard held presses, kept, are not a way of teaching as they stand: they write a tenth to a fifth of a nat of pair into a model that pays 2% to 4% of its text for it. What survives from findings 224 and 225 is the mechanism, not the delivery — what a press writes is set by what it is compared with — and three things this read adds:

  • the comparison has to be made gently and beside ordinary reading, which is what repairs the text: a paired term in the loss at a small weight, a training arm and not a press;
  • a private place has to be private: one row a word, which the piece table is not (the unit question IDEAS-UNDERSTANDING.md §7 names);
  • and the swapped word has to vary, or half of what is learned is the stand-in.

What it does not settle. Whether the same comparison as a small term in training, with the stand-in drawn afresh each time, writes pairs without the bill — the precedent is against it (two added lessons cost language at 5M, findings 206 and 217) and for it (neither was aimed at a difference). One order of pairs; 150 of them; a 20M.
