The corpus mix · 17 August 2026

The first data mix with every share measured, not guessed

The result, as the finding states it

The first corpus nobody inherited, and a row that prices the mix rather than its slice

What it cost

0 s of new scoring on record; it reads 2 cards scored for an earlier finding (nano20m_narrative10, nano20m_struct). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

Every corpus before this one carried a number it had not chosen. mix_pilot guessed narrative and temporal; the seven after it moved one slice each and left the rest where the pilot put them. mix_v1 starts from §11's prices instead — web 40%, conversational 20%, structured 10%, tools 0.6228%, all four at their measured values simultaneously for the first time — and lets narrative and temporal take the remainder at §4's declared 2:1.

Four slices move at once, so this is a corpus choice and not a controlled arm. It is reported as one, and the two results below are the ones that survive that caveat.

The structured share pays out, and the two gains turn out to be the same gain

Against a floor of 2, structured at 10% is worth +15 here and was worth +22 alone. The two effects sum to +28 and deliver +21, which reads like a ceiling until the failure modes are broken out — and then it is not a ceiling at all. The conversational slice fixes wrong_value and not_json and never touches missing_key; the structured slice fixes all three. So the +6 conversational buys at 2.5% structured is a subset of the +22 structured buys on its own, and adding both gets the larger one rather than the sum. Nothing was lost to saturation; the second slice was repairing failures the first had already repaired.

(Annotated after the thirtieth finding: the floor is 8, and the arithmetic above needs re-reading even though the mechanism does not. Structured's +22 alone and +21 with conversational present are both real, at 2.75x and 2.6x; structured's +15 given conversational is 1.9x and only just outside. But the conversational leg — 172 → 178, +6 — is *inside the floor, so "the two effects sum to +28" adds a readable number to an unreadable one. The simplest reading the sizes support is that structured moves this row by about 22 and conversational does not move it at all, which needs no subset argument to explain the shortfall. What survives independently of any floor is the failure-mode pattern, because it is an invariance within the row rather than a difference of totals: missing_key reads 18 / 18 / 5 / 6 — exactly flat across the conversational change and down 13 across the structured one. The mechanism claim is intact. The size decomposition built on top of it is withdrawn.)*

That is only readable because the row reports why it failed. Scored as four bare numbers, 172 / 178 / 194 / 193 looks like a ceiling effect and would have been written down as one.

register.conv moves 2.5 points with its own slice held byte-identical

2.5 points against the tightest floor in the harness, and the failure modes moved coherently: narrative-register failures fell as the narrative share fell, web-register failures rose as the web share rose. That is a mechanism, not noise. The row measures the register the model settles into given everything it read — the other slices compete for it — and not a property of the conversational slice in isolation.

This does not touch the twenty-fifth finding's conclusion: a 53-point move dwarfs a 2.5-point one, and the slice plainly buys dialogue register. What it removes is the right to carry the level forward. 76/200 was never a constant of the source, and quoting it at 250M — where every share will differ again — would be quoting a number that has now been shown to depend on the company it keeps.

The row that looked like it moved, and did not

tool.selection was the pre-registered risk. nano20m_narrative10 cut narrative 36.8 points and the row fell 150 → 116, so a proportional response to this 7.2-point cut would have been ~7 items against a floor of 8 — unreadable either way. It moved +1. The extrapolation does not scale down, and quadrupling structured cost it nothing.

(Annotated after the thirtieth finding. The +1 reading is unaffected — it is inside 8 and inside 44, and "the pre-registered risk did not fire" stands. tool.copy +22 is the eighteenth finding arriving on schedule. It clears its own floor of 18 and means nothing: it is the six-answer row, and tool.copy_wide asks the same question over 232 answers and moves +7 against a floor of 29. The row is still on the scorecard only because twelve earlier scorecards carry it, and this is the fourth time it has produced a number that would have been a finding if read alone.

What this does not say

Narrative and temporal are still not chosen. They sit at 19.58% and 9.79% because four other shares are fixed by evidence and something had to absorb the remainder — roughly twice §4's declared 10% and 5%, and the ratio between them is the only part of the pair with an argument behind it. Nothing here prices either one.

Nor does a four-slice move attribute anything to a single share. The two results above survive because each has a control that pins it: the structured claim is bracketed by nano20m_struct at the same share, and the register claim rests on its slice being byte-identical across the comparison, which is the one thing four simultaneous moves cannot confound.

And all of it is 25M params on a 130M-token budget. The point of building this corpus was to have something worth spending the 100M check on — validating an inherited recipe would have been four hours confirming numbers nobody chose.
