Web search · 26 August 2026

Fresh tool training, not new phrasings, fixed search routing

The result, as the finding states it

The phrasings were never the problem, and the arm without them fixed all twenty-five

What it cost

12.5 min of scoring on the 1 card first cited here (corn_jr_260m_ftwide_seed2); it also reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (corn_jr_260m). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

P3-B2, run 2026-08-26 on corn_jr_260m. Two fine-tunes from the identical frozen base, 786 iterations each, ~15 minutes a run. configs/mix_p3b2_ft_wide.py carries the pre-registration. It lands on outcome 2 of its own table, and outcome 2 was the one the control existed to make sayable.

The question, and why the pair was built

The fifty-second finding measured a phrasing defect on this checkpoint: the corpus offers 25 search phrasings and the weights keep about four. google {q} routed 8/8; see if you can find {q} routed 0/8. The reading that followed — in that finding, in DATA.md and in PLAN-PHASE-3.md's whole P3-B section — was supply: the corpus needs more distinct sentences, and the open question is where another 60 MB of them come from.

P3-B1 tested that at nano and could not read its own instrument (sixty-first finding). So it moved here, where the defect was measured and is large.

A fine-tune on fresh tool transcripts changes two things at once — the model sees more tool data, and it sees whatever carriers that data uses. So both arms fine-tune on the same 30 MB payload, and the wide arm's copy is the narrow one after gen_tools_p3b1.py swaps only the carrier line: 85 phrasings instead of 25, 31,350 transcripts differing, every other byte identical. check_p3b2.py reads that off the bytes.

The defect is real, it reproduces, and both arms remove it

harness/searchgrid.py, 400 turns per checkpoint, decline offered, nothing leaving the machine.

The base reproduces the fifty-second finding (that run read 87/200 and 53/200 on a different subject draw). Real-world subjects go from 72/200 to 198/200 — the column every question a user actually asks lands in, and the one that finding called the one nothing had measured.

The result: the extra sentences bought nothing, and the control is the only reason that is sayable

The narrow arm carries not one new phrasing and brings 24 of 25 templates to ceiling, including the two the base never routed at all. The wide arm, with 60 hand-written phrasings added, reaches 22.

On the frozen eval rows the two arms are indistinguishable:

row base narrow wide wide-narrow search.route_base 199/200 200/200 200/200 +0 search.route_wide 192/200 199/200 200/200 +1 search.route_heldout 187/200 200/200 200/200 +0 tool.validity 170/181 181/181 181/181 +0 tool.selection 171/200 187/200 188/200 +1

search.route_heldout is the sharpest of these. Its fifteen carriers are held out of both payloads and trained nowhere, ever — and the narrow arm takes them 187 -> 200. A model given no new sentences became perfect on sentences it has never seen.

So phrasing generalisation was never supply-limited. What the model needed was more, fresher tool-calling exposure. The fifty-second finding's own closing sentence — "the corpus does not need more search transcripts, it needs the ones it has to stop being concentrated in four phrasings" — has the diagnosis backwards: more search transcripts is exactly what it needed, and the concentration resolved itself.

Had only the wide arm been run, this would have been written up as a clean win for the 60 templates. That is what the pair was for, and it is the second time in two days a single-arm reading would have been wrong — the sixty-first finding's route_wide 4-of-4 sign was the first.

Do not read the wide arm as a cost. It is 22 of 25 against 24, and see if you can find {q} reads 7/16 against 14/16, at one seed with no floor measured. The honest claim is that the widened pool is not better, not that it is worse.

What was measured on the other side, because a fix like this is usually a trade

The base declined 232 of its 400 misses. The arms decline almost never, which is exactly the shape of the forty-second finding's over-declining being converted into wrong tools rather than right ones. It was not:

row base narrow wide chat.abstain_oos 33/100 38/100 47/100 chat.abstain_talk 100/100 100/100 100/100 chat.abstain_greet 67/100 77/100 81/100 refusal.no_guess 47/47 47/47 47/47 refusal.clarify 47/47 47/47 47/47 refusal.say 270/270 270/270 270/270

Every dedicated abstention row held or improved. tool.selection's base failure mass included declined: 9 — refusing items that wanted a call — and that class is gone in both arms. The one movement the other way is called_anyway, 8 -> 13 and 11, so a few items now get a call that should not: small, real, and worth watching rather than dismissing.

Rule 10's watch is clean at the ceiling, which is the strongest form this project can run it. tool.copy 120/120, tool.copy_wide 300/300, temporal.copy 353/353, temporal.route 400/400 — unchanged on both arms. The sixtieth finding licensed a fine-tune adding register over an installed capability; this extends that licence to adding exposure, at 260M, where nothing had tested it.

What this cannot say

One seed. The scored comparison is seed 1337. corn_jr_260m_ftwide_seed2 and _ftnarrow_seed2 are trained and unscored. Nobody has ever measured seed variance at 260M, so no spread here has a floor under it — the sixty-first finding's whole subject. The routing effect is 126 items of 200 and is not plausibly noise; the wide-versus-narrow difference is a handful of items and is not readable at all.

One grid per checkpoint. searchgrid has no measured floor at this size either (the sixtieth finding established it has none at nano). Same argument: the base-to-arm gap dwarfs anything a floor could be, the arm-to-arm gap does not.

The eval rows were at the ceiling before the experiment started. The base reads 93.5% on search.route_heldout, because those prompts are cut at <|call|> and ask which tool given that one is being called. configs/mix_p3b2_ft_wide.py registered that limitation before anything ran, and it turned out to be the whole story: the defect is not in recognising a lookup, it is in deciding to reach for a tool at all. Given the call, the base already picked web_search correctly for 93.5% of phrasings it had never seen.

And nothing here says a 5B pretraining mix would behave the same way. This is a fine-tune result. The sixtieth finding measured the fine-tune vehicle delivering 61% of a mix's effect on a different payload; no such ratio is established for exposure.

What follows

P3-B's supply premise is retired. Options 2 and 3 of PLAN-PHASE-3.md §P3-B — mine a corpus for utterances, have a larger model paraphrase — were both answers to "where do more sentences come from", and that is no longer the question. P3-B3 should not run. The 60 templates cost a morning, are on disk, and are not worth a corpus decision either way.

The live question this opens is narrower and better: what does "fresher tool exposure" mean, and how much is needed? The payload here is 30 MB of newly generated transcripts at ~1 pass against a base that read tools_names.txt at ~35 passes in mix_v3_5b. Repetition is the obvious suspect — arm 2's own measurement and the fifteenth finding both price it — and this experiment cannot separate "new data" from "less-repeated data", because it changed both.
