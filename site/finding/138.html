The parse and depth · 16 September 2026

He knows where the next request part starts, 80 to 93 percent

The result, as the finding states it

The level-1 cursor is in the weights and not at the ceiling: with everything before it gold he opens the next part where the last one ended at 80 to 86 per cent of interior boundaries at nano and 93 at 100M against a floor of 1, flat in offset, a lost cursor lands mid-part and no line that loses one passes, while the close is his at 98 per cent and a quarter of level-1 failures tile the utterance and fail on the decision alone

What it cost

7.6 min of scoring on the 1 card first cited here (nano20m_v3_fw_b1024); it also reads 3 cards scored for an earlier finding (nano100m_v3_funcworld_rope, nano20m_v3, nano20m_v3_funcworld). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

Read 2026-09-15 with no training and no new corpus, off the owner's question: how much of the parse's bookkeeping could be in his weights? docs/FAILURES-PARSE.md §5 proposed level-1 tiling as a runtime constraint — the parts, in order, only a joiner between them, nothing left over, true of every parse block in every functional-world corpus — and the thesis puts bookkeeping in the runtime by design. This asks whether he holds it anyway. scripts/probe_cursor.py, new, on nine checkpoints: the six rung-2 W checkpoints (nano20m_v3_funcworld, _seed2, _seed3, _rope, _rope_seed2, _rope_seed3), the C6 control nano20m_v3_fw_b1024, the mid size nano100m_v3_funcworld_rope, and B (nano20m_v3, never saw a parse world) as the floor. Cards: evals/results/<run>__cursor.json; per-boundary dumps beside four of them.

The instrument

Two distributions per gold level-1 line, teacher-forced, so a label or a cut he gets wrong upstream cannot reach the position read.

The start. After label[ of part j+1 with parts 1..j and the label gold, the candidates are every word start in the utterance, each scored as its first W tokens (the utterance from that word onward, closed with ]), W the smallest count up to four at which the gold start's tokens are unique among the candidates and the same W for every candidate at that boundary, so no length enters; identical W-token prefixes are one candidate. Softmax over the candidates; three classes against the gold start, which at level 1 is the first word after the previous part's span: cursor, before (a start already covered), after (a start that skips words). Top-1 and mass. The first boundary of every line (offset 0) is reported apart from the interior ones, because opening at the start of the utterance and continuing from the middle of it are different acts. The dump also records the likeliest start's distance from the cursor in words: one word off is a disagreement about where the cut goes, several is a lost cursor.

The close. After ] of part j, \n against a space-and-label, the only two continuations the grammar allows: early at a non-final part is truncation, runs_on at the final one is not knowing the utterance is used up.

Five rows, 572 lines, 772 interior boundaries; w_repair is out because its first gold line is the corrected deeper level, not level 1. Chance at the start read is one over the distinct candidates, 0.04 to 0.12 by row.

He knows where the last part ended at eight in ten interior boundaries at nano, at nine in ten at 100M, and at the floor's one in a hundred. The opening boundary is at ceiling on every trained checkpoint. B, which never saw a parse world, puts its mass on after at the first boundary and on before at every later one — it wants to start the utterance over — and closes early at 714 of 772 non-final parts, so everything above is learned from the world.

Flat in offset. By the cursor's position in characters, interior only:

The 41-80 bin is the lowest on every checkpoint and the 121+ bin is not below the 1-40 one. The cursor does not decay with distance into the utterance: FAILURES-PARSE §4's length driver is not the cursor.

Held-out carriers are the deficit, and it survives size. w_heldout reads 0.66-0.73 interior at nano against 0.83-0.97 on trained shapes, and 0.80 against 0.99 at 100M. On the rope seed 1 dump by holdout kind: held-out cores 53 of 71, held-out modifiers 39 of 61, both together 42 of 70; at 100M 62, 48 and 52. A modifier he never saw on the part just closed, or on the one about to open, costs him the boundary more than a core does.

One-word misses are 2 to 4 per cent of boundaries; the misses are far, both ways, in about equal measure. Of R1's 137 far misses, 124 land in the middle of a part — not at another part's start (10) and not at the utterance's start (3); at 100M, 42 of 47. A lost cursor is not a part chosen out of order. It is a position with no structural meaning, which is what a copy that drifted looks like from the outside.

The close

early, of 772 non-final runs_on, of 572 final B 714 129 nano 0-19 2-14 100M 8 0

Truncation is not in the weights' failure set. He closes the line where the utterance is used up and nowhere else at 97.5 per cent or better on every trained checkpoint. FAILURES-PARSE §6 listed truncation as a species the tiling constraint would close; it would close nothing there.

No line that loses the cursor anywhere passes level 1. The cursor is necessary. It is not sufficient: 260 of the 365 lines that held it still fail, and those failures sort by what the emitted line did to the utterance (span_ok for a span not in it, then order, then coverage):

the 398 level-1 failures, R1 cursor held cursor lost a span not in the utterance 149 90 tiles the utterance, wrong arity/label 94 28 goes back or repeats 10 10 closes early 6 8 no parts 1 2

A span not in the utterance is mostly a span that opened in the right place and drifted — 149 of 239 invented spans sit on lines where every opening was at the cursor. And 94 lines tile the utterance perfectly and fail on arity or label alone: the cut and the name, the decisions, with the bookkeeping done.

What this settles

The boundary the owner asked about, for level-1 tiling, on this world:

  • Opening the first part is in the weights at ceiling.
  • Closing the line is in the weights at 98 per cent.
  • Opening the next part where the last ended is in the weights at eight in ten boundaries at nano and nine in ten at 100M, flat in offset, ten to twenty-five points lower on carriers he never saw, and moved by size where the depth cut (findings 133, 135, 136) was not.
  • The rest of the line is a copy that can drift mid-span — the span-in-parent constraint finding 133 read lifting rows by four to twelve cells — and a cut and a label the runtime cannot hold at all.

So a tiling constraint in ParseConstraint would hold the 14 to 20 per cent of interior boundaries he loses at nano, every one of them on a line that would otherwise fail; the existing span constraint holds the drift; and a quarter of level-1 failures are the weights' alone. What the weights hold here they hold as a mechanism that generalises across entities (154-169 of 175) and not fully across carriers, which is the same shape the copy has had since finding 129: attention does the copy, and the copy is conditioned on recognising what is being copied.

What it does not say

The read is teacher-forced: every position has gold before it, so it is a bound on what free decoding, where a lost cursor compounds, would show. The crossing is one seed on one checkpoint. The 100M is one seed. w_heldout's three sub-kinds are 61-71 boundaries each. And "in the weights at eight in ten" is not "should be the weights'": a held rule has no failure rate, and the runtime's reason for holding tiling is unchanged by his holding most of it.
