Fine-tuning the base · 25 August 2026

Fine-tuning costs nothing but delivers three fifths of pretraining's gain

The result, as the finding states it

The fine-tune spends nothing and delivers three fifths, so the mix wins on efficiency and not on safety

What it cost

11.0 min of scoring on the 3 cards first cited here (nano20m_v3_p3a, nano20m_v3_p3a_ft, nano20m_v3_seed2); it also reads 2 cards scored for an earlier finding (corn_jr_260m, nano20m_v3). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

P3-A, the vehicle experiment. configs/nano20m_v3_p3a.py ×2 against configs/nano20m_v3_p3a_ft.py ×2, both reading the same 45,000,025-byte payload of real dialogue, both against the committed nano20m_v3 pair. Trained 2026-08-25: MIX 1,237s a seed, FT 238s a seed. configs/mix_v3_p3a.py carries the pre-registration and it was written before a byte trained.

The answer is the decision table's outcome 3. Both arms deliver. Neither arm spends a copy row. The fine-tune delivers 61% of what the mix delivers at equal payload exposure, separating above it by 2.01× the larger within-arm spread. So the fine-tune vehicle is safe and weak, and the mix is preferred on efficiency rather than on the deference argument that motivated the question.

What made this a different question from the sixth finding

Finding 6 measured fine-tune against pretraining and found deference does not fine-tune in — temporal.copy 100% pretrained, 82–92% fine-tuned, 48–66% with replay. That has been the live argument that Phase 3 must be a mix. Its design was the hardest possible case for a fine-tune in three ways, and this experiment removed all three at once:

finding 6 here base had never seen a <|call|> base is nano20m_v3, fully trained on the eight-slice mix payload was a capability the payload is new sentences of a register base lacked the base already has replay was narrative only replay is the v3 mix itself, so the replayed stream carries the copy- discipline examples

Finding 6's direction did not reproduce. Not weakly — not at all. Every copy row is flat or up on the fine-tune, and the prose collapse that made that arm's result arguable is absent: lm narrative reads [0.4485, 0.4536] on the FT arm against a control of [0.4506, 0.4550]. So finding 6 is a result about installing a capability by fine-tune, and it does not transfer to adding register over one already installed. That is the sharpest thing this run says.

The controls reproduced first, and it is 121 of 121 twice

Both nano20m_v3 and nano20m_v3_seed2 were re-scored, not retrained, before anything was compared to them. scripts/rowdiff.py flattens a scorecard to its scored quantities — every row's pass/n, every lm nats/byte, both reply instruments' aggregates — and diffs at zero tolerance:

nano20m_v3 121 of 121 shared quantities reproduce identically nano20m_v3_seed2 121 of 121 shared quantities reproduce identically

The only difference on either card is three rows gained — extract.span, extract.abstain, extract.brevity now sit in the default eval dir and were not in the committed cards. Nothing that existed moved.

tool.copy moved nine items in five iterations, and nothing else moved at all

The MIX seed-1338 run selected ckpt.pt at iter 5,625 and wrote last.pt at 5,630. Both were scored, because a selection argument is answered by measurement. Five iterations apart:

Every likelihood row moves in the fourth decimal and tool.copy moves by nine of 120, in the opposite direction to tool.copy_wide on the same two checkpoints. That is a floor on how finely this row can be read, and it is larger than every arm-to-control gap in the table above. It applies to PLAN-PHASE-3's rule 10, which names tool.copy as one of three standing regression rows: a swing of under ten items on that row is inside the wobble of a single run, and only a substantial collapse is readable at two seeds.

This is why the cost side above is written as a null rather than as a ranking.

The secondaries were unreadable, and one was called that in advance

register.conv — control [81, 86], MIX [80, 81], FT [82, 86], FT last [86, 88]. The control's own spread is 5 of 200 and every arm sits inside or adjacent to it. No direction.

chat_reply.distinct3 — the control pair spans 0.0363 (0.8641 to 0.9004), which is wider than any arm-to-control difference here. MIX [0.8761, 0.8817], FT [0.8697, 0.8934]. The instrument cannot see an effect this size at two seeds.

The turn2 talk cell was pre-registered as probably unreadable, in mix_v3_p3a.py's docstring, because the two controls disagree in sign (+0.0150 on 1337, −0.0239 on 1338). They still do, and the arms scatter across both signs. It stayed unreadable, as written down in advance.

daycross.with_day moved a lot — control [29, 62], MIX [67, 93], FT [31, 74]. docs/PLAN-P3A.md §9 named it a known-noisy row and forbade it becoming a headline post hoc. It is not one.

The searchgrid read is a null about the instrument, and it is a loud one

The pre-registered non-movement: the payload contains no tool utterances, so harness/searchgrid.py's carried phrasings should not move on any arm, and the interesting cell was whether the fine-tune loses them. All six checkpoints were run, no --search, nothing left the machine.

kept_subject / 400 ctl 72, 104 MIX 30, 286 FT 133, 98 templates routing at all ctl 19, 14 MIX 8, 22 FT 22, 13

The MIX arm alone spans 30 to 286 of 400 across its two seeds — an 8.5× within-arm swing that covers the entire range of every other cell in the table, including both controls. search for {q} reads 0 on one MIX seed and 16 of 16 on the other; I need to know {q} reads 0 everywhere except MIX 1338, where it reads 16 of 16.

So nothing here is readable, by ground rule 6 and by the thirtieth finding's trap in its plainest form: no arm-to-control difference approaches the within-arm spread. The registered non-movement is neither confirmed nor refuted — the instrument cannot see a change of this size at two seeds at nano scale, and that is the finding, not a fact about the arms. Nobody should quote MIX 1338's 286 as routing improving; its sibling seed is the worst cell on the board.

The practical consequence is for the instrument rather than for P3-A: searchgrid was built to characterise corn_jr_260m, where it reads one checkpoint carefully. Used as a two-seed regression watch at nano it is dominated by seed noise, and it should not be added to any arm's registered reads until something establishes what its floor is. Cards are in harness/results/nano20m_v3*__searchgrid.json for whoever does that.

What this cannot say

The payload is real dialogue, not P3-B's tool-utterance phrasings. A vehicle verdict here transfers to the phrasing payload by argument, not by measurement, and the P3-B arm pair carries its own copy-row watch when it runs.

A larger fine-tune dose is not licensed by this result. The decision table says so explicitly and it was written before the numbers: a bigger dose is a new arm needing its own pre-registration. Nothing here says the delivery gap would close if the fine-tune ran longer, and the honest reading of a 61% delivery at equal payload exposure is that the mix gets more out of the same tokens.

Delivery alone decides nothing. More same-distribution text lowering same-distribution loss was nearly assured. The whole informative content is on the cost side, and the cost side is a null.

Outcome 3 is silent on the 260M launch order, and the plan did not notice. Outcome 1 would have released the 5B corpus from waiting on P3-B data; outcome 2 would have required it. Outcome 3 says only that the mix is favoured on efficiency — which is a preference, not a constraint on ordering. The launch order therefore stays open, and it is a decision rather than a measurement now: either accept the mix on efficiency and freeze P3-B data before the 5B corpus, or pre-register a larger-dose fine-tune arm. The 260M's extraction-chain gate (HANDOFF, 2026-08-25 — now docs/SESSION-ARCHIVE.md) is untouched by any of this.

How the arms were kept to one variable

mix_v3's weights sum to exactly its total_tokens, so adding weight P and raising the budget by exactly P leaves every original target identical to the token. It held on the artefacts, which is where scripts/check_p3a.py checks it: all eight original sources report the same train_tokens as data/mix_v3 and all eight val_<name>.bin files hash byte-identical. The payload's own split is byte-identical between the two corpora, so both arms read the same payload tokens in the same order — 9,653,224 expected on the MIX arm against 9,658,368 on the FT arm, 0.053% apart.

Total compute is deliberately unequal and it is the right way round, which is finding 6's own argument: a real fine-tune is always additional compute over the base it starts from, so payload exposure is held equal and total tokens are allowed to differ.

One build detail recorded rather than discovered later: tools is 0.31% of the FT corpus, so its val split is 305 tokens and Batcher skips any split shorter than block_size. The mean-log-per-source selection rule averaged eight sources on the FT arm against nine on the MIX arm and eight on the control. Every copy row above is scored on the frozen eval set, not on a val split.

scripts/check_copyable.py's undeclared-slice check caught the payload on the first full sweep after it existed, which is the discipline working exactly as DECLARED's comment says it should. It is declared with an empty tuple on conversational.txt's own terms — mined rather than generated, unlabelled utterances, no field any example asks the model to read back — and with the cardinality written down anyway: 55,797 distinct openings over 66,445 documents, 1.19 uses each.
