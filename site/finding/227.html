A short word vector holds field knowledge; frozen, he cannot read it

The result, as the finding states it

The book with the ruler never read — a page for each of the 20,000 commonest words — answers 91 wishes of 192 and 95 fields of 141 against the 20M's 71 and 73 and 78 and 76, and squeezed to a short list of numbers a word it keeps the fields and loses the wishes: 85 fields and 72 wishes at 256 numbers, 92 and 75 at 512, and still only 83 wishes at 4,096. So the two halves of the understanding ruler are two kinds of knowledge: what a phrase's content word says about a field is compact, and what a whole description implies is a long tail of separate lines that does not fit a short vector. Handed 512-number homes through a 262,144-number input adapter, the frozen 20M reads neither: wishes 68 and 71, fields 74 and 78, failing the registered row at both seeds, though held-out web text gets 0.017 and 0.018 nats a token easier where shuffled homes give 0.000 and 0.003. The same homes laid on his next-piece scores did nothing at the registered settings (it had barely trained), and at ten times the step size and three times the steps — a cause read — lift fields to 83 and 80 and wishes to 73 and 77 and take 0.3% to 0.5% off held-out text of every kind: a direction. A first squeeze had read 47 of the second 96 wishes at 256 numbers; that book had a page for every ruler word, and with 5,000 pages and none of them the whole book reads the wishes at the 20M's own level, 71.

What it cost

the book-only reads 203.7 s of card (and three scratch reads before them, 0.2, 0.4 and 1.3 min); probe and pre-registration committed at 13:27 on 2026-11-13 (d07b777), after a 60-step smoke read (3.6 min) that moved the training set from the web range to the whole stream; the registered run 911.6 s of card, contended at 30%, for four adapters at two checkpoints; the cause read of out 635.3 s. ETA ~20 min of card for the registered run; 15.2.

The question (owner, 2026-11-13: "Figure out why this happened and everything we could do what are we not connecting", then "Yes, test it"). Findings 222 and 224 to 226 tried to write the book's knowledge into the 20M by nudging his weights. The other reading of the day was that the knowledge should not be written by gradient at all: counting writes it, and he only has to read it. Two things had to be true for that: the knowledge fits a home a word can carry, and he can read a home. The pre-registration is scripts/probe_read_homes.py's docstring.

Reading a home: the frozen 20M and four adapters

A word's home is the page side of the rank-512 summary of the ruler-blind 20,000-page book, attached at the word's last piece. in maps it into the token's input (he has to read it); out maps the mean home of the last ten finished words onto his next-piece scores (the book speaks); both; and shuf, in with every word given another word's home. Each starts at zero and trains 800 steps of 16 windows from the whole train stream at 1e-3.

What it licenses

By the pre-registration, between its second and third rows: H fails, and out moves the ruler only in a cause read. Three things stand:

  • Two kinds of knowledge, two answers. A field is compact and can ride in a short vector — 64 numbers a word beat the 20M — so the 20M's shortfall there is not room. A wish is a long tail and wants the table itself: for that half finding 197's reading, room, was right, and the book wins by being larger than he is.
  • A frozen model does not read a new input through a thin adapter. Reading a home is a skill of the trunk. Whether a trunk trained with homes learns it is still the one-hour arm, at a lower prior than this morning's.
  • The book can speak beside him at no cost — a little, on both reads, with every kind of text easier. For a judgment the runtime makes, the book's own answer (91 and 95, ruler unread) is stronger than any of these.

What it does not settle. out at one pair of settings, chosen after the registered ones failed to train. Homes from a larger table (a page for every word, not 20,000). A trunk trained with homes from the first step.
