Understanding at small scale · 13 November 2026

Pressing the difference a neighbour makes writes some of the pair

The result, as the finding states it

Finding 224's hard press, with the part of its gradient that would move the word in the pressed sentence with the earlier word swapped out taken out first, takes 0.40 and 0.34 nats off the word at the pair's other meeting and only 0.07 and 0.05 off it there with the neighbour swapped out, over 192 and 191 pairs at two seeds: the pair line, B - Bx, is +0.24 and +0.21 where the plain press reads +0.01, with 48% and 49% of pairs over +0.25 and none under -0.25. The registered row asked 0.25 in every weight, so it is a direction and not a pass, and the hold itself leaks (the word gets 0.18 and 0.16 nats costlier in the held sentence). Pressed only into the two words' own word-table rows the same step reads cleaner: 0.47 and 0.53 off the word at the other meeting, 0.04 and 0.02 where the book is silent, 0.05 and 0.00 with the neighbour swapped, for a third of the damage (0.00063 and 0.00037 nats a token against 0.0020 and 0.0018) — printed beside the row, not registered. Holding the word at eight book-silent occurrences instead writes no pair at all (+0.01). So what a press writes follows what it is held against: nothing, and it writes the word; the word elsewhere, and it writes less of the same; the same sentence without the neighbour, and what is left is the neighbour.

The question (owner, 2026-11-13: "Yes test it"). Finding 224: a hard press on a word at one meeting of a book-tied pair makes the word likelier everywhere, because that is where the loss falls fastest. The press has to be forbidden that route — the word up where its neighbour is, held where it is not. The pre-registration is scripts/probe_held_press.py's docstring.

The press

Finding 224's pairs, windows and nats-sized step. The direction changes: d = g_A - (g_A . g_h / |g_h|^2) g_h, the step -s * d / (g_A . d), so to first order a held loss does not move and the word at A still loses s nats. Two holds beside the plain press: hold Q, the word at eight other occurrences where the book is silent about it; hold X, the word in A itself with every token of the earlier word a in the ten words before it replaced by another item's a ("Whether or not your orbit has started [[leaking]]"). Read after each step: the word's cost at A, at B (the pair's other meeting), at Bx (B with a swapped the same way), at C (an occurrence where the book is silent, never held), at Ax, and held-out text. 199 of the 200 pairs have the neighbour to swap in both meetings and four or more quiet occurrences.

Beside the rows: the word-table rows (not registered)

The prediction said row — the word's own rows — "cannot hold a pair and its held step either does nothing or blows up". It was wrong. Under hold X at two nats the word's rows alone carry 0.50 and 0.59 nats to the other meeting, 0.05 and 0.01 to the book-silent occurrence and 0.01 and 0.00 to the swapped one; pair (the rows of both words) reads the same at half the damage. How a single word's row can hold a pair: the row is also the word's output vector, and the step turns it toward the part of his state that is there only when the neighbour was read. It does blow up, later: at eight nats row costs 0.085 nats a token.

Three cautions on that read, each from the items:

  • Bx shares its swapped word with the held sentence. The step may be teaching "not after orbit" as well as "after roof", which would open the B - Bx gap from the wrong side. B - C does not have that problem, and in pair it reads +0.49 and +0.43; in all only +0.11 and +0.09.
  • The medians hide a tail. In pair the word gets 0.25 nats or more easier at B on 60% and 63% of pairs and harder on 18% and 16%; at C it gets that much harder on a third of them. On average the step pushes the word down where the neighbour is absent (mean -0.32 and -0.26 at C) as much as it lifts it where the neighbour is (+0.43 and +0.41 at B).
  • It is not copying. On the 129 and 128 pairs whose word does not already stand in either meeting's own context, hold X in every weight reads B - Bx +0.36 and +0.35 (0.12 and 0.08 on the rest): the pairs he could read off the page are the ones that move least.

What it licenses

By the pre-registration, a direction: the pair is in reach of one step and small. Half a nat off a word that costs him eight is a start on a line, not a line. What it settles is the mechanism the last three findings circled:

  • What a press writes is set by what it is compared with, not by how hard it is. Plain, it writes the word (finding 224). Held against the word elsewhere, it writes less of the same. Held against the same sentence without the neighbour, what is left is the neighbour.
  • The owner's two ideas meet here. The book names the pairs; the private place — the two words' own rows — takes the press at a third of the damage and holds the cleanest read; and the press is aimed at the difference.

What it does not settle, and the reads it names. Whether presses add up: 200 pairs pressed one after another and kept, then every pair and the damage read — the same instrument, and the next read. Whether a pair written this way survives ordinary training after it. A swapped meeting with a different swapped word than the held sentence, which removes the first caution. Whether pair, registered as the place, holds the row it read here. A 20M only; and word-after-word pairs in web text, not what a description implies.
