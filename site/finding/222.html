Understanding at small scale · 13 November 2026

Pressing harder where the book points taught the 20M nothing more

The result, as the finding states it

With finding 221's book (rebuilt without the ruler's words) setting how hard each web word of mix_v3_webblend presses on the loss — 0.5 to 4 times, mean one, the web sources only — the 20M reads the understanding ruler at 72 and 69 wishes of 192 and 77 and 81 fields of 141, against the unpressed controls' 71 and 73 and 78 and 76, and against 66 and 71 and 82 and 87 for the same presses dealt to other words. Both registered rows fail; where the press is aligned adds nothing. And the kill fires, in both cells: conversational and narrative text, whose press is exactly one, read 0.5249 / 0.5235 and 0.4612 / 0.4606 nats a byte under the book press and 0.5236 / 0.5220 and 0.4602 / 0.4588 under the shuffled one, against lines of 0.5182 and 0.4579 — about a hundredth of a nat on conversation, thirty times the controls' seed spread. A control retrained the same day on the same seed reads within 0.0005 of the old one on every language row, so the cost is the uneven pressing and not the code. That retrain also prices the ruler's item-level read: against its own twin it gains 30 items and loses 30 of 333 and agrees on the pick for 234, so the 39 gained and 39 lost between a control and its pressed twin are a retrain's worth of noise, and by hand they are his default label per kind moving (finding 192).

What it cost

build, three hand reads of the score and the pre-registration 07:49-08:15 on 2026-11-13 (54e0fe7); the review that found the ruler's words in the book, 08:33-08:46 (6cb63b4); four runs and four cards 09:00-10:18, 59.4 min of training and 14.0 min of scoring (a pressed run takes 14.8-14.9 min against the control's 14.0: the second cross-entropy costs 6%); the retrained control, registered at feaea46, 14.0 min of training and 3.6 of scoring; two control re-scores 7.8 min; the reads 2.3 min. ETA ~85 min of card for the arms; they took 78, and the control that was not in the estimate took 26 more.

The question (owner, 2026-11-12: "certain areas are pressed harder while some areas pressed lighter", and "the book tells us how hard to press"). Finding 221 put the knowledge the 20M lacks in its own 130M tokens: a book of tallied lines read the ruler above it. The owner's reading of why was that every token presses the shared weights with the same gentle strength, most of it on grammar he already has, so one meeting leaves almost nothing. The arm changes one thing: the loss weight of each web token. The pre-registration is configs/nano20m_v3_webblend_press.py; corn/press.py and scripts/build_press.py argue the press and the score; scripts/read_press.py is the reader and adds no threshold of its own.

The press

A web word's score is what the ten words before it say about it: each earlier, different word with a page votes its line to this word (PPMI, window

  1. less the word's own usual pull, floored at zero. Clipped at the 99th percentile, mapped onto 0.5 to 4, divided by its mean: floor 0.507, cap 4.059, mean one over the 64,328,491 web tokens, exactly one on the other 65,951,222. A third of web tokens sit at the floor; 8.2% are pressed above two and carry 22.4% of all press; the 200 commonest words average 1.01 and words ranked 2,000 and rarer 1.40. Cell S deals the same multiset to other web words of the same number of tokens (76.2% of web tokens moved).

Three things were found and fixed before a byte trained, each by reading:

  • Two scores were dropped on the hand read. The plain sum of a word's neighbours' lines pressed the and of at 2.4 and 2.2 times the mean and snug at the floor — finding 221's stock answer. Taking the word's usual pull off the sum put two thirds of all tokens at the floor.
  • The book knew the test. Finding 221's book gave a page to every ruler word (336 beyond the 5,000 commonest) and a column to every ruler label (6 beyond the 20,000 commonest), so a press built from it pressed the test's own vocabulary. The files of 54e0fe7 were built that way and dropped; the book here never reads the ruler, and scripts/check_press.py holds that.
  • A dotted capital I lower-cases to an i and a combining dot, which shifted every aligned word after the 878,735th by one until it was spelled out. The alignment is held against the cached stream at both ends.

What the retrained control showed

configs/nano20m_v3_webblend_rerun.py was registered (feaea46) when both cells first read past the kill line on text they did not press, and two readings fitted: the pressing, or six commits to corn/train.py and corn/data.py since the controls trained on 2026-10-20.

  • The code did not move. Against the old control at the same seed: conversational +0.0004, narrative -0.0005, web +0.0004, temporal +0.0006, and the same best iteration and validation loss (5,125, 2.5270). The cost in both cells is the pressing.
  • The language rows repeat; nothing item-level does. The same retrain reads 70 wishes and 79 fields — one off each total — and against its twin it gains 30 items and loses 30, with the same pick on 234 of 333. Behaviour rows move further: tool.copy 84 against 112 of 120, daylog.open 46 against 23 of 90. Two trainings of one config at one seed are not the same model item by item (compiled kernels are not bit-reproducible, and a default label per kind amplifies it). So of the nine card rows where both K seeds sit outside the controls' range, none is read, and the 39 gained and 39 lost between nano20m_v3_webblend and its pressed twin (35 and 34 at the second seed) are nine items a side over a retrain's own churn.

Read by hand: every item that changed side, both seeds

The changes are his default label moving, not a word learned. At seed 1337 the control answers scary to films and wholesome to TV shows; the pressed run answers sad and relaxing to films and thrilling to shows. So it gains "a family says goodbye to their grandmother in hospital" (sad) and four thrilling shows, and loses four ghost stories and three wholesome shows. At seed 1338 the swap runs the other way — it gains two wholesome shows and loses two thrilling ones. The most picked wrong labels say the same: the control's are wholesome 19 and scary 14, the pressed run's relaxing 19 and sad 12. Finding 192's mechanism, unchanged by the press.

Why it did not move him (causes named, one read each still owed)

  • The press is a reallocation, not a hard press. A step averages 24,576 tokens; the cap moves a token from one part in that to four. A pairing met once is still one small nudge. What the book does with one meeting — write a line — is not what a weight on the loss does.
  • It presses much he already has. Read before training: the control's own loss is 3.12 nats on tokens pressed above two against 3.36 at the floor, and 26% of the hardest-pressed he already has under half a nat.
  • A ruler-blind book cannot reach the rare pairs. Only the 5,000 commonest words vote and only the 20,000 commonest can be pressed; finding 221's lead on the wishes rested on lines met once or twice between words rarer than that (37 of 96 without them).

What it licenses

By the pre-registration: L fired, so no press arm runs without a higher floor, and since it fired under the shuffled press too, the cost belongs to uneven pressing itself and not to the book. The crossed score (hard only where the book says meaning and he is surprised) was owed one read only if L held; it is not owed now. Finding 223 read the other half of the question the same day — whether the knowledge is in him and unread — and it is not.

What it does not settle. One dose, one score, one pass, 20M. Whether a press that is really hard — many steps on one meeting, which is rereading — moves him. Whether a floor of one (only ever pressing harder) keeps the conversation rows. Whether cell S's fields, 82 and 87, are anything: they are above the controls at both seeds and unregistered.
