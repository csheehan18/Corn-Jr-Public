Two extra seeds already on disk retire three earlier readings

The result, as the finding states it

The two extra seeds were already on disk, and the floor they measure retires three readings

What it cost

4.5 min of scoring on the 1 card first cited here (nano20m_v1_seed4); it also reads 5 cards scored for an earlier finding (nano100m_v1, nano20m_v1, nano20m_v1_chat and 2 more). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

The forty-first finding closed on "more baseline seeds are the only thing that makes tool.name_fidelity quotable at all, and they cost 17 minutes each." They cost nothing. nano20m_v1_seed3 and nano20m_v1_seed4 have been trained since the thirtieth finding and were scored on evals_para, evals_heldout, evals_wideonly and all six payload arms — but never on the frozen set with the three --add files present, which is where daycross.*, refusal.say* and tool.name_fidelity live. *__newrows.json existed for nano20m_v1 and nano20m_v1_seed2 and for nothing else in the baseline. Two re-scores, ~5 minutes each, no GPU training and no new corpus.

The two-seed spreads were not luck in the direction that would have been convenient. Every one of them survived doubling, and refusal.say got wider — 10 of 270 across two seeds, 42 across four. That is the thirty-seventh finding's caution arriving with a number: a two-seed spread is a range and not a floor, and here the range was the optimistic half.

What it settles

The forty-first finding stands, and now it is measured. Baseline [33, 64] becomes [33, 64] at four seeds — 33, 37, 44 and 64, with seed 1 sitting 20 above the next-highest. The toolnames arm reads [39, 53], still strictly inside. The row cannot read that arm, and this is no longer an inference from two points.

What it retires

1. refusal.say on the chat arm. DATA.md §11 records "refusal.say moved 162/172 → 188/196 with nothing in this corpus pointing at it". The arm's 188 and 196 sit inside the baseline's own 162–204. There was no move. The honest reading was always the suspicious one — mix_v1_chat swaps a conversational file and has no path to a refusal row — and the corpus was right and the row was wrong.

2. refusal.say across capacity. nano100m_v1 reads 190/270 against the 29.6M four-seed 162–204. Inside. The handoff's "172 → 190" is not a capacity reading. The ninth open item's conclusion — that the row was recall end to end — is unaffected and was reached another way.

3. tool.name_fidelity across capacity. nano100m_v1 reads 75/120 against a 29.6M maximum of 64 and a spread of 31. +11 over a 31-wide range at one seed is not a reading, and the "64 → 75" row in the handoff's both-sizes table should not be quoted as a capacity gain.

What survives, and is stronger for it

The fortieth finding. Its control is nano20m_v1_chat ×2, not mix_v1, so the widened baseline does not touch it directly — but it prices the instrument the finding is built on. The daycross delta has a 34-wide floor on this corpus, against the finding's baseline [-155, -120] → arm [-63, -29]: worst-case separation 120 → 63 is 57, still clear of 34.

Gap 1 does not yield to capacity, and now against four seeds. daycross delta reads -150 at 91.6M against 29.6M's -97 to -131. It is 19 outside the bottom of a 34-wide range, which is less than the range — so "the header costs 126 at 29.6M and 150 at 91.6M" is one seed against four and the two are not separable. The direction is what the finding claimed: capacity does not buy the header back, and if anything reads slightly worse.

refusal.say_unechoed is a hard zero. 0/55 on four seeds of the baseline, both seeds of the chat arm, and both sizes. Nothing in this project has ever moved it.

Why, answered 2026-08-19 — the forty-sixth finding. Because the class was never in the corpus. data/raw/tools.txt is missing the entire cold_start class its own generator emits, so the two sentences these 55 items rest on appear 0 times in every corpus through mix_v2. The zero was an absent class rather than a hard behaviour, and the first corpus that contained the class read 55/55 — by reciting two strings seen 902 times.

The pairing check now holds on four seeds, and it prices the delta

daycross.no_day is tool.selection on the same items — identical pass, n and why, with a named breakdown added. That was known and is the point: the file was built so the untreated cell is a row already in the catalogue, and the handoff records the check passing on nano20m_v1 and nano100m_v1. It passes on seeds 3 and 4 as well, byte-for-byte on all three fields.

What is new is what that costs. The delta's untreated side inherits tool.selection's measured floor of 44 (thirtieth finding), the largest in the catalogue, so a daycross delta is not two independent measurements against two small floors. Its own floor comes out at 34 — smaller than 44, because the two cells move together within a seed, which is exactly why the delta is the thing to quote and the cells are not. That was the design's assumption and it is now measured: no_day spans 44 across four seeds, with_day spans 29, and the delta spans 34 rather than the 73 the two would give if they were independent.

What this does not settle

  • The floor is mix_v1's. The thirtieth finding's rule is that a floor is a property of the arm. Four seeds of one corpus says what this corpus reproduces to, not what chatday, toolnames or toolspace_decline reproduce to. Each of those has two seeds.
  • tool.name_fidelity's distribution is not four points of noise. 33, 37 and 44 cluster and 64 does not. That could be a heavy tail, or seed 1 could be doing something the other three are not, and 120 items cannot tell the difference. More seeds would narrow it; the row is cheap to score and the checkpoints do not exist yet.
  • ~~The scorer defect is untouched.~~ Repaired in the same session, and it does not change these numbers. score_name_fidelity gains substituted_composed, meaning exactly "a name from corn.toolgen.COMPOSED_CONTACTS" — an addition after the CONTACTS check rather than a redefinition, so the three scorecards carrying the older buckets cannot move. Verified on the plan's rule-4 terms: re-scoring nano20m_v1 and _seed2 leaves 23 pre-existing behaviour rows byte-identical on both, every scored section identical, and the new bucket never fires on either — which is the property that makes it safe. All four baseline seeds share CONTACTS, so this finding's numbers were internally consistent either way; the repair is for the next arm that moves the book.
