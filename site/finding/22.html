The corpus mix · 17 August 2026

Every conversation dataset fails a vocabulary test built from web text

The result, as the finding states it

Everything that sounds right fails the ruler, and the ruler came from web text

A fourth conversational candidate, measured the way the other three were: a million words at three offsets, scripts/vocab.py's word rule, turn length beside the two vocabulary numbers. No GPU, no download into data/raw — a 40 MB range request against a 9.5 GB gzip stream, decompressed to 127 MB in the scratchpad and thrown away.

The licence was answered before the vocabulary, and it is a no

DATA.md §11 recorded OpenSubtitles as licence "unknown", which read like a gap in the mirror. It is not. OPUS's own corpus metadata carries a license field and other corpora use it:

corpuslicense
TatoebaCC BY 2.0 FR
WikiMatrixCC-BY-SA 4.0
TED2020a pointer to the TED Talks usage policy
OpenSubtitlesabsent — no license, no copyright

Eight top-level keys and neither is a licence. What it carries instead is a request — "Please, add a link to http://www.opensubtitles.org/ to your website" — with the note "I promised this when I got the data from the providers of that website". That is a personal undertaking passed downstream rather than a grant of rights, and the "unknown" is faithful to upstream.

The two apparent escape hatches both close. Helsinki-NLP/OpenSubtitles2024 is ODC-BY but is the benchmark release: dev/test only, 2,022 bilingual pairs, no monolingual English, and its own card says not to train on it. META-SHARE's CC-BY-NC-SA 3.0 record is NonCommercial plus ShareAlike — a different class from every slice in the corpus today — and could not be verified directly.

Target was >30,000 distinct at ≤50% top-100. It misses both at every offset. Stripping the bracketed production cues ([laughing], [Amy]) moves distinct by 0.4% and top-100 by 0.6 points, so that judgement call is not carrying the result.

Two things worth recording that were not the question. It is not stable across offsets and SODA was — 27,927 / 18,766 / 17,623 against SODA's 15,669 / 15,942 / 16,318 — and the text says why: offset 0 is contemporary reality television and 60/100/120 MB are all classic-era film, so era varies with position and no single sample characterises the corpus. Running the three-offset protocol is what turned that up. And turn length is the best of any candidate by a factor of three, median 5 against SODA's 14 and UltraChat's 19, with the caveat that an OPUS mono line is a subtitle cue and part of that 5 is display segmentation.

Four candidates now, and they are ordered rather than scattered

  • Written assistant prose — oasst1, UltraChat — passes vocabulary, fails register.
  • Model-generated dialogue — SODA — fails both.
  • Scripted speech — OpenSubtitles — best register of the four, fails vocabulary.

The closer a candidate gets to real speech, the worse it does on §5.0's ruler. Three candidates made that look like bad luck; the fourth makes it look like a property of speech. And it is a prediction about where §11 has been pointing: Switchboard, Fisher and CALLHOME are spontaneous speech, and spontaneous speech has a smaller vocabulary than scripted speech, not a larger one. The LDC-paid route is now predicted to fail the same bar and to fail it harder — so buying it on the argument currently recorded would be paying for a corpus this project's own ruler rejects. That is worth knowing for the price of a 40 MB range request.

So the next question is about the ruler

§5.0's bar was read off written web text and applied unchanged to a slice whose job is register. If every source with spoken register fails it and every source that passes it is written prose, the ruler is a candidate for being wrong before the search is. Three checks, none needing new data: restate the bar per share rather than per slice, since TinyStories was rejected at 47% of the corpus and a 20% slice at 55% top-100 beside a 40% web slice at 43.2% has never been arithmetic anyone did; point vocab.py at a blend rather than a slice, since the number the model actually sees is unmeasured for every corpus in the project; and check whether the two numbers predict anything downstream, where the sixteenth finding's "corpus diversity does not predict variety.distinct3" is the only evidence either way and points at no.

If the bar does not survive being restated, the conversational search is unblocked without sourcing anything — and SODA, which is right-register, freely licensed under CC BY 4.0 and was rejected on this bar alone, becomes the cheapest arm available.

What this does not say

It does not say OpenSubtitles would train badly; nothing here is a training run, and §5.0's numbers have never been shown to predict a model result. It says the corpus cannot be used — that part is the licence and is not a judgement call — and that the measurement which would have justified pursuing it fails too, in a direction that generalises to the sources behind it. And it is scripted speech, so the ASR-shape question is still untouched: nothing measured so far is spontaneous or disfluent.
