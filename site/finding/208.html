The reply judge · 22 October 2026

The shipped model answers most one-off messages coherently

The result, as the finding states it

Asked the 148 everyday messages of probe_reply.py one at a time, greedy, in the plain chat frame, the shipped 260M (corn_jr_260m_v4_ft_social8, server.json's checkpoint) answers 123 coherently by a blind hand read against the plain 5M's 70 (read beside it, blind; the 5M's labels came out at 70 for the fourth time) — 11 of 12 on good news, 16 of 18 on bad, 17 of 18 small talk, 13 of 18 advice, 15 of 15 feelings, 12 of 12 plans — and is weakest on the short and vague ("Hmm.", "Maybe.", "Never mind.": 11 of 20, below the 5M's 14). On the swap test it prefers the fitting reply in 99-100% of pairs (margins 20.6 within a category and 26.1 across, against the 5M's 8.3 and 11.5). The 5M's coherence problem, which findings 202-207 could not move by shape, decoding or a matching lesson, is mostly absent at the size and reading that ship

What it cost

the reply read on the two checkpoints 14:39-14:41 on 2026-10-22 (90 s, contended), the swap test (seconds), the blind hand read of 296 replies ~10 min. ETA 10 min of card plus the read, actual ~2 plus ~10.

The question (owner, 2026-10-22: "for me I was referring to just a one off — you ask him something and his reply is coherent to it. Have we achieved that?"). Every reply read today was on 5M research checkpoints; the shipped model had never been read on these messages.

Caveats. The plain frame (<|user|>message\n<|say|>), not the app's full prompt with its date and tool declarations — in the app some of these messages are routed to tools or the runtime first; greedy, one reader, one checkpoint (the shipped one; there is no second seed of it).

Read by hand

The 260M answers news with the right mood and a follow-up that uses the message: "Oh my gosh, congratulations! How are you feeling?" to a baby, "Oh no, that's not good. What are your symptoms?" to a cold, "I'm sorry to hear that. What was in it?" to a lost wallet, "Well, you could try setting a timer for yourself" to procrastinating, "Well, it depends. What are your goals?" to the job choice. Its failures are the 5M's habits, rarer: "That's great! I'm sure you'll do great." to a raffle win, "I'm sorry, I didn't mean to" to "Wait, what?", "You're not going to be able to do it." to "I don't know.", a dispatcher's "we'll send someone over right away" to a spider in the bath, and a stock opening ("I'm sorry to hear that", 9 times) where the 5M's was "What? Why?". On the short and vague it most often misses: "It's about 5,000." to "Maybe.", "You're really good at it." to "Yeah, I guess."

What it licenses

  • The owner's one-off target is mostly met by the model that ships: about five replies in six answer the message. The 5M is not a picture of what he talks to; its coherence failures at 47% were a size-and-reading limit, and today's 5M arms were working well below where the shipped model already is.
  • What is left at 260M is narrow and nameable: the short and vague message (11 of 20), a handful of stock lines, a misplaced role. Those are the items for the 1.0 corpus and for a reply read on every 1.0 candidate.
  • The 5M stays the cheap place to test mechanisms — but a mechanism's value for coherence is read at the size that ships, or on the swap and reply reads at 20M and up.

What it does not settle. The app's full prompt; multi-turn coherence (finding 207's thread); any 1.0 checkpoint.
