The tag spine · 12 September 2026

He learns to label his own request; forcing labels doesn't change calls

The result, as the finding states it

He learns to write the parse, half of it is label and not position, and not one of two hundred and forty forced tags moves a call

What it cost

2.8 min of scoring on the 1 card first cited here (nano20m_v3_compiled); it also reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (nano20m_v3). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

Eight nano arms trained 2026-09-09 from configs/mix_v3_decomp_{a,b,c,d}.py, two seeds each, all compiled, pre-registered in configs/mix_v3_decomp_b.py's docstring before a byte of any arm trained. Built to docs/PLAN-DECOMPOSE.md rung 1. Controls are the four already-scored nano20m_v3 seeds plus a fifth trained for this read (nano20m_v3_compiled); nothing else retrained and nothing was re-scored except where this finding says so and says why.

The rows are eight new ones added by scripts/make_evals.py --add and scored on nano20m_v3 before the corpus existed (rule 23). The floor is seven hard zeros and move.args at 39/120.

The question, and the shape of the answer

PLAN-DECOMPOSE's claim: a request is a sequence of parts, a part of a composite kind is itself a sequence of parts, he emits that parse as tags over spans of the user's own words, and because every level is a token he wrote, every level can be scored, forced and read back. Depth through him rather than through a longer forward pass.

Four arms, one variable each: A no parse line, B one-pass, C B with the labels globally permuted and the spans kept, D stepped — the runtime hands each part back and he opens it or acts on it.

The format installs and it is not padding. B reads 77 and 78 of 120 on trained shapes against a floor of zero, and 71 and 77 on shapes over entities no transcript contains. The shuffled control reads 38/37 and 29/29 — real, and half of B. So the labels carry content: C reproduces the cuts and misnames them, wrong_label 63 of its failures. PLAN-DECOMPOSE §7's "structured padding" row is refused. This replicated on two independently generated corpora and four checkpoints, seed spreads of one item.

It does not generalise. Zero on held-out orders, pairings and carriers, and zero at depth three — on every arm, both corpora, both seeds. A span-recall probe over all 216 held-out and depth-3 items says what the zeros are made of: on held-out carriers he recovers 21% of the gold cuts and 63% of the spans he emits are not in the sentence at all. That is not near-missing. It is finding 34's mechanism — handed a carrier he does not know he reaches for a trained one (go digging for comes back go and get) and when that fails he invents (Ithunlvcombe comes back Ithunlvdia). At depth three he does better — 62% leaf recall, 81% of spans legal — but a complete correct decomposition, every leaf in order, is 6 of 96.

The gate, and it is a hard zero

0 of 240. scripts/probe_depth.py --probe forced on four checkpoints: same prompt, same spans, same labels, the level-2 line's two children in swapped order — a forgery the grammar accepts and the corpus never wrote, and one that implies a different tool first.

checkpoint forced gold neither own-parse control decomp_b seed 1 0/60 60/60 0/60 60/60 decomp_b seed 2 0/60 60/60 0/60 60/60 decomp_d seed 1 0/60 59/60 1/60 59/60 decomp_d seed 2 0/60 60/60 0/60 60/60

And it is not memorisation. probe_depth.py --probe forced and gen_decomp.py share a default seed, so 28 of those 60 utterances are in the training corpus verbatim — and on a memorised sentence "answer from the sentence" is the strongest possible prior, which would read as tag-blindness whether or not the tag was blind. Re-run at --case-seed 77001, where 0 of 60 utterances appear in the corpus, both B seeds read the same: forced 0/60, gold 60/60, control 60/60. The zero survives the sentence being new.

The control is at ceiling, so these are not checkpoints that cannot answer. They read the utterance and not the line. PLAN-DECOMPOSE §10's third kill condition, pre-registered: "The forced-tag probe not moving the calls. Then whatever the rows say, the parse is decoration and the legibility claim goes with it." §9's argument — that a scored intermediate sorts every failure into the level it happened at — does not survive, because the level he wrote is not the level he acts on.

And every behavioural row agrees. move.next, move.args, e2e.tree and e2e.rebind are flat across all four arms, and on move.next the shuffled control is nominally the highest of the four. No arm beats C anywhere. The parse line changes what he writes and not what he does.

D was the alternative and D did not rescue it

§4 named adjacency in advance: "If D reads and B does not, depth was there and adjacency was the cost." The decision that reads a one-pass tag sits far from where the tag was written, and PLAN-1-0 §5c's memory probe is the precedent — 2/5 with no filler between frame and use, 0/5 with any. D writes each level immediately before the calls that read it, with the runtime handing the part back.

D reads zero on both held-out rows like everyone else, and 0 of 120 on the forced probe. Its e2e.tree 29/31 is nominally the best cell in the table and is not a separation: B's own two seeds differ by six there, and B sits below its own shuffled control. The adjacency explanation is spent.

D's parse.heldout and parse.depth3 are additionally read through B-shaped prompts — the frozen parse items carry no hand-back — so those two cells are biased low and are floors rather than readings. Every other D cell is scored under its own protocol, stamped decomp_arm on the card.

The band is the corpus, and compile is ruled out

row eager x4 compiled A B C D tool.selection 128-129 131 63/96 98/128 ... ... tool.validity 143-152 149 81/122 135/153 ... ...

tool.selection and tool.validity fall far below every control seed on the arms, with seed spreads of 33 and 30 against control spreads of 1 and 9 — the direction is certain and the size is not readable at two seeds, which is the sentence finding 74 had to write about tool.copy and is no more satisfying here. A fifth control trained compiled on the unchanged mix_v3 reads 131 and 149, inside or above the eager band, so compile is not the cause: a 5% slice declaring composed tool names displaces the tool decision, which is finding 51's arity price and finding 69's displaced prior arriving together. The copy band is untouched (tool.copy_wide above control on three arms of four); temporal.* is flat.

One cell is unexplained and is reported rather than smoothed: the compiled control reads tool.copy 103 against an eager band of 118–120, below every arm. One seed, no explanation, and it should be re-read before anyone quotes the copy band.

Three instrument defects, all of which spared the baseline

Recorded because the pattern is the finding, not the individual bugs.

  • e2e.tree grew the transcript past the 512 block and died on prefill at item ten of the first arm-B card. Arm A had scored clean; its rollouts are shorter.
  • e2e.rebind read arm B 0 of 50, no_call on every item, because corn.evals.continuation stops at the first newline and an arm that emits a parse writes parts: first and its call second. Arm A read 48 of 50 on the same instrument for no reason but the order it writes its lines in. That zero was reported as a result before it was understood.
  • Arm D's corpus was an oracle. The runtime handed back only the parts that open, which told him which parts were composite — knowledge no runtime has. An arm trained on it learns "when handed a part, open it".

Every one of the three fell on the arms that emit a parse and spared the baseline, which is the direction that makes a null look like a result. scripts/check_decomp_scorer.py now feeds every scorer the gold transcript and requires n/n on every row on every arm, with a negative group that requires the same scorers to refuse the defects above, and a protocol group that asserts the scorer reproduces each arm's own context line for line. It was red on D until the hand-back was implemented.

A fourth, in the corpus rather than the scorer. The first fix for the arm-D oracle echoed the whole span in the hand-back. That is forty tokens repeating a line he wrote above, and it took the length cap's rejection rate from 22% to 61% — the cap rejects long trees, which is to say deep ones, so the corpus lost three-turn conversations (5.1% to 0.1%), a fifth of its depth-2 trees, and put send under its exposure floor. An index costs a tenth of the tokens. Caught by reading the shape distribution gen_decomp.py prints, which no guard would have flagged.

What this licenses

Against §7's pre-registered table this is the second row — "a lookup table over trained trees; rung 2 does not run on this corpus; §5's sizing is wrong, not the thesis" — with one correction the table did not anticipate. "Lookup table" is too harsh: a lookup table would fail on held-out entities, and B reads 71 and 77 there. He learned the shapes and can refill them with strings he has never seen. What he did not learn is the rule that generates shapes.

So, precisely: decomposition over a closed move set installs as a surface form, generalises over slots, does not generalise over structure, and is not read by the thing that acts. The gate to rung 2 needed B above C on the held-out rows and the forced probe reading load-bearing. Neither holds.

Limits

Two seeds per arm, so directions rather than sizes (rule 6). The forced probe tests one axis — the order of two children — and a label-swap forgery is a different test that was not run. parse.heldout confounds held-out orders and pairings with held-out carriers and reads zero either way, so the two cannot be separated here; parse.depth3 uses trained carriers and is the clean depth read. The held-out rows are scored unconstrained, and corn/grammar.py's ParseConstraint — which forbids exactly the 63% of spans that are not in the sentence — is implemented and not used by any scorer, so those zeros are an upper bound on the failure and not a measurement of it. And these are nano readings of a dummy world: nothing here transfers to real carriers by measurement, which is what rung 2 was for.
