Re-reading the prompt each round costs launches, not tokens: 0.22 ms

The result, as the finding states it

Prefill costs launches, not tokens, so 42% redundancy was worth 0.22 ms

What it cost

0 s of new scoring on record; it reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (corn_jr_260m). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

Four runs on an idle 3080, configs/corn_jr_260m.py at bf16. scripts/prefill_redundancy.py counted the redundancy; scripts/bench_prefix_reuse.py timed what removing it bought.

The idea, and it was a good one

A turn is not one generation. corn/agent.py runs a round per tool call plus one for the spoken reply, and each round's context is the previous round's plus what that round produced. GraphedDecoder.stream opens with cache.reset(), so round 2 re-encodes from token zero every token round 1 read and every token round 1 wrote, having discarded that KV moments earlier.

Counted over 300 real single-call turns from tools.txt:

prefill tokens 116,338 already computed this turn 49,001 = 42.1% mean redundant per turn 163 tokens the declaration alone 151 tokens, every round of every turn

And it is exactly recoverable rather than approximately. corn/model.py uses learned absolute positions, so position i's K and V depend on i and what precedes it and on nothing after — a kept prefix is the history it claims to be. (Pleasingly, wpe is usually the constraint here: it is why BatchedReader cannot left-pad. Same property, paying out for once.)

prefill(reuse=True) was built, verified — 24/24 token parity, byte-identical agent output through the grammar and the tool loop — and timed.

It is a null

Round 2 of a real send_text turn on the 260M shape, 225 tokens with 163 shared:

round-2 prefill, cold 10.63 ms round-2 prefill, reuse 10.42 ms 1.02x, 0.22 ms saved

32-token reply decode 56.71 ms round 2 total, cold 67.34 ms round 2 total, reuse 67.12 ms 1.00x

72% of the tokens skipped, 2% of the time saved. Prefill is 16% of the round and reuse removes 0.3% of it.

Why, and this is the finding

Prefill time against prompt length, eager, the shipped path:

tokens 16 32 64 128 225 384 511 ms 8.82 8.48 8.57 8.36 8.65 8.12 8.45

Flat. Thirty-two times the tokens, the same wall clock. Prefill is bound by kernel launches — 20 layers of ungraphed eager work — and not by the tokens flowing through them. Cutting the token count optimises a variable the clock does not depend on.

This is the second finding's diagnosis, on the path nobody re-checked. That finding established decode was launch-bound and fixed it with CUDA graphs; the same argument applies unchanged to prefill and was never made, because every instrument since has measured decode. corn/bench.py reports prefill_ms on every run and it has been sitting at ~9 ms in plain sight.

What does move it

tokens eager compiled(dynamic) compiled(static) 128 8.50 11.32 5.06 1.68x 256 9.00 11.55 5.25 1.71x 512 8.57 10.90 7.16 1.20x

Static shapes are the whole story. torch.compile(dynamic=True) is slower than eager — guard overhead and fusion it cannot commit to — and the same compile with dynamic=False is 1.68x. That is ~3.4 ms a round, fifteen times what prefix reuse bought, from the opposite end of the problem.

Static shapes for a variable-length prompt means bucketing and padding, and the padding is safe in the way that matters. Right-padding with random garbage, comparing the KV over the real positions:

layer 0 delta 0.000e+00 exactly inert layer 1 delta 1.25e-01 scale 16.12 rel 7.8e-03 layer 11 delta 6.25e-02 scale 4.91 rel 1.3e-02 bf16 relative step 3.9e-03 -> 2-3 ULPs

Layer 0 is exactly zero, which is the causal mask doing its job: a real position never attends to padding, so the arithmetic is unchanged. What drifts from layer 1 on is SDPA tiling a longer sequence differently, at two to three bf16 ULPs — the same rounding the sixty-first commit's manual attention produces, and the same seq-length effect that appears between a 46-token and a 79-token prefill of the same tokens. So a bucketed prefill lands in the --fast-attn category rather than the --buckets one: same computation, different rounding, fine for talking and not for a scorecard.

What this settles

  • The 42.1% is real and worthless. Both halves matter: nobody should re-derive the redundancy and assume it is money.
  • prefix_reuse stays, off by default. It is correct and free, it may pay where launches stop dominating — a longer window, a batched or graphed prefill — and deleting it would invite the same day's work again. The docstring carries the null.
  • The chunked prefill it needed is the real yield. CornModel.step now takes T > 1 tokens against a live cache, which CausalSelfAttention.forward refuses outright. The sixty-second finding's draft-and-verify needs exactly that primitive, and it now exists.

What it does not settle

  • Bucketed static prefill is measured but not built. 1.68x is a timing of torch.compile(dynamic=False) on whole-sequence forwards at exact bucket sizes, not of a padded implementation with the logits read from position t-1. The reference forward returns only the last position's logits, so that indexing is a real change and is unwritten.
  • Whether prefill is worth attacking at all. It is 16% of a round here. 1.68x of 16% is ~7% of a round, against decode's 84% — worth having, and not before anything that moves decode.
  • The 8 ms floor is unexplained. Flat from 16 to 511 tokens says launches dominate; it does not say 20 layers of eager forward should cost 8 ms, and nobody has counted the launches or looked at where they go.

The method note

This is the sixty-second finding's shape twice in one week: an intervention reads near zero, and the measurement that kills it is the one that finds the real mechanism. Both times the useful move was to measure the quantity the mechanism operates on — bytes rather than tokens there, launches rather than tokens here. A null that only reports its own size teaches nothing; a null that reports why is a direction.
