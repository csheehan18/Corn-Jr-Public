The corpus mix · 17 August 2026

The two measured data shares were 4 to 25 times too large

The result, as the finding states it

The two shares anyone priced were both four to twenty-five times too big

What it cost

4.4 min of scoring on the 1 card first cited here (nano20m_balanced). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

§4's mix was written before any of the data existed, which was the honest thing to do and is why every share is a guess. Two have now been measured and both were far too generous. nano20m_balanced is the corpus that results: every slice the project has, at the share measurement supports, with no upsampling anywhere.

A quarter of the share keeps 89% of the gain and gives the tool rows back entirely. This is the first checkpoint that is best or near-best on every row simultaneously — before it, the project's two best models each won half the scorecard and lost the other half.

The marginal rate is the part worth carrying forward. The first 2.5% of budget buys about 69 schema.conform items per percentage point; the next 7.5% buys 2.9. A 24x worse trade, and not a free one — it is paid for in 21 items of tool.copy, 3 of tool.validity and about 1% of prose. Nothing about the row said this in advance; 194/200 looks like a share doing its job right up until you price the last three quarters of it.

The corpus bears very little resemblance to §4, and most of that was never decided

Writing the priced shares next to the unpriced ones is the more useful table, because it shows how much of the mix arrived by inheritance rather than by choice:

slice §4 said mix_balanced ratio how it got there general web 40% 38.95% 0.97x set once, in mix_web conversational 20% 0.00% — never sourced tool calls 15% 0.62% 0.04x PRICED, three arms narrative 10% 46.80% 4.68x inherited from the pilot structured 10% 2.50% 0.25x PRICED, three arms temporal 5% 11.13% 2.23x inherited from the pilot

Narrative is 47% of the corpus against a declared 10%, and nobody ever chose that. It is the pilot's token count, held fixed ever since so that each experiment had exactly one variable — a deliberate methodological choice that was never a corpus-design one, and never revisited once the reason for it expired. Temporal at 11% instead of 5% is the same story.

So the honest state is: one share as declared, one absent, two priced and cut hard, and two running at two to five times their declared size purely because a decision made for the pilot was never unmade. TinyStories is now the single largest influence on the model's register, at 47%, in a corpus for a voice assistant — which is worth holding next to the fact that the missing slice is the conversational one. Some of what narrative is doing at 47% may be standing in for a register nobody sourced.

Each of the four unpriced shares is one 35-minute run, and Phase 2 buys them at a hundred times the cost.

What it does not say. One seed, and tool.selection moved 32 points between adjacent checkpoints in the thirteenth finding, so the 148 → 150 on that row is noise and should not be read. The rows carrying this result are schema.conform, tool.copy and the three language rows, all of which have been stable across every run in the series.
