Aligning training blocks to transcript starts helps parsing, short of target

The result, as the finding states it

Arm A at one seed: blocks aligned to transcript starts move the level-1 cursor from 558 to 639 of 772 and cut invented spans by a fifth, short of the pre-registered third, lift unseen entities by nine and trained shapes by one, cost held-out carriers four and the deep cells ten, and put the copy band under its kill line on one of six answers, where a copied person's name comes back as an invented entity

What it cost

15.1 min of scoring on the 2 cards first cited here (nano20m_v3_fw_b1024_alignA, nano20m_v3_fw_b1024_alignA_seed2); it also reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (nano20m_v3_fw_b1024). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

PLAN-PARSE-WEIGHTS arm A, lever 7a of FAILURES-PARSE, built and trained 2026-09-15 by another session as configs/nano20m_v3_fw_b1024_alignA.py and read here the same day. One seed; the pre-registered kill needs two. The pair is the arm against nano20m_v3_fw_b1024 at seed 1337: block 1024, batch 12, 5,513 steps, compile on, one field changed (align_parse_blocks), confirmed from both checkpoints' config.json. Cards: the arm's full card evals/results/nano20m_v3_fw_b1024_alignA.json, and on both sides __parseonly.json with __parse_items.jsonl, __cursor.json with __cursor_items.jsonl, and __parsebind.json.

What the arm changes

Batcher samples block offsets uniformly; an offset inside a parse-bearing transcript now snaps back to that transcript's first token, so no parts: line trains against an utterance the window cut off. The other session measured the whole-utterance rate on the built stream at 0.898 uniform and 1.000 snapped. Read off the same stream here: snapping also raises each level's expected full-line exposures by 3 to 7 per cent (level 1 x1.03, level 2 x1.07, level 3 x1.05, level 4 x1.03), so the arm is cleaner context and slightly more parse gradient together, as its config docstring says.

The three pre-registered numbers

The cursor (probe_cursor.py, interior boundaries, first boundary excluded): 558 to 639 of 772, 0.723 to 0.828, the largest move of any arm on this instrument and into W's band (615-665). Every row rises: trained shapes 90 to 112 of 126, entities 134 to 152 of 175, held-out carriers 128 to 134 of 202, pairings 107 to 121 of 138, depth 4 99 to 120 of

  1. Far misses, two or more words off, 180 to 107. By boundary: the bare space 0.61 to 0.79, modifiers on both sides 0.26 to 0.44.

The crossing (level-1 cells of the scorer, 572 lines): passes 108 to 122; lines with a span not in the utterance 236 to 187, invented spans 286 to 220 — a fall of 21 to 23 per cent against the gate's third. Lines that tile the utterance and fail on the cut or the label rise 228 to 263: the failures that remain are more often decisions. Lines that lose the cursor anywhere fall 190 to 124, and finding 138's rule holds on both sides — 0 and 1 of them pass.

On the two deepest cells free falls by ten and twelve, and the exposure arithmetic above says that is not a shift of gradient away from deep lines; at one seed it is unread.

The copy band

tool.copy reads 100 of 120, under the band's kill line of 118, with every one of the twenty misses on one answer of six. Asked to read Alexander Fleming back from the observation he says It's Belaeskdia Fleming. or It's Torilvrin. — a name shaped like the parse world's invented entities in place of the copied one; the other five answers are 100 of 100 and temporal.copy is 352 of 353. The control and both W seeds read 120. This is the recall-over-copy species of FAILURES-PARSE §7e on the tool slice, and it is the first time an arm has moved it. One seed cannot say whether the alignment did it or the seed did; two seeds under the band kill the arm whatever the parse rows say.

Reading

The mixed row. The arm does what 7a predicted on the mechanism it names — the cursor and the invented spans move in the right direction by more than any arm before it — and misses the gate's size, costs the held-out and deep cells, and trips the copy band once. Nothing here is a result until seed 2: nano20m_v3_fw_b1024_alignA_seed2 against a nano20m_v3_fw_b1024_seed2 that does not yet exist, so the second pair is two runs. If the copy row repeats under 118 the arm is dead and the mechanism goes to §7e's line; if it does not, the parse reading is decided by the cursor and the crossing at two seeds, and the deep-cell drop is read against its seed spread.

What this does not say

One seed on each side; the control's own tool rows sit oddly low on tool.selection (83 against W's 133-139), so this control is not a quiet one. The bound decode's deep cells are 96 items with a seed spread nobody has measured at block 1024. The whole-utterance rate of 1.000 is the other session's number, read off the stream and not re-derived here.

Instruments. corn.evals full card and --only funcworld_parse --items, probe_cursor.py, probe_parsebind.py (about twelve minutes a checkpoint at block 1024), a scratchpad regeneration of the twenty copy misses with their bytes, and the exposure arithmetic over Batcher._parse_transcripts; all serialised on the card beside the owner's server, no timing read.

Amended 2026-09-16 — the second seed, and the kill does not fire

nano20m_v3_fw_b1024_alignA_seed2, one field from the seed-1 twin. The copy band recovers: tool.copy 119 of 120 against the kill line of 118, tool.copy_wide 300 of 300, temporal.copy 353 of 353. This finding's own clause — two seeds under the band kill the arm — is therefore not met, and the seed-1 reading of 100 of 120 was an excursion rather than the alignment. Arm A is not killed. The title above was written before this read and stands as the record of what one seed said.

Two rows this finding read as signal that are not. tool.selection reads 83, 128 and 75 across the control and the two arm seeds, so the observation that "this control is not a quiet one" at 83 is variance and not a property of the control — the row is simply loose at nano and should not be read either way. And extract.span falls on both arm seeds (12 on the control to 5 and 3), which is not in the table above because nothing here explains it; it is the one unexplained cost of arm A and it is owed a look before the arm ships.

Two numbers from the seed-1 write-up, re-derived. The share of drawn tokens landing in the parse-bearing segment is 5.000% with alignment on and off alike — snapping moves the window, never whether the offset is in the segment — so arm A is not a dose change in disguise. Parse lines a window rise 0.2740 to 0.2888, 5.4%, not the 15% an earlier small-sample read claimed. And the whole-utterance rate by level, in closed form over uniform offsets rather than sampled: 0.954 / 0.878 / 0.788 / 0.665 by level and 0.903 overall on mix_v3_funcworld_b1024, against 0.954 / 0.824 / 0.687 and 0.891 on W at block 512 — so the damage 7a names is concentrated at levels 3 and 4, and block 1024 is slightly better on it than 512, not worse.
