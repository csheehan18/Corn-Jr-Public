Understanding at small scale · 22 October 2026

His surprise flags unfamiliar names, not questions he cannot answer

The result, as the finding states it

On the plain 5M at both seeds, the surprise of the most surprising word in a question separates the unknowable questions of probe_reply.py from the answerable ones at AUC 0.67 — 0.92 and 0.91 among the questions that name something, 0.47 and 0.46 among those that do not; the mean word reads 0.70 / 0.69, 0.90 / 0.86, 0.54 / 0.59. An invented name is the most surprising thing he reads (Feruzza's 36-40 nats, Areskog's 35); a question about the sender's own life is ordinary words (How old is my grandmother? 6.5-7.0, below lie-in at 21-24 in an answerable one). So a surprise input could at best teach him "unfamiliar name -> I don't know", the shortcut the knowing set was balanced against

What it cost

writing scripts/probe_question_surprise.py and the read 11:52-11:56 on 2026-10-22, both checkpoints in seconds (card at 37%, contended, a bound). ETA 1 min of card, actual under 1.

The question (after finding 202). The surprise input (corn/wordloop.py) gives him his own per-token surprise to read. It can only help him say he does not know where his surprise on the question already differs between what he can and cannot know. Read before a surprise-input arm trains, on the 80 knowing questions of scripts/probe_reply.py, balanced 20 named / 20 plain and 10 / 10 on "my" in each set.

Predicted (stated before the run): the named half separates, the plain half does not, overall near 0.75. It held, with the overall lower (0.67).

Read by hand

The five most surprising unknowable questions at each seed are all invented names or words (zarbleflax, Feruzza's, Areskog's, Brannoc, Odile's); the five least are all about the sender's own world (How old is my grandmother?, What's the name of my boss?, How many people live on my street?) and read 5-7 nats — under every one of the five least surprising answerable questions at both seeds. The answerable questions' most surprising words are everyday words he meets rarely (lie-in, Halloween, cinema), not anything unknowable.

What it licenses

  • A surprise input alone will not teach him to say he cannot know. Half of what he cannot know — the sender's own life, the most common case for an assistant — is invisible to his surprise; the half it sees is the name shortcut. The surprise-input arm is not worth its 25 minutes as a knowing arm.
  • Knowing he cannot know is understanding, not unfamiliarity: that "my grandmother" is a person only the sender knows is meaning, and it has to come from text that shows it — varied, real replies that decline about the asker's own life and about unfamiliar things alike, so neither the name nor "my" carries the answer.
  • Finding 188 said the same of a shipped 260M: his surprise finds the unusual word, not the misunderstood one.

What it does not settle. Any size above 5M; whether a trained surprise input would find a use for the signal elsewhere (coherence, rare words).
