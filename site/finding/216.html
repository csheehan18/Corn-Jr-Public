The reply judge · 24 October 2026

The look-ahead holds on new messages; finishing and penalties add nothing

The result, as the finding states it

On 100 new hand-written messages, the look-ahead of finding 215, settings unchanged, lifts the plain 5M's fitting replies to 39 and 39 against greedy's 31 and 33 by a blind hand read — 21 replies pass only under it and 7 only under greedy (sign test p = 0.013) — so 215's direction replicates on messages it was never read on. Neither of the two changes aimed at its losses adds to it: finishing the five kept branches and re-ranking the finished replies reads 40 and 40 (80 pooled against the look-ahead's 78, 14 to 12 paired), under the pre-registered +5, so no new arm goes to the exam; penalising the bland reply raises variety by half (80 and 88 distinct three-word openings against 58 and 60) and costs fit (32 and 34, 66 pooled, 13 to 25 paired), and both together read 33 and 39. Finishing changes 54-70 of 100 replies and mostly swaps a failure for a failure: the finished score is led by fluency and the judge's end-of-reply vote is too small to overrule it. A linear probe on the 260M's states, fitted on 2,072 hand labels, counts every practice arm within 5.3 of the hand count — it meets its bar, though its error is the size of the gains it would have to see

What it cost

the grader and its check, the practice set, corn/judge.py section 7 and check_judge.py 7, the pre-registration 15:47-16:20 on 2026-10-24 (09f8197); generation and grading 2.0 min (contended at 39%, a bound); the blind hand read of 1,000 replies ~12 min. ETA ~2.5 h for the whole sweep, actual ~1 h.

The question (owner, 2026-10-24: "let's really nail 5M"; "do 1 and 2"). Finding 215's look-ahead lost 18 replies to two habits: a fine opening that went wrong later, and a pull toward the generic "I don't know. I just feel like...". Idea 1 finishes the kept branches and lets the judge choose among whole replies, where it reads best (finding 214's J1); idea 2 subtracts half of each word's log-probability after an empty message (finding 205's form) inside the beam and the final score (docs/PLAN-REPLY-JUDGE.md Phase 1c, corn/judge.py section 7).

The instrument

Tuning on the 148 would fit the exam, so the choice was made on a practice set of 100 new messages (scripts/reply_dev_messages.py, hand-written 2026-10-24, the 148's ten kinds, no overlap — the probe asserts it). An automatic grader was checked first against the hand labels of findings 214-215, its bar set before it ran (scripts/grade_replies.py: held-out AUC 0.85, every arm within 6 of the hand count):

grader held-out AUC arms off by bar 260M likelihood features (logistic) 0.819 up to 14 not met probe on 260M states, 0.5 cut 0.931 up to 24 not met probe, count = sum of p (fixed before — up to 5.3 met this read, fitted on 214-215 labels)

Neither chose anything here; the practice set was read by hand. The third row is the probe's counting rule tested on this read for the first time: it meets the registered bar. Its error of up to 5 per arm is as large as the look-ahead's gain per seed (+8, +6), and it puts greedy and the look-ahead within 0.4 of each other at seed 2 where the hand read gives 6 apart — so it can screen for large effects, not decide between close arms.

Read by hand

Finishing the branches is a lateral move. It changes 70 and 54 of the 100 replies and swaps like for like: "Congratulations, Kehon! I knew you could do it." becomes "That's great! Congratulations! What made you decide to become a promotion?" (a pass lost), "Let me take a look." to a sprained ankle becomes "That's great! I'm glad you're enjoying it." (lost), "Oh my god, I'm sorry. I didn't mean to hurt you." to locked keys becomes "Oh no, that's terrible. What happened?" (won). The finished score is the mean log-probability plus the judge's vote at the end marker, and the most fluent finished reply is often a stock line; the judge's vote spans about 0.7 nats (finding 214) and does not overrule it.

The penalty works as designed and costs what 205 found. Stock openings fall away ("What kind of salt?", "Wow, that's really good! What made you decide to get salt?") and odd ones come in: "Holden. Well, I'm not sure if I'm ready to go." to "Hold on.", "Thank good." to "Fine.". Raising a word because the empty message does not expect it raises rare words, and at 5M rare words are often wrong ones.

The look-ahead's gains on new messages are 215's kind: a question built from the message where greedy asks "What? Why?", and a mood turned the right way round.

The cause, and what it licenses

Both arms fail for one reason: the judge's vote is too weak and too unsure to choose among fluent candidates. The search finds better replies (the look-ahead's gain replicates), but once the candidates are all fluent, what decides is the judge, and it misreads mood on bad news and spans 0.7 nats.

  • The look-ahead is the 5M's best decoder so far, now on two message sets: +5 / +15 on the 148 (finding 215), +8 / +6 on the practice set.
  • Finishing and the penalty are dropped as defaults. The penalty stays an option where variety matters more than fit.
  • The next lever is the judge itself, idea 5 of the owner's list: retrain it on hard negatives (the wrong replies it now rates as fitting, cheerful replies to bad news first), then read the look-ahead with it on the practice set. Giving the judge more say (idea 4) waits on that, since a louder judge that misreads mood amplifies the misreading.
  • The probe grader may screen a sweep for effects over about 6 per arm; a choice between close arms still needs the hand read.

What it does not settle. Penalty strengths other than 0.5; finishing with a judge that reads mood; any size above 5M; the 148 for these arms (not run, by the registered rule).
