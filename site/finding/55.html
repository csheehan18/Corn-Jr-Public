Four hundred passages show he writes his own answer instead of copying

The result, as the finding states it

Six subjects said he recites, four hundred say he composes, and the slice was aimed at the wrong behaviour

What it cost

0 s of new scoring on record; it reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (corn_jr_260m). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

Three eval rows — extract.span, extract.abstain, extract.brevity — cut from Natural Questions dev, added by make_evals.py --add, and scored once against the frozen corn_jr_260m on 2026-08-24. No training. This is a before-number, taken because docs/PLAN-WEB-SEARCH.md's execution order puts the rows ahead of the slice, and it is the first time the extraction question has been asked at a denominator above six.

27 of 27 pre-existing rows reproduced identically, which is what --add is for and is now a checked fact rather than an argument.

The premise it was built on, and the sentence that withdraws it

PLAN-WEB-SEARCH.md specified the extraction slice from a six-subject probe, and this is the sentence the whole design followed from:

The two he got right, he got right by copying the whole paragraph. That is the substrate and it is why this is tractable: he can attend to a long observation and reproduce it faithfully. The task is to teach him to copy less.

Over 400 real NQ windows that is backwards. Of the 400 replies:

18 are copies of the window (10 on span, 8 on abstain) 69 never became a sentence at all — they reopened the JSON payload 220 are window-length prose he wrote himself

Median copied fraction across the long replies is 0.09. The cleanest single item is the tell: the window says "The winner was 19-year-old Jourdan Miller from Bend, Oregon", and he answered "The winner was 21-year-old Jourdan Miller from Bend, Oregon". That is not copying with an error. It is composing over a topic, and the number is the part he supplied.

So the task is not to narrow a span he takes. It is to get him to take one at all, and that is a different slice: the first lesson is a window arriving in the answer field means a short sentence drawn from it, which the answerable and unanswerable classes teach jointly rather than separately. A slice built on the old premise would have spent its budget on span-narrowing — a refinement of a behaviour that is not there.

Why six subjects said otherwise. They were hand-picked, and two of the six "successes" were successes because the whole paragraph came back — which reads as faithful recitation at n=6 and as a 4.5% tail at n=400. The fifty-third finding retired those same six items as a bound rather than a measurement, for a different reason; this retires the reading built on top of them.

The bucket that hid it for one baseline

The first version of the scorer called every over-long reply recited, because the plan said recitation was the failure to beat. It is a length test, and naming it for the expected answer made 192 confabulations look like copying. The first baseline therefore reported 137 recited on span and 153 on abstain and would have been read as confirming the premise it actually refutes.

continued_payload — 69 of 400, and the only falsifiable prediction here

worker_prompt ends with the say marker, so the model stands immediately after a JSON object, and the cheapest continuation is more JSON:

1979, "source": "wikipedia.org", "query": "when did frank sinatra ...

Scored as malformed rather than as content, because a reply that never became a sentence is not an extraction whatever strings are in it — and one of the twelve items that passed extract.span on the first pass passed by echoing a payload that happened to carry the gold.

It is the cheapest thing on the board to falsify. Nothing in the corpus has ever put a paragraph inside an <|obs|>, so the slice will contain this shape for the first time. If 69/400 does not fall, the envelope was not the explanation — and that is worth more than the number, because it is the one place this arm can be wrong in a way that shows.

extract.span is an upper bound, and three tightenings were rejected

The row asks whether the gold appears in the reply. That is necessary and not sufficient: reading the twelve items the first pass scored, roughly a third are incidental — an enumeration (Answers: 1971, 1972, 1973, ... brute-forces any date gold), a repetition (Wales, England, and Wales.), or the gold arriving inside a reply that answers something else.

Three fixes were tested against those twelve and all three were rejected:

grounded() drops 8 of 12, and three of the drops are correct extractions — "the current rate of interest on ppf is 7.6% Per Annum", "located in the fascia surrounding skeletal muscle". That is the fifty-third finding's defect rebuilt on purpose copied fraction does not separate them: worst junk 0.93, a correct answer 0.27 a length bound fitted to twelve observations is the twenty-third finding's bar, shaped by the sample it was read off

So the row ships permissive and says so in its docstring. The quantity to watch is movement: a slice taking it from 9 to 90 is not in doubt over three lucky items. What is excluded is continued_payload, which is a structural disqualification rather than a fitted one.

The abstention half reads zero, and it is a real absence

Two of two hundred replies carry a miss marker at all, so 0/200 is not a scoring artefact — there is almost nothing for a scorer to be strict about. It is also the row with no runtime floor: WS-C can check an extraction because the span is in the window or it is not, and it cannot check an abstention without performing the extraction it would be verifying. A model that abstains everywhere scores 100% here and 0% on extract.span, which is why the two are never pooled and why they carry different denominators.

PLAN-DONT-KNOW.md predicts this should be the tractable kind. Every "don't know" that arrives through an observation works — temporal.copy 353/353 under paraphrase, tool.copy 120/120, tool.copy_wide 300/300 — and every one requiring the weights to suppress a call fails. The evidence for "the answer is not here" is the window, and the window is in the context. So this is the most favourable regime the project has had for its largest standing failure, and it starts at zero.

What this cannot say

Nothing about a trained model. Nothing in the corpus has ever put a paragraph inside an <|obs|>, so every number here measures a behaviour that was never taught. External research entry 8 is the anchor and it points the same way: it tested instruction-tuned models only, ran no fine-tuning experiments, and named RAFT as what "significantly improves utilization". "Small models cannot do this out of the box" is not "small models cannot be taught this."

Nothing about the 260M's ceiling. One checkpoint, one seed, greedy, fp32.

Nothing about whether the split between the two classes matters. They fail identically here, which is the observation the balanced class mix was pre-registered from — but identical failure before training is weak evidence about behaviour after it.

The oracle condition is generous and the row is still 4.5%. Every answerable item's window contains the gold whole, by construction. The runtime reads several windows and votes; this reads the one that has the answer in it.

What follows

The share is pre-registered at 5%, one point, two seeds against a two-seed control — the thirty-fifth finding's "first point, chosen to make a null readable", with the thirty-seventh finding's chat2.txt at a 25th of a pass as the counterexample. The twenty-first finding argues a sweep buys nothing, since copy discipline is flat from 0.61% to 15%; this finding is the reason to doubt that transfers, because the behaviour turns out not to be copy.

The regression watch is tool.copy 120/120 and tool.copy_wide 300/300. Teaching a model to select from an observation operates on the machinery that makes it copy one, and every capability in this project has been paid for somewhere — the conversational slice took refusal.say from 172/162 to 30 of 270.

And the method is the transferable half, as it was in the fifty-third. The premise that survived four weeks did so because it was never asked at a denominator that could refute it. Six items chosen by hand, a bucket named for the expected answer, and a plan written on top of both. The row that replaced them cost one afternoon and no GPU beyond ten minutes.
