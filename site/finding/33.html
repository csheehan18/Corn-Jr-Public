Tool calls and copying · 17 August 2026

Copying depends on the tool output's shape and nothing else

The result, as the finding states it

The copy behaviour is keyed to the payload's shape, and to nothing else

What it cost

0 s of new scoring on record; it reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (nano20m_v1). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

Every arm from the twenty-eighth finding onward rewrites the <|user|> line and holds everything to the right of it. The observation's shape had never moved once, and neither had the name in the call. Two scripts move them — scripts/payload_evals.py rewrites the payload, scripts/newtool_evals.py rewrites the tool's name — and between them they say what the copy behaviour §4 rests on is actually keyed to.

Shape is worth about a hundred times the name. One level of wrapping and the model emits <|end|> immediately — not a wrong answer, no answer, on every row and both model sizes. The same keys and the same values in a different order cost ~120 of 300 at 29.6M and ~30 at 91.6M. Against that, a tool the model has never seen costs 3 of 300 and the difference sits inside a frozen spread of 41.

renamed is unreadable and should not be quoted at either size: tool.copy_wide spreads 99 of 300 across four seeds, and the 91.6M figures of 143 and 210 are inside it. No seed count this project will run fixes that.

The tool's name is not read at all, and the control is what says so

newtool puts date_lookup and fetch_answer in the call — snake_case names scanned against every corpus in data/raw/, with the build failing rather than warning if one appears. swapname puts a trained name there that is wrong for those arguments. Both make the transcript incoherent; only one makes it unfamiliar.

Both cost nothing, on six checkpoints across two model sizes. temporal.copy reads 353/353 in all twelve cells. Per-checkpoint deltas against each checkpoint's own frozen scorecard are +0 everywhere except tool.copy_wide, where newtool runs −9…+4 and swapname −2…+1. So an unseen name leans about 1% below a familiar one on the widest copy row, consistently but far inside noise — and the reading is not "incoherence is tolerated" but that the model is not consulting the name when it decides what to say. It reads the observation.

The four routing rows are byte-identical to the frozen set on every checkpoint, as they must be — this arm never touches prompt_call — and that is its invariance check.

refusal.clarify was measuring recitation — and that is a fact about the corpus

The row reads 47/47 everywhere and 0/47 the moment the tool's question is worded a way the corpus does not contain — on two disjoint held-out pools, four 29.6M seeds and both 91.6M seeds, twelve cells, no passes, 42–47 of the failures reworded. Every other row on that arm is identical to the frozen set, which is the invariance control the four structural arms cannot supply.

The row was measuring whether the model could reproduce one of four sentences it had seen 9,372 times. scripts/payload_evals.py's second pool is what makes that a claim rather than a guess: HELD_OUT_CLARIFY_2 keys off the same nine trained strings in nine wordings sharing no clause with the first pool, written without consulting the attribution files, and the 47 items are paired one-for-one across the two arms.

The second pool also retires the scarier reading of refusal.no_guess. Pool A costs 1–3 items at 29.6M and 5 at 91.6M, and on all five of those the model invented a date and called recall_day with it having just been told the tool had none. Pool B costs zero on all six checkpoints. The five losses reproduce on pool A and vanish on pool B, and they are visible from both sides — pool A's five did_not_ask on refusal.clarify are the same five items as its five guessed here. So it was that wording, not novel wording: VISION §9's most important behaviour is not generally fragile under an unseen refusal, it is broken by one sentence, and the live hypothesis is that the sentence reads as an instruction to produce something.

And the zero is a property of the corpus, not of the model or the scale — mix_v1_sayvar (45a98e9), which is the treatment this result asks for and which landed while the rest of this finding was being written. CLARIFY_POOLS takes the tool's question from 9 carriers at 2,096 uses each to 3,301 at 5.7 — answer's regime rather than clarify's — holding generator, seed, anchor window, expressions, left-context arm, resolved dates and the <|user|> line, and matching mix_v1 at 130,279,711 train tokens:

Eight baseline cells at exactly zero against four treated cells at 8–30. The row cannot fall below zero, so the existence of the effect needs no seed argument. So the correct reading of 0/47 is not that the row cannot measure deference and not that 29.6M is too small to relay an unseen sentence — it is that a corpus repeating one sentence 2,096 times per carrier teaches recitation, and one that does not, does not. The cost is seven items of frozen refusal.clarify on one of the two seeds and nothing else: temporal.copy 353/353 and temporal.route 398–399 on both, val 2.2554 and 2.2370 against 2.2516.

What remains open there is the dose. 2,096 recites and 5.7 reads; nothing between them has been run, so the knee is unbracketed exactly as left edges were before mix_v1_mid. The treated spread is also 8 against 24 on pool A — item-level instability of the kind churn.py exists to price, and there is still no attribution pass for the payload arms.

What this buys the tool layer

VISION §6 says tools are cheap to add and means it about the code, where REGISTRY has always made a tool a declaration. This is the same claim measured against the model, and it comes apart into three pieces:

  • Read-back on a new tool is free. Measured, six checkpoints, two sizes.
  • Payload shape is the contract, and it is the expensive one. A new tool returning a bare object with its own fields, or {"ok": false, "reason", ...} carrying say/clarify, inherits the behaviour. One returning a nested object does not, at any size tested.
  • Routing to a new tool is untouched and remains a training question. This arm holds prompt_call fixed by construction, so it says nothing about whether he would choose the new tool — only that he handles its answer once called.

What this does not settle

  • Why nested collapses. Total failure at every seed and both sizes is the cleanest signal on the board and the least explained. Nothing here distinguishes a parse failure from a model that has never seen a { in that position.
  • The dose curve is not a control-and-arm. reorder was written as this file's retemplate and is not one: on the payload side the only perturbation leaving an object's meaning and bytes untouched is the object itself, so key order is already a treatment. Read the four as a dose curve.
  • Share is not priced. resolve_date is 9.79% of the corpus against tools' 0.62%, and temporal.copy is the row most robust to shape. That is one plausible reading of the same fact and it is a share arm away from being a claim.
  • What refusal.clarify measures on an untreated corpus. sayvar shows the row moves when the carrier count moves, so it is not a dead instrument — but on mix_v1 it cannot separate a model that defers from one that recites, and every checkpoint in the back catalogue is mix_v1-shaped. Read every 47/47 in this document as "recited correctly" unless the corpus under it says otherwise. refusal.say does not fix this: 55 of its 150 items are answerable without reading the observation.
  • Whether the same treatment is needed anywhere else. resolve_date's payload is 9.79% of the corpus and the copy rows sit at 1.2 uses per value, which is why they read rather than recite. No audit exists of which other fields sit in clarify's regime rather than answer's, and scripts/check_copyable.py is the shape of the tool that would do it.
