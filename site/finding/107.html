Later layers know slightly more; the shipped checker already has it

The result, as the finding states it

The say marker knows more about the pick at layers 14–19 than at 12, by two points of AUC, and the shipped verifier already knows all of it: stacked over its margin no layer adds a thousandth, so A1 closes at its cheap read

scripts/probe_hlast_layers.py, 2026-09-04. One forward per Population A window (3,893) keeping the say marker's row at every block output and at ln_f, 24 s of GPU, 168 MB on F: (popa_hlast_all.pt); then logistic probes cross-fitted by passage inside Population A, on both seeds' labels. evals/results/hlast_layers_s{1,2}.json. Nothing trained is kept; nothing ships; corn/reader.py is untouched.

0. The arm and its pre-registered kill

PLAN-READER-REPRESENTATION.md A1 (audit L9): the verifier reads layers {8, 12, 16}, a band chosen for location by a linear probe on 800 windows on v4 (finding 83); the verifier's question — speak or decline — is a next-token question, and no verifier has read layers 17–19 or ln_f. Cheapest read: the say marker at every layer over Population A, the cross-fitted AUC per layer on the verifier's own label (the shipped span head's argmax on this window is a gold pair), printed as a curve and not selected on. Kill: no layer beats layer 12 by ≥ 0.01 AUC on either seed.

The say marker's knowledge of whether the pick is right rises through the stack and does not stop at 16: ln_f on seed 1 and layer 18 on seed 2 are the best single layers, at +0.023 and +0.025 over layer 12. Layer 8, the bottom of the shipped band, is the weakest of the three by four points on both seeds. So the band chosen for location is not the band that carries the verifier's question, and the pre-registered kill does not fire.

2. The anchor, and the read it forced

The same label scored by the shipped verifier's own margin — the head that reads the argmax pair's two boundary rows and three scalars, not the say marker — reads AUC 0.8816 and 0.8923. Every say-marker probe at every layer is fourteen points under it. The kill in §0 was written as layer against layer; the number that matters is layer against the head that ships, and the question the arm actually has to answer is whether a late layer's say marker carries anything the verifier's inputs do not.

That read is stacked and cross-fitted twice: each layer's probe out of fold, then a two-feature model over (margin, probe score), also out of fold. (A single 1,025-feature regression over margin and the raw row reads 0.78, ten points under the margin alone on both seeds: 1,900 training rows cannot find one strong feature among 1,024 weak ones, and that number is the probe overfitting, not the representation. It is recorded so it is not re-read as a result.)

No layer adds a thousandth of AUC over the margin on either seed. The verifier already reads, through the argmax's boundary rows at {8, 12, 16}, everything the say marker at any layer knows about whether that argmax is right. The late layers' two-point lead over layer 12 in §1 is a fact about the say marker's row alone; it is not information the head that ships is missing.

3. Verdict

A1 closes at its cheap read, on the second kill rather than the first. The pre-registered kill (§0) did not fire; the anchor made a sharper kill necessary and it fired on both seeds: the largest addition over the shipped margin is −0.0004. The two variants the plan named — all twenty layers of the say marker concatenated, and {8, 12, 16, 19, ln_f} — would be trained on inputs that carry nothing the current inputs do not, and the 30-minute harvest of the say marker for 200,084 training windows is not spent.

What it says for the plan: the verifier's admission error is not a layer-band problem at the say marker. PLAN-READER-REPRESENTATION.md §1's next reads are A2 (the prompt) and A3 (the layer band for the span head, whose inputs are the candidate rows and not the marker; this finding says nothing about them).

4. Limits

Population A is a test set; the probes are cross-fitted inside it and are diagnostics, not heads. A logistic probe is linear; a non-linear reader of the late-layer marker could in principle extract what a linear one cannot, but the shipped verifier is itself a small MLP over its inputs and the stacked read gives it the layer's linear score as one more input, which is the comparison the arm proposed. The label is the shipped head's argmax being a gold pair, the verifier's own target; a different target (is any pair in the ten right) was not read. Two seeds' labels over one set of states, since the trunk is the same.

5. On disk

scripts/probe_hlast_layers.py (new): harvests the say marker at every layer over Population A once (168 MB, reused), prints the per-layer curve, the shipped margin's AUC as the anchor, and the stacked-over-margin table, with both kill rules stated. evals/results/hlast_layers_s{1,2}.json.
