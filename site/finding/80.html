Fine-tuning the base · not yet committed

Contradicting a trained behaviour needs that behaviour's data replayed

The result, as the finding states it

The class installs every time, and what buys back the class it contradicts is that class's own slice

What it cost

0 s of new scoring on record; it reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (corn_jr_260m_v4). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

Entry 4 of docs/PLAN-FINETUNE-V4.md — a bare unmarked question after a lookup turn, the class the corpus has 0 of 849 of — trained four times on corn_jr_260m_v4 between 2026-08-30 and 2026-08-31, seven arms across six configurations. Every arm installed the class completely. Every arm was discarded. The rows they spent were different each time, and it took the last two arms to find out why — the first five moved two variables together and could not tell them apart.

arm 1 mix_v4_ft_4 unpaired payload turn2 0.070% 786 it 2 seeds arm 2 mix_v4_ft_4b matched pairs turn2 1.684% 1,212 it 2 seeds C mix_v4_ft_4c matched pairs turn2 1.436% 1,422 it 1 seed D mix_v4_ft_4d matched pairs turn2 1.196% 1,706 it 1 seed E mix_v4_ft_4e ellipsis pairs turn2 1.684% 1,200 it 1 seed F mix_v4_ft_4f unpaired payload turn2 1.684% 799 it 1 seed

F is the arm that answers it, and it is arm 1 with one thing changed.

1. The class installs totally, and finding 74's discount does not appear

harness/historygrid.py on the base against every arm, same settings, same before-cards read hours earlier on the same machine:

cell base every arm attribute after lookup_ok 2/8 8/8 attribute after lookup_fail 2/8 8/8 attribute after lookup_pack 0/8 8/8 arithmetic after a lookup 0/4 4/4 splice column, pack history 4/8 0/8 6b wrote-when-not-asked 4/4 0/4 the empty-room control 8/8 8/8 (unmoved)

Every re-issue column and every splice column zero. Finding 74 measured a 4x fine-tune discount on exactly this family and pre-registered it as the expected shape; it appeared on none of the four arms. Partial installation was the prediction. Total installation is the result, four times.

2. Copy discipline is never what a fine-tune of this base spends

tool.copy 120/120, tool.copy_wide 300/300, temporal.copy 353/353, refusal.say 270/270 and live searchgrid 400/400 on every checkpoint of every arm. Finding 75's "ceilings matched rather than spent" survives four more arms.

What gets spent is routing priors, and the clearest single number is arm 1's turn2.chain_pronoun failure mode moving from wrong_message: 10 on the base to wrong_tool: 13, wrong_message: 8 on the arm. The tool choice relocated; the copying did not.

3. The inverse class is recoverable, and matched pairs do it alone

Arm 1's payload was "turn 2 shares no content word with turn 1" at 50% of the arm, against a replay carrying turn2 at 13,731 tokens — 715:1 — and it took turn2.chain_ellipsis from 49/50 to 7/50.

Arm 2 rebuilt the payload as matched pairs: one first exchange, two transcripts, the four lines above them byte-identical, so the second turn is the only thing that can predict the behaviour. That is corn/turn2gen.py's own rule-5 construction and P3-E's matched share, which docs/PLAN-FINETUNE-V4.md's interference note had already named as the remedy for entries 2, 2b and 6c.

chain_ellipsis base 49/50 arm 1 7/50 arm 2 48-50/50 C 49/50 D 47/50 chain_pronoun base 40/50 arm 1 29/50 arm 2 39-40/50 C 38/50 D 39/50 chain_result base 50/50 arm 1 45/50 arm 2 50/50 C 50/50 D 50/50

This section said the matched pair holds the chain family on its own. That was wrong, and §8 is the arm that shows it. The chain rows do not move across a general-replay share from 29.6% to 50% — but every one of those arms also carried a 36x boost to the replay's turn2 slice, arriving at the same moment as the matched payload, and the sweep held that top-up fixed. Two explanations, never separated.

4. The prediction this finding first existed to record as false

After arm 2, docs/PLAN-FINETUNE-V4.md's regression-set section was amended to say the missing rule was a floor on the general replay. The reasoning: arm 1 held tool.name_fidelity at 112/120 with the general replay at 50% of the arm and tools_5b_balanced at 0.293% of it; arm 2 lost it to 108/120 at 29.6% and 0.174%. The controlling variable looked like a slice's fraction of the arm.

C and D were built to bisect that, with both endpoints already measured — D lands tools on 0.293%, arm 1's fraction to three figures.

tools % of arm 0.293 0.174 0.235 0.293 arm 1 arm 2 C D name_fidelity 112 108 106 106

It got worse, monotonically, as the replay grew. D sits at the identical slice fraction arm 1 held and reads six items below it. So the general replay share is not the knob for this row, and the amendment made after arm 2 is wrong in the quantity it named.

But the floor is real and §8 finds it in the right quantity. What matters is not the general replay's share, nor a slice's share in the abstract, but the share of the specific slice carrying the class the payload contradicts. This section measured tools, which carries nothing this payload contradicts. §8 measures turn2, which carries the class it does.

What the sweep held constant is what actually separates arm 1: the payload. Arm 1 trained ft4_secondturn.txt; arms 2, C and D trained ft4_matched.txt. The matched payload carries the chain_result twin — 31,051 transcripts whose second turn writes a save_note copying turn 1's answer — and tool.name_fidelity scores an argument value written into a telephony slot. Both are "put the right value in the slot". That is a mechanism worth testing with one variable (the matched payload minus its chain_result twin) and it is a hypothesis, not a result; nothing here demonstrates it.

5. What did behave as predicted, and one thing that fits nothing

configs/mix_v4_ft_4c.py pre-registered that react is payload-limited rather than replay-limited, on the evidence that arm 2's 24x increase in turn2 bought one item. It held:

turn2 % of arm 0.070 1.684 1.436 1.196 arm 1 arm 2 C D react 116 117 118 118 (base 120)

A 24x swing in the slice that teaches the class moves it two items and never reaches the ceiling. Every one of the 97,043 transcripts in ft4_matched.txt has a second turn that calls something; react is the class whose second turn calls nothing. The repair, if one is wanted, is a payload carrying no-call second turns — not a replay share.

The counter-evidence, recorded because it fits neither story: turn2.reask is also a no-call class and it went up — 40/96 on the base, 49 on arm 1, 55-62 on arm 2, 49 on C, 57 on D. Whatever separates reask from react under this payload is not named by anything above.

6. Two rules that collided, and one premise that is false

Ground rule 15 calls say, daycross and namecollide free invariance controls "because no payload arm touches their inputs". Ground rule 14 now asks every arm to choose its own replay, and these four chose mix_v4_5b's slices — which carry temporal and daylog, the two slices daycross is scored out of. daycross.with_day moved on every arm (118 base; 134, 113, 122, 138) and the movement tracks the replay share rather than anything the payload did.

So the premise under rule 15's invariance controls does not hold for a v4-slices replay, and configs/mix_v4_ft_4b.py pre-registered that before training rather than arguing it afterwards, which is the only reason daycross did not invalidate three separate reads. refusal.say is the one control that stayed genuinely free: 270/270 everywhere.

7. What this leaves

The vehicle question entry 4 was chosen to answer is answered, and more favourably than the entry expected: a fine-tune of this base installs a missing class completely, at ~16-36 minutes an arm, without spending copy discipline. What no arm has yet done is ship, because each spent a routing row through its payload's content — which is a harder thing to predict than a share and a more tractable one than a property of the vehicle, since content is chosen deliberately.

The regression set grew twice on this evidence: turn2.chain_ellipsis, turn2.chain_result and turn2.react after arm 1, and all three earned themselves back immediately — react is what discarded arm 2, and a set that had not just grown would have passed it.

Costs, for the next person budgeting: an arm is 964s at 786 iterations and 2,169s at 1,706, uncontended on the 3080 at ~23k tok/s. The first arm of this sequence read 6,963s because a guard sweep was running beside it, which also stretched that sweep from 417s to 3,447s. Neither number meant anything.

8. The separating arm, and the claim it overturns

Sections 3 and 4 were written from five arms in which the matched payload and a 36x boost to the replay's turn2 slice always arrived together. §3 credited the matched pair. Arm E is what exposed the confound: it pre-registered that turn2.chain_result must fall once its training class was removed from the payload, and chain_result read 49/50 with zero chain_result transcripts in the payload. A class that survives the deletion of its payload is being held up by something else.

F separates them. It is configs/mix_v4_ft_4.py to the token — same ft4_secondturn.txt, same 9,820,078-token payload weight, same 19,640,156-token budget — with turn2 and compound_v2 raised to the share every recovering arm had, the general replay absorbing the cost:

payload arm 1 unpaired F unpaired arm 2 matched turn2 % of arm 0.070 1.684 1.684 chain_ellipsis 7/50 45/50 48/50 chain_result 45/50 50/50 50/50 chain_pronoun 29/50 38/50 39/50 react 116/120 119/120 117/120

The boost is the whole effect: 7 → 45 of the 41 items available, with the payload byte-identical. The matched pair adds three more. So §3's claim that "matched pairs do it alone" is wrong, and the honest ordering is that the replay does nearly all of it and the matched pair is a refinement.

And the floor §4 declared non-existent is real, in a quantity §4 did not test. §4 swept tools, which carries nothing this payload contradicts, and found no floor. turn2 carries chain_ellipsis, chain_result and react — the classes this payload's "every second turn is unrelated and calls" directly contradicts — and boosting it recovers all three. The rule is per-class: when a payload teaches a class that contradicts a trained one, the contradicted class's own slice has to come back up in the replay. Not the general replay, and not any slice — that one.

tool.name_fidelity is then cleanly ruled out as a replay effect from the other direction: F sits at tools 0.274% and reads 114/120, while D at 0.293% reads 106. Same slice share, eight items apart, so what separates them is the payload. Ordered by payload: unpaired 112 and 114, ellipsis-only pairs 111, full matched pairs 106-108. The chain_result twin remains the best candidate and still has no clean test — arm E recovered it to 111 but changed the payload's class balance at the same time, which is a smaller version of the mistake this section exists to correct.

F is the best arm of the seven and still discards: chain_ellipsis 45/50 against a base of 49, four off its ceiling. Everything else is at or above base — name_fidelity 114 against 112, note_fidelity 108 against 99, react 119 against 120, reask 64 against 40, every copy row at ceiling. The direction is unambiguous and untested past this point: 24x on turn2 bought 38 items, and nothing here says what 50x buys.

What this costs the reader of §§3-4: two claims in this document were made from arms that could not support them, and both are corrected above rather than edited out. The pattern in both is the same — a variable moved alongside the one under test, and the arm was read as though it had not.
