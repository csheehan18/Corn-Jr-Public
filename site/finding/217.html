The reply judge · 24 October 2026

A judge built into the 5M learns, is ignored, and costs him

The result, as the finding states it

A reply judge built into the 5M from the first step — one block inside the model, taught on swapped replies with its gradient reaching every layer, its fit for every word added to the word scores at a weight the model learns — learns its lesson (0.90 and 0.89 of its training quads right at the end, from chance) and is switched off by the model it sits in: the learned weight falls from 1 to about 0.2 by iteration 750 and stays near zero from 1,500 on (0.007 at the end, both seeds). The lesson costs language at both seeds — narrative / web / conversational 0.5156 / 0.9862 / 0.6040 and 0.5141 / 0.9817 / 0.6009 against lines of 0.4913 / 0.9552 / 0.5629, the pre-registered row (d), as predicted — and replies far more: on the practice set 21 and 6 of 100 answer the message against the plain 5M's 31 and 33 (12 replies pass only under the arm, 49 only under the control), and its own beam reads 26 and 12 against the plain look-ahead's 39 and 39. Decoding with the weight forced to 0 changes 0 and 1 replies of 100: the damage is in the trunk, not the vote

What it cost

CornModel.judge_bias, JudgeExercise, the train hook, check_judge.py 8 (34 checks), four 1,000-iteration settings runs, the pre-registration 16:05-16:25 on 2026-10-24 (48f3fb0); the chain 16:25-16:45, 19.6 min (two runs 548 and 546 s, 1.0x the control's wall clock, contended at 40%, a bound); the blind hand read of 800 practice replies ~12 min; the cause probes 1 min. ETA ~2 h, actual ~1.3 h.

The question (owner, 2026-10-24: "Why can't the judge just be a layer in the model itself?" ... "Let's do 3 now"). Findings 214-216: a judge fitted beside a frozen 5M reads mood weakly and votes too softly; a frozen trunk holds only what next-word reading needed. Built in, its lesson could teach the trunk to keep fit, and the model could learn how much to use its vote (docs/PLAN-REPLY-JUDGE.md Phase 3, version 3; the config docstring configs/nano5m_v3_webblend_judge.py is the pre-registration).

One design fix before the runs (settings check, probe only — not on disk): with next-word loss reaching the judge, the 12,288 reading tokens a micro-batch turned it into a second word head and its lesson stayed at chance (0.50-0.57); the fit was detached from next-word loss so the judge learns only its lesson and reading trains only the weight. The check also showed the lesson's cost: 0.06 nats a token at 1,000 iterations, the same with the weight started at 0 — so the cost is the lesson's gradient in the trunk.

Read by hand

Seed 2's replies break: an opening loses its first word ("a great, I'm so happy to be able to help you out.", "'s a great bike."), numbers and punctuation run on ("will be up to 1000000...", "::::"), and 19 of 100 are loops or junk. Seed 1 keeps its sentences more often but fails the same way less. Where both arms pass they write the control's kind of reply ("Me too! I've been looking forward to it for weeks.", "What kind of bike?").

The cause

  1. The vote did nothing. Next-word reading turned the weight down to 0.007 by the end: on real conversation the next word nearly always belongs to a fitting reply, so a fit score rarely changes which word is likeliest, and reading pays nothing for it. The judge's job — breaking ties at a reply's opening among fluent candidates — is one reading does not reward. Forcing the weight to 0 changes 1 reply of 200.
  2. The lesson broke the trunk, and the shared word table is the likely channel. The judge scores a token by its state against the token's embedding, and the embedding table is also the output layer (tied). Its lesson therefore moves the output rows of the words it judges — reply openers most. Read against the same-seed control (same initial weights, same batches), the reply openers' rows moved furthest: cosine with the control's row 0.719 and 0.723 for the 30 commonest openers against 0.758 and 0.766 for everything else (probe only — not on disk). That is a hint, not a measurement of the channel; the opening words lost in seed 2's replies are what it predicts.

Corrected by finding 218 (2026-10-24). Cause 2's channel is withdrawn: with the lesson kept out of the word table at both ends the cost is the same to the third decimal, so the openers' drift was a symptom; the damage is the lesson's gradient in the blocks.

What it licenses

  • Version 3 as built is dropped: it costs language and replies at both seeds, and the model refuses the vote.
  • The probe the cause names: the same arm with the lesson kept out of the word table (the judge scores against a detached embedding, or a table of its own) — one variable, one seed first. If language and replies come back near the control, the shared table was the channel and the question of a trunk taught by the judge is open again; if not, the lesson's gradient in the trunk is the cost at any route.
  • The vote has to be set, not learned from reading. Reading never rewards it, so a built-in judge can only steer if its weight is fixed at decode — which is the fitted judge's picker again (findings 214-216).
  • The 5M's best decoder stays the look-ahead with a judge fitted beside the frozen model (findings 215-216).

What it does not settle. A lighter lesson weight; versions 1 and 2 of Phase 3; the lesson kept out of the word table; any size above 5M.
