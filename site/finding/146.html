The parse and depth · 26 September 2026

A runtime gap-filling rule breaks nothing but fixes little on real requests

The result, as the finding states it

The gap rule breaks nothing on requests it was not designed on and fixes almost nothing: every firing on a parse he had right comes back a connective, the few groups it opens at a non-carrier all land on parses already wrong, and natural requests rarely hold the flat carrier that made it worth 0.95 on the chains

What it cost

6.5 min of GPU for the whole read (19:39-19:46, about 95 s a checkpoint over 450 requests, 510-528 decodes each with the memo), no training; the set, the scorer and its CPU checks took a few minutes without the card.

PLAN-PARSE-WEIGHTS section 8.2, read 2026-09-26 — ROADMAP step X1. Pre-registered in scripts/probe_gap_misfire.py, committed at df45bf0 before a line was decoded, the bar set by the owner. 450 fresh requests from arm F's own generator at seed 20260930 (evals/funcworld_gapfire.jsonl), which nothing had read; 300 at the trained level are the gate, 150 at heldout_all are read. One greedy parse a request, the one-member wrapper unwrapped in both arms, and the rule of finding 145 applied to every group he wrote — gapc firing only on a gap that ends in a comma, gap2 on any gap beyond "and" and commas, each falling back to the flat member when the re-shown text opens no group. Scored on gold groups as sets of lookups, with and without the rule, on identical decodes (committed: evals/results/nano20m_v3_fw_*__gapfire.json).

The pre-registered read

On the 300 trained requests:

checkpoint right without gapc broken / fixed / fired gap2 broken / fixed / fired S s1 175 0 / 0 / 8 0 / 0 / 64 S s2 169 0 / 0 / 7 0 / 1 / 50 F s1 185 0 / 0 / 7 0 / 0 / 59 F s2 174 0 / 0 / 6 0 / 0 / 45

gapc breaks 0 of 300 at both arm S seeds against a bar of 6, and fixes as many as it breaks: it passes as registered, and so would gap2. On heldout_all each rule fixes one item on S seed 1 and breaks none anywhere.

What the lines say

On parses he had right, the rule cannot hurt. Across the four checkpoints gap2 fired 15 to 25 times on items that were already right, and every one of those re-shown views came back a connective — he wrote the re-shown text as a single lookup, and the member went back where it was. gapc barely fires there at all (0 or 1).

The groups it opens at a non-carrier all land on parses already wrong. Where his first line had moved members between groups, the gaps hold material that is not a connective, and re-shown they can open a group that is not in the gold — "and, separately," read as a carrier (train-014), "on top," as one (S s2 train-180). None of these is counted as a break because none of those items was right to begin with; the count of zero is honest about the rule and says nothing about how such a line should be repaired.

Most of what it opens, it cannot place. Three times (train-053, -192, S s1) the re-shown view recovers a group the gold really has — "question, A, B and C", "care to tell me A, B and C" — but his first line had swallowed that group into its neighbour, and the rule can only nest inside the group he wrote. It never splits a group into siblings, so the item stays wrong.

The fixes are the finding-145 case, on natural text. Two items become right: heldout_all-033 on S seed 1, where "the both of them," — a carrier held out of training — was written flat and, re-shown, opened; and train-280 on S seed 2, where "both" did the same. The mechanism works on a carrier nobody drilled. It simply has little to work on: the rule touched 8 to 12 of roughly 200 wrong parses under gapc and 37 to 44 under gap2, and the failures that fill the rest are other species.

What it does not say

That the rule is worth building for 1.0. It is safe and nearly inert on the requests this generator writes, which hold a nested group in 47 of 300 and a deep chain of carriers in none; its 0.95 on the depth probe (finding 145) is a property of long carrier chains, a shape no fresh request here has. Nano, the synthetic world, one greedy decode a request; the scorer is a hand-built instrument checked on gold (450 of 450) and on a stand-in model, not pinned — every number is a bound.

Licenses keeping the rule as a cheap, safe part of the windowed interpreter if that interpreter is built (PLAN-TAG-LANGUAGE rung 1), with gapc preferred because it fires about eight times less for the same result, and not spending more on depth until something shows real requests need it — which is ROADMAP decision D7's to weigh, not this finding's.
