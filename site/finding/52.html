Of 25 search phrasings taught, he kept about four

The result, as the finding states it

The corpus taught twenty-five phrasings and four of them carry the tool

What it cost

0 s of new scoring on record; it reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (corn_jr_260m). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

harness/searchgrid.py against corn_jr_260m, run 2026-08-24 on the 3080. 400 turns in 135s: 25 templates × 8 subject shapes × {invented, real}, against a table-backed instance at temperature 0 with decline offered. Nothing left the machine — routing and argument extraction both happen before the tool runs, which is why this grid needs no --search.

It started as a complaint about one turn. Talking to a live instance, "how tall is Mount Everest" came back decline while web_search sat in the declared set and the answer he had just given to "what can you do" promised lookups. Fifteen natural questions scored 4 of 15, and every one of the four opened "look up".

The obvious reading was wrong, and a longer list would have kept it

The reading was: he learned the imperative and not the question. It survives a list of any length, because a list of natural questions is a list of real-world subjects in whatever phrasings occurred to the person writing it. It does not survive a grid.

Grammatical class explains none of it. search for {q} names the tool in the imperative and scores 2 of 8; what's {q} only implies it and scores 5. have a look for {q} and see if you can find {q} are imperatives and score zero in both worlds. What the four winners share is a lookup verb, short, at the front — google, and three spellings of look up. Every phrasing that buries the verb behind a hedge is at or near the floor, and three of the twenty-five never route at all.

So the corpus offers twenty-five phrasings at roughly equal frequency and the weights kept about four of them. This is the twenty-eighth finding's phrasing effect, measured on the tool it matters most for and at a resolution no eval row has: evals/ scores a frozen prompt set, so it reads whatever phrasings were sampled into it and cannot see the distribution behind them.

Twenty-one of twenty-five down to one of twenty-five, across shapes that appear in FACTS 700–1,200 times each. Whatever this is, it is not frequency in the corpus, and no instrument in the project was looking at it.

The real/invented split is the smallest of the three, and it is the one that was predicted

Pooled: invented 87/200 (44%), real 53/200 (26%), a 17-point gap that holds in nearly every row of both tables.

This is the fiftieth finding one step earlier in the turn. That one showed a real entity reopens the weights when he speaks — handed answer: "Augusta Ada King" for look up Ada Lovelace he produced 700 characters of invented biography. This shows a real entity also makes him less likely to reach for the tool at all.

It matters more than 17 points sounds, because the two columns are not equally occupied. FACTS is 8,697 invented entries, so every search row the project has ever scored sits in the left column. The right column is where every question a user actually asks lands, and until this grid nothing measured it.

Two things that are working, and are worth recording as such

The argument is right whenever the call is made: kept_subject 140/140. Every web_search he emitted across 400 turns carried the subject that was asked about. He reshapes the span — look up Ada Lovelace produced ada-lovelace, a URL slug — and the subject survives it. The copy slot is not the problem; routing is the whole problem.

The misses are refusals, not wrong actions: decline 258, place_call 2. On this axis he declines rather than misroutes, which is the safe failure.

decline is the symptom and not the cause

The fifty-first finding's routed 6/16 → 11/16 under --no-decline invites the reading that withholding the escape fixes the routing. On this class it does not. Six real-subject questions, what's {q}:

decline offered web_search 1/6 decline, place_call, web_search, decline, decline, decline decline withheld web_search 1/6 save_note, place_call, place_call, place_call, save_note, web_search

Identical, and withholding it converts five refusals into five wrong tools, three of which write to disk. Live, the same substitution turned "read back what happened today" into a save_note that saved the question itself. The escape is not what is stopping him reaching web_search; it is where he goes because he is not reaching it.

What this cannot say

Nothing about the answer half. The grid stops at the call. Whether he reads a real result back faithfully is the fiftieth finding's subject and needs --search, and it is the harder half — a real answer competes with the weights in exactly the regime this finding shows also degrades routing.

Nothing about why a shape is hard. the river that runs through … scores 1/25 and the length of the … scores 21/25 at comparable corpus frequency. The grid measures the effect and does not explain it.

One checkpoint, one seed. The invented subject per shape is drawn seeded from FACTS; a different --seed draws a different grid, and nothing here establishes the spread across draws.

What follows

The corpus does not need more search transcripts — it needs the ones it has to stop being concentrated in four phrasings and eight shapes, and it needs a share of subjects the model has priors about, which every slice in the project has deliberately avoided since the thirteenth finding. That avoidance was right for the reason it was adopted, and this is its bill: a search index too small to memorise also taught him that lookups are about things that do not exist.

The grid is the instrument for reading whether the next run fixed it, and its invented column is the control that says whether a bad number is a bad checkpoint or a hard cell.
