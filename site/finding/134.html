He learns to write a summary line of conversation state

The result, as the finding states it

He learns to write the state line at nano, sixty-seven and a hundred and one of a hundred and twenty from a floor of one, a value comes from whichever slice carries it, a line merely present moves the repeat on the cells trained on it and not on the others, the line he writes reads as a hand-inserted one on the cell that writes it whole, and the first scoring read zero because the prompt ended on a space token the corpus never wrote

What it cost

0 s of new scoring on record; it reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (nano20m_v3_turn2). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

PLAN-TAG-SPINE phase D, the conversational half, read 2026-09-14. The 2x2 at nano pre-registered in configs/mix_v3_turn2_state.py: the state line on or off (corn/stateline.py's rule over turn2_v1.txt, 89,974 lines, 84% answered and 16% open) crossed with the dependent turn on or off (corn/socialgen.py's dependent kind, 280 pairs, 0.066% of the mix and under finding 35's floor, which the config says in advance). One seed a cell, 5,513 iterations, compile=True in every cell, the base cell retrained compiled so the four are mode-matched (EXPERIMENTS §2's eager caveat is retired by it). mix_v3 byte-identical across the four — the first 130,279,819 tokens of every train.bin are the same bytes and only the turn-2 block differs. Floors on the shipped trunk and the eager nano base. Full cards; the repeat probe on every cell under three conditions; one post hoc read, labelled.

The question

Finding 131's A2: a state: asked line hand-inserted above the second turn of a transcript he never trained on halves the repeat under the route header (40 to 19 of 192) on the shipped weights. So the record is read when present. Does he learn to write it, and does the record he writes move the repeat? And beside it, whether the class the repeat is made of — the person answers what he asked and hands back — moves anything at a dose the config expected to read as a direction at best.

What was caught before it could count

The first scoring of the state-on cell read state.value 0 of 120, on a line the cell had seen ninety thousand times, and 100 of the 120 answers were {. The eval prompt ends on state: and the corpus line is state: answered; bpe8k writes that as st ate : and the one token answered, so a prompt ending on the space encodes a lone token (id 32) that no state line in the corpus ever produced after the colon, and the likeliest thing after a bare space in this corpus is a JSON brace. The probe's --state written path ended its prompt the same way and read the same junk. Both now end on state:; the value he writes carries its own leading space and strip removes it; the cards below are re-scored. The lesson is finding 130's at the tokenizer: a prompt that is one token off the corpus is a prompt about a different corpus, and a zero on a trained row is the first thing to distrust.

Three readings, in order of how much they can bear.

A line merely present moves the repeat on the cells trained on it and not on the others. Hand-inserted, it takes the state cells down 17 and 21 (83 to 66, 99 to 78), the base down 8, the dependent cell up 5. On the shipped weights it took 31 off; what the training buys is not a larger move but a smaller one that is now his own vocabulary.

The record he writes reads as the hand-inserted one, on the cell that writes it whole. The both cell writes a legal value on every draw (asked 241, answered 142) and reads 80 under its own record against 78 under ours and 99 under none. The state-only cell writes a legal value on 38 of 192 bare draws and reads 25 there against 52 with no line; under the route header, where its corpus never put a state line after a social say, it writes a tool declaration and the second turn refuses 116 of 384 times where the base refuses 99 — the 1 of 192 on that half is a degraded turn and not a read record. So the pre-registered first row — he writes the record and reads it — holds within each state cell and does not take either below the base cell's own 74, because the on-cells' no-line rates sit above it (83, 99 against 74) at one seed.

The dependent class did not carry the repeat down at this dose, and it moved other things hard. Its cells read 97 and 99 with no line against the state-only cell's 83, with a route/bare split of 93 to 4 on one card that the next section explains. What 280 pairs at 0.066% did move is the social band: social.declared_nocall 13 and 10 on the cells without them, 69 and 70 on the cells with them; social.voice 65 and 52 against 94 and 105; social.family 26 and 11 against 46 and 52. The mix_v3 nano carries no other social payload, and a class with nothing competing installs from under the floor — finding 35's floor was measured against a slice that had rivals.

The kill clause did not fire cleanly and did not clear. turn2.reask_yn falls from 40 to 17, 15 and 14 on every on-cell — including the dependent cell, whose turn-2 bytes are the base's own — so the fall is not the state line's; on those cells he answers a yes/no re-ask without the polarity (no_polarity 20, 23, 8), which is what a payload of conversational second turns would teach a cell that has no other. turn2.reask under the plain items falls 43 to 29 and 13 on the state cells; under the post hoc read that renders state: answered after every earlier say — the shape their corpus gave every say and the one corn/agent.py renders back when the words were his (--state-history, stamped on the card) — it reads 37 and 24 against the base's 38. Half the state-only cell's fall is the scorer showing it a say without its line; the both cell's remainder is shared with the dependent cell (25). One seed a cell, on rows whose eager spread is 9.

What this licenses

D2 stays as built: the runtime reads the line back, because a line present moves the cells trained on it. The awareness claim is a direction and not a result — the pre-registered second seed on the both cell and on the base is what turns it into one, and the read to beat is stated: the both cell under its own record below the base cell's no-line 74 at both seeds, with turn2.reask_yn inside the eager band. The asked value needs a source in the 1.0 corpus, and the dependent payload is one at 560 lines. The open value is the rule's remainder and reads weakest; it is the first candidate for a definition rather than a default. The dependent class goes to the social fine-tune at dose, where it was always going, with the note that under the floor and unrivalled it moved four social rows by 30 to 60 items.

Instruments

  • The trailing-space token, above: caught because a trained cell read zero on its own line, fixed at both prompt sites, the cards re-scored.
  • --state-history in corn/evals.py: one regex over the turn-2 family prompts, stamped on the card, a post hoc read and labelled one.
  • The repeat ruler is lexical by design (three quarters of the shorter question's words, two at least): "How are you?" repeats "how are you today", "How about you?" does not, and the dependent cell's 93 to 4 route/bare split is that phrasing choice under two headers. Read with it a count of any question back at all: base 60/58 of 192, state 48/93, dependent 114/62, both 78/98 — the same directions, wider.
  • Four cells at 944-959 s each; probes at 22 s a card once the card was uncontended; the state-line smoke run before the cells.
  • The four hand-inserted probes ran beside the first minutes of the mid-size W run's compile; no timing number was read from either.

Limits

One seed a cell, so directions (rule 6); the base cell moved off the eager band on one row. Nano, on a mix with no social payload but the dependent one, which is why 280 pairs read like a slice. The written-record read puts a hand-written say in front of him and asks for a record of it; the runtime's condition is a say he wrote. The given arm's six cases are wellbeing openers, the family the defect was reported on and no other. The held-out say row is hand-written and one of its 60 says ("Any time.") is also a mined social reply, found by check_social.py on the sweep; the drawn row's says are corpus strings by construction (59 of 120 are mined replies), which the guard now reads past for that file's say and prompt fields alone. Nothing here touches the shipped checkpoint.
