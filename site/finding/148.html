Picking a template by letter never installed; one seed in three learned

The result, as the finding states it

Rung 3's letter-first pick never installed, not even on the pairings he trained on, at one dose or four; written evidence first, one seed of three reads the wish — held-out 113 and 97 of 288 against a gate of more than 95, and swapping the request's soft term moves the pick onto the new wish's item — while the other two sit at chance, so rung 3 reads seeds split, a direction and not section 7's kill

What it cost

about 3.4 h of GPU — 2.8 h training nine nano runs (17.6-20.4 min each, card_cost.py on the copy cards), about 36 min of prepare_mix, probe and copy cards inside the five chains (10:15-11:08, 12:03-12:49, 13:20-14:03, 15:42-16:24, 19:01-19:21 on 2026-10-02, each under its registered ETA), and a few minutes a checkpoint of scratch probes no card sees (trained-trace scoring, the forced letter, the wish-swaps). Amended 2026-10-03 with arm V: 55.4 min of training three seeds (card_cost.py on the copy-wide cards) against a registered hour, prepare_mix, probe and copy cards untimed, and about 12 min of hand-read probes on 2026-10-03 (probe_filter_traces.py on six checkpoints, probe_filter_split.py on nine).

By the letter, arm T lands on the pre-registered kill row — 95 or fewer at both seeds with the hard half passing. Arm F, never shown a trace, reads under both: the rows do not leak.

Why, as a hypothesis the next arm was built to test. In pick: C because[...] the whole judgment sits on one letter, behind a two-hop lookup (find the fitting description, then the id written before it in the JSON), and everything after the letter can be copied from that letter's own text — so nothing in the training text pushes the soft term toward its description; one pointer token a trace carries the gradient (finding 145's shape: the wall was the carrier word). A letter-free check was tried first and was weak: forcing a neutral letter and asking which description he wants to write after it, he copies the forced letter's own text on 126 of 144 (probe only), so it measures the lookup again and says nothing about the judgment.

It did not. The habit moved seeds instead of leaving: seed 1337 lost it (83 of 288 first) and still reads chance (48, 83, habit-free 82); seed 2337 gained it (147 of 288) and reads trained 70, held-out 97 — two over the gate — with the habit raced out at 91, just under that column's bar. Registered: seeds split again. The third seed reads chance on every column (47, 69, habit-free 69). Description-first installs on one seed of three at nano.

Read beside it: the held-out band near the gate is crowded by things that never trained the order — T4 seed 2337, which only ever wrote the letter first, scores 94 of 288 on the ev line and 92 habit-free — so a reading of 97 is not distinguishable from noise by the count alone. What separates seed 2337 is the swap.

Swap the wish and the pick follows it — on seed 2337 only

scripts/probe_filter_swap.py (probe only, not yet guarded): on every soft row, rewrite the soft term in the request and the goal: line to the phrasing of a shown ok distractor's concept (the first in list order whose concept does not clash with the gold's), keep the items byte for byte, and score every ok line again. A reader of the request moves onto the new wish's item and off the old gold; a reader of the shown set does not move.

checkpoint held-out old gold the new wish's item E 2337 113 -> 76 63 -> 93 E2 2337 97 -> 62 115 -> 169 E2 1337 83 -> 79 77 -> 81 E2 3337 69 -> 70 93 -> 95

(The new wish's item is often the first ok item, so a seed with the habit already favours it unswapped — E2 2337's 115; the move is what counts.) Arm F seed 1337, never shown a trace and scored in the evidence-first order, barely moves: held-out gold 80 -> 72 and the new wish's item 40 -> 46, trained 35 -> 37 and 25 -> 25 (probe only — read off the session's console, not on disk). Seed 2337 reads the wish, not the shown set, in both arms; the other seeds read neither.

An instrument hole, found by the rung-4 builder and owed here. The decoy rule that keeps a held-out answer from being the only stranger in its list draws each distractor from a family fixed by the phrasing — decoy_families: family (p + c' + 1) mod N for each other concept c', against the gold's (p + c) mod N (corn/filtergen.py). So on every held-out row the gold is the one shown item whose family less its concept, mod N, is one below every other item's: the answer is derivable from the set of shown description families by arithmetic, without reading the request, and none of the four floor predictors tested that. Rung 4 found the same shape in its own rows (a shown-clause set alone scored 0.984) and closed it there with one Latin square before arm US trained. The swap shows seed 2337 is not using the shortcut — it holds the items fixed and the pick still follows the wish — but the eval owes a set-only floor before any other checkpoint's held-out number on these rows is read as judgment.

A hypothesis, not tested

In rung 4's passing arm (arm US, finding 149) the clause he matches sits on the same line as the id he writes after it. Here the description is the last field of a JSON object, behind "price", "km" and "rating", away from its "id". A runtime view that puts each item on its own line with the description beside the id — item 1: <about> (price, km, rating) — may be what rung 3 needs to install on more than one seed. Nothing here measures it.

What it licenses

The owner's decisions of 2026-10-02, taken on this read:

  • Description first is the format. A pick is written because[<span>] then the id, here and in rung 4; the SPEC-LANGUAGE §6 amendment (and the bind's) follows this finding.
  • The hard-only pick is the runtime's. Without a soft term he writes no pick:; E2 showed removing it costs nothing and buys nothing on its own.
  • Rung 3 is "seeds split, unreadable — a direction" by both E and E2's pre-registrations, now one seed of three. It is not §7's kill: the kill row needs the trained cell installed and the held-out cell not, and on the letter-first arms neither installed. G3 is read.

What it does not say

That judgment does not train at nano — one seed of three does read the wish, on held-out combinations, and moves with it. That it does: two seeds of three sit at chance on every column, and the third seed was not pre-registered. That the letter-first null was the format alone: E's order and E2's missing hard picks each changed which seed read, not how many. That the held-out numbers on these rows mean judgment for any checkpoint the swap was not run on, until a set-only floor is computed. That dose is closed: T4 is dose by repetition, not by fresh traces. Nano, the synthetic world, one draw of rows.

Three things follow, each a bound (rule 28: both probes are unpinned beyond the rows check_filter.py holds).

  • V's null is not the instrument. On the span column, where the judgment is, every V seed sits at chance and the swap moves none by more than 3. The readers are the seeds this finding already named: E2 2337's span falls 98 -> 53 and the new wish's rises 91 -> 153; E 2337's 119 -> 76 and 68 -> 102.
  • The card was reading the description all along. whole tracks span within six on every checkpoint, so the held-out counts above and in the tables before this amendment measure which description he chooses.
  • The letter after the description never installed — on any arm, any seed, or layout. Given the right description already written, he picks its letter at chance everywhere (69-83 of 288 against 80), the reading seeds included, and V included, where that letter opens the description's own line a few tokens above. What he writes there is a position: the first ok letter on 177 of 288 at E2 2337, and on 210 at arm F seed 2337, which never saw a pick line. So the first-ok habit this finding reports lives mostly in the letter (E2 2337's span is first on 112). Of the two hops the letter-first section named — find the fitting description, then the id before it — the second is the one that never formed, at nano, in every format tried.

The free decodes, every held-out soft row, every seed (a second hand read, 2026-10-03, after eight of seed 2337's rows; from the committed __filter_full_items.jsonl, CPU): each greedy pick: line classified by whether its description is a shown item's and whether the letter after it is that item's own.

The letter after his own description is its own on 23-33% — chance at three to five shown items — on every checkpoint, so the split's id column was not a teacher-forcing artefact. Two failures the counts above never showed, both of which the §9 bind would refuse: a description that is on no shown item (5-48 rows; seed 3337 writes "a cafe with oak beams from the 1600s" and "a cider house in a medieval building", neither shown — and the seeds that read the wish invent most, 48 and 42), and a description of an item check: already marked unmet (22-39). The greedy line's gold-description count sits under the scored span column (E2 2337 67 against 98): the decode is noisier than the ranking.

What it licenses — a proposal, the owner's to take. The description he writes identifies exactly one shown item on every soft row (432 of 432, no duplicate, no prefix of a second item), so the letter can be the runtime's. SPEC-LANGUAGE §9 already specifies a bind for D-A1.18's order — the id limited to the item the span was copied from — and lists it owed; this says the bind is not a safety net here but the only thing that would write the right letter. The other route is that the runtime resolves the pick from the span by exact match and his letter is dropped from the line, which is a §6 change to D-A1.18. Neither is made here. Rung 4's passing arm writes because[clause] then the template id the same way, and its id is not read either: finding 149's amendment (arm USN's hand read) finds it its clause's own on about one in three at two to four templates.

What the amendment does not say. That the lookup cannot train: no arm targeted it, and the dose was never raised. That the layout is irrelevant at 260M. Why seed 2337 lost the wish when the view was added — one seed, read once. Nano, the synthetic world, one draw of rows.
