A space after the bracket shifts his default to the first option

The result, as the finding states it

A space after the menu's bracket, so the option is written in the request's own tokens, moves his default from a taste in place phrases to the first option listed (79% of picks) and leaves the binding exactly as unformed (209 of 400 on his own training traces); the phrase ranking was the spelling cost of a choice he had not learned

What it cost

19.3 min of training one nano run (1,157 s, contended — the card at 30-40%, a bound), two reads at 15-18 s, one copy card 8 s, the control read 15 s, the free-decode hand read 7 s and the own-trace read ~7 s on the card; the build 14:02-14:07 on 2026-10-14, the chain 14:07-14:27, the read 14:27-14:28.

The owner's question after finding 173. The flip-word run's free decode ranked every place phrase opening on "a" (0.43-0.89 when offered) above every one opening on "somewhere" (0.26-0.41), and how often each was offered or was the gold explained none of it. The tokens did: glued to the menu's bracket, next[somewhere to eat] spells the phrase [ s ome where — pieces the request never contains, where it reads some where — and next[a spot to eat] spells [ a. With a space after the bracket the option's tokens are the request's exactly. The owner's suggestion: test it now. Pre-registered in configs/mix_v3_funcworld_branchflip_spaced.py's docstring at 3a9b495 before a byte trained; one change from finding 173's arm: the space (branchflipgen.FlipBranch.spaced; check_branch.py section 9 holds the payload and rows byte for byte but for it). One seed, as finding 173. The gate fails. The registered outcome is the row "gate fails, ranking flattens — the instrument", with the flattening only half met: the prediction said no phrase above 0.65 when offered, and the top phrase is at 0.77.

Finding 169's rows are not readable on this arm. Read by hand — the free decode first

hand_flip.py (scratch, probe only — not on disk), 16 quads on the flip rows, every prompt, each line beside its request and result. He writes the continuation the request names first. Row 054: "a place for drinks" on both results while drinks is listed first, then "a place to sleep" on both once the action twin lists it first; rows 126, 130 and 149 the same. The tally follows from that. On plain families, where the first-named continuation is the one the condition selects, he is right on 10 of 10 prompts when the condition holds and 2 of 10 when it does not. On negated families, where the first-named is the one taken when it does not hold, he is right on 1 of 6 and 4 of 6. Over all 432 quads (the full-option card):

run first option picked one place on all four phrase pick rate (when offered) flip-word (finding 173) 583 of 1,728 (0.34) 276 of 432 0.26 - 0.89 flip-word, spaced 1,373 of 1,728 (0.79) 199 of 432 0.34 - 0.77

The default moved; the decision did not form. With the bracket glued, the cheapest line to write was the one with the cheapest spelling, so the "a" phrases won. With the space every phrase costs what it costs in the request, and the cheapest line became the one named first. Neither tracks the result: the pick follows it on 9-19 of 288 twin pairs a cell, the control's 7-20.

On its own training traces

400 payload traces from the front of the file (own_traces.py, scratch, probe only — not on disk): 209 right, median margin +0.07 nats (finding 173's unspaced run: 202, +0.01; finding 169's installing seed: 367, +2.27). Nothing formed where it trained, with or without the space.

The copy row, a side column: tool.copy_wide 290 of 300, inside the 20M family's spread (finding 170).

What it licenses

  • The phrase ranking was an instrument of the format, not a cause of the failure. The spelling cost decided which option an unlearned choice fell on. It did not stop the binding forming — removing it changed only where the choice fell. Finding 173's cause stands: the harder conjunction does not form at one pass and 2.2% of the mix.
  • The space is still the better format. A bracketed copy whose tokens differ from its source's (next[, soft[, because[, relax[ in SPEC-LANGUAGE) makes some words cost more to write than others for no reason, and a default that falls on spelling is harder to read than one that falls on position. It is a D7 question, with this finding as its evidence, and not a lever for learning.
  • The two levers from finding 173 remain: the binding written by him beside the request (the hop in the bytes), or more fresh evidence.

What it does not settle. Whether the space helps where the copy is the decision (because[...] copying an item's words), which a format arm on rung 3's payload would read; and why position became the default rather than nothing.
