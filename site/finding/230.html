Kept far lines add up from afar, not for the next piece

The result, as the finding states it

100 of finding 229's pairs stepped from afar one after another and kept — the far cost held against the neighbour swapped out, two nats each, in the two words' own word-table rows — stay stepped (0.99 at both seeds) and add up on their own read: asked from afar, the word comes 0.44 and 0.46 nats down at the pair's other meeting against a swapped meeting whose stand-in the hold never saw, a direction against a bar of 0.5, and the first 50 hold theirs with 50 more on top (+0.35 and +0.33 after 50, +0.38 and +0.34 after 100). Asked the way he is asked, as the next piece, the same lines are worth +0.04 and +0.07: P fails. The held-out text costs 0.041 and 0.042 nats a token more, between, and 18% and 21% of the pairs never stepped come out a quarter nat harder, between: less than finding 226's churn and not none. A second hold on fresh web text each step cuts the damage by 18% and 5% and the scrambling not at all. At half the strength the damage is little (0.008 at both seeds) and the scrambling 12% and 9%, for half the far pair (+0.19 and +0.22). Finding 226's next-piece step kept on the same pairs reads +0.09 and +0.20 as the next piece and +0.12 and +0.18 from afar at 0.121 and 0.031 nats a token.

What it licenses

By the pre-registration, its second outcome with the third beside it: the lines add up on the far shelf and do not reach the question he is asked, and shared pieces still scramble — less, not none.

  • A far line is a far line. One step or a hundred, in every weight or in the word table, what is written from afar is read from afar. Findings 229 and 230 together close the simple form of the distance idea: asking from further back does not teach the next-piece question.
  • Nothing washes out and nothing compounds. The first 50 pairs keep their far read with 50 more on top; the per-pair gain is the same at 50 and at 100; the damage adds up a little faster than the steps.
  • Gentle is cheaper than hard, again. Half the strength: half the far pair, a fifth of the damage, half the scrambling (finding 226 saw the same). If far lines are ever worth writing, they are worth writing small and often — which is a training run, not a press.
  • The scrambling is the place, not the question. Far or next-piece, a fifth of the pairs nobody touched move a quarter nat. Finding 226's licence stands: a private place has to be a row a word.

What it does not settle. Whether a far read is worth having for its own sake: the book answers the ruler from afar, and a net that held far lines could be asked from afar by the runtime rather than through a sentence — nothing here read the ruler that way. A trunk trained with both questions from the first step, where the two stores might not be two. Pairs met often.
