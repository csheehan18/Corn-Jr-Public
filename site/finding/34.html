Routing and declining · 17 August 2026

He picks tools he never saw in training, but can't say no

The result, as the finding states it

He routes to tools he was never trained on, and cannot decline

What it cost

0 s of new scoring on record; it reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (nano20m_v1). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

The thirty-third finding showed the answer half of a tool call surviving a tool the model has never seen: an unseen name in <|call|> costs 3 of 300. The choice half was untestable, because the runtime prompt is <|user|>{text} and nothing else — no declaration reaches the model, so it cannot fail to route to a tool it was never told about. corn/toolspace.py puts one there: a plain-text tools: line above <|user|>, no new marker, because bpe8k.json ships seven specials and an eighth would make every checkpoint in the project incomparable.

Three arms, 29.6M, two seeds each, scripts/score_toolspace.py. The floor is measured rather than assumed: nano20m_v1 has never seen a declaration and reads 0/161 at every level.

modifier is the question and the wide arm answers it: 206 and 203, against its own in-distribution control at 207 and 213. Held-out equals trained, within two items on one seed and ten on the other. Corn-Jr calls a tool declared only at inference at the same rate as one he was trained on, and the name being novel costs nothing.

The narrow arm read zero, and it is the control rather than a failure

mix_v1_toolspace reads 0/326 and 0/329 on both held-out levels. Across 1,310 held-out items the emitted name appeared in the declaration zero times, the verb was right 78% and the noun 84%, and a trained modifier was substituted every time: find_meadow_harvest_route came back as find_morning_route. That is not a model limit, it is the corpus. The narrow slice gives 38,395 names at 17.3 uses each — which the design targeted — and builds them from 24 verbs, 16 nouns and 22 modifiers at 27,000–48,000 uses each, which it did not.

Name diversity is not token diversity. The model learned sixty-two words and composed from them, and never had to copy an unfamiliar string because no name it ever saw contained one. The wide arm replaces the modifier with an invented word from _invent_facts's prefix-free pool, partitioned 18,000 trained / 4,500 held out so the two share a shape distribution and differ only in which strings they hold — 162,027 names at 1.0 uses each, modifiers at 58.7 against 48,088. Nothing else moves: same domains, verbs, values, frames, payload shape, share, and total_tokens at mix_v1's 130,279,711 in all three arms.

So this is the sayvar result a second time. A row at exactly zero across seeds and checkpoints, looking like a capability limit, moved by changing one file. It is now the third instance of one mechanism — refusal.clarify at 2,096 uses per sentence, this slice's parameter value at 2,130, and its name tokens at 48,088 — and the generalisation is that the thing the model must reproduce needs cardinality, and counting the compositions hides that the parts have none.

What it reads is the name, not the description

domain stays at 1, 8, 0 and 11. That level moves the noun, the parameter, the value pool and the utterance frames at once, so its failure has four candidate causes and this arm separates none of them — it is not clean evidence that he cannot read a description. What can be said is narrower and is what the modifier result already says: he identifies which declared tool to use through vocabulary the corpus already contains, and copies its name faithfully once identified. At modifier level a quarter of the losses pick the wrong declared tool rather than mistyping the right one, and median similarity between emission and target is 0.56, so the residue is selection rather than transcription.

He cannot decline, and three corpus fixes did not touch it

absent — items where the asked-for tool is deliberately not declared — reads 0 in all twelve cells of the first two arms. Diagnosis found three genuine faults in how the slice asked: the refusal was one sentence at ~36,000 uses, absent declared 2.51 tools against route's 3.49 so the classes were separable by counting, and 18% is a share at which always-calling is right 82% of the time. All three were fixed — 128 composed refusals at 163 uses each, 3.51 declared on both sides with matching per-count distributions, 34.7% absent.

It moved absent from 0/142 to 1/276 and 5/276, and left routing unharmed at 61–64%. That is not a repair, and the reason is a mechanism none of the three fixes addressed: on absent items he does not pick the nearest declared tool, he invents a name that is in no declaration at all — 216 of 276 and 190 of 276, against 59 and 81 that picked a declared one. There is no membership test to strengthen. The behaviour is generate-a-name-for-the-request, biased toward the declaration when it happens to contain a match, and raising the price of a wrong call cannot reach a decision the model never makes.

What this means for the tool layer, and one thing it does not

VISION §6 says tools are cheap to add and means it about REGISTRY. Measured against the model, it comes apart into three pieces, of which two now have numbers:

  • Read-back on a new tool is free — the thirty-third finding, +0 across six checkpoints.
  • Routing to a new tool works at the same rate as a trained one, provided the request maps to it through familiar vocabulary and the corpus taught name-copying rather than name-recall.
  • Declining does not work at all, and is the gap a runtime has to close elsewhere. corn/grammar.py already constrains the emitted name to REGISTRY; constraining it to the declared set turns 78% of these failures into either a declared tool or no call. That is §6's schema-constrained decoding doing what it was specified for, needs no training run, and still does not produce a refusal — it prevents a call to a tool that does not exist.

What this does not settle

  • Two seeds per arm, and absent is a row whose floor is zero by construction — it cannot fall further, so the effect's existence needs no seed argument and its size has no interval.
  • Utterance variety is 274 across the whole slice, and is not an axis any arm varies. Nothing here speaks to routing under paraphrase, which is the twenty-eighth finding's question.
  • The value row proves nothing and was built not to. 77 strings at ~2,130 uses each, declared below floor in scripts/check_copyable.py; it reads perfect whenever the name is right, in every cell of every arm, which is what a recitable slot does.
  • Arms one and two are pinned to git, not to the file. The refusal fixes changed what generate emits for every vocabulary, so their corpora regenerate only at ec2b954 and its parent. vocab stayed switchable because it is an axis under study; the refusal design was not an axis, it was a bug.
