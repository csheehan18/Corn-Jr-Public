When the runtime orders the menu, 'the other one' is perfect

The result, as the finding states it

With the runtime writing the menu in his line's order, branch is his pick and both halves excel: "the other one" reads 864 of 864 at all three starts, his own quads 141-144 of 144 on every cell, his line 0.992-0.998 — RULES 21 untouched; and every fine-tune from arm E2's second start pays the copy row in one species, a thousands figure's leading 4 written as 3

What it cost

three fine-tunes of 650 iterations at 20M (143 s each, contended — the card at 39-42%, a bound), three three-way reads at 87-88 s, three slot reads and copy cards at 7-8 s, the real-wording probe 18 s: one chain of 754.5 s, 20:15-20:28 on 2026-10-18, registered ~15 min; the copy hand read 26.6 s; the build 20:11-20:15 on CPU.

The owner's pick after finding 179: "try A2 first: the runtime lists his line's continuation first, so the pick stays his." Finding 179's A1 had shown the line excellent and the complement unformed: from a right one-slot line, on an unmet condition, his trained pick read 430 / 658 / 655 of 864 against 0.90. Pre-registered in configs/mix_v3_bind_ft_order.py at cccdf6d before a byte trained. One change from A1: the payload's menu order — the runtime, which holds his line, writes options: with the continuation his line names first and the other second (branchflipgen.FlipBranch.line_order, gen_funcworld_branchflip.py --line-order). Finding 175's payload byte for byte but for 3,705 options: lines of 7,424; the pick becomes met: first, unmet: second. The same two passes, replay, 650 iterations, pick under loss and arm E2's three starts. check_branch.py section 15 holds it (a mutant that points the mix at the request-order payload fails it).

The read is probe_branch_bind.py --line-order on evals/funcworld_branchflip_bind_order.jsonl: in own and forged the menu is rebuilt from the line actually spliced in, his or the forged one, as the runtime builds it in serving — checked on CPU over all 1,728 prompts before any read (a gold line leaves the prompt unchanged; a forged line puts its continuation first and the gold option keeps its text).

Read by hand

The three-way probe prints his line beside the gold on four quads a run: every shown line exact ("provided it isn't snowing", "except if it's foggy"), every own pick right on both results. Own quads equal the runtime-resolved quads on every cell of every run (probe_bind_resolve.py): his pick adds no error of its own, and every miss is a wrong line — 14, 10 and 4 of 1,728, each naming the same continuation on the request's action twin, the plain families as often as the flip ones. A forged line is followed 576 of 576: the pick reads the result and the runtime's order, which is the design; whether the line is right is the line's job.

The real-wording probe (finding 179's 29 hand-written shapes) reads the line here exactly as there — pairs right pos 8-12 of 26, neg 14-18 of 20, unless 9-12 of 12, the same ten-odd shapes failing. The menu order changes the pick, not the line, and finding 179's limit stands: new words in the eight trained clause structures transfer, new structures do not.

The copy row, read by hand

tool.copy_wide 298, 248 and 296 against the starts' 298, 295, 293 — "not readable" by finding 170's rule. But the second start's fine-tunes read 253 (A1), 249 (B2) and 248 (A2), and no other start falls. Greedy-decoded with the row's own scorer (scratch, probe only — not on disk): the start misses 5 of 300 and the A2 fine-tune 52, and of its first 40 misses 23 write a thousands figure's leading 4 as 3 — "4,086 degrees Celsius" said "3,086", "4,717 metres at its deepest" said "3,717" — and 8 cut a three-decimal figure to two ("up to 2.031 metres" said "2.03"). The start makes neither. It is the digit species findings 168 and 169 read, now one start's in three arms; the branch payload carries no such figure, so the cause is the fine-tune at that start (its replay or its optimiser path), not the payload's content. Not read further here.

What it licenses

  • Branch installs as his pick, by fine-tune, at three starts of three, with every number at or near ceiling: the binding line (his reading of the flip words, 0.99+) and the move (the pick from the result, 864 of 864). The runtime writes the menu in his line's order — a line it already holds — and computes nothing of the decision. RULES 21 is never touched: the pick is his and trained, the menu the runtime's and never in his loss as his.
  • Two routes now stand for the owner, both rule-clean: this (A2, the pick his) and finding 179's B2 (the runtime resolving the move). They read the same line; A2 keeps the move his at no measured cost.
  • The binding read's limit is unchanged (finding 179): the clause structures, not the words. A 1.0 branch slice needs many structures before either route reads a person's ninth way of building the sentence.
  • The second start's copy species is owed a cause before a fine-tune from it is quoted on the copy row.

What it does not settle. Pretraining at one pass in this format (every read here is by fine-tune, at ~40% of 650 iterations); structures; the copy species' cause; and whether a fine-tuned branch survives the next fine-tune.
