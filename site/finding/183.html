More words fixed most misses; an unseen 'unless' word still reads 'if'

The result, as the finding states it

With the word lists widened and a backward cut for a parenthetical condition, three of five gates pass at all three starts — structures 0.986-0.995 (the parenthetical cells 140 of 144), held cells 0.979-1.000, finding 180's rows 0.988-0.995 — and two miss at all three on one kind of word: an unseen unless-word is read as a plain "if" ("aside from if" 8 / 10 / 56 of 96, while an unseen if-word, else-word, default and switch verb read 90-96), and the hand-written probe reads 46 / 41 / 34 of 58 against 48

What it cost

three fine-tunes of 650 iterations at 20M (157, 143, 144 s, contended — the card at 36-38%, a bound), two span reads a run (145-147 s and 167-169 s), slot reads and copy cards at 7-9 s, the real-wording probe: one chain of 1,466.4 s, 12:10-12:34 on 2026-10-20, registered ~22 min; the zero-training control 525 s; the build 11:46-12:09 on CPU.

The owner's go, 2026-10-20: "let's do 1 and 2 together" — finding 182's two misses at once. Finding 182 put the condition's clause first (cond:, then if: [not ]v -> X) and composed structures installed and transferred; what missed was words the lists lacked and a parenthetical condition the cut rule ran forward. Two changes, read on separate columns (corn/branchwidegen.py, pre-registered at d4ad2e5 in configs/mix_v3_bind_ft_wide.py):

  1. every word list widened, branchstructgen's extended and never edited — unless-words 2 to 9 ("but not if" among them), if-words 5 to 10, else-words 5 to 11, defaults, switch verbs, negations, and a word between the condition word and "it" ("unless perhaps it's v") — with one word a slot held out (HELD_WORDS);
  2. the parenthetical order ("A, unless it's v, in which case B") and a cut rule that runs a condition clause holding no continuation back when the next clause opens on an else-word or an in-which-case word.

Everything else is finding 182's: the format, the starts, the recipe, the weight (1.86 passes against 1.88). check_branch.py section 18 holds it.

The held words, one at a time

Every held word transfers but the unless-word. On "aside from if" his clause is exact and his if: line has no "not" — the unseen negative word read as the "if" it ends in. Six trained unless-words of the same build ("apart from if", "other than if", "except for if", "excepting if") did not make a seventh one readable: nine words were learned as a list. The asymmetry is the finding: an unseen condition word is read as a plain condition — which is right for every if-word and wrong for every unless-word.

The far probe, by shape

  • Connectives no list holds (part 1): "anything but" 1 to 4, "is the backup" 1 to 3, "then definitely ... but if it's not then" 0 to 1, "save for if" 0 to 0 (the clause exact, no "not" — the unless-word again), "in the event that" 1 to 0 (he cuts "A by default", reading no condition in the clause that holds one). What it licenses

  • The structure layer is done for branch at nano by fine-tune: composed structures, the parenthetical with its backward cut, and finding 180's eight all read at 0.97-1.00 at three starts of three, and the structural held cells at 0.98-1.00.
  • Widening a word list transfers to unseen words of the same role — except where the role is a negation the word itself must carry. An unseen if-word, else-word, default or switch verb is read; an unseen unless-word (or "doesn't", at one start) is read as positive, because that is what an unknown condition word defaults to. Nine unless-words did not teach "unless" as a meaning.
  • For the 1.0 branch slice: the negative markers need coverage, not a sample — the unless-words and negations people use, mined from real text, in the dozens; or the polarity is checked against the request by the runtime. Which is the owner's.

What it does not settle. Whether real-text unless-words in the dozens teach the meaning or only a longer list; a "but"-led parenthetical; the third start's "doesn't"; pretraining at one pass in this form; and the second start's copy species.
