The tag spine · 12 September 2026

A label in the text is a hint, never the decision

The result, as the finding states it

The tag in the stream is read as a prior and never as the decision: zero of seven hundred and twenty forged maps at the embedding and at block 2, with and without the request under loss, while the pass schedule costs a fifth of the parse rows and the map gives none of it back

What it cost

0 s of new scoring on record; it reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (nano20m_v3). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

Eight nano arms trained 2026-09-12 to docs/PLAN-LOOP.md: F (the tag map added to the embedding), G (the same loss schedule with no map), H (the map added to the residual entering block 2), two seeds each and pre-registered in configs/mix_v3_decomp_f.py before a byte trained; and F2, a post-hoc arm designed after F was read, two seeds, registered as post hoc in the same file and in PLAN-LOOP §5. All four read arm B's file data/raw/decomp_b.txt byte for byte (rule 1) under a new loss policy; all compiled; B's own cards (finding 129) are the untouched comparison. Nothing else retrained. Scored under --decomp-arm F (one prefill per level with the map the prompt's parse lines imply) for F, H and F2, and under B's protocol for G.

The question, and the read that set it up

Finding 129 read five text-tag arms at 0 of 60 on every forced probe, and the repair arm E — position no longer predicting execution order — read the same zero on the label forgery at both seeds. Before this run, scripts/probe_taglayer.py read the residual stream directly: a linear probe reads each request token's part label at 0.82 on unseen strings in nano20m_v3, a checkpoint that never saw a parts: line, peaking at blocks 2-3 and holding within two points through block 7; the parse arms sit 1-4 points above the world-only arm A. So the acting layers already have a label for every token by block 2, computed from the words, and a text line forty tokens later is a second copy of it. Does the same tag, moved onto the tokens it labels and added to the stream, get read by the layers that act?

The gate: no

scripts/probe_depth.py --probe forced --forge label, greedy, the spans kept and the labels swapped, scored on the argument. --inject both prefills the forged prompt with the map its forged line implies; --inject map prefills the gold prompt with the forged map, so text and stream disagree and the argument says which he reads.

The map is read — as a prior, at the wrong place

The tag table trained: row norms 0.47-0.68 (F), 1.11-2.34 (H), 0.59-1.07 (F2) against an init of 0.45. On the payload's own validation split, the true map lowers the loss and a label-permuted map raises it, and the per-kind split says which tokens are doing the reading:

val:decomp loss by target, loss-bearing targets true map no map permuted F utterance words 1.237 1.263 1.466 parse lines 0.065 0.069 0.071 call lines 0.128 0.129 0.134 H utterance words 1.228 1.238 1.264 call lines 0.130 0.130 0.131 F2 parse lines 0.072 0.084 0.106 call lines 0.122 0.129 0.147 say 0.433 0.451 0.467

Under F the map's content is read strongly at the request's own words, where a label predicts the next word of its span (B-find -> "look up"), and the call lines move a thousandth of a nat. The utterance loss that the "pass" policy kept to bound the loss-free run was where the table's gradient came from. F2 removed it (context loss on the tools: declaration instead, loss="pass_decl", same draws and same map): the calls then read the map three times as much — a permuted map costs 0.019 nats there — and still not one of 120 forged maps changed an argument. Teacher-forced on the forgery cases, the gold argument's NLL under F is 0.0191 with no map, 0.0162 with the gold map and 0.0164 with the forged map: any map makes the right argument likelier by the same amount, and which labels it carries is worth two ten-thousandths. H, at the layer where the internal feature forms, reads it less than F everywhere.

The mechanism is finding 129's, one level down: the words already determine the call, so no loss ever has to prefer the tag, and what he learns is a prior that the words override. Adding the signal at the layer that computes the feature does not change that; removing the competing gradient triples a number that was a thousandth.

The map changes nothing behavioural: F, G, H and F2 sit inside each other's seed spread on every row. The pass schedule costs against B: about 18 of 120 on trained parse shapes, 22 on unseen entities and 8 of 60 on the full tree, on every scheduled arm alike — the price of loss on 29% of the payload's tokens where B bears it on all of them, declared in advance as G's job to measure. The rows are far from zero, so the pre-registered "unreadable" clause does not apply and the fallback (the payload doubled) was not needed. parse.depth3 read through F's third prefill is zero like everything else, as PLAN-LOOP §3 said it would be: pass 1 is the same parser.

The band

tool.selection reads 123/128 (F), 104/139 (G), 118/146 (H), 114/140 (F2) against B's 128/118, with seed spreads up to 35 — finding 129's sentence again: the direction is the world's 5% declared-tools slice and the size is not readable at two seeds. tool.copy dips to 108-109 on one seed each of F, G and F2 while H holds 119/120 and the other seeds hold 118-120; the dips land on the map-less arm as readily as the mapped ones, so they are not the map, and finding 129's compiled control already read 103 on the same row. tool.copy_wide 289-300, temporal.route 394-399, temporal.copy 353/353 everywhere. No arm fails the copy band relative to G.

What this licenses

PLAN-LOOP §6's pre-registered third row: "an additive tag is not read at this scale either; position iv (attention structure) is the next arm, or stop." With F2, the one confound the table did not anticipate is closed too. The map's content is demonstrably in the stream and demonstrably readable — the permuted map costs a tenth of the payload's loss — and it is never what decides a call. A tag that is to govern what he does has to be the door the decision goes through, not a signal beside it: docs/PLAN-TAG-SPINE.md is that design, and the owner's direction of the same day.

Instruments, and what was caught before it could count

  • The id scheme changed while building, and the plan says so. A label-only map makes ask[...] ask[...] one region; the map went to begin/inside pairs (17 ids) before any arm trained.
  • The batcher's per-source glob took the new tag files for token streams. Found by a six-iteration smoke run of the real trainer before the arms, not by a guard, and fixed in corn/data.py.
  • The trunk init would have differed between a tagged and an untagged config at one seed because the table's construction consumed the global generator. Caught by scripts/check_model.py's first run; the table is drawn off a forked generator, and the guard now holds the trunk equal.
  • The scorer's map is the build's map by construction and by guard. One parser in corn/tagmap.py cuts both; scripts/check_decomp_scorer.py re-derives forty rendered documents to one (turn, level) draw each and scripts/check_decompose.py re-derives 400 documents of each built mix off the bytes, both green before training.
  • The utterance-loss confound was not pre-registered. It was found by a diagnostic after F read zero, and F2 was designed to close it. F2 is reported as post hoc and its result changes no pre-registered reading.

Limits

Two seeds per arm, so directions (rule 6); the band's sizes are not readable. Nano readings of a dummy world, and PLAN-LOOP §3 says which literature lesson does and does not transfer. The forgery tests one axis — two labels swapped on a two-child do — and the per-kind losses are read on 96 windows of the payload's validation split with one permutation. The diagnostics separate "read" from "decides" by loss and by argument; they do not say what the acting layers would read if the words were taken away, which is PLAN-TAG-SPINE rung 1's question and not this finding's.
