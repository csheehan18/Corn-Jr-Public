The boundary model finds answers but can't prefer them to silence

The result, as the finding states it

The boundary head locates the span and cannot prefer it to the null

What it cost

6.3 min of scoring on the 1 card first cited here (corn_jr_260m_v4_ft4g). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

docs/PLAN-EXTRACTION-READER.md R1, run 2026-09-01/02 on the frozen corn_jr_260m_v4_ft4g trunk, fp32, two seeds of the head plus their synthetic arms. Every pre-registered bar fails on every arm, the pre-registered failing branch fires four times out of four, and the mechanism is not the one the branch names.

What was run

A pair scorer s(i,j) = w gelu(A h_i + B h_j + C (h_i * h_j)) over block outputs at layers {8, 12, 16}, 0 <= j - i < 16, plus a null logit s_null read at the say marker; one cross-entropy over every valid pair and the null, with all occurrences of a gold summed. The trunk never saw a gradient. Training data is data/raw/spans_nq.jsonl (R0-a): 80,000 windows sampled at the file's natural 24.8% positive balance, 75,788 train / 4,212 held out by article on seed 1 and 76,013 / 3,987 on seed 2, two epochs at ~643 s each. The head is 3.1M parameters. Scored by rendering its span as the worker's own It's {a}. through the real reduce and the frozen scorers, so every number lands on the existing ladder and no scorer or row was touched.

The threshold is cross-validated on the frozen eval and applied cold to the phrase eval (house rule 4); Population A's is cross-validated separately by scripts/probe_reader_passages.py.

The mechanism, and it is not what the branch named

The pre-registration said (a) under 250 or (b) under 35 means the frozen mid-stack representation does not carry the boundary, and R2 runs. Both fire. But the selection metric says something more specific, and it only says it once split by class.

Held-out argmax accuracy read 0.7593 then 0.7600 across the two epochs, which looked like a converged plateau. The held-out slice is 24.3% positive, so answering null to everything scores 0.757. Split, all four arms:

nulls positives s1 NQ 0.9865 0.0547 s1+syn 0.9857 0.1101 s2 NQ 0.9983 0.0431 s2+syn 0.9963 0.0530

The head declines 94.5% of gold-bearing windows on seed 1 and 95.7% on seed 2, and the synthetic arm's relief from that does not replicate — 0.1101 on seed 1 against 0.0530 on seed 2, which is most of the way back to the NQ arm. The null column is above 0.98 on every arm. It has not failed to locate the span: with the null out of the argmax it reads 84/200 tight on both seeds of the NQ arm, 42% of the file, and 89 and 95 on the synthetic arms. It has failed to prefer the span to the null. The loss agrees quantitatively — 1.0110 at 24.8% positives is about 4.0 nats on a positive row, so the gold pair carries roughly 1.8% of the mass against a uniform 0.09% over ~1,100 candidates. The head concentrates about twenty times on the right pair and still loses the argmax to s_null on almost every one.

The synthetic arm is the closest thing to a control on that reading, and it moves in the predicted direction on every axis. spans_synth.jsonl is 59% positive, so adding 20,000 of its windows takes the training mix from 24.8% to 31.6% positive — a modest shift — and the positive column of the selection metric goes 0.0547 to 0.1101, gated frozen tight 34 to 57, other 0.140 to 0.429, extract.paired 68 to 107 with took_first 21 to 10, and the held-out verifier AUC 0.597 to 0.773. The reading is confounded — those files were built to teach pointing, so content and balance moved together — but nothing here is consistent with "the states do not carry the boundary" alone.

Seed 2 keeps half of that and drops the other half. The two axes that are about pointing replicate and get stronger: extract.paired 81 to 109, took_first 28 to 5, sibling answers 35 to 9 — the best clause-flip numbers any arm here produced. The two that are about preference do not: the selection metric's positive column goes 0.0431 to 0.0530 rather than doubling, and gated frozen tight goes 49 to 52 rather than 34 to 57. So the synthetic files buy the ability to choose between two rival spans, on both seeds, and buy little or nothing on the decision to speak at all. That split is a sharper reading than seed 1 alone allowed, and it is the reason the confound is worth separating rather than accepting.

So the honest statement is that R1 as specified fails, and it fails in a way that is at least partly about the class balance of the objective rather than only about the representation. An unfrozen trunk in R2 would not distinguish the two, which is worth knowing before an hour of GPU is spent deciding between them.

What this does and does not license. It does not weaken the finding: every arm fails every bar by margins far outside the spread — the nearest miss is (a)'s joint at 231 against 270, and gated tight's best is 57 against 125. It does mean that no within-arm difference on this file smaller than about 15 points on the frozen eval or 35 passages on Population A may be read as a direction, and that includes most of what the synthetic arm appeared to buy on preference. The pointing gains survive the test because they are larger than it: took_first 21/28 down to 10/5 is a change of the same sign and rough size on both seeds.

What survives, and it is the one thing the record asked for

(f) reads 0.7727 on the held-out phrase file with the synthetic arm, and 0.8250 when seed 2 repeats it — above the pre-registered 0.75 on both seeds, on the file that killed the previous candidate. It is the only bar anything here passes and the only one that replicates. The NQ arm does not pass it on either seed (0.4444, 0.7143), so what replicates is the synthetic arm specifically. probe_spanhead's confidence read 0.62 on the frozen eval and 0.4348 on the phrase eval, below chance, and the eighty-first finding retired the isolated score as a willingness meter at 0.5288 on the identical question. Every margin on this project's record reads 0.53-0.67 frozen and at or below chance held out.

And it is a weaker result than that sentence makes it sound, in a way this file found by measuring the other end of it. The bar is AUC | attempted, and attempted is whatever the threshold admits — 19 of 89 items on seed 1 and 13 on seed 2. Drop the threshold so every item is attempted and the same heads on the same file read 0.5754 and 0.6714. So the margin separates correct from wrong within the band it is already confident about and is close to uninformative over the whole file. That is a real property and not a null — a verifier that works on its own confident subset is still a verifier — but the pre-registered number is a subset number and quoting it without the all-items number beside it would be the thirtieth finding's shape, a figure with no floor attached. The floor is the part that improves least across seeds: 0.5754 and 0.6714.

The claim it supports is "there is a correctness signal here worth a proper measurement", not "the verifier is built". corn/gate.py's willingness is a presence detector (AUC 0.7863 answerable-vs-empty, 0 of 12 clause-flip catches); this is a correctness signal, and the two are the pair the sealed read said nobody had tested together.

What this retires and what it does not

It does not retire the plan's §1.3 argument. That argument was that probe_spanhead beat generation under five handicaps and the missing null class was the reason it did not survive its gate. This file supplies the null class and the head still loses to generation — 84 to 95 ungated across four arms against greedy's 94, and worse in every type bucket rather than better on names — ungated name 0.529-0.559 against the generative arms' 0.636, number 0.250-0.417 against 0.688, other 0.086-0.161 against 0.278 — which is not probe_spanhead's shape, where names improved and phrases did not. Only one arm of four reaches greedy at all, and it does so on the type buckets' worst spread. What it shows is that a null class is not free: it is a class the objective can collapse onto.

It does not test R2. The trunk was frozen throughout, by design, so nothing here says what an unfrozen reader trunk would read.

It does not test the plan's own scale. 80,000 of 403,234 available windows, two epochs, HIDDEN = 256. Two head seeds now, so rule 6 is satisfied and the numbers are directions; the scale is not. The --positives-only probe below was built to test exactly that and did not finish.

The three suspects, and how far the separation got

R1's failure has three candidate causes and only one of them justifies R2. The head is underfit — 3.1M parameters, HIDDEN = 256, two epochs, 80,000 of 403,234 windows. The null class is collapsible in a shared softmax and demonstrably collapsed. Or the frozen representation does not carry the boundary. R2 unfreezes the trunk, which moves the third while leaving the first two exactly where they are: a pass would not say which was fixed and a fail would not say which was fatal. So R2 cannot be the experiment that chooses between them, and running it first buys an hour a seed of uninterpretable evidence.

The diagnostic is train_reader.py --positives-only, pre-registered in that file's docstring on 2026-09-02 before it ran. It removes the null entirely — gold-bearing windows only, softmax over the candidate pairs with no null term — so the only thing the objective can express is which pair, and it spends the freed budget on the other two suspects: all 100,084 positives rather than an 80,000-window sample, HIDDEN 512, six epochs, selection on held-out exact-pair accuracy. Scored ungated through the same reduce and the same frozen scorers. Its primary bar is frozen tight ungated >= 125/200 (ceiling 193, greedy 94, R1 read 84-95): a pass means the states carry the boundary and R1's failure was the null; under 100 on both seeds means the representation is the limit and R2 is justified as written.

It ran on seed 1 and was stopped in epoch 3 at the owner's call, so no bar was read. The trainer wrote --head-out only after the epoch loop, so no head reached disk and neither eval was scored. What survives is the curve:

epoch 0 loss 2.1774 held-out exact-pair 0.3492 834 s epoch 1 loss 1.6945 held-out exact-pair 0.3348 833 s epoch 2 loss 1.3853 held-out exact-pair 0.3428 832 s epoch 3 killed at step ~2800, running loss 1.2528

One suspect moves and it is the first. This file read R1's ~4.0 nats on a positive row as the gold pair holding ~1.8% of the mass, "concentrated twentyfold on the right pair and still losing to s_null" — and treated that as a statement about the objective's balance. One epoch of the larger head reaches 2.1774 and two reach 1.3853, on the same frozen trunk and the same labelled rows. R1's head was not converged on the positive class at all, so its loss was a fact about a two-epoch budget on 80,000 windows and not about what the states carry. The sentence "the head concentrates about twenty times on the right pair" stands as arithmetic and is retired as evidence about the representation.

The second reading points the other way and is weaker. Training loss falls 37% across three epochs while held-out exact-pair sits at 0.3492 / 0.3348 / 0.3428 — flat inside its own noise on a 4,880-row slice. Capacity and epochs buy training fit and not generalisation, which is the shape of a representation ceiling. But this is not the pre-registered bar. It is exact-pair argmax on a held-out slice of spans_nq.jsonl; bar (a) is tight on evals/extract.jsonl through the real scorers. Nothing on the record maps one to the other, and R1's arms cannot calibrate the map, because their positive column was crushed by the null while their ungated tight was not.

So the honest state is: underfit is retired as the explanation of R1's loss, the null class remains the demonstrated mechanism of R1's failure, and the representation is neither convicted nor cleared. The number that would decide it — frozen tight ungated against 125 — has not been read on either seed. R2 should not run on this evidence: the case for it is one flat held-out curve over three epochs on the wrong population, and the diagnostic built to settle it is an hour a seed away from finishing.

The suspects, separated

The probe above finished on 2026-09-02, off the state cache, and the answer is that the frozen states carry the location and the head was the thing that could not ask for it. Three arms, two head seeds each, six epochs, all 100,084 positives, HIDDEN 512, positives-only, scored ungated through the same reduce and the same frozen scorers. What changed between the arms is one term at a time and nothing else.

The measurement had to be fixed twice before it was worth reading

The epoch went from 834 s to 111. scripts/train_reader_cached.py trains off the harvested cache, and its first form still walked a Python loop around every window of every micro-batch: a pair index, a pair_slot lookup, a host-to-device copy of the gold slots, two logsumexp calls, and in the held-out pass an .item() — a device synchronisation — per window. padded_scores keeps the window axis instead of concatenating it away, and the padding needs no re-indexing anywhere because of how the enumeration is ordered: pair_index walks j outward and gives j a block of min(j, 15) + 1 pairs, so a slot's index does not depend on n. Width N's enumeration is width n's followed by the pairs whose j is past the end, the valid set is exactly jj < n, and pair_slot is the same map at either width.

the live fp32 trunk, per window 834 s the cache, per window 263 s the cache, padded and masked 111-171 s

The remaining spread is the page cache, and it is the floor. F: reads 0.44 GB/s cold against an epoch's 38 GB of fp16 states, so ~90 s of the 171 is disk that no batching removes and the 111 is a warm re-read. The arithmetic is ~3e14 FLOPs, which is seconds. This loop is I/O-bound and the card is the cheap part.

--check-batched is the assertion, on real windows rather than random input: pair scores agree to 4.7e-6 of their own scale, 0 argmax pairs move, and 208/208 query rows equal the cache's say-marker row exactly — the last because a pointer arm whose query was a pad row would train, would improve, and would be measuring nothing.

The initialisation was the last suspect with a mechanism and the three-seed spread could not speak to it: train_reader.main seeds, builds the 260M, and then draws PairHead, so the live head's initial weights come from a point in the generator's stream no --seed on the cached path can reach. --init-live reaches it, rebuilding the trunk shell for its draw alone:

Same head, same starting weights, same 95,204 windows, same 4,880 held out, seven points apart. The signs point one way and it is not proved: the cached loop's held-out column rises across epochs where the live one fell while its training loss fell 37%, which is a regulariser's shape, and the one candidate with that shape is the fp16 store rounding states whose coordinates run to ~80. The pointer arm's first run was an artefact, and the loss said so

As first written the query term read the raw states, and 1/sqrt(H) is the right scaling for unit-variance inputs while these are block outputs whose coordinates run to ~80. It read:

epoch 0 loss 23.2192 held-out exact-pair 0.3941 epoch 1 loss 26.1455 held-out exact-pair 0.4406 epoch 2 loss 27.1240 held-out exact-pair 0.4301 epoch 3 loss 27.1369 held-out exact-pair 0.4457

A cross-entropy over ~875 pairs is ln(875) = 6.77 nats at uniform and the base arm reads 2.24, so 23 to 27 is a softmax saturated on one wrong pair, and a rising loss beside an improving held-out column is logits growing in scale rather than in information. It was stopped in epoch 4, no bar was read and no card was written, and 0.4406 is not this arm's number. The fix, before any bar was read on either seed: a LayerNorm per 1,024-dim chunk on the query term's inputs only — the pair term keeps the raw states, because out(gelu(z)) is bounded by a trained out and already reads 2.2 — and the two query projections zero-initialised, so at initialisation the term is exactly 0 and the arm is the base arm. Its score scale went 28.1 to 23.2, the base arm's own. This is a deviation from the pre-registration and is recorded as one; what was pre-registered is the query term, what was wrong was its parametrisation, and it was found by reading the loss rather than the bar. A 300-step loss guard and a per-epoch logit-std column now make the same failure loud instead of quiet.

What this changes

R2 is not justified by this evidence and should not run. The case for it was that the frozen mid-stack representation might not carry the boundary; two seeds of a properly parametrised head read 130 and 138 on a file where the same states under the old head read 95, so the representation carries it and the head was the binding constraint. What the plan's R1 asked of the frozen trunk is not met — no bar passes end to end — but the reason is no longer the one R2 was built to fix.

The null class is now the only untested suspect of the three, and it is the one the eighty-third finding's own mechanism section named. Every arm here is positives-only by construction: abstain is 0 on both evals, and the question of whether a head that can locate a span can also decline to speak is exactly what these arms cannot answer. That is the next experiment, and it is a binary head on the say marker rather than a class in the shared softmax — because what R1 demonstrated is that a null inside the softmax collapses.

The null as its own head: abstention arrives, and the frozen eval stops being able to measure it

Run the same day, scripts/train_null_head.py, pre-registered in that file before it ran. The span head is the built arm loaded from disk and never trained; the new thing is a binary classifier on h_last alone — the say marker's row, which the pair score never reads. Two seeds, each paired with its own span head, so the two are independent systems.

Its data is 100,000 null windows harvested beside the 100,084 positives, and the sampling is not incidental. 303,150 nulls is ~116 GB against F:'s 118 GB free, so the class has to be capped, and a prefix would have been the wrong instrument: gen_spans.py writes in NQ's article order and the head is split by article, so the first 100,000 nulls would have been a different article population from the positives and the two would have landed on opposite sides of the split for most of the corpus. --sample-seed draws uniformly over the whole file instead. That leaves the file at ~50% positive against the world's 24.8%, and pos_weight = 0.3298 restores the prior in the objective rather than in the directory.

Only h_last is read, one row a window, so both caches are 1.23 GB held in RAM and an epoch is 2 s — against the span head's 111.

Who owns the residue: the null head refuses more than it misreads

336 right of 721 leaves 385 answerable passages, and the levers left are expensive enough that choosing one from a pooled number would repeat the mistake the whole thread is a record of. scripts/probe_reader_residue.py partitions the residue into the three things that can own it, exclusively and in order, at each passage's own held-out fold threshold:

  • NULL — no gold-bearing window was admitted. Nothing downstream ever had a chance.
  • LOCATION — a gold-bearing window was admitted and on every such window the head's best pair does not score tight against the gold.
  • REDUCE — a gold-bearing window was admitted, the head pointed right on it, and the passage is still wrong or silent.

Gold-bearing is corn.extract.span_of on the window text; "pointed right" is score_extract_tight on It's {span}. for that one window — the same scorer the passage verdict uses, applied one window at a time, so the difference between a right window and a right passage is exactly reduce.

The REDUCE example is also the reason 3a is not free. Step 3b: a much better presence detector that reads no more passages

NULL held the plurality on both seeds, so the pre-registered arm was the margin-feature null head — the binary head on h_last given three more numbers, read off the frozen span head at the one place the pair scores exist: s_best, s_best - s_second, and the top pair's length. Written into scripts/train_null_head.py's docstring before it ran, with the bars, the stopping rule and the void condition. Nothing about the span head, reduce, corn/extract.py or corn/gate.py moved.

Two normalisers ran, and the second is a control that was pre-registered as one. "The three scalars LayerNormed" read literally is nn.LayerNorm(3): three numbers with their mean and scale removed carry one degree of freedom, so the literal reading compresses a score, a margin and a length into a single direction before the MLP sees any of them. ln3 is the arm the bars are read on; std — each scalar standardised by its own training-split mean and standard deviation, frozen into the head — answers whether a miss is the normaliser or the features.

The void condition passed before anything trained. s_best is harvested off the training caches by the padded path and computed at inference by read_cached's per-window path; --check-features asserts they agree to 1e-4 of their own scale on real windows and read 3.0e-07 and 1.0e-06 on the two caches. The harvest is 112 s a cache on a shared card, 200,084 windows, cached to F:/corn-jr-data/states/feats_reader_built_s{1,2}.pt.

The residue partition is a partition of the arm's own errors and not a causal claim. LOCATION says the head pointed wrong on an admitted gold-bearing window; it does not say a better trunk would point right. NULL says no gold-bearing window was admitted; it does not say the window was readable. The owners are exclusive and ordered by construction, so a passage whose gold window was admitted and whose other windows outvoted it is REDUCE and not LOCATION, whatever else was also wrong with it. span_of is the gold-bearing predicate, so a gold that appears in a window only in inflected or partial form is not counted as borne there and its passage lands in NULL.
