Conversation and the second turn · 23 August 2026

Second-turn data teaches him to talk, at a cost to deferring

The result, as the finding states it

The second turn learns to talk, and pays for it out of deference

What it cost

0 s of new scoring on record; it reads 2 cards scored for an earlier finding (corn_jr_260m, nano20m_v1). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

configs/nano20m_v1_conv.py on configs/mix_v1_conv.py, run 2026-08-22 on the

  1. 53 minutes to train, ~9 minutes per scorecard, three scorecards. The slice is corn/convgen.py at 5% out of web: the mixed second turn, in three classes, none of which the corpus contained at any depth.

The class it targets was empty rather than rare. Over data/mix_v3_5b, 1,720 transcripts carry a second <|user|>; the marker after it is <|call|> in 1,720 and <|say|> in 0. And the live failure it was written for, on corn_jr_260m with decline withheld: in_kind 5/6 at turn 1, 5/6 at turn 2, 0/4 across turns 3 and 4, every one of them a talk beat, with "thanks" answered by web_search and "great, thanks" by save_note.

The samples are the finding in one line. Second turn, after a tool call:

baseline "I don't have a week, I can't get a week." "I can't control the river." arm "Anytime!" conversational replies, in register

The baseline is not failing to be varied. It is answering a conversational turn in tool register, because tool register is the only thing a second <|user|> has ever preceded.

first moved too (+0.019), which is not a broken pairing — the slice carries turn-1 material in talk_then_tool, so a turn-1 effect is expected. It is much smaller than the turn-2 effect, which is the right shape.

And it costs deference, which nobody pre-registered

refusal.say falls by 132 of 270 and the whole of it lands in reworded. That row asks whether he says the tool's sentence verbatim; reworded means he said the same thing in his own words. So the slice did not damage a capability — it changed a policy, and it changed it in exactly the direction the slice teaches. Nine thousand transcripts of "answer in your own words" bought "answer in your own words" everywhere, including the places where §5.2 wants the tool's words read back unchanged.

This is the project's central tension arriving as a number. VISION §6 pushes wording out of the parameters and into the tools precisely so a 260M model never has to compose; conversational register is the one thing it must compose. Those pull opposite ways and this slice found the seam.

refusal.no_guess holds at 47/47 on every checkpoint, so he is rewording the refusal, not inventing a capability. That distinction is the difference between a trade and a regression.

The arm is above both baselines on both rows. It should not be quoted, and the forty-first finding is why: a two-seed spread of 44 of 200 is wider than most effects this project has ever claimed, and 179 clears the top of it by 23. The honest statement is that routing did not fall — which was the pre-registered stop condition — and that a claim it rose needs the two further nano20m_v1 seeds that already exist on disk to be scored on this eval set.

Held, for the record: temporal.route 398/400 on all three, schema.conform 190-194, chat.engage 100/100, chat.abstain_talk 95/99 → 100.

What it settles, and what it does not

Settles: the second turn's register is a corpus property, and 5% of a 131M-token mix moves it decisively. The failure at 260M was not a limit of the architecture, the context assembly, or the model's capacity. It was a class nobody had written.

Does not settle: the share. 5% was chosen to match the WS-E carve and was never priced, and refusal.say at 30/270 is what an unpriced share looks like when it is too big. configs/mix_v1_conv.py pre-registered "turn2_reply.second rises and turn2.second falls -> the mirror bug, and the share is too big"; what actually happened is the mirror bug one row over, in refusal.say rather than in turn2.second, which the pre-registration did not anticipate and should have.

The next arm is the same slice at a smaller share, read against refusal.say and tool.copy as much as against turn2_reply. That is a dose problem and not a design problem — the slice does what it was built to do.

One instrument note

turn2_reply is reported at the top level of the scorecard rather than in behaviour.rows, so it does not appear in the row sweep and a reader looking for it there will conclude it was not scored. It was; it is beside chat_reply, and both are split first/second and again _by_preamble. Worth knowing before the next person reads a card.
