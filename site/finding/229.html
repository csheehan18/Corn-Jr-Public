Asked from afar, a step writes a pair the next-piece question misses

The result, as the finding states it

One step on the far cost — what a word's first piece costs him at every position from its neighbour's last piece to two before the word — writes the word's likelihood, like a press on the next piece: the drop where the book is silent is 80% and 84% of the drop at the pair's other meeting, and B - Bx is 0.00. Holding the word's commonness at eight random windows leaves no pair either (B - Bx +0.01 at both seeds, 3% of the drop at B): Q1 fails, as finding 225's hold Q did at the next piece. Held against the pressed sentence with the neighbour swapped out, the far step does write the pair — as read from afar: B - Bx +0.40 at both seeds, with the swapped meeting moving 0.08. Read as the next piece, the way he is asked, the same step reads +0.05 and +0.06, under the next-piece step's +0.24 and +0.20 (the anchor, reproducing finding 225): Q2 fails, by -0.19 and -0.14. Each question writes what the other does not read. The exception is the two words' own word-table rows: there the far step reads +0.98 and +1.03 from afar and +0.21 and +0.18 as the next piece, the next-piece step +0.46 and +0.34 and +0.33 and +0.25 from afar, each at under 0.001 nats a token of damage.

What it licenses

By the pre-registration, its third outcome: both rows fail. Where a pair is asked from does not change what one step writes for the next-piece question. Three things stand beside that:

  • Free commonness is not the comparison. Held at the next piece (finding 225) and held from afar, it leaves no pair. The comparison that writes one is the neighbour swapped out — the book's "more than usual" is not enough for him, though it is enough for a count.
  • A lesson asked from afar would teach a store the ruler does not read. The ruler asks by the next piece. A far side-lesson in every weight would learn and not be used, the shape finding 217 saw with a built-in judge. This is the first measurement under finding 228's distance, and it cuts against the simple form of the idea: he is not short of far knowledge because nobody asked from afar; asking from afar fills a different shelf.
  • The word table is the shared shelf, and it takes a far line cleanly. One row against another is what the book's line is, and finding 228's counted table of that shape reads 83 fields where his reads 58 to 61. A far, swapped lesson written only into the word table is the form of the owner's idea that this read leaves standing — against finding 226's caution that kept presses in shared rows churn.

What it does not settle. Many steps: finding 226 kept 150 next-piece presses and the damage added up; nothing here was kept. Pairs met often and far apart, which is what the fields are — these pairs are rare and within ten words. Whether a trunk trained from the start with a far lesson learns to carry it to the next piece; one step on a trained trunk cannot say.

Amended 2026-11-13 — the rows' cross-read, against a stand-in no hold saw

The table above reads the pair as B - Bx, and Bx carries the stand-in the hold itself used. Finding 230 kept 100 far steps in the rows and read every pair against a second stand-in (By) as well:

far | hold swap, the two words' rows against Bx (as above) against By asked from afar +0.89 and +1.02 +0.44 and +0.46 as the next piece +0.17 and +0.18 +0.04 and +0.07

So "a far step carries a fifth of its own read across to the next piece" holds against the hold's own stand-in and not against a fresh one: there the cross-read is about a twentieth of a nat. The word table is the one place a far line shows at the next piece at all, and it shows very little. The third licence above ("the shared shelf") is weaker than written; "two questions, two stores" stands, in the rows as well.
