The reply judge · 24 October 2026

A reply judge beside the 5M moves his replies level

The result, as the finding states it

A judge block fitted beside the frozen plain 5M on swapped replies — 853k parameters, 20,000 steps on 702,653 pairs of his own conversational reading — recognises held-out conversation as well as he does or better (same-dialogue quads right at the end of the reply 0.913 and 0.912 against his own 0.904 and 0.881; on the first word 0.793 and 0.785 against 0.792 and 0.760), and re-ranking his top 20 words by it moves the blind hand read level: 55 and 51 of 148 replies answer the message against greedy's 53 and 46 on the same checkpoints (the gate was +7 at both seeds), the pre-registered row (b), as predicted. On the 148 hand-written everyday messages the judge is far weaker than he is — swap pairs within a category right 0.73 and 0.75 at the end of the reply against his 0.91, 0.58 and 0.64 on the first word — and its fit on the words that decide a reply ranges only 0.45-0.9, about 0.7 nats of push against LM gaps of one to two: where it is wrong it does no harm, and where it is right it is outvoted. The free control, sixteen samples re-ranked by his own whole-reply score, reads 54 and 56, also level

What it cost

corn/judge.py, scripts/train_judge.py, scripts/check_judge.py (104 guards), scripts/probe_reply_judge.py, a settings check and the pre-registration 14:28-14:45 on 2026-10-24 (e69cf43); the chain 14:46-15:00, 13.97 min (judge fits 354 and 343 s, contended at 38%, a bound); the blind hand read of 888 replies ~10 min; the hand look at the judge's scores 2 min. ETA ~1.5 h, actual ~50 min.

The question (owner, 2026-10-24: "one is evaluating how well the predicted token fits and another layer that pushes a successful token forward to generate and show", inside the model). docs/PLAN-REPLY-JUDGE.md Phase 1: findings 204-208 show the 5M recognises a fitting reply in 91-96% of swapped pairs and writes one about half the time. A part whose only job is recognition, voting on every word — does it close that gap?

What was built (corn/judge.py argues each choice). One transformer block reads the trunk's last states over the message and the reply so far and scores every next token against its embedding; it trains on corn/contrast.py's pairs, each reply shown once after its own message (fits) and once after another's (does not), at every reply position, so a reply's general likelihood cannot predict the label. The picker adds lam * log sigmoid(fit) to the LM's log-probability over its top 20 and takes the argmax, lam 1 by Bayes' rule. The trunk never changes.

The reader. This read's greedy counts, 53 and 46, sit under the 70 and 58 earlier sessions gave the same greedy replies: a different reader, stricter on a reply whose second sentence breaks the fit ("That's great! I'm sure you'll do great." to a raffle win, "Yes, I am. I'm just about to go to the movies."). Read by hand

The replies. The picker changes 60 of 148 replies at each seed and mostly swaps one failure for another: "What? Why?" to a baby stays at seed 1; "Oh, that's great!" to a spilled coffee becomes "Oh, wow! That's great! How did you get it?". Its gains are the small talk and plans where any address to the person beats a self-statement ("What kind of party?", "That's great! What are you going to do?" to camping), and seed 2's odd messages (3 to 6). No category moves by more than a seed's spread.

The judge's scores, read at the first and second words on six messages the greedy replies fail (seed 1's judge, a scratchpad read; probe only — not on disk):

What the judge reliably knows at the start of a reply is whether it addresses the other person: openings that begin a self-statement ("I", "It") score 0.08-0.39 on every message. Mood it reads weakly and sometimes backwards on these messages — "Oh, no" to a scratched car under "That's great". And its scores are compressed: sigmoid 0.45-0.9 is at most ~0.7 nats of difference, so even where it is right ("That's great" 0.51 against "okay" 0.72 for the scratched car) the LM's 0.30 against 0.08 wins.

The cause

Two, both read above, neither a fault of the build (check_judge.py holds the trunk, the symmetry and the top-K bound):

  1. The judge does not carry to the everyday messages. It is trained on SODA turns, and on SODA it matches or beats his recognition; on the 148 hand-written messages it falls well under him (within 0.73-0.75 against 0.91). Finding 209 measured the gap it crosses: everyday messages are far more surprising to the 5M than SODA's openers (3.91 against 2.25 nats a token). His recognition on the 148 is the LM's whole conditional; the judge's is one block fitted to one source.
  2. One word is too early, and the push is too small. The words that decide a reply come second or third ("That's great"), the judge's accuracy rises with every word read (J1), and its fit spans too little to overturn an LM gap of two to four times.

What it licenses

  • A judge trained on swapped SODA turns, re-ranking one word at a time, is not the lever at 5M. The recognition it learns is real on held-out conversation and does not reach the messages the owner sends.
  • The probe the cause names, before any heavier version: fit the judge on pairs in the owner's register — short-reply OpenAssistant and EmpatheticDialogues (findings 211-213 found them closest) — and read J2 first, seconds of card: if within-category J2 does not pass his own 0.91, the picker has nothing to add and nothing more is run. Only if it does, the look-ahead picker (the owner's suggestion, 2026-10-24: try a few openings a few words deep, keep what the judge likes) addresses cause 2. A larger lam alone would amplify a judge that is wrong on mood.
  • Phase 2 is not opened (its condition was (a)). At 260M his own recognition is 99-100% (finding 208), so a judge there has a much higher bar to pass, not a lower one.

What it does not settle. Any judge trained off SODA; more than one block; the look-ahead picker; lam other than 1; any size above 5M; Phase 3.
