The corpus mix · 29 August 2026

At 5B tokens, fresh text is no worse than re-reading text

The result, as the finding states it

The fresh-strings bet holds at 5B, and the one row that moves down fails by over-resolving the date

What it cost

3.1 min of scoring on the 1 card first cited here (corn_jr_260m_v4); it also reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (corn_jr_260m). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

corn_jr_260m_v4 trained 2026-08-29 on a DigitalOcean H200 — commit 4eae0dbc, configs/corn_jr_260m_v4.py, data/mix_v4_5b verified onto the droplet at the recorded sha256 before the run. 23,511.5 s at ~223k tok/s, $22.47 of training. The control is corn_jr_260m — same shape, same schedule, same token budget, both compiled, the corpus the only variable — and its card evals/results/corn_jr_260m.json is quoted as committed; nothing was retrained or re-scored. Full provenance in 260M Run Data v4/RUN-NOTES.md.

What this run is the first evidence of, stated before the numbers: mix_v3_5b re-read five of eight slices more than 7 times and three more than 27; mix_v4_5b regenerates them at held shares so nothing passes arm 2's 15.21-pass cost point and 95.3% of the budget is under the 3.6-epoch knee. The bet, as configs/corn_jr_260m_v4.py put it: fresh strings from the same distribution are no worse than the same strings repeated. No nano run could see this — at 131M every slice is under a pass either way — so this is the bet's first test, and there is one of it.

The bet holds, on every instrument that can read it

scripts/rowdiff.py over the two cards: 68 of 121 shared quantities reproduce identically, including every row the repetition could have been paying for:

tool.copy 120/120 -> 120/120 identical tool.copy_wide 300/300 -> 300/300 identical temporal.copy 353/353 -> 353/353 identical refusal.say 270/270 -> 270/270 identical

Of the rows that moved, nearly all moved up: tool.selection 171 → 192, turn2.first 171 → 192, daylog.open 4 → 83, daylog.targeted 4 → 31, daylog.empty 0 → 26, notefid.trained_phrasing 74 → 99, notefid.novel_phrasing 66 → 80, chat.abstain_greet 67 → 85, tool.name_fidelity 106 → 112, register.conv 71 → 77. On the untouched prose instruments: lm.conversational 0.2905 → 0.2880 and lm.temporal 0.1847 → 0.1641 improve; lm.narrative and lm.web give back 0.0011 and 0.0021 nats/byte; variety.distinct3 0.9108 → 0.9179 and self_repeat 0.026 → 0.0167 both improve. Fresh strings were not merely no worse; at equal budget the corpus bought more.

(val_loss 1.5320 → 1.6046 is quoted nowhere above because it compares nothing: the val stream went from eight sources to thirteen, four of them new and harder than what they displaced.)

The one row down, and its failure is one species

daycross.with_day 188 → 118 — seventy items, far outside the ~10-item single-run wobble floor — while daycross.no_day goes 171 → 192. The why buckets say what happened:

daycross.with_day v3: chose_resolve_date 5, called_anyway 3, declined 4 v4: chose_resolve_date 67, called_anyway 14, chose_web_search 1 daycross.no_day v3: declined 9, wrong-tool 12, called_anyway 8 v4: declined 1, chose_schedule_personal_note 6, chose_resolve_date 1

The failure is chose_resolve_date, 5 → 67 — one species is 82% of the row. He did not forget how to handle a dated request; he resolves dates too eagerly, reaching for the tool when the day is already in the utterance. The same training that did it shows on the other side of the pair: on no_day, where resolving is the move, declines and wrong tools nearly vanish. Day handling got stronger and now over-fires on the half where it should stand down — finding 73's see-saw shape, arriving on the day axis.

The suspects are the day-heavy additions — daylog_5b.txt at 5.00% (whose own rows went 4/200 → 83/200) and the day-context chat file — but at n=1 that is a hypothesis, not an attribution. What would separate it is the same instrument P3-E used: a with-day/no-day matched pair on one carrier. Worth noting alongside: daycross was already the one behaviour measured worse at 91.6M than 29.6M (the scale-transfer caveat both 260M configs carry), so this row was the pre-registered place to look for trouble.

chat.abstain_oos 33 → 14 is not a second flag: the owner retired that row in favour of grid A's decline column (2026-08-27), and a drop against decline-trained corpora is the documented expected reading.

Instrument facts found while scoring

  • ckpt.pt and last.pt are the same model — every tensor torch.equal. DATA.md §9's "score both" question closes: their cards differ only in the ckpt path and in turn2_reply.
  • Which means turn2_reply is not per-item-deterministic. Identical weights moved that section and no other. A turn2_reply delta between checkpoints is not evidence until its seeding is pinned.
  • The anneal replicated its own number. select moved 0.1329 across the anneal against last run's measured 0.133 — wsd's anneal-does-the-work observation, reproduced on a different corpus.
  • Both cards were scored on the droplet's H200 where every prior card was scored locally. Greedy decode is not formally bit-stable across GPU architectures; any row that reads strangely should be re-scored locally with --only before being believed. The rows quoted here were cross-checked against expectations from local nano runs and none reads strangely.

Limits

n=1 — one seed, one run, no spread on anything above. The repetition repair and the daycross regression are both single observations; finding 68 says frozen-row seed variance at 260M-scale fine-tunes is small, but no second 5B base exists to say it for pretraining. The read stands on the failure-species structure, which seed noise does not produce.
