The leading learning rate finished last; 3e-4 stays

The result, as the finding states it

The rate that leads for sixty per cent of the probe finishes last, and the decision rule had an unmeasurable term in it

What it cost

10.2 min of scoring on the 1 card first cited here (corn_jr_260m). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

configs/corn_jr_260m_lr{3e4,4p5e4,6e4}.py, run 2026-08-22 on the 3080. Three 2,000-iteration probes, 49,152,000 tokens each, 37.9 / 37.6 / 37.6 minutes, 1.89 hours for all three. This is the 1.7 hours corn_jr_260m.py priced against 2.5 days of regret, and it is spent.

lr was the one unmeasured number in the Phase 2 config. It is now a screened one, which is a weaker thing than a measured one and is exactly what the instrument was built to deliver.

Nothing was vetoed

The pre-registered disqualifiers were NaN, an unrecovered loss spike, and val loss climbing across consecutive post-warmup eval points. None of the three rates hit any of them. All three exited 0, and throughput held 23.0–23.2k tok/s throughout — which incidentally confirms the 23.5k planning figure the 220-iteration smoke test established, at 40× the length.

The comparison is paired, and iter 0 is the proof. All three configs carry seed=1337 and differ only in lr/min_lr, which consumes no RNG, so the data order and the val batches at each eval point are identical across the three runs. Val at iter 0 agrees to four decimals in all three columns. This is a stronger design than three independent runs and it is stronger by accident rather than by intent — the probes were written to hold everything fixed, and pairing fell out of that.

The ordering flips, and that is the result

6e-4 is the best rate at five of the first six eval points and the worst rate at the last one. It leads through iter 1,250 — 62% of the probe — and finishes third. 4.5e-4 takes the lead at 1,500 and holds it to the end.

That is the finding, and it is empirical support for a pre-registration that until now was only an argument. corn_jr_260m_lr3e4.py declared before any probe ran that the instrument vetoes rates and does not rank them, on the a priori grounds that 2,000 iterations of a 203,451-iteration run cannot say which rate wins at 5B. It now has evidence: the ranking did not survive even to the end of the probe itself, at 1% of the run. A screen that had stopped at iter 1,250 — still 25 minutes of GPU, still a plausible place to stop — would have promoted 6e-4 on a lead it was about to lose.

The uptick at 1,750 is common to all three columns and is therefore the val draw, not the rates: eval points sample 40 fresh batches each, so absolute val at a single point carries roughly 0.05 of sample noise (the 1,500 → 1,750 move against a downward trend). Common-mode, so it cancels in the paired comparison — but it is the reason no single eval point can be read alone.

3e-4 stands, and not because 4.5e-4 lost

The pre-registered rule: if all three survive, 3e-4 stands unless a higher rate is below it at every post-warmup eval point by more than the eval noise.

4.5e-4 satisfies the first clause. It is below 3e-4 at 8 of 8 points, sign consistent throughout, under a paired comparison. The margin runs 0.0452 at iter 250 down to 0.0071 at iter 2,000.

The second clause cannot be evaluated, and that is the honest outcome. Because the arms share their val draws, the sample noise that the 1,750 uptick measures cancels in the paired difference — so the relevant floor is not eval-sample noise at all but run-to-run seed variance on the paired gap, and there is one seed per rate. The thirtieth finding's rule is that a floor is a property of the arm and a single seed cannot establish one. So 0.0071 is not a small effect; it is an unpriced one.

The rule as written contained a term nobody had arranged to measure. It named "the eval noise" without saying which noise or how it would be counted, and the paired design — which was not anticipated when the rule was written — changed the answer to that question. This is the same class of defect as the forty-sixth finding's guard that read the generator instead of the artefact: the instrument was fine and the thing that decides what the instrument means was not checked. It cost nothing here, because the rule's default clause resolves the case either way.

So the config launches at 3e-4, unchanged, on the pre-registered default rather than on a demonstration. Changing a 2.5-day run's learning rate on a consistent-sign 0.0071 with no floor under it would be precisely the error this project has now caught four times.

What this does not settle

  • Whether 4.5e-4 is better at 5B. 8 of 8 on a paired comparison is real evidence of a sign and no evidence at all of a size, and the sign was also consistent for 6e-4 right up until it reversed. The cheap thing that would settle it is a second seed on 3e-4 and 4.5e-4 only — 75 minutes, and it gives the paired gap a floor for the first time.
  • Anything about 6e-4 at length. Its late fade is consistent with the small-batch argument in corn_jr_260m.py's docstring — 24,576 tokens per step against the ~0.5M the recipes quoting 6e-4 use — but a fade inside a 1% probe is not that argument confirmed. It is one crossing.
  • The trunk beyond 2,000 iters. decay_frac=0.0 means these read the wsd trunk with no anneal, by design. The lr 3.00e-05 printed on the iter-2,000 line is lr_at's post-loop display for it >= max_iters; no step trained at min_lr, and iter 1,999 reads the full rate, verified against lr_at directly before the probes ran.
  • Scale. These are 260.6M probes, which is the size they were meant to screen, so the usual transfer caveat does not apply here — but 49.2M tokens is 1% of the budget and none of the behaviour rows exist at this length. Nothing here is a corpus reading.
