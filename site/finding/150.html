On real requests the runtime's machinery works but his own parse doesn't

The result, as the finding states it

On the real carriers the door works and his cut does not: given the gold parse, the two-note row reads 18 and 26 of 48 from 0 and 2, with his own parse 2 and 7; the forced card's zeros are his parse line where the prompt has none, and the one cost that survives staging is a chat label read off the tools: header

What it cost

70.5 min of training four nano runs (16.7-18.6 min each, card_cost.py; registered at ~18 min) and 12.1 min of scoring four standard cards; about 10 min of prepare_mix and the guard; probe_a2.py about 6 min a checkpoint on the registered rows and about 3 min on the post hoc guard rows, two checkpoints each (10:43-12:52 on 2026-10-04 end to end, against a registered ~2 h).

ROADMAP A2, PLAN-TAG-SPINE §12, pre-registered in configs/mix_v3_a2_on.py before a byte trained (b2ea2ff). The first read of the tag language on the real carriers: mix_v3 plus the turn-2 and compound payloads, off against on, the on cell carrying a parts: line (SPEC-LANGUAGE §3) under every user turn of the six turn-shaped slices, written after the fact from what each turn did (corn/parseline.py; 722,019 lines, 1.86% of the mix, transcripts matched document for document). Seeds 1337 and 2337, compile=True. The owner's decisions of 2026-10-03: act the ninth label (D-A1.21), every no-call turn parts: chat, the role table in the runtime (corn/door.py). Cards: evals/results/nano20m_v3_a2_{off,on}{,_seed2}.json (forced), nano20m_v3_a2_on{,_seed2}__a2.json and __a2_items.jsonl (the staged protocols), __a2_guards.json and __a2_guards_items.jsonl (post hoc, below). probe_a2.py is a hand-built instrument held by scripts/check_parseline.py and nothing more: every staged number is a bound (rule 28).

That reading is the record; what it is made of is the next section, and the owner takes which governs.

The forced card's zeros are his parse line

Every real row's prompt either ends after the user line or forces the next line (<|call|>, <|say|>) straight under it; the on cell trained on a corpus where that line is always a parts: line. On every forced react and reask prompt, both seeds, his first line is a parse line — parts: chat on 117 and 119 of 120 react prompts, 96 and 95 of 96 reask, 40 and 40 of 40 reask_yn — which the row's scorer reads as "no say". Staged so that he writes it first (bound), the same rows read at the off cell: react 117 and 119 against 116, reask 53 and 48 against 50 and 32, chain_result 50 and 50. No registered hold or guard row falls at both seeds under bound. The forced collapse is finding 134's lesson at scale: a prompt one line off the corpus is a prompt about a different corpus. The registered table's "forced or bound" clause was the error; the on cell's forced card measures the protocol.

One row falls at both seeds: social.voice — his reply to a social opener, read under a route header. By hand, the cause is the label: on those prompts his bound parse is parts: ask, 91 and 102 of 112 — "alright cool", "how was your day" — and the say that follows is tool register ("Right, that no tool covers this request", "Add a meeting | decline() — ..."). The same openers with no declaration line (social.bare_nocall) are labelled chat and read above the off cell. In the tagged corpus no chat turn ever sits under a tools: line: counted over all six twins, 0 chat and 200,000 other labels under one (every one toolspace's), 214,518 chat and 307,501 other without — so the line learned is "a declaration means a task", read off the header, not the words. That is SPEC §0's coverage argument (a layer on only some slices is a cue for those slices) arriving one level down, at a label value. Given the right label (gold), the declared no-call row reads 112 and 112, the door holding the call shut, and the voice row 57 and 85.

Read by hand: what fails, where

  • The cut. On compound.items his bound parse is a single bare part — parts: note, treating both notes as one — on 37 and 19 of 48; it carries the gold labels on 2 and 9. The eval rows join their two notes with "and also that", "plus that", ", and another that"; the corpus templates with "and that", "and one that", "and another saying", ", then that" (corn/turn2gen.py). He does not cut at a connective he never saw cut. When he does cut he is sometimes right (note[note down the landlord's office shuts at half four] note[that the spare key is with the neighbour] -> both notes, passed) and sometimes runs into nesting the grammar still allows (note[note[note[size act[note[note[is]]]]]]). compound.pipe at seed 2337 fails under bound the same way — parts: ask, or the whole request wrapped in one find[...] — so after the search no note part is left and he says instead (19 of 19 failures said_instead).
  • The extent, under gold. With the right cut the door makes him write the second note, and the remaining failures are where the copy ends: of wrong_text, 19 of 20 and 10 of 14 are spans of the request cut short ("the filter size is twenty by twenty", "-five" dropped; "the good parking"), and wrong_contact copies too much ("Thaddeus saying call me when you're free"). The copy half admits any span of the part; nothing says where a note ends.
  • Two ways round the copy half, an instrument hole named: at seed 2337 the door let through "contact": "Free" and "contact": "You" — a JSON escape and a capital the request does not have. The call object's copy rule still folds case both ways and decodes escapes (SPEC-LANGUAGE D-A1.14: span_ok until H3). Every gold number is a bound for that reason too.

What it licenses — for the owner

  • The parse is the runtime's to propose on real carriers at nano (the registered row), or 1.x: the door carries what a right parse gives it (compound.items 0 and 2 to 18 and 26), and he does not write the right parse for a connective he never saw. D7's row for the parse line reads: the door is worth riding; his cut is not yet.
  • The registered forced-clause kill reads the instrument, by the hand read above; the owner decides whether it governs. If it does, the tagging of turn2, compound, tools, toolspace and chat is killed in this form.
  • Two fixes the record now names, neither made here: the corpus puts chat turns under tools: lines (a social or chat turn inside a declared transcript), so the label stops being read off the header; and the door gets an extent rule — a note's text runs to its part's end, a contact stops at the contact — PLAN-EXTRACTION-READER's extent problem in the door.

What it does not say

That the parse cannot be learned on real carriers: one dose (1.86%), one set of templates whose connectives the eval does not share, nano. That the door's lift is the parse line's: gold inserts a line the off cell never sees, so gold against off forced is the door plus the line. That the staged guard rows are the registered reading — they are post hoc. Anything at 260M.
