2048-token blocks cost 1.2x training time and fit in memory

The result, as the finding states it

The block's costs: at the 260M step 2048 trains at 1.20x the 512 wall clock and 1024 at 1.06x, a fine-tune fits at 2048 in the memory it uses at 512, decode is free on a short turn and 100 tok/s deep in a full window as shipped, and a 2048 window holds ten documents with a third of its attention inside one

What it cost

~12 min of GPU in four chains under scripts/gpu.py, all marked contended (card at 31-44% light use): 3 min (killed — a spill I should have priced first), 5.2, 5.8 and 0.9 min; CPU ~5 s for the packing probe and 79 s for the 2048 conversation build; the bench options, the probe, the decode fix and its guard and this write-up took about 25 min (16:25-16:50 on 2026-10-10).

ROADMAP P0: the block's costs, no slice needed: training at the H200 shape, fine-tune memory on the 10 GB 3080, bucketed decode at 2048, and what a block of five-plus packed documents attending across each other costs. It feeds the owner's block decision (D2) and the 1.0 date; P1's nano pilot is the other half of D2 and is not read here. Every timing below was taken beside 31-44% light use of the card and is a bound; the three blocks were read back to back in one sitting under the same load, and the ratios between them are what this finding quotes. Cards: evals/results/block_costs__160.json (every bench row) and evals/results/block_packing__160.json (the stream and the v5 slices).

1. Training, at the H200 step

corn/trainbench.py --compile on configs/corn_jr_260m_v4.py's shape and its 24,576-token step, the tokens per micro-batch held equal across blocks:

block rows x accum x block step ms tok/s TFLOP/s vs 512 by FLOPs peak / reserved 512 8 x 6 x 512 1,135 21.6k 36.6 1.00 1.00 7.73 / 8.03 GB 1024 4 x 6 x 1024 1,206 20.4k 37.1 1.06 1.08 7.74 / 8.05 GB 2048 2 x 6 x 2048 1,357 18.1k 37.6 1.20 1.23 7.76 / 8.10 GB

The step costs what its arithmetic says. Achieved TFLOP/s is flat, and the ratio sits just under the FLOP count's (flops_per_token: the quadratic term is 245,760 x T a token against 6N = 1.56 GFLOP, so attention is 7.4% of the step at 512, 13.8% at 1024, 24.2% at 2048). PLAN-1-0 §4's estimates — ~1.3x at 1024, ~2x at 2048 — priced attention as a larger share than it is at 1,024 wide.

On the H200. The v4 run was 6 h 43 m and $25.52 at 512 (~$23.10 of it training). At the 3080's ratio, 2048 is ~8.0 h and ~$30, 1024 ~7.1 h and ~$27 — an extrapolation: the H200 holds the 3080's ratio if its flash kernel keeps the same efficiency against its matmuls, and the FLOP count (1.23x) is the ceiling if it does not. D11's ~1B-token pilot at 2048 is ~1.6 h of H200 at v4's sustained rate.

The pilot's own shape (nano20m_v3, compiled, 24 x 2 x 512 and its equal-token twins): 137, 151 and 178 ms, 1.10x and 1.29x (by FLOPs 1.14 and 1.42 — attention is a larger share of a 512-wide model). P1's three arms cost about 3.4 runs of the 512 arm, which is PLAN-1-0 §4's ~50 minutes.

2. Fine-tune memory on the 10 GB card

Memory is linear in tokens per micro-batch, not quadratic in the block — SDPA's flash kernel never builds the T x T matrix — so at equal tokens the three blocks reserve 8.03, 8.05 and 8.10 GB of 10.00. A fine-tune at 2048 runs 2 rows x 6 accumulation, 24,576 tokens a step — 4j's step exactly (8 x 6 x 512) — in the same memory, at 1.2x its time (4j's 17 minutes of training becomes ~20). PLAN-1-0 §4's "about four times 512's at the same batch" is true at the same rows; nothing needs more accumulation.

What does not fit: 4 rows at 2048 (8,192 tokens a micro-batch). The first chain asked for it, the card went to 9,998 of 10,240 MiB, and it was killed before a step was timed — WDDM spills into host RAM rather than raising, as trainbench's docstring warns, and §1's line puts it at ~12.6 GB. So did 16 rows at 512, which was cut from the chain unrun.

3. Decode

corn/bench.py gained --block-size, --buckets, --compile-step and --paths. bf16 is finding 2's condition; fp32 with buckets and compile_step is what harness/serverconfig.py ships. tok/s, graphed path (with sampling and readback in brackets), and the 32-token reply in ms:

dtype buckets compile positions block 512 block 2048 bf16 off off 128-256 425 (394) 87 226 (218) 153 bf16 on off 128-256 502 (461) 75 503 (459) 75 bf16 off off 1792-1920 — 225 (217) 170 bf16 on off 1792-1920 — 234 (225) 165 fp32 on on 128-256 297 119 303 117 fp32 on on 352-480 234 157 — fp32 on on 1792-1920 — 100 412 fp32 on off 128-256 — 276 128 fp32 on off 1792-1920 — 97 422

Finding 2 reproduces: 425 against its 424 at this shape. The 554 that PLAN-1-0 §4 and the P0 brief quote is finding 2's 12L x 1280, a shape that never shipped.

Unbucketed, 2048 halves every turn (226): the static cache attends all 2048 slots. Bucketed, a short turn costs exactly what it does at 512 — 503 against 502 in bf16, 303 against 297 as shipped — and the server already runs buckets, so a short conversation pays nothing for the wider window.

Deep in a full window is the cost, and no bucket removes it — the window is full. As shipped, a turn at positions 1792-1920 decodes at 100 tok/s and a 32-token reply takes 412 ms (91 ms of it prefill), against VISION §3's 250 tok/s and 100 ms; 512's own deepest point reads 234. bf16 reads 225 at the same position with a 28 ms prefill. So the 10-20-turn goal pays ~2.3x a token at the end of a long conversation as shipped, and dtype is the measured lever — but fp32 is a measurement condition (corn/sample.py: every scorecard is fp32), so serving bf16 is an H3 and owner choice, not a corpus one. attn="manual" (2.2x at decode in model.py's own measurement) was not read.

A hole, fixed: compile_step silently fell back past eight buckets. Dynamo caps one function at 8 compiled versions; a 2048 window has 16 buckets, the ninth raised FailOnRecompileLimitHit under fullgraph=True, and _capture's broken-toolchain path caught it — leaving buckets 1-8 compiled, 9-16 eager, and the card saying compile_step: false. 1024 has exactly 8 buckets and 512 has 4, so nothing shipped ever met it. corn.decode.ensure_recompile_room raises the limit to twice the bucket count before compiling (never lowering it); scripts/check_decode.py section 0 checks the helper without compiling, and --compile now builds sixteen compiled buckets on the nano checkpoint and requires compiled and 96/96 greedy parity — 96/96 and compiled with the fix; with the helper made a no-op it fails on both counts. What the fix buys is honesty more than speed: 3% deep (100 against 97), 10% on a short turn (303 against 276).

4. Packed documents

The premise was a reading of six generated files. "No document exceeds 431 tokens" is docs/MEASURED-TURNS.md §4's table, and its six files are all generated. The stream train.py samples is 46.4% web, and scripts/probe_block_packing.py reads it per source (400 random windows a source a block, 2M-token samples for lengths): web's median document is 834 tokens, p90 2,541; 93% of web tokens are in documents longer than 512 and 45% in documents longer than 2048. Every other source sits at a median of 48-228 tokens, with at most 5% of its tokens (narrative) in documents over 512.

What a boundary is had to be read, not assumed. The first cut used <|day|> where a source had no <|endoftext|>; by hand, five sources (structured, tools, extract, turn2, paired) have neither and separate documents with a blank line, and compound_v2 heads only some documents with a day line, so its p90 read 1,133. A blank line is the boundary in every source without <|endoftext|>; chat then reads a median of 48 and compound_v2 130, against MEASURED-TURNS' independent 45 and 128.

Weighted by each source's share of the stream (which is its share of windows):

block documents a window five or more same-document pairs cut head whole 512 3.1 16% 58% 49% 35% 1024 5.3 52% 45% 37% 48% 2048 9.7 57% 33% 25% 61%

Same-document pairs: the share of causal (query, key) pairs whose key is in the query's own document — a FLOP share. Cut head: tokens whose document began before the window. Whole: tokens in a document wholly inside it.

"Five-plus packed documents" is true of 57% of 2048 windows — every window outside web (11 to 43 documents) and 8% of web windows (2.6). The costs, each priced:

  • Compute. Two thirds of attention pairs at 2048 cross a boundary, and attention is 24.2% of the step: 16% of a 2048 step is spent attending across documents (3% at 512, 8% at 1024). It is already inside §1's 1.20x.
  • Stopping it. A document mask through SDPA's boolean path (trainbench --doc-len 150) steps in 1,534 ms against 1,357 at 2048, +13%, memory unchanged (8.13 GB); at 512 no cost is readable under the contention (1,101 against 1,135). A boolean mask takes SDPA off flash and skips nothing, so it pays extra not to attend. A block-sparse kernel (FlexAttention, or flash's varlen path) would skip the masked blocks and could recover up to the 16%; not measured, because it is a model change.
  • What the window shows. At 2048, 61% of tokens sit in a document wholly in view (35% at 512) and 25% are read without their document's opening (49%). The wider window shows more documents whole although it packs more of them.
  • Whether attending across a boundary hurts what he learns is a quality question, not a cost; it would show in P1, which has no arm for it.

The slice packing bites is the one 2048 is for. C9's conversation build at 2048 (gen_conversation.py --block 2048 --turn-reserve 21, 1,144 conversations, a scratch build — probe only, not on disk): median 887 tokens, p90 1,594, max 1,816, ten turns at the median. Under random offsets only 49% of its tokens are in a conversation wholly inside the window, and 24% are read without its opening — for a slice whose point is a follow-up reaching 6-18 turns back, the antecedent is before the window often enough to matter. The repo has the fix in kind: arm A's align_parse_blocks (corn/data.py) snaps an offset that lands inside a parse transcript back to its first token, leaving every source's delivered share unchanged; the same rule on the conversation source would put every 2048 window over a whole conversation at no compute cost. Not built and not decided — a sampler choice for P1 and the owner. The on-disk 1024 build reads 331 / 536 tokens, 80% whole at 2048.

5. What it changes

  • PLAN-1-0 §4's three unmeasured costs are measured: training 1.06x and 1.20x, not ~1.3x and ~2x; fine-tune memory unchanged at equal tokens; decode free on a short turn and 100 tok/s deep in a full window as shipped. Its "Cost" section moves with them.
  • D2. P0 finds no cost that rules out 2048 for training or for the fine-tunes. The price of 2048 is paid at serving, and only by a conversation long enough to use the room; and the packing question goes to P1 with one sampler option attached.
  • The 1.0 run: ~8 h and ~$30 at 2048, from 6 h 43 m and $25.52.
