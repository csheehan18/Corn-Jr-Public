Read by hand

Every composed and literal label above was read against the query the server actually issued. Composed: and brazil? -> the population of brazil. Literal: and peru? -> and peru?. Reissued: and peru? -> the currency of norway. The instrument reads what the text says.

The pronoun grid is new here (§12 printed only Grid C), and it is zero everywhere, with the misses changing shape. The base re-issues turn 1's query on four of six (is it growing? -> the population of kenya), surrenders one and keeps the pronoun on one. Every fine-tuned checkpoint, at both seeds and under both recipes, searches the pronoun as written (how old is he? -> how old is he). On 2026-09-08 the base's card read reissued 5 and surrendered 1. Today's one literal comes from the runtime's code or defaults since then, not the weights, because temperature is zero and the checkpoint is the same file.

What it licenses

  • D5 has its evidence. 4j is the better recipe on every row that separates the two at both seeds, and the separation is the chain family that ft4 broke. Its measured cost is compound.pipe, 5-7 of 48 below the base. The decision is the owner's; this is what it waited on.
  • Neither recipe composes a carrier it has no neighbour for, and neither resolves a pronoun. PLAN-1-0 §12 concluded the next variable is share, not breadth. That still stands, and is now read at two seeds. A 1.0 arm built on 4j inherits both gaps, which NOW §4 already discloses as follow-up composition and pronoun follow-ups.

Limits

chaingrid is n=4 per cell, so 0 of 4 against 4 of 4 is a gap and 3 of 4 against 4 of 4 is not. The pronoun grid is n=6. The fine-tune ran beside light desktop use, so its 1,019 s is a bound on the speed and says nothing about the weights. Two seeds per recipe; 4i was not re-run.
