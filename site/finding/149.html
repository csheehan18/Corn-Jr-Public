The tag language · 2 October 2026

With checkable rules filtered, he picks the template matching the request

The result, as the finding states it

Rung 4 passes split like rung 3: when the runtime drops every template failing a clause it can check, he picks the one whose soft clause answers the request by meaning, 254 and 254 of 432 held-out combinations against a gate of more than 175 and arm F's 147 and 152, and the pick follows the wish when the wish is swapped — while the accept-versus-none decision installed in no arm, and arm U's failed gate was a zero-shot paraphrase test whose one real selection rode on the template names' words

What it cost

about 2.7 h of GPU on 2026-10-02 — 2.4 h training eight nano runs at 17.4-19.5 min each (arms U, UD-id, UD-ev and US, two seeds; registered at 17-18 min a seed, so on its ETA) and about 20 min of scoring and probes between 11:08 and 17:08 (the cards, the full re-score, the swap and the trained-frame reads); corpus builds and floors on CPU, minutes. Amended 2026-10-03 with arm USN: 36.2 min of training two seeds (card_cost.py on the copy-wide cards, against a registered 17.5 min a seed), about 5 min of prepare_mix, the six checkpoints' scorer and swap and two copy-wide cards (11:31-12:12 end to end, the chain's log), and about 4 min of trained-frame probes, plus about 15 min of hand-read decodes (probe_templates_usn_decode.py on four checkpoints).

PLAN-TAG-LANGUAGE §6 rung 4 (ROADMAP G4), templates accept, deny and near misses, read 2026-10-02. Four arms, each arm F's mix with a template payload added on top, two seeds each (1337, 2337), arm F re-scored beside every one and never retrained: arm U (template names, use when, requires; use: <name>|none; configs/mix_v3_funcworld_templates.py), UD-id and UD-ev (no names, a per-frame id; use: <id> and use: because[<use when>] <id>; ..._templates_id.py, ..._templates_ev.py), and arm US (rung 4 split like rung 3; ..._templates_us.py). Each was pre-registered in its config before a byte trained. The story of the day is in the commits 24ff732 through b202d4e, and every number in them says where it was measured.

Cards (evals/results/, committed unless said): arm U greedy <ckpt>__templates.json; every option in full __templates_full.json; UD __templates_{id,ev}_full.json; US __templates_us_full.json (arm F's two land with this finding); __copy.json and __copywide.json. Instruments: scripts/probe_templates.py (greedy, bound and free), probe_templates_full.py, probe_templates_desc_full.py, probe_templates_us_full.py, and probe_templates_us_swap.py. Read by hand (EXPERIMENTS §5.6): the cell made every string new

The held-out, near-miss and forgery cells drew every clause and every name word from held-out pools — none of their 2,400 strings appears in training, which uses 34 clause strings — so the gate asked for zero-shot paraphrase, not selection. Unseen name spellings cost about 20 nats against none, so greedy said none on 193 and 188 of 208 accept frames: 110 and 115 are the always-none answer (majority reads 112), the near-miss column is low because he accepted almost nothing, and the hint verdict rested on about ten non-none picks of 96. Scored in full — every option's log-prob, no greedy — the same cells read at chance: selection 97 and 87 of 208 against 86.67 (arm F 88 and 93), and the forgery even, sig 36 and 42 against name 43 and 37.

On frames he trained on (181 accept frames from his own corpus, probe only): selection installed, 102 and 118 of 181 against chance 74 (arm F 67); accept-versus-none did not, AUC 0.54 and 0.59 against arm F's 0.53. So the eval was the problem for selection, and the decision itself was not in the weights.

Arm US: rung 4 split like rung 3 (e6597ee, a8d545a)

Owner-approved the same day, on rung 3's lesson (finding 148): he selects, the runtime enforces. The runtime checks every clause it can — domain and the hard field — and drops the templates that fail; the ones it shows agree on both word for word and differ only in the soft concept clause, of which exactly one answers the request's soft term by meaning. He writes the clause he matched, then its id; none is the runtime's (nothing survived) and he is never trained to write it:

template 1: use when a bar or a pub, a minimum rating, a hushed interior template 2: use when a bar or a pub, a minimum rating, a converted mill template 3: use when a bar or a pub, a minimum rating, a bustling scene <|user|>look for a place for drinks in Varunthgard, preferably steeped in the past, rated above 3 parts: find use: because[a converted mill] 2

Held out by combination: phrasing pi of each concept never trains beside the clause of its held-out family — 24 of 72 (phrasing, clause) pairings, rung 3's Latin square — and never beside a distractor it would be new beside either; all 24 clause strings train. Two shortcuts the guard caught before anything trained: rung 3's decoy rule let a lookup on the shown clause set alone score 0.984 of the held-out cell, and a per-request distractor draw gave one concept a 0.454 prior against chance 0.333. Both closed (one square for every concept, clash-free sets drawn first) and check_templates.py section 9 fails on either.

What it licenses

G4 passes in the split design (owner decisions, 2026-10-02):

  • Templates are shown as template <id>: use when <clauses> — no names, no requires. The runtime filters every clause it can check and owns none.
  • He judges the soft clause, written description first, then the id: use: because[<clause>] <id>. The SPEC-LANGUAGE §7 amendment follows.
  • Who selects: he does, among what the runtime lets through — "he selects, the runtime enforces". PLAN-TAG-LANGUAGE §7's second kill cannot fire: the runtime selects on every checkable clause, and he selects on the one it cannot.

What it does not show. The accept-versus-none decision — deny when nothing fits, deny a near miss — installed in no arm, on held-out, combo or trained frames (AUC 0.46-0.59 everywhere, arm F 0.45-0.53); in this design it is the runtime's, and a soft-only near miss is something he ranks rather than refuses. Matching three clauses at once: arm U's combo selection was the names. A held-out string: every US clause trains, only the pairing is new. Anything at 260M. Nano, the synthetic world, one corpus construction per arm, and a hand-built scorer.

  • He never writes none. The first token after use: is none or because[, and none never wins it — not on a held-out row, not on a trained row, and not on one of the 167 none frames he trained on. Its margin separates none rows from accept rows at 0.54-0.59, a few hundredths over arm US, which never saw it. The refusal did not install even on the frames that taught it.
  • The cards' over-deny and false-accept columns are the instrument. Every template line opens because[, so the mass a decode puts on writing some template is split across two to four lines, while use: none<|end|> keeps its own whole; ranked line against line, none tops 384 and 424 of 432 accept rows that the decode answers with a template on 431 and 432. The AUC column, a per-row margin, is not moved by that split the same way and stands as registered; the decision, false-accept, over-deny and top=1 columns of probe_templates_usn_full.py, and the swap's none on top counts, are not reads of what he writes.
  • On a none row he takes a shown template or names one that is not there. Seed 1337 takes a shown clause on 110 of 216 and writes an unshown one on 106 — 102 of them a clause of the wish's own concept ("full of energy" -> because[a bustling scene], with no such template shown). That is not a hidden refusal: he does it on accept rows too (130 of 432), and arm US does it as much (134 of 216 none rows, 187 of 432 accept). It is the wish's concept recalled, not copied off the menu.
  • On accept rows the concept is mostly right and the words often are not. Free, seed 1337 writes the gold clause on 155 of 432 and another clause of the wish's concept, not on the menu, on 130 — the right kind of place on 285 (seed 2337 307, US 344 and 317), the copy of the shown words on about half of them. CLAUDE.md's rule, again: score structure and vocabulary apart.
  • The template id after the clause is not read here either. Where he copies a shown clause, the id is that clause's own on 91 of 287 and 97 of 295 (US 84 of 237, 118 of 312) — about one in three, chance at two to four templates. Finding 148's amendment found the same of rung 3's letter; this is that split, owed there, read on rung 4's free decode.

What it says, plainly. Given a few clauses, he can tell which one fits a wish best, even on pairings he never saw — by kind, if not always by the menu's own words. He cannot tell that none of them fits: taught 30% none, he never once writes it, even on the frames that taught it. And he does not reliably write the number of the template he chose. At nano, none, the copy of the shown words and the id are the runtime's: the bind (use_usn_accepts) already limits him to a shown pair or none, and the id can follow from the clause.

What it does not say. That a refusal cannot train at all: one share (30%), one construction, no dose. Anything at 260M. That the runtime can read none off his confidence — arm US's own held-out AUC is 0.58-0.60 on the whole-line margin and 0.49-0.50 on the first token.
