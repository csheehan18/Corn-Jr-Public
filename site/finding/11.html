Tool calls and copying · 17 August 2026

A twenty-fifth of the planned tool data buys most of the behaviour

The result, as the finding states it

A 25th of the declared share buys most of the behaviour

What it cost

7.6 min of scoring on the 2 cards first cited here (nano20m_tools15, nano20m_tools_1pass); it also reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (nano20m_web_bpe). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

DATA.md §4 gives tool transcripts 15% of the budget. The tenth finding showed the slice caps at 2.3 MB, so 15% is only reachable by Source(repeat=True) re-reading the file ~25 times — which §8 says is exactly the trade a hardware-capped budget cannot afford. Nothing had measured it. Two arms, both at 130,279,711 tokens and nano20m_web_bpe's config verbatim except data_dir — 2 differing fields of 30 across all three runs, so composition is the only variable and the existing run is the 0% point for free:

One pass over 2.3 MB — 0.6075% of the budget, a 25th of what §4 asks for — moves selection from nothing to 73% and validity from 61.9% to 92.3%, and costs nothing. Narrative is identical to the no-tools run at 0.3666; temporal and web come in fractionally better. That is the result, and it was not the expected one: the share §4 declared before the data existed turns out to be mispriced by a factor of twenty-five for most of what it was buying.

The two tool rows move in opposite directions, which means they are two levers

Spending the other 14.4% is not free and not useless. It buys 26 more selections (146 → 172) and it costs 12 validities (167 → 155), plus 1.6% of narrative and 1.3% of web. Splitting tool.validity and tool.selection into separate rows was a tenth-finding decision made on the argument that a formatting failure and a routing failure are different questions; this is the run where that decision pays. A merged row would have read 313/381 for the one-pass arm against 327/381 for the 15% arm — making the upsampled corpus look like a straight improvement, in the exact run where it broke the schema.

The regression is one tool and one field. Re-running all 45 send_text items on both checkpoints:

send_text failureswhy
nano20m_tools_1pass0 / 45—
nano20m_tools1516 / 45missing:message ×16

Every one picks the right tool and then truncates the argument object to place_call's one-field shape. send_text and place_call share contact, and send_text is the only one of the five tools with two required parameters — so what collapses is the tool with the most structure, into the neighbour it shares a field with.

And where the contact slot should hold a name it has never seen, the upsampled arm reaches into the training set instead:

want contact='Thaddeus' message='thanks for today' got {"name": "send_text", "arguments": {"contact": "Those point of mercury"}}

the freezing point of mercury is a web_search query in the training world and appears 231 times in the corpus. Read 25 times over, the model has memorised the strings and falls back into one when the sentence goes somewhere it cannot follow. This is only visible because of §7's structural holdout — EVAL_WORLD shares every template and no lookup table with the training generator, so a held-out contact name is a place memorisation has nowhere to hide. An eval drawn from the same contact book would have scored this arm higher and taught us nothing.

So §8's rule survives with a sharper mechanism than the one it was argued from. "Every duplicate displaces a token you cannot afford" is true here — 18.8M tokens of real language went, and both prose rows moved — but it is the smaller half. The larger half is that repetition does not merely fail to add information, it actively installs the wrong prior, and it does so in the most structured corner of the format.

Three things worth recording

temporal.copy is 353/353 in both arms — a fourth distribution, and the first in which a second, third, fourth and fifth tool were competing for the same call slot. It has now held under templates, under real web text, under retokenization, and under four rival tools.

The question the slice was built for is still open, and the missing piece is an eval row. The tenth finding shaped web_search to return an answer field so that deference — read back what the tool said, never compose — could be tested on a second tool. Both arms have now read that slice, and it still cannot be scored: every item in evals/tools.jsonl stops at <|call|>, and score_validity/score_selection both read the call. Nothing presents an <|obs|> and scores the <|say|> after it, which is the only thing temporal.copy does. The rows measure routing and schema on five tools and deference on one, still. HELD_OUT_FACTS already makes the fix trustworthy; it needs items with a prompt_after, added via make_evals.py --add.

Checkpoint selection had nothing to disagree about, twice more. Best-val and final were iter 5,237 in both arms — weights compared tensor-by-tensor and identical — so ckpt.pt and last.pt are the same model and the rule-versus-ruler tally is still empty after four runs. That matters more here than it looks: val_tools.bin in the 15% arm is a fragment of a 25th pass over text the training stream already holds 24 times, so its 0.1311 is memorisation and not validation, and selection_score weights it as one of four equal sources. A quarter of that arm's selection signal was a memorisation readout. It happened to have nothing to overturn. The rule was left alone rather than patched mid-experiment, which is why both arms were scored on a checkpoint whose provenance is stated rather than assumed.

What this does not settle: 25M parameters reading 6,470 distinct sentences ~38 times cannot be separated, by this pair, from 25M parameters reading them at all — the collapse may be repetition or may be capacity. The test is the same pair at a larger distinct count and unchanged 15% share, which is what DATA.md §11's sentence-sourcing question is now load-bearing for. And the wall clocks (1,024.8s / 1,027.2s against the 0% arm's 992.4s) are the four per-source val splits costing an extra eval_iters=40 at each of 42 evals, not the corpus.
