No declines hide in unscored wording; the rest looks like salience

The result, as the finding states it

Nothing abstains in words the row cannot read, and what is left looks like saliency

What it cost

0 s of new scoring on record; it reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (corn_jr_260m). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

An audit, not an arm. No training, no card time, no scorer change. Six checkpoints already on disk: the two WS-B pilots and WS-E's four, read at --max-samples 400 so every one of the 400 eval items is retained per run.

The hypothesis under test is the fifty-sixth finding's third premise, and it was the last one the fifty-eighth finding left standing:

the abstention vocabulary this slice can teach is bounded by the row that scores it … and a model abstaining in words the row does not credit will read as a failure it is not

score_extract_abstain requires two things: a phrase from MISS_MARKERS present, and no content word outside _MISS_VOCAB, the question, and FRAME. The gate is real at generation — three of the first nine MISS_SAY drafts failed it, "I'm afraid" on the m an apostrophe leaves behind, "This doesn't say" because FRAME carries that and not this, and "There's nothing here about" because nothing about is a marker and nothing here about is not. The question is whether it also costs anything at scoring time.

The reconstruction is checked before it is used

The verdict logic was re-run over the retained samples and compared against the scorecard each run had already written. Exact agreement, four of four:

ctl s1 card 156/200 {no_miss 42, wrote_own 2} recon identical ctl s2 card 152/200 {no_miss 46, wrote_own 2} recon identical arm s1 card 172/200 {no_miss 26, wrote_own 2} recon identical arm s2 card 130/200 {no_miss 60, hedged 7, wrote_own 3} recon identical

This is the forty-sixth finding's rule applied to an audit rather than a corpus: a reader that disagrees with the thing it is auditing is measuring itself.

Both directions read zero

A reply was flagged if it carried any negation or uncertainty token outside MISS_MARKERS — nothing, unclear, unsure, unable, doesn, isn, sorry, afraid, unfortunat, seems, appears, without, missing, unspecified, and twenty more.

unanswerable half, no_miss failures carrying an uncredited hedge 0 / 265 answerable half, wrong_span/invented replies that read as a decline the vocabulary missed 0 / 260

Zero, both ways, across six checkpoints. Every no_miss failure is a confident assertion rather than an abstention phrased differently:

"Looks like Rod Cameron." "North India." "Looks like Budweiser." "It's September 1977." "Nacio Herb Brown, according to wikipedia.org."

What that settles

extract.abstain is not understating abstention. The row is measuring what it claims to measure on every checkpoint this project has scored it on.

And the fifty-eighth finding's decline rate is not understated, which is the reason the second direction was run at all. That rate is _is_miss over the answerable half, so a decline in uncredited words would have been counted as an attempt and the null would have been softer than reported. It was not.

The gate stays a real generation-side constraint and is not a measurement defect. The distinction matters: MISS_SAY was written around the row, and that cost is paid once, in the pool's diversity. Nothing since has been mis-scored because of it. No scorer change follows, so ground rule 4's re-scoring obligation does not arise.

What is left, stated as a hypothesis and not a result

The items were characterised because the audit made it free, and the pattern is sharp enough to record.

All 36 other golds appear verbatim in their windows, exactly as names and numbers do — 100% for all three types. So other is not harder in the sense of the answer being absent, and the eval items are sound.

other items declined by ALL four WS-E runs 24 / 36 declined by NONE 0 / 36

Not one other item survives every run. And the golds look like this:

where does eye of the round roast come from -> the rear leg of the cow what was the book wealth of nations about -> what builds nations' wealth what type of business is chamber of commerce -> a form of business network

These are descriptive noun phrases woven into running prose. A name or a number is lexically salient inside a window; "the rear leg of the cow" is not.

If the mechanism is saliency rather than frequency, the fifty-eighth finding's null is explained rather than merely recorded — answer-type frequency in the corpus cannot teach a model to locate a span that has no lexical marking, so tripling that frequency would move nothing, which is what it did.

This is a hypothesis and it has no instrument. Naming what would falsify it comes before proposing an arm — the twenty-third finding's shape is exactly the risk of building a slice against an observed failure whose cause is still a guess.

Correction — the instrument this asked for cannot be keyed on answer_type

Added the same day, after trying to build it.

The paragraph above asked for something that separates "cannot locate an unmarked span" from "has learned that answers look like names". Those are not two hypotheses. scripts/prepare_nq.py's answer_type is purely orthographic:

if re.search(r"\d", gold): return "number" if gold[:1].isupper(): return "name" return "other"

So "he declines on other" is "he declines when the gold carries no digit and no leading capital". The label and the marking are one measurement wearing two names, and no split keyed on answer_type can pull them apart. The instrument was proposed without checking that the thing it would key on could carry the distinction.

What a split by in-window occurrence does say, over the four WS-E runs:

type how the gold appears in the window items decline name capitalised, mid-sentence 73 63.0% name capitalised, sentence-initial 46 44.0% number capitalised, mid-sentence 11 45.5% number lowercase 33 67.4% other lowercase 35 89.3%

The coarse reading survives: a gold carrying no marking at all is declined most, and anything with a digit or a capital sits 20-45 points below it.

The within-name split goes backwards for a saliency account, though. A capitalised proper noun mid-sentence is the most findable thing in a window and it is declined more (63.0%) than one at a sentence start (44.0%), where the capital is grammar rather than a name marker. Saliency predicts the opposite. That is 73 items against 46, four runs each, so it is not rounding.

And this is where the analysis stops, on the seventeenth finding's grounds. There are 200 answerable items, 36 of them other, and they have now been cut by type, by gold length and by in-window occurrence, with several cells at n=1. That is a lot of post-hoc splits on one small sample. The defensible conclusion is not a mechanism but a limit: this eval cannot answer the question, because the label is the orthography and the items where marking and category come apart are a handful.

Separating them needs items built to separate them — proper nouns appearing lowercase, common nouns capitalised mid-sentence, the same fact asked in a marked and an unmarked form. That is an eval-construction task and it is PLAN-WEB-SEARCH.md's WS-F.

Cost

Nothing. Six checkpoints already existed, scored at --max-samples 400, and the audit is arithmetic over their retained samples. The one thing it required was the sample cap being raised — at the default 8 this could not have been asked, which is the point commit 2321f6e made and this finding is the second use of.

Why the thread is parked here, which is a judgement and not a measurement

Recorded so the next session does not re-derive it. WS-F is pre-registered and unbuilt on purpose. Everything below is reasoning, not evidence, and it should be overturned by evidence rather than deferred to.

Where this sits in the chain is the whole argument.

recognise 53/200 on real subjects, 87/200 invented finding 52 retrieve works — 22/24 provider responses had it WS-A1 reduce 8/24 answers thrown away before the model WS-A1 extract 9/200 on frozen corn_jr_260m, upper bound finding 55 abstain 0/200 finding 55

The fifty-fifth through fifty-ninth findings are all link four. Link one is failing on roughly three quarters of real subjects, and a failure there means the rest never runs — extraction can be perfect and buy nothing on a turn that never became a search. The other asymmetry is real and it lives inside 36 items of a 200-item row on the fourth link; finding 52's defect is 147 of 200 turns on the first.

Five hypotheses retired and no cause found is itself information. Class balance, gold position, corpus type-frequency, the scorer, and the instrument proposed to replace them. Each retirement was cheap and each was correct. But the returns are diminishing in a way the next candidate does not obviously reverse.

And a positive WS-F might not be actionable, which is the part worth writing down. Suppose it reads "marking drives the decline". The intervention that follows is a slice teaching extraction of unmarked spans — and the fifty-eighth finding is a measured null on corpus-side dosing of very nearly that behaviour. So the cheapest remaining measurement would buy a conclusion whose follow-up is already under suspicion. That is not a reason the result would be wrong; it is a reason it may not pay for the next step after it.

The contrast that decides it: finding 52's defect has a known cause — 25 phrasings offered, about 4 kept — and an intervention already written up, in PLAN-PHASE-3.md P3-B. This thread has an unknown cause and no intervention with a clean precedent.

What would restart it. Any of: WS-F becoming necessary because link one is fixed and link four is then the binding constraint; a cheap way to build the crossed items falling out of other work; or evidence that the asymmetry costs something at 260M that it does not cost at nano. The items are cheap to build and need no card to read — if the thread is being closed rather than parked, building them without scoring them preserves the option for nothing.
