Understanding at small scale · 22 October 2026

Steering mostly teaches how likely a word is, partly where it fits

The result, as the finding states it

On finding 198's runs, a steered rare word's surprise falls in sentences where it does not fit as well as where it does: on the held-out targets the scheduler steered at 10-30 and 30-100 sightings, the drop in eight wrong sentences is 0.58 and 0.71 of the drop in the word's own eight (seed 1: own −1.75 / wrong −1.12 at 10-30, −0.41 / −0.17 at 30-100; seed 2: −0.98 / −0.81, −0.44 / −0.23) — the pre-registered mixed row at both seeds, against a predicted 0.8. The unsteered targets and the common words move neither column. By hand, the two columns move together word by word (celebs −4.3 own / −4.0 wrong, nosebleeds −6.1 / −4.3); how much more he expects a word where it fits grows on 20 of 27 and 16 of 29 steered words

What it cost

writing scripts/probe_word_fit.py and a CPU dry run 08:20-08:30 on 2026-10-22, pre-registered at 565d9dd; the read 4 checkpoints in ~10 s (the card free, run directly — two launches through gpu.py run without --detach stalled at 99% of the card and were killed, cause unread); hand read 3 min. ETA 15 min, actual ~25 with the two stalls.

The question (the review of finding 198, 2026-10-22). Finding 198 showed steering lowers his surprise on a steered word in sentences he never read. That is necessary for knowing the word, not proof: three extra sightings could simply raise the word's prior — how likely it is anywhere — which lowers its surprise in every sentence alike. Knowing a word is expecting it where it fits more than where it does not.

The read (scripts/probe_word_fit.py). Each of the 300 held-out targets and 60 common words (evals/word_surprise.jsonl) is scored in its own eight held-out sentences (right) and put into eight other targets' sentences in place of that target (wrong), the partners drawn once by seed from words of the same suffix class (-ing, -ed, -ly, plural, noun and adjective endings), so the slot mostly takes the same part of speech: "the evidence of dune iridescent at the mid-section", "denounce sexual demolition". Fit is wrong less right. Unit: summed nats on the word's tokens, as probe_word_surprise.py.

Read by hand

The 27 and 29 steered 10-30 words, one line each, read in full (evals/results/wordfit__...json). The two columns move together: the words that fell most in their own sentences fell most in wrong ones too — polytheistic −6.2 / −4.4, nosebleeds −6.1 / −4.3, euphemism −5.2 / −3.4, celebs −4.3 / −4.0 (seed 1); illusory −4.3 / −2.8, voluptuous −4.2 / −4.4 (seed 2). A few gained fit cleanly (thermoregulatory −2.5 / −0.6, sauerkraut −1.8 / +0.6, striations −2.7 / −0.8). Medians: seed 1 right −1.44, wrong −0.62; seed 2 −0.93, −0.82. Fit grew on 20 of 27 and 16 of 29.

What it licenses

  • Most of the per-word gain is prior, not fit. Three or four extra sightings at 5M mostly teach him that the word exists and is likely; between three tenths and two fifths of the drop (1 - 0.71, 1 - 0.58) is specific to sentences where it belongs. That is learning — a 5M's base fit on a rare word is only 2.25 nats — but it is the shallow part.
  • Report fit beside surprise from here. A held-out surprise drop alone overstates what a reading intervention taught; probe_word_fit.py is the column that separates them, and the random-word control (pre-registered at 12fd610) is read through it too.

What it does not settle. Whether the wrong sentences are wrong in meaning or partly in grammar (the suffix classes are a proxy for part of speech; other holds 109 of 300 words); any size above 5M; whether a longer dose per word moves fit more than prior.
