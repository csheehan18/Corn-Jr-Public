The reply judge · 22 October 2026

People asking an assistant fits the owner best; the answers do not

The result, as the finding states it

After the owner kept Apache 2.0 for the weights and left the non-commercial EmpatheticDialogues out (2026-10-22), two permissively licensed real-dialogue sources were read the same way as finding 210. OpenAssistant OASST1 (Apache 2.0, human-written; English: 15,979 prompts and 25,326 replies, 3.6M words) fits the owner's own typed messages best of any source read — bigram perplexity 426 against Taskmaster's 648, EmpatheticDialogues' 718 and SODA's 938 — because its prompts are people asking an assistant things, which is how he writes to Corn-Jr. Its replies are the problem: median 93 words, list-heavy and encyclopedic, some in a model's voice ("As a language model AI..."), one confidently wrong. Google's Taskmaster (CC BY 4.0, verified in the official README) is short task talk ("I need a seafood place.", "Make it a medium.") but narrow and the most formulaic source read (perplexity 55 on its own held-out messages, under SODA's 86)

What it cost

licence checks, downloads (34 MB, 66 MB, 37 MB, 21 MB to F:/corn-jr-data/convcand/), the read (scripts/probe_conv_sources.py, scripts/probe_owner_register.py) and the hand read of 40 pairs, 16:12-16:30 on 2026-10-22. No card. ETA 30 min, actual ~18.

The question (owner, 2026-10-22: route (a) — keep Apache, leave ED out; find a permissive source). Finding 210: the owner writes to Corn-Jr as to an assistant — 69 messages, median 6 words, 0.38 short, 0.38 questions, 0.06 first person (counts only).

Licences. OASST1: Apache 2.0 on the HF dataset card, no message flagged synthetic in the ready set. Taskmaster: "made available under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 License" — Google's README, the same terms as SODA. PersonaChat: no licence found, not read.

Read by hand (20 pairs each)

  • OASST prompts are the owner's register: questions and requests to an assistant, typos kept ("What isbthe best programing language for 2023", "How can i Build myself", "Can I make it bigger?").
  • OASST replies are another thing: median 93 words (37% are 60 or fewer), numbered lists, encyclopedic answers on chemistry, git and relativity, a model's voice in places, and a confident wrong fact (Satoshi Nakamoto as the century's most influential mathematician). Trained on, they teach reciting knowledge — against the north star (navigate, not know) and Corn-Jr's short replies.
  • Taskmaster is short, real-sounding task talk — bookings, orders, car repair — with a fifth of its messages four words or fewer, but its domains are few and its phrasing repeats.

What it licenses

  • OASST's prompt side is the closest text to the owner's own messages in any source read, under a licence the weights can carry. How to use it without its answers' habits is the open question: threads whose replies are short (37% of replies), or the prompts with replies he would write.
  • Taskmaster at most as a small share, for short requests, under a cap: its repetition is a shortcut risk.
  • A 5M arm is the next read, pre-registered like finding 211, on the same register gate (the owner's messages, counts only).

What it does not settle. Whether short-reply OASST threads move his register; any size above 5M; the owner's register beyond 69 messages.
