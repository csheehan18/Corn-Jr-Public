Batch decoding 32 to 128 windows: both predictions about why were wrong

The result, as the finding states it

Thirty-two windows to a hundred and twenty-eight, and both predictions about why were wrong

What it cost

0 s of new scoring on record; it reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (corn_jr_260m). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

Two decode-time changes, measured on corn_jr_260m on the 10 GB 3080, 2026-08-24: StaticCache gains a slots argument, and --dtype bf16 serves the weights at half precision. Together they take WS-B-alt's batch ceiling from 32 windows to at least 128. No training, no corpus, no model change — and the reasoning that motivated each was wrong about which resource was scarce.

The measurement is harness/backends/read.py's Reader at its real prompt length — 115 tokens, 32 new — rather than A3's synthetic 96, best of two after a warm run.

The starting point, and a cliff worth naming

N total ms ms/window peak GB 1 315.1 315.07 2.06 8 350.5 43.82 2.83 32 709.0 22.16 5.46 64 390,641.2 6,103.77 8.98 <- paging 256 OOM

Sixty-four windows took six and a half minutes, because the allocation crossed what the card holds and WDDM began paging. The failure is not a gradual slowdown and nothing should discover it at runtime.

The cache was sized for a conversation and used by a worker

StaticCache reserved all block_size slots for every row — 40 MB to hold a 147-token sequence. The asymmetry nobody had named: a turn may run to block_size and cannot know when, so GraphedDecoder must allocate the whole window; a worker knows its exact length before it starts, because corn/extract.py cuts to a fixed token count and the budget is an argument.

N before ms before GB after ms after GB 32 709.0 5.46 478.1 3.68 64 390,641.2 8.98 736.7 5.41

Attention runs over every allocated slot, so the smaller allocation is less work as well as less memory — 32 windows got 33% faster on a change made for footprint.

A wrong slots does not crash, which is why it is guarded. The mask is arange <= pos, so undersizing changes which keys are visible and the reply stays fluent — the worst failure shape this project recognises. scripts/check_decode.py runs the same batch against a full-window cache and requires the two byte-identical, 4/4 rows. cache_slots on read exists only as that seam, in the sense --stop-at does in corn/train.py.

bf16 buys nothing at one sequence and 73% at sixty-four

The prediction, stated before the run: decode at batch 1 is memory-bandwidth-bound, so halving the weight bytes should approach 2x there and shrink as larger batches turn compute-bound.

dtype weights N ms ms/window peak GB fp32 994 1 335.9 335.93 2.00 bf16 497 1 342.6 342.62 1.49 fp32 994 8 376.7 47.09 2.38 bf16 497 8 364.7 45.59 1.68 fp32 994 64 758.0 11.84 5.41 bf16 497 64 437.2 6.83 3.20 bf16 497 128 534.0 4.17 5.33

Backwards. Two per cent slower at N=1, inside noise, and 1.73x at N=64. The premise was wrong about which resource is scarce at one sequence: 32 tokens in 336 ms is ~10 ms a token for a 260M model, which is kernel-launch-bound. That is precisely what GraphedDecoder exists to fix and what BatchedReader deliberately does not capture, so halving bytes that were never the constraint buys nothing. By N=64 the kernels are large enough for traffic to matter.

This is the second decode-time optimisation this project has mispredicted, and both errors have the same shape. torch.compile was expected to grow with model size and shrank — 1.16x at nano against 1.07x at 250M, because the kernels were already compute-bound. Both times the mistake was assuming which resource was scarce instead of measuring it, at both ends rather than one.

What it adds up to

128 windows — 4,096 tokens, longer than most article bodies — in one batch in 534 ms, which beats fp32 at half the batch size. Sixty-four windows went from six and a half minutes to 437 ms. The ceiling was 32 and is now at least 128; where it sits exactly is unmeasured, because past the cliff a single point costs six minutes to print.

The low end matters for a different reason. Four windows cost 6% more than one (333.7 ms against 315.1), because the weight traffic is paid once regardless of batch. So a constrained device running a single process still affords a 4-window quorum — which is the part of the design that would otherwise not survive, since grounded is free Python and runs anywhere but the vote needs two readings, and one reading is what a naively prompted model already has.

What this cannot say

bf16 is a measurement condition and it is not established as safe. Every scorecard, eval row and finding in this project was read off fp32 weights, and greedy decoding turns a swapped logit into a different sentence rather than a slightly worse one — which on the extraction path can flip a window between a grounded span and a refused invention, presenting as a model regression that is not one. On the iron passage both precisions produced byte-identical replies on 3 of 3 windows. That is one passage. --dtype defaults to fp32, harness/server.py reports it in wiring, and check_decode.py asserts the flag reaches the weights, because a --dtype that silently loaded fp32 would label every row with a condition it was not measured under and nothing would look wrong.

Nothing about other hardware. One card, one driver model. The paging cliff in particular is a WDDM behaviour and a Linux box would fail differently.

Nothing about whether reading more of a page helps. The ceiling is a capacity result. Whether 128 windows extract better than 8 is the slice's question, and reduce prefers agreement over presence, so more windows may buy coverage or may merely buy more ways to disagree.

What follows

Sizing beyond one batch is now a real option rather than an arithmetic exercise: a full article body fits, and iterative batches with an early exit — stop when k windows agree, never at 1 — become the design for anything longer. The remaining decode lever is that BatchedReader does not capture a CUDA graph, which the N=1 number above says is the whole cost at small batch and nearly none of it at large.
