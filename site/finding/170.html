The copy test's failure threshold was measuring seed noise

The result, as the finding states it

The copy band's standing kill measured seed noise: seeds of one arm on identical data spread 0 to 54 items of 300 on tool.copy_wide (median 8, 90th percentile 20), the kill's band was a binomial 3 to 4, and the two kills it reported on the ladder (findings 168 and 169) are not readable

What it cost

2.3 min of tool_copy_wide cards on the 16 family checkpoints that had none (8-9 s each, contended — the card at 38%, a bound), on 2026-10-14 between 09:23 and 09:27; the tabulation, the guard and the filing on CPU, to 09:29.

Why it was read. After finding 169 the owner proposed that the 20M shape is what is costing the copying — that each ladder payload fires the copy kill because the small model has no room for it. Before pricing a larger model for that, the question is whether there is a cost to explain. The kill (EXPERIMENTS §5.7) compares an arm's tool.copy_wide with its control's at the same seed, with probe_move.half_width as the band: 2.8 items at 298 of 300, 4.3 at 295. That band is the sampling error of one checkpoint's 300 items. It is not the error that matters, which is how far a second training of the same arm lands.

The read (scripts/copy_floor.py, every nano20m_v3_fw* checkpoint, 47 of them, block 1024, RoPE, the funcworld family the ladder trains in; 16 cards scored here, the rest on disk):

arm seeds (copy_wide of 300) spread filter_ev2 (the control) 298 295 293 5 filter_view 284 298 278 20 templates_id 242 296 54 recover_named 265 290 25 branch 292 282 10 recover 298 287 11 filter_ev 295 283 12 combine4 296 285 11 filter4 298 287 11 armf 280 288 8 ... 10 more arms spreads 0-8

within-arm spreads, 20 arms: 0 1 1 1 2 4 5 6 7 8 8 8 10 11 11 11 12 20 25 54 median 8; 90th percentile 20; every checkpoint 242-300, median 291

Seeds of one arm, on the same bytes, differ by as much as the kill's "falls" by the median arm, and by five to fifteen times its band in the tail. Arm V (filter_view), one payload at three seeds, reads 284, 298 and 278: under the old rule its seed 3337 would have killed its seed 2337. And a seed number does not pair two arms: a different mix is a different data order, so "below the control at the same seed" compares two independent draws.

What it does to the record. EXPERIMENTS §5.2 already says a separation smaller than the seed spread is not readable; §5.7's band broke that rule for one row. Read by the spread:

arm arm seeds control (E2) seeds old rule by the spread recover (167) 298 287 298 295 293 holds not readable recover_named 265 290 298 295 293 fires (168) not readable branch 292 282 298 295 293 fires (169) not readable

The "the copy kill fires at both seeds" in findings 168 and 169 is not readable, and neither is a payload cost on the copy band. The ladder payloads sit inside the family's own spread (the branch arm's mean 287 against E2's 295, a gap smaller than recover's two seeds, 298 and 287). The digit-shift misses read by hand in both findings are real outputs, and both seeds of the named arm and of branch-2337 showing one species is worth keeping in mind. But a species in 9 to 26 misses is not a cost the row can see against this noise.

For the owner's hypothesis: the copying is not shown to be costing anything at 20M on this ladder, so it is not evidence about scale either way. The seed fragility of the picks is a separate question: findings 168 and 169 each split, and one seed installed outright.

The rule now

EXPERIMENTS §5.7, amended 2026-10-14: the kill reads only when every seed of the arm sits below the control's lowest seed by more than 20 items — the 90th percentile of the within-arm spread — and otherwise reads "not readable". scripts/copy_floor.py holds SPREAD and verdict; scripts/check_copy_floor.py re-derives the percentile from the cards on disk (a new card that moves it fails the sweep until the constant, §5.7 and this finding move with it), shows that no 3-seed arm split against itself falls, and that a real fall still fires. The rule is coarse on purpose: at two seeds a side, anything finer would be estimating a tail from twenty arms.

What it does not settle. Why the spread is this wide — one garbled answer on the 232-answer row is a few items, and templates_id's 242 is many — and whether tool.selection and the other standing rows read through half_width have the same problem. They were not tabulated here and are the next instrument to check.
