The official split overlaps documents: 14% of the eval leaked into training

The result, as the finding states it

The official split guarantees questions and not documents, so 14% of the eval was in the slice, and the training half cannot be read by the code that reads the eval half

What it cost

0 s of new scoring on record; it reads 2 cards scored for an earlier finding (nano20m, nano20m_v3). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

docs/PLAN-WEB-SEARCH.md's WS-B, built on 2026-08-24: corn/extractgen.py, scripts/gen_extract.py, scripts/check_extract_slice.py, configs/mix_v3_extract.py and two nano configs. Nothing has trained. No weights changed at any point, and every number here is a property of a corpus file or of the code that reads one.

The slice itself came out where the pre-registration said it should — balanced 50/50, one pass at 5%, no repeat:

NQ train rows 19,920 65 shards, 14,096 answerable / 5,824 not dropped, article 409 2.05%, decontamination — see below transcripts 48,478 24,239 answerable / 24,239 absent tokens 8,168,451 1.25x the 6,546,719 pledged at 5% distinct windows 44,855 1.08 uses each distinct replies 31,699 1.53 uses each

1.08 uses per window is the number that matters for the thirty-fourth finding's rule: the two slots that taught reading sat at 1.0 and 1.2 uses and the three that taught recitation sat at 2,096, 2,130 and 48,088. Nothing here is close to the bad end.

What is worth recording is that three of the premises the slice was built on were false, and two of them were the kind that pass their own guard.

1. The split is annotated to two different depths, and every filter read the wrong one

prepare_nq.py was written against NQ dev and its filters all turn on annotator agreement — "is this paragraph even on topic should not be one annotator's opinion" is the docstring's own argument. Measured directly rather than taken from the card:

dev len(annotations["long_answer"]) == 5 on every row train len(annotations["long_answer"]) == 1 on every row

So on train:

agreed = [c for c, n in picked.items() if n >= 2]

is unsatisfiable, and one shard read 1,071 rows and kept 0 — 1,061 dropped as "no agreed long answer". That is the loud failure and it cost one 192 MB shard to find instead of the 13 GB a full pull would have been.

The quiet one is worse and would not have failed anything. The unanswerable arm read

elif 5 - n_with >= 4:

with the 5 hardcoded. On train n_with is 0 or 1, so 5 - 1 = 4 passes too: the filter fails open and labels a row whose annotator did answer as having no answer. Those rows would have gone straight into the abstention class — the class that exists to teach an honest miss, and the one the plan already names as having no runtime floor, so nothing downstream could have caught it.

prepare_nq.agreement(n_ann) now returns both thresholds from the split's depth: min_agree is 2 at five annotators and 1 at one, min_silent is max(1, n_ann - 1) — 4 of 5 on dev, 1 of 1 on train. Written as n_ann - 1 it would have been 0 on train and admitted everything, which is the same defect one layer along.

data/raw/nq-dev.jsonl regenerates byte-identically, sha256 1f92680e..., 721 answerable and 282 unanswerable unchanged — asserted twice, once after the annotator change and once after the HTML filter below. The frozen extract.* rows cut from it did not move.

2. "Decontamination by construction" passes its own check and is not true

This is the one to remember. Both prepare_nq.py and PLAN-WEB-SEARCH.md argue that cutting the eval from dev and the slice from train means the two never touch, and check_nq.py §7 asserts it — no shared id, no shared question — and passes.

The first build of the slice then contained:

56 of the frozen eval's 396 windows 14.1% 0 shared ids 0 shared questions

NQ annotates the same Wikipedia paragraph against different queries in both splits. 65 dev passages used by the eval appear verbatim in train, carrying 113 train rows, and the queries are near-paraphrases of each other:

eval where is the inscription on the statue of liberty train who wrote the message on the statue of liberty train when was the statue of liberty poem written

One paragraph, three questions, two splits. 409 train rows share a Wikipedia article with an eval item.

An official split is a statement about questions, not about documents. That sentence is the finding. The dataset's guarantee is exactly the one it advertises and it is not the guarantee an extraction eval needs, because the eval scores a model on a window of text and the leak is in the text.

The exclusion is therefore by article, not by window and not by passage. A window match catches only the exact 64 tokens and STRIDE is 32, so a window offset by one stride leaks half its text and matches nothing; a passage match catches the paragraph but not the adjacent paragraph of the same article that some other question selected. The article closes both. It costs 2.05% of the train rows, which is cheap against a row this is the before-number for, and it is read out of the frozen eval's own url field rather than out of gitignored nq-dev.jsonl — a decontamination step should not depend on a file that can be regenerated differently.

This is the second time this project's hygiene has needed a boundary its source did not supply, and it lands the same way as gen_tools.py's rule that a shared first name between the composed book and the holdout fails the build.

3. The row bounds the vocabulary the slice can teach

extract.abstain passes a reply only if it contains a phrase from MISS_MARKERS and carries no content word outside _MISS_VOCAB, the question and FRAME. Three of the first nine abstention phrasings written for the slice read as perfectly good abstentions and scored as failures:

Nothing useful about {q}, I'm afraid. hedged_answer — the m that an apostrophe leaves behind This doesn't say anything about {q}. hedged_answer — this; FRAME carries that and not this There's nothing here about {q}. no_miss — nothing about is a marker, nothing here about is not

The phrasings were changed rather than the scorer, and that direction is forced: extract.abstain is frozen at 0/200 and widening _is_miss would make the baseline the pilot is read against incomparable. But the cost is real and worth naming — the abstention vocabulary this slice can teach is bounded by the row that scores it, so the cardinality argument that carried sayvar is weaker here, and a model abstaining in words the row does not credit will read as a failure it is not.

A fourth constraint appeared at the same place. score_extract_abstain runs _length_verdict first, so a long question echoed into a reply is long against a short window and reads wrote_own whatever it says. That is why five of the fourteen phrasings do not name the query back: they are a length budget, not a style. Twelve transcripts in the first build tripped it.

4. The rule that falls out: never train a target its own row scores wrong

Those twelve are a small number attached to a large rule, now enforced in corn/extractgen.scores_as_target: every transcript in the slice is a target its own eval row would score as a pass, checked at generation by calling the scorer, and re-checked by the guard on the file.

It matters more than the twelve. The pilot reads movement on extract.span and extract.abstain, so a slice teaching replies those rows refuse would depress the very numbers it was built to move — and the arm would read as a failed intervention rather than as a mislabelled corpus. That is the thirty-seventh finding's shape (an arm that cost a run and taught nothing) with a cause nobody would have looked for.

5. Two smaller things

BPE decode → encode is not the identity, and the runtime batches on the assumption that it is. windows() cuts equal WIDTH-token slices and returns them decoded; re-encoding those strings gives 65–68 tokens for 7 of 1,197 dev windows, so 4 of 300 passages produce prompts of unequal length. BatchedReader raises ValueError on those deliberately rather than padding (StaticCache.pos is one shared scalar and corn/model.py uses learned absolute positions, so neither padding direction is available). So read_passage will throw on ~1.3% of passages. It cannot be fixed in windows() — make_evals.py calls it, and changing its output would move the frozen eval windows — so it belongs at the read_passage/BatchedReader seam and is tracked separately. The slice is unaffected; it only embeds the string.

One row in 19,921 carried a leftover HTML tag, </ref > — a wiki citation closer that NQ tokenises with a space, so is_html never flags it and it survives into the passage. None in dev, which is why it took a slice-sized source to surface. Now a counted drop rather than a scrub, on the same terms as the U+FFFD check beside it.

What this does not say

Nothing here is a claim about a trained model. The slice is built, guarded and unread by any optimiser. The pilot — nano20m at 5%, two seeds against a two-seed nano20m_v3 control, read as range against range — is what turns any of it into a result, and the pre-registered quantities are unchanged: extract.span from 9/200, extract.abstain from 0/200, continued_payload from 69/400 as the falsifiable one, with tool.copy 120/120 and tool.copy_wide 300/300 as the regression watch.

lm web is expected to move and is not a regression: web is the donor at 49.3773% → 44.3773%, and the forty-fourth finding measured that response as linear over two doses with no behaviour row moving.
