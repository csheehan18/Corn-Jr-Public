Reading the whole passage tells the checker nothing new

The result, as the finding states it

The passage tells the verifier nothing the window has not already said, cross-window agreement is at chance, and the six arms that assume otherwise flip sign between seeds inside the threshold's own fold noise

scripts/probe_passage_features.py, 2026-09-03. Replay only — no GPU, no decode, no weight read, no head retrained — over evals/results/nullpr_s{1,2}_popa.json at each fold's own cross-validated threshold under hybrid. Baselines 367 right / joint 600 and 368 / 575.

The shipped reader decides one window at a time: score it, take best - null, compare to a constant. reduce then votes among whatever cleared it. So every cross-window fact — that this margin is the best of eight or the worst of two, that three windows agree on the span and two do not — reaches the decision only after the threshold has thrown windows away. Seventy per cent of the residue is that one binary decision (REFUSED_RIGHT 113/91 plus REFUSED_WRONG 136/139 of 354/353), and four findings have gone into choosing the constant while none has asked whether the scalar is the right scalar.

It is. The passage has nothing to add.

1. The information is not there — read (a)

Two targets, a cross-fitted logistic, AUC against the single-feature baseline max_p. A logistic on one feature is a threshold on it, so the baseline AUC is the shipped decision's own separability stated per passage.

delta AUC over max_p alone seed 1 seed 2 bar SPEAK (the passage is answerable) -0.0012 -0.0061 +0.02 HAS_IT (gold at the argmax window) +0.0097 +0.0025 +0.02

Eight passage-level features — window count, mean, spread, top-two gap, three agreement measures — and on the target a speak/stay-silent decision is actually predicting, they make it worse on both seeds.

The feature the idea rested on is at chance. agree_top, how many windows produced the same span — the signal reduce already uses downstream, moved upstream of the threshold — reads:

agree_top AUC 0.4937 / 0.4863 (seed 1) 0.4815 / 0.4906 (seed 2) agree_frac 0.5093 / 0.5416 0.4777 / 0.5365 distinct 0.5356 / 0.5238 0.5278 / 0.5013 n_win 0.4819 / 0.5498 0.4819 / 0.5534

Below 0.5 in three of four cells for agree_top. Agreement does not predict whether the passage is answerable, and it does not predict whether the head has the answer.

2. And not where it would decide something — read (a2)

A whole-population AUC dilutes: most passages are not close calls. So (a2) restricts to passages the reader was silent on and separates the ones he should have spoken on — answerable, gold at the top of some window — from the ones he correctly refused. That is REFUSED_RIGHT against correct silence, which is the exact trade a better presence decision would move.

seed 1: 76 should have spoken, 233 correctly silent seed 2: 68 should have spoken, 207 correctly silent

max 0.7212 / 0.7764 <- the margin, already in the runtime mean 0.6763 / 0.7352 <- a monotone shadow of the same thing sd 0.5176 / 0.4599 agree_frac 0.5283 / 0.5026 agree_top 0.5124 / 0.5029 distinct 0.4885 / 0.4293 gap12 0.4831 / 0.4576 n_win 0.4792 / 0.4875

Every feature that is about the passage's shape rather than its margin sits inside 0.43–0.53. The margin separates at 0.72 and 0.78 on exactly the population where the residue lives.

3. Six arms, twelve measurements, no arm positive twice

Read (e) holds t at the baseline's own cross-validated value and changes nothing else, so what it reports is the rule and only the rule, paired passage by passage. (Read (b) re-fits t under each rule, which is correct but mixes the rule with a different operating point.)

rule seed 1 seed 2 sign gap 0.5 -2 p = 0.75 +7 p = 0.12 flips gap 1.0 -1 p = 1 +5 p = 0.18 flips gap 2.0 -1 p = 1 +2 p = 0.63 flips boost 0.5 -7 p = 0.32 +4 p = 0.64 flips boost 1.0 -20 p = 0.031 -12 p = 0.22 both - gap 1.0 + boost 1.0 -5 p = 0.61 -4 p = 0.71 both -

The only two rules that agree with themselves across seeds are the two that lose, and the only cell reaching p = 0.1 is a loss. GAP on seed 2 looks like a real precision gain — 375 right against 368, precision 61.3% → 62.8%, acc | att 0.701 → 0.718, attempt rate flat at 72.4% — and on seed 1 the same rule is −2.

Read (c) supplies the scale that makes that unremarkable, and it takes the scale from the baseline rather than from an arm. At its own silence cost the baseline sits +1 above the plain rule's trade curve on seed 1 and −7 below it on seed 2. That spread is the cross-validated threshold choice wandering between folds; it is ±7 wide; and every arm effect above fits inside it. Read (d) flips the same way — best joint anywhere on the curve is plain 600 against gap 0.5's 598 on seed 1, and plain 588 against 593 on seed 2.

GAP is also narrower than it sounds: it can never silence a passage entirely, because the argmax window always satisfies m_i >= max_p - g. At the seed-1 threshold it removes a window on 75 of 1,003 passages and changes the verdict on about seven.

What this cannot say

It does not test a retrained verifier. These are mask rules over a head that was trained without passage features. A head trained on them could in principle find structure a hand-written rule and a nine-feature logistic cannot. What §1 and §2 establish is that the features carry almost no univariate or linear signal about the two targets a presence head is trained against — which is the cheap evidence that would have justified the GPU, and it does not.

One trunk, two seeds, one population, Population A at the CV'd threshold under hybrid. The g and b grids are declared, not fitted, and every value is printed.

The lexicon of features is not exhaustive. Nine were chosen because they are free from the harvest. A feature requiring a second forward pass — the question re-encoded against the whole passage, say — is not tested and is not free.

Two things worth keeping that are not the result

The trade curve is an arch, not a slope. Sweeping t on seed 1: 364 right at 13 correct silences, rising to 417 at 114, back to 366 at 234. Admitting more windows makes reduce disagree more, so over most of the range speaking less buys right answers. An intermediate version of read (c) interpolated between bracketing grid points — which averages across the arch and means nothing — and reported a spurious +43. The committed form is a dominance test: the most right the plain rule reaches while conceding at least as much silence, which is generous to the plain rule and so conservative for a claim that an arm is worth having.

The null column is dead and has been all along. s_max and max returned identical AUCs to four decimals because null is identically zero across all 3,893 windows in both nullpr_* and null_* — one distinct value — so load_reader_harvest's best - null has been returning best. Nothing downstream is wrong and no number moves, but a documented per-window field carries no information and every "margin" in this workstream is the raw score under another name.

What follows

Do not retrain the verifier with passage features. That was the arm this probe existed to price and §1 is the reason not to spend the GPU.

The cheap side of the reader is now closed. Three ideas were live at the start of the day and all three are measured and dead: the sixty-sixth finding's locality rejection (ninetieth), a corpus arm teaching question-constraint discrimination (ninety-first, which found the constraint classes are the ones he is best at), and passage-level verification (here). None cost GPU and none bought a passage.

What is left that is not the trunk is one decode pass. No one has measured right(head) ∪ right(generate) on Population A. The eighty-seventh finding checked fourteen questions live and saw no disagreement, which settles nothing; corn/evals.py never builds a Reader at all, so no card can see it. If the two arms fail on different passages a router beats both and it is nearly free; if they fail on the same ones, that is itself the strongest statement yet that the limit is the representation.

Otherwise PLAN-EXTRACTION-READER.md §7's trunk levers are the only route named to the rest, and after three negative results in one day the honest question for the owner is whether 85% is a requirement or an aspiration — the measured ceiling of the current architecture is 65.5%/65.3%, and that number was computed before any of today's three arms failed.

Left unexplained, and stated here with the whole trade rather than half of it. The arch's peak replicates across seeds:

seed 1 417 right at 114 correct silences (CV point 366 at 232) seed 2 415 right at 119 correct silences (CV point 339 at 233)

The first draft of this section quoted that right column and the silence cost and stopped there, which is the error this repo's own reporting rule exists to prevent. Here is the rest of it, seed 1:

t right a-wrong a-silent | u-silent u-WRONG | wrong prec -1.420 366 138 217 | 232 50 | 188 66.1% -2.022 381 196 144 | 200 82 | 278 57.8% -2.713 408 225 88 | 150 132 | 357 53.3% -3.375 417 253 51 | 114 168 | 421 49.8%

Moving from the shipped floor of 200 correct silences to the arch's peak buys +36 right answers and costs +143 wrong ones — 82 to 168 confident false claims on passages that do not contain the answer, and 196 to 253 wrong answers on passages that do. Precision falls 57.8% to 49.8%: at the peak he is wrong more often than he is right. Seed 2 agrees, 50.1% at its own peak.

So the honest statement is not that the floor stands between the reader and its best rate. It is that joint cannot referee this at all, because it scores a right answer and a correct silence as equal and a wrong answer as zero rather than as a cost — and every threshold protocol in the record optimises joint. The floor is doing the work the metric will not do. Whether 200 is the right floor is an owner question; whether it should be decided by joint is not.

And the threshold's own ceiling is worth quoting against the target. The best right any threshold reaches on this head is 417 and 415 of 721 — 57.8% and 57.6% — against an owner target of 85%. Roughly twenty-seven points of that gap cannot be bought at any operating point and have to come from reading.
