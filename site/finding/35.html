Routing and declining · 17 August 2026

One percent of non-tool turns teaches him when not to call

The result, as the finding states it

The corpus never showed him a turn that does not call, and one per cent was the whole of it

What it cost

4.0 min of scoring on the 1 card first cited here (nano20m_v1_chat); it also reads 2 cards scored for an earlier finding (nano100m_v1, nano20m_v1). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

The thirty-fourth finding left declining as the gap a runtime has to close elsewhere. This asks the neighbouring question — not is this request covered, but is this a request at all — and answers it with a corpus class that has never existed. Two seeds trained against two baseline seeds; configs/mix_v1_chat.py.

Read the ranges, not the cells. Two of the readings written from the first seed were wrong in opposite directions and are withdrawn at the end of this file. A single cell of this arm supports neither of the things it appears to say.

Weighted by what the model reads: ~99,000 marked-up transcripts, ~1,000 of them no-call — 1%, at one pass. The 20% of the budget that makes him conversational carries no markers, so dialogue is learned as unmarked continuation and <|user|> is learned as a cue to emit a call. The two halves of what §3 wants are learned in disjoint universes.

The 1,000 that exist are not badly built — 132 refusal sentences at 7.6 uses each, which is not the recitation regime. They still mostly do not survive. Three utterances verbatim from that training pool, through corn/agent.py at temperature 0: put the radio on refuses cleanly; book me a flight and please delete my emails both call a tool.

The quantity was already in every scorecard, unpooled by nobody

score_selection has scored abstention since it was written — item["tool"] is None for a request no tool covers and called_anyway is its failure reason. Read out of evals/results/, it is at floor on every checkpoint in the project: 13 to 19 of the 19 out-of-scope items, across four seeds, two model sizes and nine corpora including one with no tool slice at all. On nano100m_v1, 18 of the 19 items tool.selection fails are that one failure, so its 181/200 has been read as tool competence when almost all of its error mass is a single behaviour.

Nothing was wrong with the measurement. It was pooled with a six-way tool choice, it had 19 items, and all 19 asked for an uncovered capability — none was ordinary conversation. evals/chat.jsonl unpools it into three cells of 100, and the third is a control rather than a measurement: without items that want a call, a model that never calls scores 100%.

The control first, as pre-registered. Conversational went 20% → 15% unmarked plus 5% marked, and register.conv on the dialogue half reads 81/76 against 81/77. The twenty-fifth finding priced that row at exactly this share against a 1.0-point floor, the tightest in the project. It did not move, so the arm is readable. The donor was chosen for this: the new slice is made of the old one, so total dialogue exposure is 20% in both corpora and the only thing that changes is whether some of those sentences sit behind <|user|>.

chat.abstain_oos is the result and its size is not readable. [0, 5] against [7, 29] does not overlap, so the direction survives. 7 and 29 is a fourfold spread and no interval can be quoted from it. More seeds are the one thing that prices this.

The replies got longer, not more varied. Median words 8 → 10 and 11 against the corpus's 11 separates cleanly. Pooled distinct-3 does not: the arm's range straddles the baseline's entirely, and arm seed 2 reads below both baselines.

Cardinality was necessary and was not sufficient. The slice's replies are cut from SODA rather than written, at 1.19 uses per distinct reply over 163,287 of them — answer's regime (1.2) and the wide toolspace arm's (1.0), not clarify's 2,096. That is the mechanism the thirty-fourth finding generalised, and it bought a real move on oos and on reply length. It did not buy reply variety, which is the first time in this project that getting the cardinality right has not moved the row it was aimed at.

The instrument has a ceiling on one class, and it was built to avoid exactly that

chat.abstain_talk reads 95 and 99 in the baseline. Five items of headroom — which is the thirty-first finding's complaint about evals_wideonly ("twelve items whose untreated range touches its own ceiling, which answers nothing at any seed count") committed again, by the file written to prevent it.

The cause is legible in the items. talk prompts are long SODA openings ("Mom, what's going on? Why is the school closing for a week?") and those already read as conversation to an untreated model. The failures visible in corn/agent.py — good morning, did you sleep well? — are short greetings, and MIN_USER = 16 plus SODA's opener distribution keeps them nearly out of the pool. The row measures the easy end of its class. Fixing it means a hand-written short-greeting pool, which is quantised by construction; that is a trade to decide rather than assume.

What this does not settle

  • Two seeds, and oos's spread of 7-to-29 means the effect exists and has no interval.
  • absent is a different question and this does not transfer to it. The thirty-fourth finding's absent asks whether a request is covered by a declared list and fails because he invents a tool name rather than running a membership test. This asks whether an utterance is a request. Both being no-call classes does not make them one question.
  • refusal.say moved and nothing here asked it to — 162/172 → 188/196, no overlap, ~25 items, unexplained. It is the loose end most likely to be a real finding.
  • refusal.say_unechoed is 0/55 in all four cells, unmoved, as everywhere.
  • The tool-word screen is a heuristic. corn/chatgen.py's TOOL_WORDS drops any dialogue opening that looks like a request a tool covers, because teaching <|say|> on "remind me on Friday" would train against eleven findings of tool behaviour. It cannot catch a request phrased without any of its words, and those transcripts are in the slice.
  • 5% is unpriced. The first point, chosen to make a null readable. The thirty-first finding's warning applies in full: interventions here keep turning out cheaper than the arm that found them.
  • Multi-turn is untouched. The new slice is single-turn like every other; no transcript in the corpus has a second <|user|>.

Two readings from seed 1, withdrawn

  • ~~"He learned that ordinary conversation isn't a request; he did not learn that an uncovered request is uncovered."~~ Backwards. Seed 1 read oos at 7 and talk at 100/100, which looked decisive until the baseline arrived at 95 and 99 on talk — near ceiling all along — and arm seed 2 read oos at 29.
  • ~~"The arm closes 80% of the gap to the corpus on reply variety."~~ Does not replicate. Read off seed 1's 0.9052 against a 0.9130 corpus reference before a second seed existed.

And tool.copy's −15 on seed 1 was seed noise. Seed 2 reads 100/120, identical to both baselines. The nineteenth finding says that row moves 15.0 points on the seed alone; it did exactly that, and one cell nearly bought a regression that is not there.

A separate thing this cost, and it was free to fix

_invent_held_out_facts compiled a fresh regex on each of 5,004,876 iterations — 600 candidates × 8,697 train answers × two directions — thrashing re's 512-entry cache against 8,697 distinct patterns. import corn.tools took 87.5 seconds, paid by corn/agent.py, corn/evals.py, corn/chat.py and all fourteen guard scripts before any of them printed a character. Compiling once outside the loop takes it to 2.7s, and the agent from cold start to two answers from 91.1s to 6.3s.

FACTS is in every mix and therefore every checkpoint; HELD_OUT_FACTS_WIDE is in evals/tool_copy_wide.jsonl, which fourteen scorecards read. Both are now pinned by sha256 in check_tools.py group 10, against hashes captured before the change and verified identical after. Nothing stood between a well-meaning edit to that generator and every number in the project quietly ceasing to be comparable.
