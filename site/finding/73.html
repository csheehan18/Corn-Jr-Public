Routing and declining · 27 August 2026

Training both answers on one phrasing breaks the serve/decline trade-off

The result, as the finding states it

The see-saw breaks when one carrier carries both answers, and the supply lines buy the serve column on top

What it cost

0 s of new scoring on record; it reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (corn_jr_260m). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

corn_jr_260m_fte{1,2} and both their seed-2 twins, 2026-08-27, trained from the frozen checkpoints/corn_jr_260m/ckpt.pt on P3-E's balance payloads and measured by harness/toolgrid.py under the conditions of every run it is read against: 808 turns each, greedy, table-backed (search: table, nothing leaves the machine), scratch --notes, eight tools declared with decline offered, hatched: 0 on every cell. Pre-registered in docs/PLAN-PHASE-3.md §P3-E before the corpus was regenerated; built to docs/PLAN-P3E-BUILD.md. Controls are the base and the four already-scored p3b2 arms — nothing retrained.

The question, and why no checkpoint had answered it

Finding 67 measured a see-saw and finding 69 aimed it. The base declines 201/208 and serves 118/600; every arm that saw the p3b2 tools payload reverses it, serving 362–406/600 and declining 57–114/208, and ftwide collapses to the same 88/208 as ftextract with no extraction transcript anywhere in its corpus. So the tools payload moved the routing prior, and no checkpoint on disk decided between serving and declining. P3-E asked whether a corpus in which the two are trained against each other installs the judgement.

The treatment, in one sentence and one property

A new generator class draws one carrier and renders it twice — around a request no tool covers, and around a request a tool serves — and scripts/gen_tools.py writes the pair atomically. So over the matched subset the carrier distribution of decline-shaped and serve-shaped transcripts is identical by construction: 27,121 each over 30 carriers in E1, 27,169 in E2, read off the bytes by scripts/check_p3e.py as two equal Counters. The only thing left that can predict the label is the request.

E1 is balance alone. E2 is E1 plus the three lines finding 67 licensed: dates and entities inside note text, the messageless text family, wider contact carriers.

Every P3-E arm holds the base's decline column while serving three to four times what the base serves. The decline gap to the p3b2 arms is 77 to 149 turns of 208, against the 26- and 55-turn seed spread finding 69 measured on exactly this column — the first cell in this project's history to clear that spread on the decline axis. The base's own 201 is inside it, which is the correct reading: P3-E is at the base's decline level, not above it, and that is the whole point, because the base pays 482 serve-cases for its 201.

Against the pre-registration, stated exactly rather than rounded up

The registered cell reads: discrimination installs — grid A trained AND grids B+C routed both clear the see-saw's best ends simultaneously, beyond the 55-turn seed spread. What happened:

  • Decline: clears. 191–206 against the p3b2 arms' 57–114 is 77–149 turns, beyond the spread on both seeds of both arms.
  • Serve: matches, does not clear. E2 reads 411 and 423 against ftnarrow's 406. Finding 69 puts the seed spread on B at 37 turns and on C at 16, so a 5- to 17-turn gap on the serve column is not a separation and must not be quoted as one. E1's 362 is below the best end outright.

So the honest statement is the see-saw is broken rather than beaten: no checkpoint before this reached both ends at once, and none of these clears the serve end. "Discrimination installs" is met on the column that had never moved and matched on the column that had.

The honest null is refuted. It read: E2 separates from E1 on grids B/C but neither moves grid A. Both arms move grid A enormously and E2 does separate from E1 on serve — 411/423 against 362/362, a gap of 49–61 turns against within-arm spreads of 0 and 12. So the supply lines are real and balance was the lever, which is the one combination the pre-registration did not enumerate.

What the supply lines bought, and what they did not

Dates and entities inside note text worked, and it is the cleanest cell in the arm. Grid C holds the carrier identical and crosses the content four ways; finding 69 reproduced the collapse on five checkpoints.

date_bearing moves 21–24 → 31 and 30 and real_entity 15–22 → 40 and 42, on both E2 seeds, while plain does not move — which is what a supply line looks like when it is the content and not the carrier that was short. real_entity is the odd one: E1 already reads 34–37 without a single new note body, so most of that cell is balance and only the last few turns are supply.

Wider contact carriers bought back part of what balance cost. send_text runs 39 and 47 of 200 on E1 against 59 and 62 on E2 — recovered, but still short of ftextract's 109. place_call is unbothered throughout (188, 197, 202 and 217 of 224 across the four P3-E seeds).

The messageless family is the line that does not read, and the reason was registered before the run. send_text declares message required, so a text request naming no message has no well-formed call and E2 teaches it as a question. Grid B scores the family expect="send_text" and therefore counts every correct clarification as a miss; the readable row is where the misses go, and it does not separate:

The ask_agent sink shrinks from 16–20 to 6–14 — but E1 does that with no messageless transcript in its corpus at all, and E2's 6 and 10 sit at or inside E1's own 8 and 14 on a 24-turn cell. So balance shrank the relay sink and the dedicated supply line cannot be credited for it on this evidence. What E2 does add is three turns of the taught shape appearing verbatim — (no call) with a question, on both its seeds — which is the behaviour being asked for arriving in a column that scores it as a miss.

Where the cost actually is, because there is one

The decline call now absorbs turns it should serve. Grid B's misses:

fte1 ask_agent 96, decline 85, save_note 13 fte1_seed2 ask_agent 110, decline 62, save_note 6 fte2 ask_agent 98, decline 54, save_note 8 fte2_seed2 ask_agent 75, decline 48, save_note 5, place_call 12

62 and 85 contact requests on E1 are declined outright, falling to 54 and 48 on E2. That is the shape of the trade this arm makes and it is why the serve column matches rather than clears: the model has learned to decline, and it over-applies it to send_text. Nothing else in the grid shows it — place_call runs 188 to 217 of 224, and grid C's decline misses fall from 35 and 51 on E1 to 16 and 29 on E2.

What this licenses, and what it does not

The Phase 3 balance variable is answered: judgement is installable by fine-tune at 260M, from a corpus where the request is the only thing that predicts the label. VISION §8's "this phase is what makes him useful" survives one more test than the sixth finding left it.

What it does not license is a share claim. The registered share is 27% declared and 26.0%/25.5% delivered, and this arm has one dose. Whether the send_text over-decline is a dose effect is the share arm the pre-registration names, and it now has a reason to exist that is a measurement rather than a hunch.

Limits

The supply is nearly spent and the count says so. The decline space is 30 carriers x 210 authored requests x 6 refusal wordings = 37,800 distinct transcripts. Priced at the bracket's ends before registering: 25% declared delivers 25.0% with the class at 68.3% of its space; 40% declared delivers only 33.2%, draws 95.4% of everything there is, drops 62.2% of every draw, and reduces the file's distinct utterances from 87,297 to 79,717. The top of the pre-registered bracket is not deliverable at 30MB, and a share arm has to buy more sentences before it can ask for a bigger dose.

The bracket's floor is not reachable by declaring it either: built at --decline-share 0.25 the payloads delivered 24.95% and 24.31%, below the pre-registered floor on both arms, and were rebuilt at 0.27 rather than reinterpreted.

Grid A's novel column is a weaker holdout after this arm than before it. Its 32 action requests remain string-disjoint from the payload and no payload request sits within three content words of one — check_p3e.py asserts both, and six requests were rewritten to satisfy the second. But the payload now teaches the class those items belong to, so the column measures generalisation inside a trained class rather than to an untrained one. The trained column's frames were always trained; that has not changed.

Two seeds a side, directions not sizes (ground rule 6). Authored fills, not user traffic. Greedy only. The smart_home cell of grid A is still novel-only on every checkpoint. And the decline side of the corpus carries ~6,630 distinct utterances against the served side's ~80,000, each recurring about four times under a different refusal wording — not a copy-slot regime, since a decline transcript copies nothing, but it is the ceiling the next dose runs into.

Addendum, same day — the full frozen-row diff, and the two rows the grid read does not see

scripts/rowdiff.py from the base card to each of the four arm cards, written by a second session after the finding above was committed. The moved set is near-identical across all four arms, and almost all of it moves up: turn2.first and daycross.no_day join tool.selection at 171 → 200/200, turn2.second 177 → 199–200, tool.name_fidelity 106 → 116–119, notefid.trained_phrasing 74 → 101–105, notefid.novel_phrasing 66 → 85–90. The likelihood rows tick up in the third decimal (lm.conversational 0.2905 → 0.2946 nats/byte), the same size and direction as every p3b2 arm. The new rows read as expected for a corpus with no extraction slice and no second-turn class: extract.span 16–21/200 with abstain 1–2 (the no-extraction controls' numbers), turn2.react/reask/ chain_result at the eight-checkpoint baseline's zeros. One direction worth a line: compound.pipe_first reads 32–37/48 on all four arms where ftextract collapsed it 41 → 18 — the nano floor spans 15–41 so this is a direction, not a size, but four arms agreeing at the base's level where one arm halved it is the direction.

Two rows move down, and neither is a regression in the treatment's own terms — but both need recording. chat.abstain_oos drops 33 → 9–13 on every arm, and the named split says why: the row's gold is silence on an uncovered request, and 91 of 100 turns now route to decline — where the base split 36 decline, 30 wrong-tool (save_note 17, web_search 6, place_call 4, send_text 3) and 1 invented tool, the wrong-tool calls vanish entirely. The routed refusal is grid A's gold and the runtime hatches it into a spoken reply; the row's silence gold scores the strictly better behaviour as failure. That is the same tension configs/mix_p3e_ft_e1.py registered before the run, now surfaced on a frozen row: re-gold it to accept a decline call, or retire it in favour of grid A — a decision, not a fix, and it is recorded here as open. Second, smaller: daylog.open and daylog.targeted go 4 → 0 of 90/84 with the why flipping from the base's majority-no_say (63/23 silence over confabulation) to majority-confabulated (86/4) — finding 71's conversion of silence into recitation, arriving as a side effect of a tools fine-tune on rows whose supply gap is already recorded. chat.abstain_greet moves both directions across the four arms (63–77 against the base's 67) and is not readable at two seeds.
