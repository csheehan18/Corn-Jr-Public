The parse and depth · 16 September 2026

Written as one nested line, he builds a structure never trained

The result, as the finding states it

With the parse written as one nested line he writes a three-member group inside a group, a structure the corpus never contains, eighteen times across two seeds and 5.8 times more often where the gold calls for one than where it does not, on the leg that had never given anything

What it cost

14.7 min of scoring on the 2 cards first cited here (nano20m_v3_fw_armf, nano20m_v3_fw_armf_lines). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

PLAN-PARSE-WEIGHTS arm F, read 2026-09-16. Two nano cells on the nested format (nano20m_v3_fw_armf, seeds 1337 and 2337) against a line-format control at the same depth profile (nano20m_v3_fw_armf_lines), all three token- and step-matched to arm W under the C6 protocol. scripts/probe_nestgen.py, scripts/probe_nestfork.py, corn.evals. The gate is a within-checkpoint comparison and the two instruments before it could not make one; that is most of this finding's story.

The result

A three-member group nested inside a group appears nowhere in the corpus: it is nest_three, the holdout parse.w_pairing is cut from, and a group has only ever nested two. Decoded greedily and scored structurally — did he nest, and with how many members — against w_nest_seen, a trained two-member nesting cut at the same fork depth from the same generator:

seed row nested wrote a three rate 1337 w_nest_seen 17 1 0.059 1337 w_pairing 25 9 0.360 2337 w_nest_seen 17 1 0.059 2337 w_pairing 28 9 0.321

both w_nest_seen 2/34 = 0.059 both w_pairing 18/53 = 0.340 5.8x

Both seeds land within 0.04 of each other on the held-out row and identically — 1 of 17, twice — on the trained one. Rule 6 is met: two seeds, one direction, one magnitude.

The mechanism is the thesis's own. A three-member carrier (three things: {a}, {b}, {c}) is trained at the outer level, where GROUP_SIZE puts it at 0.30; nesting is trained with two-member groups. Neither piece is new and the combination is. He reads a known carrier's arity and applies it at a position where he has only ever seen arity two, which is what THESIS section 1 means by recombination over a closed set.

Why it took a third instrument, and why the first two were wrong

parse.w_pairing reads 0 to 1 of 96 on every cell in the line-per-level format — W, both doses, b1024, wide1024 — and C6 widened the nesting inventory from 10 branching structures to 53 and moved it on none of three instruments, two of which bypass copy. The leg had never given anything.

The fork. probe_nestfork.py reads the one token where a bracket opens. Its own docstring says the rows are not comparable to each other, only the column is, because the token before the fork and the tokenisation of the gold token differ between them — 79 of 96 w_nest_seen forks sit immediately after [ and take 'ask', while w_pairing splits 48/48 and takes ' ask' or 'ask'. A gate was nonetheless built on comparing two rows there. It read 70 against 67 on one cut of the rows and 22 against 55 on the next, and both were artifacts of the comparison rather than readings of anything.

The first cut of the comparison row was wrong too, and in the way this phase keeps being wrong: w_nest_seen was cut with a deepest_group that counted a do's lookup group as a nesting. A do composes a lookup with its consumers and is not a group, so on most items the row forked at a structurally different place; only 18 of its 96 items carried a true nesting at all. That is the fourth wrong number in this phase from the same distinction — a 455-span leak, 1,160 false positives in check_funcworld.py, "a group of one" on 216 turns, and this — and it should be one function every tree-walker calls.

What works is probe_nestgen.py: decode the parse, parse it back with the guard's bracket reader, ask only how many members he gave the nesting. One checkpoint, one scoring rule, no local-token term, exact-match not used because half of wrong_arity on the line format was an invented span (FAILURES-PARSE section 1) and a copy slip would swamp a structural signal it has nothing to do with.

The format, and what it is not evidence for

The arm acquires the format: on the fork it reads 22 and 55 of 96 where every line-format checkpoint reads 0 of 96 and the never-saw-a-parse cell reads 3 and 4. That is a model that opens nested brackets, which none before it would, and it says nothing about shape — both rows need a bracket opened in a format only the arm trains. The nested form is also 56.5% of the line form's parse tokens on the 632 frozen items, so it should lift trained rows for length reasons alone; the control exists for that and the parse rows are not compared across formats, because they cannot be.

The kill did not fire, and a pattern worth recording

row W b1024 armf LINES NEST s1 NEST s2 tool.copy 120/120 120/120 102/120 120/120 tool.copy_wide 290/300 269/300 280/300 288/300 temporal.copy 353/353 353/353 353/353 353/353

Seed 1's 102 is an excursion. This is the second single-seed tool.copy dip in this phase that did not replicate — arm A read 100 of 120 and recovered to 119 (finding 139, amended) — so a copy-band kill wants its second seed before it is called, and twice now the first seed would have killed a live arm.

What it does not say

Eighteen productions across two seeds, 0.340 against 0.059, is a signal and not a capability — the same caliber as parse.w_heldout's 10 to 17 of 140. Between 12 and 21 of 96 emissions a row do not parse at all, so the denominators are partial. It is nano, one synthetic world, and rung 4 on real carriers is untouched. parse.w_d5 is unchanged and unread: it forks at bracket depth 4, where this corpus has a node with children in 0 of 3,602 cases, so finding 136's zero-support result holds in this format too and only a positive there could have counted.

And the reading is contestable in one specific place, which is better argued than assumed: following a trained carrier's arity to an untrained position may be called carrier-following rather than structural generalisation. The answer here is that a carrier is a member of the closed set section 1 describes and its arity at a new position is exactly the recombination claimed — but anyone quoting this should quote that sentence with it.

Licenses the arity ladder as the next arm: four-member carriers already exist (GROUP_CORES_MARKED_4, unused under base shapes), so a profile training arities 2, 3 and 4 at the outer level while nesting only 2 puts rung 3 and rung 4 in one corpus, each with the rung below as its floor and the whole comparison inside one checkpoint. One rung can be a fluke of one carrier; three in order is systematic. Five is not available — the carrier pools stop at {d}.
