His word vectors hold less than his framed answers do

The result, as the finding states it

Asked the way finding 221's book is asked — add up, over a description's words, how close each word's vector sits to each candidate label, no sentence and no next-word prediction — the two 20M controls answer 27 and 32 of the second 96 wishes and 61 and 57 of 141 fields from their word table, under their own framed read (35 and 37, 78 and 76) and far under the book (51 and 97). Every one of seven cells reads under the frame: the word table, his state half-way up and at the top for a word read alone, raw or with the common direction taken off, and his own top state for the whole description. The same holds at size: the 100M reads 27 and 69 from its table and the 260M 19 and 60 (34 and 63 at its best cell) against framed reads of 37 and 80 and 50 and 101. So the knowledge the ruler asks for is not sitting in him unread; what he holds comes out through the sentence, and a bag of his word vectors holds less. The book-pressed runs read 23 and 27 and 62 and 50, with the shuffled ones and the controls.

What it cost

The question (owner, 2026-11-13, brainstorming why the book press failed: "Yes", to reading his word table the way the book reads its pages). Finding 222's press moved neither ruler read. That left a list in two halves needing different work: the knowledge is not in him (the press was too soft, pressed the wrong tokens, or he has no room — finding 197), or it is in him and the ruler's read, a label's probability after a framed sentence, does not reach it. Finding 161 saw the second shape once: a linear reader on his state read 46 to 55 of 100 where he wrote 12 to 40. The pre-registration is scripts/probe_table_read.py's docstring.

The read

Every ruler item as probe_book.items() cuts it: the description's words, every one voting, and the candidate labels. A word's vector is read three ways — the mean of its pieces in the word table; his state after half his blocks at the word's last piece, the word read alone; the same after his last block — and a label's score is the sum of the cosines between it and the description's words. Each raw, or centred on the mean vector of the 20,000 commonest words. A seventh cell reads his top state for the whole description against the label's. The cell was chosen on the first 96 wishes by the mean of the two controls, and the second 96 and the fields were read once.

Read by hand: the second 96 at seed 1337

The table and the frame are right about different items: 16 the table gets and the frame misses, 24 the reverse. The table gets descriptions that carry a word near the label — "lazy mornings in a hammock and naps every afternoon" (relaxing), "a candle on every table and roses for two" (romantic), "food on the table within three minutes" (quick). The frame gets the ones that need the whole phrase — "something knocks from inside the walls every night at three" (scary), "an old dog waits at the station for an owner who never comes back" (sad), "a whole meal for the price of a coffee" (cheap) — where the table answers exciting, exciting and quick. By kind the table is near chance (4 of 24) on films, books, songs and shows (5, 4, 6 and 5) and best on holidays (12).

What it licenses

By the pre-registration, T fails: it is not in him by any read here. The book press result (finding 222) stands as "not learned" and not as "learned and unread"; the list narrows to how hard a press can be — a real one is many steps on one meeting, which is rereading — and to room (finding 197). It also says where his understanding lives: a 20M's and a 260M's framed answers both beat every read of their vectors, so the sentence is not what hides it. The framed ruler is the better instrument of the two.

What it does not settle. A reader that is trained — finding 161's was a linear reader on his state, and this one is an untrained cosine — could find more than a sum of cosines does; that is a different probe and needs held-out items the ruler does not yet have. The sixteen items the table gets and the frame misses are not nothing: the two reads err differently at every size, as the book and the 260M did (17 against 16 in finding 221).
