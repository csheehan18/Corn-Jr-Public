Wordings seen over ten times get memorised; key phrases become lists

The result, as the finding states it

Memorise or learn is readable off the data: a wording seen more than ten times is labelled from memory (A3's on cells 74-100% right past ten sightings, 6-47% under eleven, 29-33% never seen), a carrier that settles the answer is learned as a list (62 openings settle every ask/find label of the old parse world and he never cuts a held-out one; the functional world's best key settles 7% and he does), and A3b halves A3's lookup mass — a family contrast registered here before its re-run (probe only)

What it cost

CPU only, no training: probe_lookup.py's three reads about 3 min together (the two parse worlds about a minute each, the move slices and the sightings read about 15 s); a scratch scan of finding 52's openings over six mix_v3_5b sources about 4 min; 10:59-11:13 on 2026-10-10 end to end.

The owner, 2026-10-10: the findings hold both outcomes — a skill learned as the idea on one slice and memorised as a list on another — and which one a slice will teach should be known before it trains. corn/copyaudit.py already answers that for one kind of skill, a span he must copy (findings 12, 13, 14: a value space small enough to learn as a set is recited, not read). This asks it for a decision — a move, a part's label, a route — on the cases already on disk. Post hoc throughout, every number off committed items files and slices: probe only.

The rule read

He takes the cheapest route that fits the training lines. A table is such a route when a cheap key of the input — the whole line, its first one to three words — settles the answer (one value on at least 95% of that key's lines) and recurs often enough to be learned as an entry. The settled mass of a key family is the share of training lines its settled, recurring entries cover; the largest over the families is the slice's lookup route (scripts/probe_lookup.py, cards evals/results/a3_a3b__lookup.json, decomp_funcworld__lookup.json, decomp_funcworld_askfind__lookup.json).

How often is often enough: ten sightings, at nano, one pass

A3's slice trains once (configs/mix_v3_a3_on.py: the weight is the file), so a wording's count in data/raw/social_a3_move.txt is how many times he saw it. His label on every A3 probe item that has a gold move, against that count (--sightings):

Past ten sightings he labels the wording from memory at both seeds; at ten or fewer he is near his rate on wordings he never saw (pooled 9 of 34 against 116 of 374). The off cells' 19-20 of 41 at 61+ are the stock forms the word alone primes (finding 157). Ten is a nano, one-pass figure: a larger model or more passes memorises sooner (finding 52, below, at ~50 passes).

The pair that separates: the old parse world and the functional one

Sixty-two two-word openings settle every ask/find label of the old world, each seen hundreds of times, and no whole part ever repeats — so the table he could build was the opening, and a held-out opening read zero on every arm. A3b's 1,021 hand-written phrasings halve the whole-line table (0.756 to 0.378) and put the slice between the two groups, which is what makes its re-run a test of where the line is rather than a confirmation.

Two readings the bracket gets wrong, both kept

The cheapest settled key is not always the one he takes. In A3, rough opens 41 training lines and every one is consolation; on "rough day at work / school / the shop / the office" he writes greeting on all four at seed 1337, and open on three and greeting on one at seed 2337 (hand read, the items files). consolation's wordings are seen 0-16 times each, mostly under ten, so by the curve above they were never memorised, and the settled first word did not stand in. In the old parse world, where no whole part repeats, the opening was learned as the list. So which key he memorises is not predicted by settledness alone — the whole line when it repeats enough, a cheaper key at least sometimes when it does not — and the bracket reports every family rather than pretending to know. The thin family also says the second condition: a slice with little lookup mass still needs enough distinct evidence per answer, and 136 consolation wordings taught neither the list nor the move.

Finding 52, read on openings across the whole mix, does not fit. The twenty searchgrid templates' openings were scanned over mix_v3_5b's six sources with user turns, scoring how often each opening is followed by web_search: ten of the twenty are pure (1.000) and they route anywhere from 0 to 15 of 16; Spearman against routing -0.19 (scratch scan, not committed — a bound). Read on whole lines it fits: the tools slice is 2.5 MB at a weight that makes about 50 passes in the 5B run (bytes/4 as tokens), so each of its 2,986 distinct search lines was seen ~50 times — a whole-line table covering the slice, and on a new subject only four templates route. Why those four (google, three spellings of look up) is not in the slice; the guess is the web text's priors, the same guess check_copyable.py writes down for the contact slot, and it is not measured.

The copy audit, read the same way

No new numbers. check_copyable.py's two controls — 28 search answers at 107 uses, 400 field names at 170 — are tables whose entries the request settles and which recur far past ten; both were recited. Its counterexample, 1,442 dates at 122 uses read 353/353, is where it concludes "cardinality and not exposure": read here, a date is not settled by the request (it moves with the anchor), so no table fits however often a date recurs. That is a reading, not a measurement of the slice.

Registered before A3b's re-run

A3b died twice on 2026-10-09 (a chain stopped at iter 5,200 of 5,337, then a power-off at ~4,720) and has no finished checkpoint at either seed; nothing from it has been read. The pre-registration of record stays configs/mix_v3_a3b.py's; this adds one contrast the rule makes and the word counter does not:

A3b family max settled mass distinct wordings word counter, unseen (of 20) wellbeing 0.81 458 14 thanks 0.70 575 12 consolation 0.37 478 10 farewell 0.50 497 17

  • The contrast. On the unseen probe (HELD_OUT_PROBE, 20 a family), his labels on consolation + farewell (40 items) beat wellbeing + thanks (40) by at least 5 at both seeds. A bag of words fit on the same slice has the two pools level (27 against 26), so a gap of that size is the lookup route and not lexical ease. The confound named now: if a seed's default value lands in one pool it inflates that pool; the read reports how many of each pool's items carry the seed's commonest label beside the count.
  • The curve replicates. On A3b's own slice, items whose wording is seen more than ten times are labelled right on at least 80% at both seeds.
  • No call on the total. The registered gate (unseen +10 over A3) is where A3b falls between the groups above; a pass puts the line above a 0.38 whole-line route at nano, a miss puts it below.

What it licenses

  • A design rule for any slice that teaches a decision, before the freeze: no example line recurs more than about ten times in a nano pass (cap it or widen it); where the answer is structural, no cheap key settles it (funcgen's one pool across labels); and every answer gets enough distinct evidence. probe_lookup.py --moves / --parts reads the first two on a slice in seconds.
  • Not a guard yet. The bracket is fit on four cases chosen after reading them; a guard like check_copyable.py waits on a single-variable test — the same slice with its lookup route cut and nothing else changed — and on A3b's contrast above.

What it does not say

That the lookup route is the cause: every case here varies more than one thing, A3b included. Anything at 260M, where ten sightings is too many (52). Which key he takes when several settle the answer (the rough read). That a slice with no lookup route teaches the idea: it is necessary on these cases, not sufficient (consolation). A key nobody lists — a left edge mid-line (findings 28-29's mechanism), a length, a position — reads clean here and is not.

Amended 2026-10-10: the bracket on extraction finds nothing to fix (probe only)

Asked by the owner: does the lens explain why extraction stalled (the whole-passage reader at 526 of 721 on Population A, finding 117, against a target above 85%)? Four reads, CPU only, through scratch scripts not on disk — every number below is a bound on an unpinned instrument: the gold of a training window is the longest run of the say's words found in the window, and a "name" is a capitalised word of four letters or more that is not a sentence's first word or a question word.

No cheap rule settles the training answers. On extract.txt, extract_5b.txt and C3's readback_passage.txt, the gold is the first value of its type in 33-43% of windows that carry a same-type rival, and the value nearest the question's words in 13-33%. Finding 66's "first value of the right type" is not something these slices reward.

The hard case is not rarer in training, and he is not worse on it.

nearest same-type rival Population A (share, reader right) extract.txt extract_5b.txt readback_passage.txt within 5 words 52% 76% 49% 50% 51% 6-15 words 27% 71% 27% 26% 27% 16+ words 14% 77% 9% 10% 16% no rival 7% 84% 14% 14% 6%

By sentence the same: a rival in the gold's own sentence is 70% of Population A's typed answers and 66-72% of the slices', and the reader is 75% right with one there and 75% with one only elsewhere. So neither half of the rule — a table to take, or a case the data never forces — explains the residual at this resolution. What does split: golds that are neither a number nor a name (descriptive phrases, 125 of 721) read 61% right against 75% for typed ones, and ten wrong answers drawn at random (Random(1)) show extent-heavy golds ("204,408 in 2013", "7.6% Per Annum", "the Indian national cricket team") — the boundary and label question findings 104 and audit_refused_wrong.py already price, not a data shape this bracket reads.

For C3: the read-back slice matches Population A on every axis read here, so nothing in this amendment asks it to change before F1. Finding 66's own case — two values in one clause told apart by a single verb — is finer than a word count can see, and stays the open one.

Amended 2026-10-10 (second): the causal test did not show the lever

Finding 159 read both registrations this finding made or fed. The pair (cap against rep at the same size, word counter level): level at seed 1337 (-6), unreadable at seed 2337 — no guard is built. The family contrast registered above fails: +5 and +2, the one pass sitting on the seed's default label. What stands from this finding is the description — a wording seen past about ten sightings in a nano pass is labelled from memory, and a slice whose answer is settled by such a table teaches the table — not the recipe: a slice with the table removed interpolates to near phrasings at one seed and still reaches far ones no better (finding 159's three regimes).
