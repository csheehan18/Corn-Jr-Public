Measurement and noise · 26 August 2026

At 260M the seed barely moves scores, measured for the first time

The result, as the finding states it

The seed moves nothing on the frozen rows, and that is the error bar every 260M finding has been missing

What it cost

12.6 min of scoring on the 1 card first cited here (corn_jr_260m_ftnarrow_seed2); it also reads 2 cards scored for an earlier finding (corn_jr_260m, corn_jr_260m_ftwide_seed2). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

corn_jr_260m_ftwide_seed2 and corn_jr_260m_ftnarrow_seed2, 2026-08-26, scored by corn.evals against the frozen evals/ set. R4 of docs/PLAN-P3-RULERS.md, which pre-registered no threshold on purpose: the deliverable is the spread itself, to be quoted by every later 260M finding. No training — both checkpoints were on disk and unscored, which is why the plan called this the cheapest read in the repository.

The two things that make it a comparison

Both arms are the seed-1 run with seed: 1337 changed to 1338 and nothing else: same init_from (the frozen corn_jr_260m), same 30MB payload, same 786 max_iters, same lr, same dtype. And the invocation is the seed-1 one flag for flag — bare python -m corn.evals --ckpt … --json …, every section on, --max-samples at its default of 8, --data-dir defaulting to the checkpoint's own. Ground rule 4 of the plan is that comparability is declared rather than assumed, and a scorecard taken under different flags would have broken it inside its own workstream.

One difference is not the seed and has to be said. The selection rule (mean-log-per-source) picked iter 700 for ftwide_seed2 and iter 600 for ftnarrow_seed2, against 786 for both seed-1 runs. So "the seed" here means the seed and the checkpoint it caused to be selected, which is what a re-run of this rig actually varies.

The widest movers anywhere in the behaviour block are not in that list:

notefid.novel_phrasing 86/120 → 80/120 87/120 → 79/120 extract.brevity 238/400 → 224/400 243/400 → 231/400 chat.abstain_oos 47/100 → 50/100 38/100 → 37/100 tool.selection 188/200 → 190/200 unchanged turn2.second 178/200 → 180/200 182/200 → 180/200

So the error bar, stated as a number to quote: ±2 items on extract.span, ±14 of 400 on extract.brevity, ±8 of 120 on notefid.novel_phrasing, and zero on every row at ceiling. A 260M effect smaller than that is wobble; the sixty-third finding's 72/200 → 198/200 and the sixty-fifth's 9/200 → 70/200 are two and three orders of magnitude outside it.

val_loss is not the instrument, and this is the cleanest demonstration yet

checkpoint iter val_loss behaviour rows that moved ftwide 786 0.9305 — ftwide_seed2 700 0.8380 9 rows, none by more than 6 items ftnarrow 786 0.9344 — ftnarrow_seed2 600 0.9252 7 rows, none by more than 8 items

ftwide_seed2 sits 0.093 nats below its seed-1 twin — the largest val gap anywhere in this family — and scores the same behaviour to within a handful of items, with the four lm rows agreeing to three decimal places (temporal 0.1923 → 0.1910, the rest inside 0.0002). A run that picked the checkpoint by val alone would have called that a materially better model.

What it does not say

This is the spread of the frozen rows, and it does not transfer to live instruments. The sixty-ninth finding measures the same four checkpoints on harness/toolgrid.py and reads a seed spread of 26 to 55 turns of 208 on the decline column — an order of magnitude wider, on the same weights, on the same day. Two seeds a side is also two, not five: the nineteenth and forty-third findings both had to widen a floor when the third and fourth seeds arrived, and nothing here is safe from that.
