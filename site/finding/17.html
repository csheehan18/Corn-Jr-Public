Measurement and noise · 17 August 2026

One test moved 18 items on the random seed alone

The result, as the finding states it

The row that never moved moves eighteen items on the seed alone

What it cost

7.1 min of scoring on the 2 cards first cited here (nano20m_balanced_seed2, nano20m_narr10_temp11); it also reads 1 card scored for an earlier finding (nano20m_balanced). Backfilled 2026-09-26 from the cards' seconds; training and probe time were never recorded, so this is a floor.

Corrected by the eighteenth finding. The headline number below is real and its explanation is wrong. tool.copy's 18 items are not 18 independent draws — that row is 120 prompts over six answers, and the fall is one of them. The general claim, that the generation-scored rows are noisy where the language rows are not, survives on tool.validity and variety.distinct3; the tool.copy half of it is a resolution limit rather than variance. Read the two findings together.

Two runs, and the second one invalidates part of the first.

The attribution arm. The sixteenth finding cut narrative and temporal together and tool.copy fell 120/120 → 111/120 on a tool slice byte-identical to balanced's, which left the regression unattributed. nano20m_narr10_temp11 restores temporal to balanced's exact 14,570,815 tokens and leaves narrative cut at 10.00%, so the three corpora form a triple with one moving slice between each adjacent pair. 1,050.9s.

The seed replicate. nano20m_balanced_seed2 is nano20m_balanced with seed 1337 → 1338 and nothing else — same data_dir, same bins, same bytes on disk, 2 differing fields of 30 and one of them only names an output directory. 1,045.7s. tool.copy moves 18 items on the seed alone, and the corpus change the sixteenth finding wrote up moved 9. The row has read 120/120 on every corpus with an unrepeated tool slice and was treated throughout as a stable ceiling; it is not one. tool.validity moves 13 items on the seed alone. Both are larger than any corpus effect either row has been asked to report since the thirteenth finding.

The language rows are nearly deterministic and the behaviour rows are not

This is the shape of it, and it is why the confusion lasted sixteen findings. Across the seed pair the three nats/byte rows reproduce to 0.0002, 0.0003 and 0.0004 — and the same holds across corpora wherever a share is held fixed: the two corpora sharing narrative at 10.0000% read 0.4667 and 0.4670, the two sharing temporal at 11.1283% read 0.0944 and 0.0943. Slice share determines the language rows to four decimal places regardless of seed.

The behaviour rows, on the same checkpoints, swing by 10–18 items. They are a few hundred greedy generations scored exact-match, and nothing about a clean loss curve constrains them. So the pipeline is reproducible and the rulers built on top of it are noisy, which is the opposite of the failure the project was watching for.

The attribution question is answered, and then made moot

Restoring temporal restored everything temporal — lm temporal to within 0.0001 of balanced, temporal.copy to a perfect 353/353, temporal.route to 399/400 — and left tool.copy at 114/120. On its face that exonerates the temporal slice: it recovered its own rows completely and did not carry tool.copy with it.

The seed replicate then says the question was never readable. 120 → 111 → 114 spans 9 items against an 18-item swing from nothing at all, and the failure modes never agreed either — wrong_answer 9 in one arm, trained_answer 6 in the other, wrong_answer 18 in the replicate. temporal.copy and tool.copy ask the same question of two different tools, and whether the first buys the second is still open, now for want of a measurement rather than for want of an arm.

The third corpus did leave one thing that is not noise. It sits at an endpoint or between the other two on all three moving shares, and reads outside both on tool.validity, schema.conform and variety.distinct3 simultaneously. No slice share can produce that, and naming it is what prompted the replicate.

What this reprices, and what it does not touch

The large effects are untouched, and it is worth saying which, because the reflex after a result like this is to distrust everything:

The middle row is the one that costs something. "2.5% is better on every axis that matters" was carried by tool.copy 99 → 120 (21 items), tool.validity 168 → 171 (3) and temporal.route 398 → 400 (2). Only the first is even comparable to an 18-item seed swing, and the one movement clearly outside noise — schema.conform 194 → 172, 22 items — runs the other way. The honest form is that 2.5% costs 22 items of schema conformance and buys nothing yet shown to exist. That does not restore 10%; it means the share is unpriced, which is a different and worse position than the one §11 recorded.

variety.distinct3 moved 1.85 points on the seed alone, against 0.1 across the largest corpus change the project has made and 1.39 across all three corpora. Its first noise estimate is larger than every corpus effect yet measured with it.

Three things worth recording

The row treated as unreadable moved least. tool.selection has carried "differences under ~35 are not readable" since the thirteenth finding, inferred from adjacent checkpoints of one run. Across the seed pair it moved 4 items, while tool.copy — assumed a ceiling — moved

  1. The two assumptions the series has been running on were both backwards, and no amount of staring at scorecards would have shown it.

The blended val is noise-dominated, again. The seed pair's val differs by 0.0481 while its frozen-set language rows differ by 0.0004 — two orders of magnitude, on identical data. §9's finding that composition sampling is 71% of that row's variance now has an independent corroboration it did not ask for.

One pair is not a distribution. This establishes that an 18-item movement on tool.copy occurs with no cause. It does not give a standard deviation, and treating 18 as a threshold would be the same error one level down. What it licenses is the weaker claim that small deltas on the generation-scored rows were over-read, and the practical one: behaviour-row comparisons need replicates, and at 17.5 minutes a run there is no excuse not to have them.
