The reply judge · 22 October 2026

The small model answers half of even its own style's openers

The result, as the finding states it

The 148 messages of the reply read are not his conversational style: at matched length (4-9 words) the plain 5M finds them 3.91 nats a token surprising against 2.25 for real conversational openers he never read and 2.39 for ones he did — so he has not memorised his openers, and the hand-written messages sit far outside the SODA-style dialogue he learned conversation from. But the register is the smaller part of his incoherence: on 60 real openers in his own style that he never read, he answers coherently 29 and 37 times (0.48 and 0.62, blind; the real replies 60 of 60) against 0.47 and 0.37 on the everyday messages. 21 of the 148 messages appear verbatim as a turn in his reading (mostly "Hi.", "Thanks!", "How are you doing today?"); he passes them a little more often (0.67 and 0.43 against 0.44 and 0.36), as do short easy messages generally

What it cost

the overlap search (CPU, ~1 min), scripts/probe_message_style.py (seconds of card), scripts/probe_reply_instyle.py (generation ~1 min, contended) and the blind read of 180 replies, 14:55-15:15 on 2026-10-22. ETA 15 min, actual ~20.

The question (owner, 2026-10-22: "are you asking things that would have been in their training?"). Findings 202-206 read the 5M's coherence on probe_reply.py's hand-written everyday messages and found it near 47% whatever was changed; finding 208 found the shipped 260M at 83% on the same.

The reads

Overlap. Of the 148 messages, 38 occur somewhere in the decoded conversational and chat reading, 21 as a whole turn — the short and generic ones. Blind pass rates on those 21 against the other 127: 5M seed 1 0.67 against 0.44, seed 2 0.43 against 0.36; the 260M 0.81 against 0.83.

The blind is imperfect on the real reply — it is longer and more specific than the model's and easy to pick out.

Read by hand

In his own style he handles the greetings and set pieces cleanly ("Hey, Pablo. What's up?" -> "Not much. Just hanging out."; a shop's "We have a great selection of shoes! What type are you looking for?") and fails as on everyday messages: "Oh, thank you so much! I really appreciate it." to a pay cut, "Oh, wow! That's amazing! How did you do it?" to a twisted ankle, the stock "I'm sorry, I didn't mean to hurt you" to "Why the hell did you do that?". The SODA openers themselves (the unread sample) are what the owner's messages are not: named greetings ("Hi Mirabel. How are you feeling today?"), set roles (doctor, mother, shopkeeper), melodrama ("I'm scared of tomorrow. I don't want to die.").

What it licenses

  • The 5M's coherence ceiling is mostly the model, not the register: in its own style it still answers about half. Findings 202-206's flat results stand on in-style ground too; finding 208's 83% at 260M is the size and reading.
  • The register gap is real and costs something on top (a 5M is roughly 10-15 points better in its own style; 60 items, noisy): the conversation he learns from does not contain the everyday, short, first-person messages the owner sends — "Maybe.", "Long day.", "I spilled coffee on my keyboard" — which is also where the 260M is weakest (finding 208: short and vague 11 of 20). That is a corpus item for 1.0: real conversational text in the owner's register, varied, not generated.

What it does not settle. One reader; 60 in-style items; whether real everyday-register text moves the 5M, or only the larger models.
