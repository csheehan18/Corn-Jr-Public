The corpus mix · 17 August 2026

The vocabulary test really measures how much web text is mixed in

The result, as the finding states it

The bar measures how much web is in the corpus

§5.0 measured five slices in isolation, rejected narrative at 6,863 distinct words, and has gated the conversational search ever since. What it never measured is the blend — and no slice is what gets trained on. scripts/vocab.py --bins samples a built token stream at 800 even intervals, decodes it, and measures the vocabulary the model actually sees.

The per-slice reading badly overstates the damage. mix_balanced is 46.80% TinyStories and 11.13% temporal — the two slices §5.0 names as the worst offenders, at 6,863 and 182 distinct words — and it blends to 31,544 at 50.6%, which is within a rounding error of the bar rather than far beneath it. And mix_narrative10 blends to 45,369, higher than FineWeb-Edu's own 42,755, because registers contribute vocabulary that does not overlap: a blend can carry more distinct types than any of its parts.

The bar does not predict the thing it exists to protect

Blend vocabulary spans 66% across these eight corpora, 27,364 to 45,369. Over the same eight, variety.distinct3 spans 2.33 points — against its own measured seed floor of 1.85.

A 66% swing in the quantity the bar measures produces essentially nothing in the quantity the bar was built to defend, and what little there is runs the wrong way. What blend vocabulary does predict, at −0.976, is lm web — because it is very nearly a restatement of how much web is in the corpus, and the nats/byte rows measure that directly, at a floor of 0.0038, without the intermediate step.

So §5.0's bar should not gate the conversational slice. It is a proxy for web share dressed as a quality threshold, and the project already has the direct measurement.

The caveat is real and cuts at the other ruler

Eight corpora, one model size, one budget. More importantly the outcome variable is barely resolvable: variety.distinct3 spans 2.33 points across every corpus the project has built and 1.85 of that is seed noise. A null correlation against a near-unresolvable outcome is weak evidence of no effect — it is equally consistent with the output ruler being too blunt to see one. §11 already carries that complaint about variety.distinct3, including that half its prompts are TinyStories openings, so this finding implicates both rulers and the honest statement is "no relationship detectable at this resolution", not "no relationship".

That does not rescue the bar. A screen that cannot be shown to predict anything downstream, and which correlates at −0.976 with a row already measured better, should not be what blocks the largest unsourced share in the corpus.

What it unblocks

SODA comes back. Right register — everyday two-person dialogue, median turn 14, p90 31 — CC BY 4.0, freely fetchable, already measured, and rejected on this bar alone. At 15,942 distinct it would pull the blend down, and nothing here says that costs anything a nats/byte row would not show. The test that was always the right one is an arm rather than a screen: SODA at 20%, read against lm narrative, lm web and variety.distinct3, priced the way every other share in this project has been.

A bug in the tool, caught by two corpora agreeing too well

The first version of --bins filled a word budget in file order and stopped when it was full, which read the first 58% of the stream. Sources are written in spec order, so that silently dropped whichever slices were declared last. It reported mix_narrative10 and mix_narr10_temp11 as identical to the digit — two corpora that differ only in the temporal slice, which sits in the tail. Taking a fixed quota from every window fixes it. A second pass found that trimming words by line discarded whole windows on long-line sources, biasing the blend against web, the widest-vocabulary slice; words are now trimmed by token fragment and only turn lengths by line.
