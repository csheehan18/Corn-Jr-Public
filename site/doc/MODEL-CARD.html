Corn-Jr

A 260M-parameter assistant trained from scratch — tokenizer, corpus, weights, harness, everything — to run entirely on your own machine and route what you ask into a small set of real tools: web search, phone calls, texts, notes, date resolution, day recall, and a relay to a larger agent. He is not a chat model with tools bolted on. The corpus was purpose-built around tool transcripts, and the project's defining rule is that he never fabricates what an observation did not say.

Release 0.5 is text-only. A voice client exists in harness/voice/ (push-to-talk, local Whisper in, local TTS out) and is deliberately not part of this release: it has not been measured on the shipped checkpoint, and an unmeasured half is not something to hand somebody on day one.

Corn-Jr, in his own words

The runtime speaks these verbatim. Asked who he is, from IDENTITY_REPLIES in corn/agent.py:

"I'm Corn-Jr, a small AI assistant that runs on this machine and gets things done by calling tools."

"Corn-Jr. I'm a language model somebody trained from scratch, wired up to a handful of real tools."

"Corn-Jr, at your service. Small model, your machine, real tools."

Asked what he can do, he opens with a line like "What I can actually go and do:" and then reads the live tool registry — composed fresh each turn from the tools that process actually has, so it can never go stale or claim a capability that is not wired up.

Asked about a life he does not have — how his weekend was, what he had for lunch — he says so plainly (finding 125, NO_LIFE_ROUTED_REPLIES):

"That one's not a thing for me. I'm a model, not a person."

One line about his name, because it matters if you rename him. The name lives in packs/persona.md and the owner's name in packs/about-me.md. With neither file present he is Corn-Jr and never says your name at all — neither name is in the weights, and that is on purpose (VISION §4: the shipped artefact is user-neutral, a running instance is one person's).

What this actually is

  • 260,613,120 parameters — 20 layers, 1024 embed, 512 context, a custom 8k BPE tokenizer (bpe8k) trained on the project's own corpus.
  • Pretrained on ~5B tokens of a purpose-built mix (mix_v4_5b): FineWeb-Edu web text, SODA-derived dialogue, Natural Questions-derived extraction, and generated tool-transcript slices from the repo's own generators.
  • Shipped checkpoint: corn_jr_260m_v4_ft_social8, a fine-tune from ft4g that added the declared social turn at depth (finding 123 — a second social turn on a shared opening with one unrelated exchange in front of it). It ships paired with a reading adapter, reader_whole_social8_s1.pt, re-fit on this trunk (finding 124).
  • Ships with its harness: a local server, a grammar-constrained decoder that holds tool arguments to copyable spans of your own words, a confirm gate on side-effectful actions, and a terminal client.

A question about a row of one of your CSV packs is resolved in code — the row matched by name, the column by its own words then by a synonym tier — and the sentence composed from the cell, so there is nothing for a 260M model to get wrong. What he cannot do — from the findings, not from modesty

  • The first turn of a conversation routes far worse than the second, and the fix is a runtime patch rather than a cure. The corpus puts a tool call after every second <|user|> and teaches the first turn nothing, so on an empty history "note down buy milk" declined. Measured across 3,644 turns: notes 177/1000, texts 84/648, calls 32/174 on an empty history against 702, 352 and 132 after any tool turn. A seeded first turn ships on by default and reproduces the after-tool column to the cell. What it does not buy: nine held-out and novel note phrasings stay at zero, "text {contact} {message}" stays at 6/36, and a trailing full stop still halves calls.
  • He calls and texts only you (runtime change, 2026-10-13, after the numbers on this card were read). A request for anyone else is refused with an offer to text you a reminder — "I can't message Sam — only you. Shall I text you a reminder to let them know?" — and nothing leaves the machine for them. The weights still write the name they heard into the call, and on "Text Carla Mendez I'm running late" that slot can swallow the message; the refusal sheds it, so it names Carla Mendez and not the sentence.
  • Out-of-scope requests are refused, but often by serving the refusal rather than holding it back — chat.abstain_oos reads 12/100, and checkup's held_back 2/5.
  • Extraction is real and is not a search engine. Through the shipped reading adapter on Population A: 517 of 721 answerable passages right at 197 of 282 correct silences, at ~23 ms a passage (finding 124, finding 117). The generate path on the same population reads 230 right at 256 silences — so the adapter trades some silence for many more answers, and that is the trade you are getting.
  • Multi-turn is on, the window keeps about four turns, and he uses one. The transcript carries across turns by default. The context is 512 tokens; with the shipped reserve and history fold (--budget 120 --fold-history, defaults since 2026-09-07) the trimmer holds the last four tool turns every time and six chat turns, where the earlier defaults held one tool turn. Nothing reissues a previous query or user line at depth 1 or 3 (0/36 and 0/72). But the multi-turn shape is ~0.34% of the training mix, a pronoun chain across turns reads 34/50 (turn2.chain_pronoun), and a follow-up composes off the turn immediately before it or not at all — a lookup, one turn of anything else, then "what about spain?" composes 0 of 5 whichever settings keep the lookup in the window. Say the whole question.
  • He is not a companion, and the arm that tried to make him one failed. Asked for a joke, a preference, an opinion, or handed a compliment, an insult or affection, he answers 10 of 78 acceptably: he engages, and then does not hand the conversation back. Arm 11 wrote 144 lines for exactly this and moved the row not at all — what answers instead is the pretraining prior, where "I love you" is answered by a person who loves you back (PLAN-PERSONALITY, the twelfth section). A consolation family does exist and reads 13/28.
  • He does not know who he is without the file. Asked his name, the weights say somebody else's; the runtime answers from the identity pool instead. Four training arms failed to put the name in the weights (PLAN-PERSONALITY, arms 9 through 9h4), and the honest reading is that a 260M model pretrained on 20% dialogue has a prior about "who are you" that a fine-tune does not outvote.
  • Live search routes far better than it answers. Routing is at ceiling (searchgrid 400/400 routed, 400/400 kept the subject), but what comes back from the web is a lead sentence or a reader's span, and on a full 2026-09-06 sitting that meant "It's Apollo 11." for when the first moon landing was, "William Faulkner" for who wrote Hamlet, and "c" for the speed of light. Wikipedia's abstract is the first provider, and the answer is only as good as the abstract. Wikipedia also rate-limits after a burst of queries — a few hundred in minutes — and he then says "Wikipedia's blocking my searches right now" until it lifts.
  • Follow-ups that need the last answer are answered from the weights. "Who is the president of France" then "how old is he?" gets "He's 18" with no tool called; "what about pikachu?" composes a fresh lookup but "and how fast is it?" does not. The chain rows (chaingrid) read 0 of 8 composed at depth 2 and 3 on both carriers. Ask the whole question.
  • The calendar is read by code, not by the model, and it reads ahead as well as back. "Last Wednesday", "three days ago" and "the 14th" resolve, and since 2026-09-07 so do "30 days from now", "in two weeks", "the 25th of December", "Christmas", "Easter", "Thanksgiving" and "two weeks after Christmas". "How many days till Christmas" is answered with the count and the date; "how many days till my birthday" reads the birthday: line in packs/about-me.md and tells you where to put one if it is missing. What it still does not do is a range ahead ("next month", "later this week"), and it says so instead of asking you to repeat the date.
  • A tools.d tool is reached by its summary, not by magic. Grid A shows him routing to declared tools he never trained on at 22/24 — but on their own carriers. "List my notes" was saved as a note and "echo hello" was answered as chat on the shipped examples. Write the summary the way you will ask for it, and test it in the studio's tools tab.
  • No reminders, no message reading, no calendar. He will tell you so.

Hardware and latency

Developed and measured on a single RTX 3080-class card (10 GB). Full fp32 weights fit with room for a fine-tune; the model loads in 3.7 s, which a resident server pays once and a per-turn process would pay every turn.

On the shipped revision, VISION §3 budgets the whole round trip at 500 ms and the measured turn is median 172 ms, p90 351 ms, max 401 ms. A turn that is only speech is 54 ms; a turn with a tool in it, 190 ms. Date and recall turns come back in 1–6 ms because the runtime answers them without a decode. Reading a whole passage costs ~23 ms.

A full fine-tune of the model is ~25 minutes (1,200 iterations at batch 8 × grad-accum 6 × block 512, compile=True). One warning from experience: at 10 GB, another application holding a couple of gigabytes of video memory will push a training run into host-memory fallback, where it slows by an order of magnitude and can stop returning altogether. Give training the card.

CPU-only is untested. The latency numbers the client prints per turn will tell you immediately what you are in for.

Privacy

Everything runs locally, and --search is the single flag that sends anything off your machine — it is opt-in, it says so when enabled, and without it web_search answers from a table baked into the corpus. A privacy backstop refuses to forward questions that are about your own records ("what did I note down", "what's my name", "read me my texts") even when search is on; those are answered from your local files or not at all. The backstop reads both the query he wrote and the line you typed — since 2026-09-07, because on a seeded first turn the query that left was sometimes the seed's own "what day is it" while the private question sat in your line — and a query built out of words nobody said is re-decoded without the seed or replaced by your own line before it goes anywhere. Your notes, people, schedule, packs and conversation logs are plain files in your own folder, gitignored from birth and never in the weights. Personalisation lives in those editable local stores by design, so a shipped update never carries anyone's name and an upgrade is a checkpoint swap rather than a migration.

The runtime answers some turns itself, and here is exactly which

A small set of turns never reaches the model. This is disclosed rather than hidden because you would otherwise credit the model for them:

  • the identity hatch — who are you, are you an AI, are you a person, are you some other assistant, and questions about a life he does not have (35 phrasings plus 31 life carriers, finding 125);
  • the social hatch — greetings, wellbeing, thanks, farewells, acknowledgements and a stated low mood, answered from hand-written pools;
  • the temporal hatch — "what day is it", "what time is it" and date recall, answered without a decode (the clock is the machine's own; before 2026-09-07 "what time is it" went to the web and came back "It's 1989.");
  • the store hatch — 37 phrasings of "what do you know about me", routed to your own local store rather than trusted to the weights;
  • the confirm gate — the yes/no on a call or a text;
  • the seed turn — one hatched date turn the runtime runs itself at start and at every reset, so no conversation opens on an empty history.

Every turn in the session log carries an author field saying whether the model or the runtime wrote it, and where a pool covered for him it also records the draft he would have said. You can audit exactly how often the net catches him.

⚠️ The warning that goes in bold

The server has no authentication and it executes tools. Binding --host beyond 127.0.0.1 is a decision, not a default. The confirmation gates protect you against the model; they do not protect you against the network.

Versioning and upgrades

A release is weights + harness at a matched pair of versions (info reports both halves, and every scorecard is stamped with the git revision it was read on). Your personal files — notes, packs (the people table among them), the schedule, transcripts — live outside the model in plain JSON, JSON Lines and text, so an upgrade is a checkpoint swap and nothing of yours is inside the thing being swapped. They do not yet carry a format version of their own: server.json is checked key by key and refuses an unknown one loudly, but a future harness meeting an older notes file would read what it recognises rather than stop. Expect new tags as training runs survive their regression gates, with this card's numbers re-run each time rather than re-worded.
